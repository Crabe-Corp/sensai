# Qin Ce

## Qin 1


### Wei Yang Fled Wei and Entered Qin

Wei Yang fled from Wei to Qin. King Xiao appointed him as prime minister and granted him the fief of Shang, bestowing upon him the title "Shang Jun." Shang Jun governed Qin, and the laws and decrees were strictly enforced. Justice was impartial; punishments did not avoid the powerful, and rewards did not favor relatives or intimates. The law even applied to the crown prince, who had his tutors subjected to facial branding and nose-cutting. After one year, there were no lost items left on the road for others to pick up, and people did not take things without right. The military became greatly strengthened, and the feudal lords feared Qin. Yet his rule was harsh and lacking in mercy; it merely imposed dominance through strength. King Xiao implemented these policies for eight years, but fell seriously ill and did not recover. He wished to pass the throne to Shang Jun, who declined and refused it. After King Xiao died, King Hui succeeded him. Soon after assuming power, Shang Jun requested to return home. Someone advised King Hui, saying: "When a minister holds too much power, the state is in danger; when those close to the ruler are overly intimate, there is deep peril." Nowadays, even women and children in Qin speak of Shang Jun's laws, not mentioning the King's laws. This means that Shang Jun has become the real ruler while Your Majesty has become his subject. Moreover, Shang Jun is indeed Your Majesty's enemy; I beg you to deal with him." When Shang Jun returned home, King Hui had him torn apart by chariots, and the people of Qin did not pity him.


### Su Qin Begins to Advocate Horizontal Alliances

Su Qin began to advocate the strategy of horizontal alliances, persuading King Hui of Qin, saying: "Your Majesty's state has the advantages of Ba, Shu, and Hanzhong in the west; the resources of Huhe and Dai horses in the north; the natural barriers of Wushan and Qianzhong to the south; and the strong defenses of Xiao Pass and Hangu Pass to the east. The land is fertile and beautiful, the people prosperous and wealthy; there are ten thousand chariots for war and a million soldiers ready to fight. The rich plains stretch for thousands of li, with abundant reserves of supplies. The geographical position is strategically convenient—this is why it is called the Imperial Granary, the mighty power of the world. With Your Majesty's wisdom, the multitude of your people and soldiers, the use of chariots and cavalry, and the teachings of military strategy, you can unite the feudal lords, conquer the world, proclaim yourself emperor, and govern. I hope Your Majesty will give this some thought; please allow me to present its effectiveness."

King Qin said, "I have heard that a bird with un 丰满 feathers cannot fly high; one who has not completed his writings cannot impose punishments; one whose virtue and morality are not substantial cannot command the people; and where government policies and teachings are not in order, it is inappropriate to trouble the senior ministers. Now you, Master, have come all the way from a thousand li away with such solemn instruction in court; I would prefer to discuss this on another occasion." Su Qin said, "I was originally doubtful that Your Majesty could not put it into practice. In the past, Shen Nong attacked Bu Sui; Huangdi attacked Zhuolu and captured Chiyou; Yao attacked Huandou; Shun attacked Sanmiao; Yu attacked Gonggong; Tang attacked You Xia; King Wen of Zhou attacked Chong; King Wu of Zhou attacked Zhou. Duke Huan of Qi relied on military strength to become the hegemon of the world. From this, one can see—how could there be anyone who does not wage war? In ancient times, the wheels of envoys' chariots rolled ceaselessly and words were exchanged to form alliances, unifying the world into one. Treaties bound them in horizontal alliances; weapons and armor were not stored away; Eminent scholars gathered together, while the feudal lords became confused and disoriented; Ten thousand matters arose simultaneously, too numerous to manage; When detailed regulations were in place, the people became full of deceitful behavior; With dense annotations in written records and decrees, the common people lacked sufficiency. The upper and lower classes were mutually distressed, and the people had no means of support; Though clear words and orderly reasoning were presented, arms and armor continued to increase; Persuasive speeches and grand displays of rhetoric failed to stop the ceaseless wars and attacks; Elaborate citations and elegant words did not bring about good governance in the world; Tongues grew weary, ears became deaf, yet no success was achieved; Upholding righteousness and keeping promises did not bring the world closer together. Therefore, they abandoned reliance on words and turned to military strength, generously nurturing warriors willing to die, reinforcing armor and sharpening weapons, seeking victory through the battlefield. Merely staying in place to gain benefits, or sitting idly while expanding territory—although ancient emperors such as the Five Di, Three Wang, and Five Hegemons, along with wise rulers and virtuous sovereigns, often wished to achieve this through assistance, their circumstances did not permit it. Therefore, they resorted to war to continue their efforts. When space is ample, two armies attack each other; when confined, they clash with staves and halberds. Only then can great achievements be accomplished. Therefore, military victories are achieved abroad while righteousness is strengthened within the state; Martial power stands firm above, and the people submit below. Now, if one wishes to unite the world, surpass a ten-thousand-chariot power, subdue rival states, control within the seas, protect the common people, and make feudal lords submit as subjects, nothing can be achieved without military strength! The current successors to the throne neglect the highest principles, are confused by instruction, disorderly in governance, misled by words, bewildered by speech, immersed in debate, and drowned in rhetoric. Judging from this, Your Majesty's state cannot put it into practice."

Su Qin submitted his proposal to King Qin ten times, but the king did not accept it. His black sable fur coat was worn out; his hundred jin of gold were exhausted, and lacking further resources, he left Qin and returned home. He walked barefoot in tattered shoes, carrying books and a bag on his back; his appearance was gaunt and emaciated, with a face ashen and darkened, showing the look of one returning home. When he returned home, his wife did not stop her sewing; his sister-in-law refused to cook for him; and his parents would not speak to him. Su Qin sighed and said, "My wife does not recognize me as her husband; my sister-in-law does not acknowledge me as an uncle; my parents do not regard me as their son. This is all my fault." So at night he opened his books, examined the contents in his bamboo cases, and discovered a strategy from the "Tai Gong Yin Fu." He studied it diligently, refined its teachings, and used them for contemplation. When reading, he would almost fall asleep; so he pierced his own thigh with a awl to stay alert, and blood flowed down to his feet. He said, "How can there be a person who advises a ruler but is unable to obtain gold and brocade or attain the rank of high minister?" After a year of contemplation, he said, "This truly enables me to persuade the rulers of my time!"

He then traveled to Yan and gathered at Wuji Que, presenting himself before King Zhao under the grand hall, where they clasped hands in conversation. King Zhao of Zhao was greatly pleased and conferred upon him the title of Wu'an Jun. He received the seal of a prime minister, one hundred chariots, a thousand bolts of brocade, a hundred pairs of white jade discs, and ten thousand yu of gold to follow him. He formed an alliance of vertical unity, breaking up horizontal alliances in order to curb powerful Qin. Thus, when Su Qin served as prime minister of Zhao, the passes were closed and did not allow passage. At that time, with the vastness of the world, the multitude of its people, the power of kings and marquises, and the authority of strategists, all wished to decide matters according to Su Qin's strategy. Without spending a single measure of grain, without troubling a single soldier, without mobilizing one warrior, without breaking a bowstring or bending an arrow, the feudal lords became closely allied, more harmonious than brothers. When a virtuous person is in power, the world submits; when one capable man is employed, the whole world follows. Therefore it is said: "Success lies in governance, not in bravery; "Success lies within the court and ancestral temple, not beyond the four borders." During Su Qin's prime, ten thousand yu of gold were spent; chariots and horsemen traveled in procession, dazzling the roads. The states east of Mount Tai followed like the wind, bringing great prestige to Zhao. Moreover, Su Qin was merely a man from a humble alley with a thatched door and a simple wooden latch. Yet he controlled the reins of his carriage, traveled across the world horizontally, presented himself in court to advise rulers of feudal states, silenced those at their sides, and no one under heaven could rival him.

When Su Qin was traveling to persuade King Chu, he passed through Luoyang. Upon hearing of his arrival, his parents cleaned the palace and cleared the road, arranged music and prepared drinks, and met him in the suburbs thirty li away. His wife looked at him with respectful eyes and listened attentively; his sister-in-law crawled forward like a snake, knelt four times on her own initiative, and apologized. Su Qin said, "Sister-in-law, why were you so arrogant before but now so humble?" His sister-in-law replied, "Because your position is noble and you have much gold." Su Qin said, "Alas! When one is poor and lowly, even parents do not treat him as a son; when one becomes wealthy and noble, relatives become fearful. How can the power, status, wealth, and honor of life in this world be taken lightly!"


### King Hui of Qin Addresses Hanquanzi

King Hui of Qin said to Hanquanzi, "Su Qin has deceived me. He wants to use the wisdom of one man to turn around the ruler of Dongshan and then use that to deceive Qin." Zhao relied on its large population, so it first sent Su Qin with gifts and silk to make an alliance with the feudal lords. The feudal lords cannot be united; this is as clear as how tied chickens cannot all stop roosting together. "I am greatly enraged, and have been harboring anger for a long time; I want to send Wu'anzǐ to go forth and convey my meaning." Hanquanzi said, "No." "Regarding attacking cities and toppling towns, please send Wu'anzǐ. "As for sending envoys to the feudal lords to benefit our state, please send the guest minister Zhang Yi." King Hui of Qin said, "I accept the order."


### Ling Xiang Advises King Qin

Ling Xiang said to King Qin, "I wanted to serve you with Qi's affairs and have you attack Song." "If Song is defeated, the State of Jin will be in danger, but Anyi belongs to King." Yan and Zhao hate the alliance between Qi and Qin; they must cede territory to satisfy your Majesty's desires. Qi will surely be valued highly by Your Majesty, so my attack on Song was intended to intimidate Qi and elevate your Majesty's status. Why should Your Majesty resent Ling Xiang's attack on Song? "I assumed that Your Majesty, with your wisdom, would already know this beforehand, so I did not mention it."


### Zhang Yi Persuades King Qin

Zhang Yi said to King Qin, "I have heard that not speaking when one does not know is unwise, and not speaking when one knows is disloyal. As a subject, failing to be loyal deserves death, and speaking without careful consideration also deserves death. Nevertheless, I wish to fully state what I have heard; may the Great King judge my offense."

I have heard that in the north, Yan and Wei are secretly allied; to the south, Jing and Qi are firmly united; and the remaining states of Han have been gathered together to form a coalition. They will move southwestward to create difficulties for Qin. I secretly find this amusing. There are three ways a state may perish, and when the world gains from it—this must be what is meant by that saying! I have heard it said: 'He who attacks a well-governed state with chaos will perish; he who attacks righteousness with injustice will perish; and he who attacks order with rebellion will perish.' Now, the granaries and treasuries of the world are not full; their storehouses are empty. They have mobilized all their people to raise armies in the tens or hundreds of thousands. With swords at the front and axes behind, yet they all flee and cannot die. This is because the common people cannot be made to die, and their rulers cannot kill them. When it comes to rewards, they are not given; when it comes to punishments, they are not carried out. Since rewards and punishments are not enforced, the people do not die.

Now Qin issues orders and carries out rewards and punishments; they neither attack nor engage in unnecessary warfare. They are taken from the arms of their parents, never having seen an enemy before. Upon hearing of battle, they stamp their feet and bare their chests, rushing forward to face swords, treading on burning coals, and dying by the hundreds in front. Dying for a decisive cause is different from merely choosing death. And the people do so because they value valor. One can defeat ten, ten can defeat a hundred, a hundred can defeat a thousand, a thousand can defeat ten thousand, and ten thousand can conquer the world. Now, Qin's terrain allows for cutting off the long and extending the short; its written records cover a thousand li. It has hundreds of thousands of elite troops. The orders, rewards, and punishments of Qin, as well as its advantageous terrain, are unparalleled in the world. With this strength against the world, it is more than enough to conquer and possess all under heaven. This is why Qin has never failed in battle, has never failed to capture what it attacks, and has never failed to break through whatever it confronts. Expanding territory by a thousand li is indeed a great achievement. However, the armor and weapons are worn out; the soldiers and people suffer from hardship. Stores have been exhausted, fields lie fallow, granaries stand empty, and neighboring feudal lords do not submit. The title of a hegemon has not been achieved. There is no other reason for this—counselors have all failed to give their full loyalty.

I dare to speak of the past. In the past, Qi defeated Jing to the south, broke Song in the center, subdued Qin to the west, and crushed Yan to the north. It controlled the rulers of Han and Wei. Its territory was vast and its army strong; it won battles and captured territories, issuing commands throughout the world. The clear waters of the Ji River and the muddy waters of the He were sufficient as boundaries, while the Great Wall and massive fortresses were enough for defense. Qi was a state that had fought in five major wars. One defeat and Qi would be lost. Hence, from this we can see that war determines the survival or destruction of a great state with a thousand chariots. Moreover, I have heard it said: 'Cut down the pillars and dig out the roots; do not let misfortune be your neighbor, and calamity will not exist.' Qin fought against the people of Jing, greatly defeating them. It captured Ying, took Dingtai, Wudu, and the southern bank of the Yangtze River. The king of Jing fled in panic and took refuge to the east at Chen. At that time, if Qin had pursued Jing with its troops, Jing could have been completely conquered. Conquering Jing would mean having a sufficient population to exploit and enough land for profit. To the east, it could strengthen Qi and Yan; in the center, it could dominate the three Jin states. Thus, with this single action, the title of hegemon or emperor could be achieved, and neighboring feudal lords would come to pay homage. Yet the strategists did not act on this; they withdrew their troops and made peace with Jing. Now, the people of Jing have gathered their scattered population and restored their state. They have established altars to ancestors, set up temples for ancestral worship, and commanded all under heaven to face westward in opposition to Qin. This alone shows that they no longer follow the path of a hegemon or emperor. When the world united with shared intent and marched on Hua Xia, Your Majesty used deception to defeat them. His troops reached the walls of Liang, encircling it for several weeks; thus, Liang could have been captured. Capturing Dai Ma would mean Hui could be conquered. Conquering Wei would cut off Jing and Zhao's ambitions. When Jing and Zhao lose their ambitions, Zhao becomes vulnerable. Zhao is in danger, and Jing stands alone. To the east, Qi and Yan would grow strong; in the center, they could dominate the three Jin states. Thus, with this single move, the title of hegemon could be achieved, and neighboring lords would come to pay tribute. Yet the strategists did not do so; they withdrew their army and made peace with Wei, allowing Wei to gather its scattered people, restore its state, establish ancestral altars, and set up temples. This is a second clear sign that it no longer follows the path of hegemony or imperial rule. Previously, when Zhang Hougong governed Qin, he used the forces of one state but wished to achieve the accomplishments of two. Therefore, his troops remained exposed to hardship abroad for life, while the people and soldiers suffered internally. The title of hegemon was not achieved—this is a third clear sign that it no longer followed the path of hegemony or empire.

Zhao, being a central state, is where mixed populations reside. Its people are fickle and hard to use; its commands are not well managed, rewards and punishments lack credibility, the terrain is inconvenient, and its rulers cannot fully harness their people's strength. That state already shows signs of collapse, yet it does not worry about its common people. Mobilizing all its soldiers and civilians, Zhao stationed an army at Changping to contend for Shangdang in Han. Your Majesty used deception to break them and captured Wu'an. At that time, the Zhao court and people were not united; nobles and commoners did not trust one another. Thus, Handan could not be defended. Capturing Handan would complete control of Hejian, then withdrawing troops to attack Xiuwu in the west, crossing Yangchang Pass, and subduing Dai and Shangdang. Dai's thirty-six counties and Shangdang's seventeen counties would all become Qin's without a single soldier being wounded or a commoner suffering hardship. Dai and Shangdang would fall without a battle and become part of Qin; Dongyang and the area beyond the He River would revert to Qi without resistance, and all land north of Zhonghu Chi would be under Yan's control without fighting. Thus, if Zhao is conquered, Han will inevitably fall; and once Han falls, Jing and Wei cannot stand alone. When Jing and Wei can no longer stand alone, this single move would destroy Han, weaken Wei, dominate Jing, and to the east subdue Qi and Yan. Opening the mouth of Ba Ma would flood Wei's territory. With one move, the three Jin states would perish, and their allies would be defeated. Your Majesty need only sit back and wait; all under heaven will follow in submission, and the title of hegemon can be achieved. Yet the strategists did not take this course; they withdrew their troops instead and made peace with Zhao. With Your Majesty's wisdom and the strength of Qin's army, the great cause of hegemony should have been achieved; a position of supreme honor was within reach. Instead, we were deceived by a fallen state—this is the incompetence of our strategists. Moreover, Zhao was destined to fall but did not; Qin was meant to become a hegemon but failed. The world naturally judges the incompetence of Qin's strategists for this alone. Then, mobilizing all troops once more to attack Handan, they failed to capture it. Discarding armor and weapons in anger, the soldiers trembled with fear and retreated—this is how the world now measures Qin's strength for a second time. The army withdrew and gathered at Li Xia. Your Majesty combined forces to confront them, but did not achieve a decisive victory; the two sides ended in stalemate and mutual retreat—this is how the world measures Qin's strength for the third time. Internally, they measure our strategists; externally, they test our military might. Viewed in this light, I believe that breaking the alliance of all under heaven is not difficult at all. Internally, our armor and weapons are worn, the soldiers and people are weary, stores have been exhausted, farmland lies fallow, and granaries stand empty. Externally, the world has united with a very strong shared resolve. I hope Your Majesty will carefully consider this matter.

Moreover, I have heard that one must be cautious and vigilant day by day, growing more careful each passing day. If one carefully follows the right path, all under heaven can be possessed. How do I know this to be true? In the past, King Zhou of Shang ruled as Son of Heaven and led a million troops across the world. To his left, they drank from the Qi Valley; to his right, from the Huan River. The Qi dried up, and the Huan ceased to flow, yet he still opposed King Wu of Zhou. King Wu led only three thousand white-armored soldiers, fought for one day, and destroyed King Zhou's state. He captured the king himself, occupied his land, and ruled over his people; all under heaven mourned this. Zhi Bo led the forces of three states to attack Zhao Xiangzi at Jinyang, diverting water to flood it. After three years, the city was nearly captured. Xiangzhu consulted tortoise shells and counted yarrow stalks to divine omens, seeking signs of fortune or misfortune. He asked which state could be persuaded to surrender and thus sent Zhang Mengtan. Thus, he secretly left the city and betrayed Zhi Bo's alliance. He gained the forces of two states to attack Zhi Bo's own state, captured him personally, and completed Zhao Xiangzi's great achievement. Now, Qin has territory where the long is cut and the short extended; its domain spans thousands of li. It commands hundreds of thousands of elite soldiers. The decrees, rewards, punishments, and advantageous terrain of Qin are unparalleled in all under heaven. With this strength to confront the world, all under heaven can be conquered and possessed. I dare to risk my life to present myself before Your Majesty, explaining how we can swiftly break the alliance of all under heaven, conquer Zhao and destroy Han, subdue Jing and Wei, and bring Qi and Yan into our fold. And Yan, thus achieving the title of hegemon and establishing a path for neighboring feudal lords to pay homage. If Your Majesty listens to this plan, in one move the alliance of all under heaven will not be broken; Zhao will not fall, Han will not perish, Jing and Wei will not submit, Qi and Yan will not become allies, the title of hegemon will not be achieved, neighboring lords will not pay homage. Then, Your Majesty may execute me publicly within the state as an example of one who plotted disloyally."


### Zhang Yi Wants to Borrow Qin Troops to Rescue Wei

Zhang Yi wanted to borrow Qin troops to rescue Wei. Zuo Cheng said to Gan Mao, "You will not give it to him." "If Wei does not return the Qin troops, Zhang Zi will not return to Qin." If Wei returns the Qin troops, then Zhang Zi would have achieved his ambitions in Wei and would no longer dare to oppose Qin. "If Zhang Zi does not leave Qin, he will certainly look down on you, Master Gao."


### Sima Cuo and Zhang Yi Argue Before King Hui of Qin

Sima Cuo and Zhang Yi argued before King Hui of Qin. Sima Cuo wanted to attack Shu, but Zhang Yi said, "It would be better to attack Han instead." The king said, "Please explain your reasoning." Zhang Yi replied, "By forming close relations with Wei and maintaining good relations with Chu, we can deploy troops to the Three Rivers region, block the Huan Yuan and Gou Shi passes, control the road at Tunliu, cut off Wei's access to Nanyang, and have Chu approach Nanzheng. With Qin attacking Xincheng and Yiyang, we would be positioned before the suburbs of the two Zhou states, holding the Zhou ruler accountable for his crimes, while also encroaching on the territories of Chu and Wei." The Zhou court would realize that it could not be rescued, so its nine tripods and other treasures would surely come forth. Possessing the Nine Tripods, securing the maps and records, and holding the Son of Heaven as a figurehead to issue commands to all under heaven—there would be none in the world who dared not obey. This is the path to establishing an imperial legacy. Now, Shu is a western frontier state and the leader of Rong and Di tribes. Wearing out troops and exhausting the people would not be sufficient to gain renown, and acquiring its land would not yield significant benefit. I have heard it said: 'Those who contend for reputation do so in the court; those who contend for profit do so in the marketplace.' Now, the Three Rivers region and the Zhou dynasty are the marketplaces and courts of all under heaven. Yet you do not contend there, but instead focus on contending with Rong and Di tribes; this is far removed from establishing an imperial legacy."

Sima Cuo said, "That is not correct. I have heard that those who wish to enrich their state must strive to expand its territory; those who wish to strengthen their army must ensure the people's wealth; those who aspire to kingship must cultivate broad virtue. When these three resources are in place, kingship will naturally follow. Now, Your Majesty's territory is small and the people poor; therefore, I wish to pursue what is easier first. Shu is a frontier state in the west and the leader of Rong-Di tribes, yet it suffers from chaos akin to that of Jie and Zhou. For Qin to attack it would be like sending wolves to chase a flock of sheep. Capturing its land is sufficient to expand our state; acquiring its wealth is enough to enrich the people; Mobilizing troops without exhausting the populace, and they will be subdued. Thus, conquering one state would not be seen as brutal by all under heaven; gaining advantage up to the western sea would not be considered greedy by the feudal lords. This is one action that would bring both reputation and tangible gains, along with the noble title of curbing violence and correcting disorder. Now, attacking Han to threaten the Son of Heaven—threatening the Son of Heaven is a bad name, and it may not even be beneficial; furthermore, it would earn us the label of injustice, as we attack what all under heaven do not desire. That is dangerous! I beg to explain the reason: Zhou is the ancestral house of all under heaven; Qi is an allied state of Han and Zhou. Zhou, realizing it has lost the Nine Tripods, and Han, knowing that it will lose the Three Rivers region, would certainly unite their strength and conspire together to seek alliances with Qi and Zhao in order to gain relief from Chu and Wei. They could give the tripods to Chu and the land to Wei, something Your Majesty would be unable to prevent. This is what I mean by danger; it is not as secure as attacking Shu." King Hui said, "Well said! I shall follow your advice."

### Zhang Yi Wants to Give Hanzhong to Chu

Zhang Yi wanted to give Hanzhong to Chu and requested from King Qin: "Possessing Hanzhong is a burden." Those who plant trees in inappropriate places will surely be harmed by others; A family possessing ill-gotten wealth harms its foundation. Hanzhong's southern border is advantageous for Chu; this would be a burden to our state." Gan Mao said to the king: "A large territory brings more worries to a state!" If there is turmoil in the world, Your Majesty cedes Hanzhong as an offering for peace with Chu; Chu will surely break away from all other states and ally exclusively with you. "If Your Majesty now gives Hanzhong to Chu, when turmoil arises in the world, how will Your Majesty be able to buy Chu's allegiance?"


### Chu Attacks Wei: Zhang Yi Advises King Qin

Chu attacked Wei. Zhang Yi said to King Qin, "It would be better to give something to Wei in order to strengthen it. If Wei wins a battle, it will once again have to listen to Qin, and certainly cede the area beyond the Xihe River; if it does not win, Wei will be unable to hold on, and Your Majesty can surely take it." The king followed Zhang Yi's advice, took 10,000 soldiers from Pi Shi, and gave Wei 100 chariots. Xi Shou won a victory over King Wei. The Hui army was exhausted and fearful of Qin, so it indeed offered the area beyond the Xihe River.


### Tian Shenzhi for Chen Zhen advises King Hui of Qin

Tian Shenzhi advised Chen Zhen on behalf of King Hui of Qin, saying: "I am afraid Your Majesty will be like Guo Jun." The Duke Xianggong of Jin wanted to attack Guo, but he was deterred by the presence of Zhou Zhiqiao. Xun Xi said: "The Book of Zhou says, 'A beautiful woman can break tongues.'" He then sent her female entertainers to disrupt its government. Zhou Zhiqiao advised but was not heeded, so he left. They then attacked Guo and eventually destroyed it. He wanted to attack Yu next, but was deterred by Gong Zhiqi's presence. Xun Xi said: "The Book of Zhou says, a handsome man can break an old man." He then sent them handsome young men and taught them to despise Gong Zhiqi. Gong Zhiqi advised but was not listened to, so he fled. They then attacked Yu and eventually captured it. Now, Qin considers itself a king, but the state capable of harming a king's realm is Chu. Chu values Hengjun's skill in military strategy, and with the wisdom of Chen Zhen, so they proudly used Zhang Yi to deal with five states. If you come, you must despise these two men. "I hope Your Majesty will not listen to this." Zhang Yi indeed came to bid farewell, and took the opportunity to mention Chen Zhen; King Hui became angry and did not listen.


### Zhang Yi Again Slanders Chen Zhen to King of Qin

Zhang Yi again slandered Chen Zhen to King of Qin, saying: "Zhen has been traveling between Chu and Qin. Now, instead of being friendly toward Qin, Chu is friendly toward Zhen. If this is the case, then it means that Zhen acts for himself rather than for his country." "Chen Zhen wants to leave Qin and go to Chu. Why does Your Majesty not allow it?" The king said to Chen Zhen, "I have heard that you wish to leave Qin and go to chu. Is this true?" Chen Zhen said, "Yes." The king said, "Zhang Yi's words are indeed true." He said, "It is not only Zhang Yi who knows this; even people on the road know it. He said: "Xiaoyi loved his parents, and all under heaven wished to take him as their son; "Fan Xu was loyal to his ruler, and all under heaven wanted to take him as a minister. "A servant or concubine who is sold in the lanes and alleys is a good servant or concubine; "a woman who marries off to her native village after being divorced is a virtuous wife." "If I am not loyal to my lord, how can Chu regard Zhen as loyal?" "Even if loyalty leads to being discarded, where else should I go besides Chu?" King of Qin said, "Well spoken." And so he did not go.


### Chen Zhen Leaves Chu for Qin

Chen Zhen left Chu for Qin. Zhang Yi said to King Qin, "Chen Zhen is your Majesty's minister, yet he often conveys state secrets to Chu. I am unable to serve alongside him; I wish Your Majesty will expel him." "If he returns to Chu, I hope Your Majesty will execute him." The king said, "How dare Zhen go to Chu?" King Qin summoned Chen Zhen and told him, "I am able to heed your words. Where do you wish to go? "I shall prepare a carriage for you." Chen Zhen replied, "I wish to go to Chu." It is necessary to punish and attack the chaotic state of Song, strengthen virtue in Qi, and thereby secure a noble title for yourself. This is also an opportunity that comes once in a hundred generations!"


### Saying to Wei Ran That Chu Would Defeat Qin

He said to Wei Ran: "If Chu defeats Qin, it will no longer be able to hold equal weight with Qi." Qin has accumulated advantages over three generations in the states of Han and Wei, while Qi's virtue is newly added. If Qi and Qin compete for dominance, and Han and Wei listen to Qi in the east, then Qin will be attacked. Qi possesses the land of Dongguo, spanning a square thousand li. Chu encompasses the Nine Yi tribes and also spans a square thousand li; to its south lies the Fu-li pass, and to its north lies the Gan-yu ford. Controlling Song and Wei is like controlling Sang'e and Zhen'er. Possessing two regions each spanning a thousand li, and wealth monopolized by Yue-li, how can Qin possibly hold equal weight with Qi in Han and Wei, or extend its control to the fertile lands of Fangcheng to weaken Zheng? "If military forces rest only briefly before rising again, it will be sufficient to injure Qin, without necessarily waiting for Qi."


### Five States Withdraw from Chenggao

The Five States withdrew from Chenggao, and King Qin wanted to seek the position of prime minister in Han and Wei for the Lord Chengyang; however, Han and Wei did not agree. The Queen Dowager of Qin said to King Qin on behalf of Wei Ran, "Because of the king's cause, Lord Chengyang was in distress and had to take refuge in Qi. Now that the king sees him rising to prominence and wants to bring him back, can he truly win his loyalty?" The king said, "Not yet." The queen dowager said, "If one does not support someone when they are in hardship but only rewards them when they have succeeded, I fear he will not serve the king faithfully; and furthermore, accepting Lord Chengyang would be against the interests of Han and Wei."


### Fan Zi through Wang Ji Enters Qin

Fan Zi entered Qin through Wang Ji and submitted a letter to King Zhao, saying: "I have heard that an enlightened ruler administers justice, and when there is merit, it cannot go unrewarded; when there is ability, the person cannot fail to be given an official post. Those who labor greatly receive generous salaries; those with many achievements hold high ranks; and those capable of governing the masses are given important official positions. Therefore, those who lack ability dare not assume such posts, and those with ability cannot remain hidden. If Your Majesty considers my words acceptable, then implementing them will further benefit the way of governance; But if you do not intend to act on them, keeping me here for a long time would be meaningless. The saying goes: "A ruler rewards those he loves and punishes those he hates." An enlightened ruler is not like that; rewards are necessarily given to those with merit, and punishments are definitely imposed on the guilty." Nowadays, my chest is not broad enough to bear the wooden staves of punishment, and my waist is not strong enough to face the axes and halberds; how could I dare to raise doubts or speak about uncertain matters before Your Majesty? Even if you consider me lowly and insult me, would those who later hold important positions before Your Majesty not repeat the same behavior? I have heard that Zhou had Di'e, Song had Jielv, Liang had Xuanli, and Chu had Hepu. These four treasures were overlooked by artisans but became renowned artifacts of the world. If this is so, then what a sage king discards would it be insufficient to strengthen the state? I have heard that those who are good at strengthening their families take from the nation; Those who are good at strengthening the state take from the feudal lords. When there is a wise ruler in the world, the feudal lords cannot act unilaterally to enrich themselves. Why is that so? Because they would cause the glory of the state to decline. A skilled physician knows whether a patient will live or die; an enlightened ruler understands the matters of success and failure. If something is beneficial, he implements it; if harmful, he abandons it; if doubtful, he cautiously tries it out. Even if Yao, Shun, Yu, and Tang were to be reborn, they could not challenge this! The most profound words, I dare not record in writing; and the shallow ones are not worth listening to either. Perhaps it is because I am foolish and my words do not align with Your Majesty's mind! Or perhaps those who speak of me consider me lowly and unworthy of being heard! If it is not so, then my wish is merely to be granted a brief audience, just enough to catch sight of Your Majesty and present myself." After the letter was submitted, King Qin was pleased. He then thanked Wang Ji for his recommendation and sent someone with a carriage to summon him.


### Fan Jui Arrives at Qin

Fan Jui arrived at Qin, and King Ting welcomed him, saying to Fan Jui: "For a long time now, I should have personally received your commands." "Now that the matter of Yiqu is urgent, for many days I have been personally requesting an audience with the Empress Dowager." "Since the matter of Yiqu has now been resolved, I am finally able to receive your commands in person." "I personally feel humble and unresponsive; I respectfully observe the rites of host and guest." On that day, when people saw Fan Jui, none failed to change their expressions and demeanor. King Qin dismissed his attendants; the palace was empty of people. King Qin knelt and asked, "Master, what advice do you have for me?" Fan Jui said, "Yes, yes." After a while, King Qin asked again, and Fan Jui replied, "Yes, yes." This happened three times. King Qin sat upright and said, "Is it that you are unwilling to instruct me?" Fan Jui replied with thanks, saying, "I dare not do so." "I have heard that when Lü Shang first met King Wen, he was merely a fisherman casting his net by the banks of Weiyang." "At that time, their relationship was distant. "After one conversation he was immediately appointed as Taishi and taken back with him; this was because his words were profound." "Therefore, King Wen indeed achieved success through Lü Shang and eventually dominated the world, establishing himself as emperor." "If King Wen had kept Lü Wang at a distance and not spoken with him in depth, then the Zhou would have lacked the virtue of an emperor, and Kings Wen and Wu would not have been able to achieve their status as sovereigns." "Now I, a wandering minister, am distantly connected to the king. What I wish to present are matters of advising and assisting Your Majesty; they concern intimate relationships within your family. I wish only to express my humble loyalty, yet I do not know the king's mind. This is why the king asked three times and I did not respond." "I am not afraid to speak out of fear, but I know that if I speak today before you, I may be executed tomorrow. Yet I do not dare to fear this." "If Your Majesty truly follows my words, death is not a concern for me; fleeing is not a cause of worry. Even if I were to smear my body with paint and pretend to be leprous, or let down my hair and act mad, it would not bring shame upon me." "The sage Five Emperors died; the benevolent Three Kings died; the virtuous Five Hegemons died; Wu Huo, with his strength, died; and Peng Ju, with their bravery, died." "Death is something no one can escape." "To deal with an inevitable fate, yet still be of some slight benefit to Qin—this is my greatest wish." "What should I fear?" "Wu Zixu carried his belongings and fled through Zhao Pass, traveling at night and hiding by day. When he reached the Ling River, he had no food to put in his mouth; he sat on his knees and crawled forward, begging for food in Wu's market. In the end, he rose up and revived the state of Wu, making Helu its hegemon." "If I were allowed to present my arguments like Wu Zixu, yet was further imprisoned in secret and never seen again, it would mean that my advice has been accepted. What should I fear?" "Jizi and Jieyu smeared their bodies with paint to pretend to be lepers, let down their hair to act mad, yet they had no intention of serving Yin or Chu." "If I could walk the same path as Jizi and Jieyu, if smearing my body might still serve a virtuous ruler, it would be my greatest honor. What shame should I feel?" "What I fear is only this: that after my death, the world will see me as completely loyal yet having suffered failure. Therefore, they may close their mouths and refrain from moving forward, unwilling to come near Qin." "Your Majesty above fears the Empress Dowager's severity, and below is deceived by the ministers' deceitful appearances; " "dwelling deep within the palace, never leaving the hands of your guardians and tutors;" "you will remain in ignorance all your life, with no one to illuminate the deceit; " "in the worst case, the ancestral temple will be destroyed and overturned; at minimum, you yourself will be isolated and in peril." "This is what I fear!" "As for the disgrace of being in dire straits, or the danger of death, I dare not fear them." "Dying while benefiting Qin is better than living." King Qin sat upright and said: "Master, what words are these!" "The state of Qin is remote and isolated, and I am foolish and unworthy. You have kindly come to this place; it must be Heaven's way of using me as a means to honor you, thus preserving the temple of our former kings." "I am able to receive your command because Heaven is showing favor to our late kings and not abandoning their orphaned heir." "How could you say such a thing, Master!" "Matters of no size—whether above to the Empress Dowager or below to the ministers—I wish you would teach me everything, Master." "Do not doubt me." Fan Jui bowed twice, and King Qin also bowed twice. Fan Jui said: "Your Majesty's state, to the north has Ganquan and Gu Kou, to the south is bordered by Jing and Wei rivers; on its western side are Long and Shu, while on the eastern side lie Guan and Ban; "it has a thousand chariots for war, and an army of hundreds of thousands." "With the bravery of Qin soldiers and the numerous cavalry, confronting the feudal lords would be like releasing a Hanlu hound to chase a slow rabbit; thus, the cause of hegemony or kingship can be achieved." "But now you remain closed and dare not look upon the armies in Shandong; this is because Rang Hou did not act loyally when planning for the state, and Your Majesty's strategy has been flawed." The king said: "I wish to hear what my mistake was." Fan Jui said: "Your Majesty, crossing through Han and Wei to attack the powerful Qi is not a sound strategy. "A small expedition would be insufficient to harm Qi; "but a large one would be harmful to Qin itself." "I suspect Your Majesty's plan is to send out only a small force, yet mobilizing all the troops of Han and Wei would be unjust." "Now that you see these allied states cannot be trusted, is it acceptable to cross a foreign land and launch an attack?" "That would be a grave miscalculation!" "In the past, when Qi attacked Chu, they won a victory, destroyed an army and killed its general, yet even after expanding their territory by thousands of li, not an inch was actually gained. Was it that Qi desired land? No—it is because the situation did not allow them to hold it." "When the feudal lords saw Qi's exhaustion and weakness, as well as its lack of unity between ruler and subjects, they raised their armies to attack. The king was humiliated and his army defeated, becoming a laughingstock for all under heaven." "The reason this happened is because Qi's campaign against Chu only strengthened Han and Wei." "This is what is called providing weapons to thieves and food to robbers." "Your Majesty should instead make distant alliances while attacking nearby states. Every inch gained is an inch for the king; every chi gained is a chi for the king." "Now, to abandon this strategy and launch distant attacks—would that not be mistaken?" "Moreover, in the past, Zhongshan's territory was a square of five hundred li. Zhao alone controlled it; when its achievements were realized and its name established, benefits attached to it, so no power under heaven could harm it." "Now Han and Wei are located in the central region of the world and serve as its pivot. "If Your Majesty wishes to become a hegemon, you must closely ally with the central states and make them the pivot of all under heaven, thereby intimidating Chu and Zhao." "When Zhao is strong, Chu will submit; when Chu is strong, Zhao will submit. " "When Chu and Zhao submit, Qi must surely fear; in fear, it will use humble words and generous gifts to serve Qin. Once Qi submits, Han and Wei can be left empty-handed." The king said: "I wish to be close to Wei, but Wei is a fickle state; I cannot trust it. "Please advise—how can I establish closeness with Wei?" Fan Jui said: "Use humble words and generous gifts to treat it." "No, that will not work. You must cede territory as a bribe." "No, you should raise an army and attack it." Thus, the king raised his troops to attack Xingqiu. After Xingqiu fell, Wei requested submission. "It was said: 'The geography of Qin and Han is interwoven like embroidery.' " "Han's presence within Qin is like a worm in the wood, or an illness in one's heart and abdomen." "When there are changes in the world, no state poses a greater threat to Qin than Han." "Therefore, Your Majesty should take control of Han." The king said: "I wish to bring Han under my control, but it does not listen. What can I do?" Fan Jui said: "Raise an army to attack Yingyang; then the road through Chenggao will be cut off; "cutting northward across Taiping Dao, and the troops of Shangdang cannot descend; " "if one force attacks Yingyang directly, then its state will be divided into three parts. "When Wei and Han see that their destruction is inevitable, how could they not obey?" "If Han obeys, the cause of hegemony can be achieved." The king said: "Well spoken." Fan Jui said: "While I was in Shandong, I heard of Tian Dan within Qi, but not of its king. "I have heard of the Empress Dowager, Rang Hou, Jingyang, and Huayang in Qin, but not of its king." "To control the state is to be a king; to have exclusive power over benefits and harms is called royal authority. To possess the power of life and death is what defines kingship." "Now, the Empress Dowager acts arbitrarily without regard; Rang Hou moves about without reporting his comings and goings. Jingyang and Huayang wield power without restraint or caution. When these four powerful figures are all in place yet the state is not in danger—such a situation has never existed." "Because of these four, those below rightly say there is no king at all." "If this is the case, how can power not be tilted, and how can commands possibly originate from the king?" "I have heard: 'A wise ruler of a state strengthens its internal authority and enhances its external power.'" "Rang Hou's envoys wield the king's authority, breaking apart feudal states and issuing decrees to all under heaven. They wage war against enemies and conquer nations, and none dares refuse." "When battles are won and territories captured, the benefits go to Tao; " "when the state is weakened, it becomes subject to the feudal lords; " "if battles are lost, resentment accumulates among the people and disaster falls upon the state." "The Book of Songs says: 'When a tree bears too much fruit, its branches bend; when the branches bend, the heart of the tree is harmed.' " "Excessive expansion of a capital endangers the state. Elevating one's ministers too highly diminishes the ruler." "Naochi controlled Qi's authority, pulled out King Minwang's tendons, hung him from the temple beam, and he died within a day." "Li Dui controlled Zhao, imprisoned King Zhu Fu, who starved to death after one hundred days." "Now in Qin, the Empress Dowager and Rang Hou hold power, with Gaoling and Jingyang assisting them. In effect, there is no King of Qin—this situation is just like that of Naochi and Li Dui." "I see now the king standing alone in court. Moreover, I fear that if this continues, future rulers of Qin will not be Your Majesty's descendants." King Qin was frightened. Thus he deposed the Empress Dowager, expelled Rang Hou, banished Gaoling, and sent Jingyang beyond the Guan Pass. King Zhao said to Fan Jui: "In the past, Duke Huan of Qi gained Guan Zhong and at that time regarded him as an elder father. "Now I have found you, and I also regard you as a father."


### Yinghou Advises Zhao Wang

Yinghou said to Zhao Wang, "Have you heard of Shengsi having a divine grove?" Shengsi had a fierce young man who made a request at the divine grove, saying: "If I defeat the divine grove, it will be registered as my god for three days; if I do not defeat it, the divine grove will trap me." He then threw with his left hand on behalf of the divine grove and with his right hand for himself. He defeated the divine grove, and it registered its deity to him. Three days later, the divine grove went to seek him out, but he did not return. Five days later the divine grove withered, and seven days later it perished. Now, a state is like Your Majesty's divine grove; Power is Your Majesty's deity. If people register themselves with this, will there not be danger? I have never heard of a finger being larger than the arm, or an arm larger than the thigh; if such were the case, then the illness would certainly be severe. A hundred people carrying a ladle while hurrying is not as effective as one person holding it and running quickly. If truly a hundred people carry the ladle, the ladle will surely break. Now in Qin State, Huayang uses it, Ranghou uses it, the Empress Dowager uses it, and Your Majesty also uses it. If one does not call the ladle a vessel, then that is fine; Once one calls the ladle a vessel, the state will surely be torn apart. I have heard it said: "When a tree bears too much fruit, its branches must spread out; when the branches spread out, they injure the heart of the tree." "When the capital is too powerful, it endangers the state; when ministers are too strong, they endanger their ruler." Are there among those in the capital from the rank of doushi and above, down to magistrates, palace attendants, and those close to the king, any who are not men of the Chancellor? If there is no trouble in the state, then that is fine; But if there are troubles in the state, I will surely hear and see Your Majesty standing alone on the Tang terrace. I secretly fear for Your Majesty: I fear that after ten thousand generations, if there is a ruler of the state, he will not be Your Majesty's descendants. "I have heard that in ancient times, those who were good at governing would support their authority internally and spread their ministers' influence outward. The four administrative policies were orderly and not chaotic or rebellious; envoys traveled straight paths and dared not act improperly." Now, the Empress Dowager's envoys are dividing up the feudal states and distributing seals throughout the land. They wield the power of a great state, forcefully conscripting troops to attack the feudal lords. After victories in battle and territorial conquests, all benefits flow entirely into Tao; The state's coinage and silk goods are drained into the Empress Dowager's household; All profits within the borders are divided and transferred to Huayang. This is exactly what was called in ancient times "the path leading to a ruler's peril and the state's destruction"—it must begin from here. Three powerful factions are draining the state to secure their own safety; yet, how can orders still issue from Your Majesty? How can authority remain undivided? This means that Your Majesty indeed holds only one-third of power."


### Qin attacks Han and surrounds Xing

The state of Qin attacked the state of Han and besieged Xing. Fan Jui said to King Zhao of Qin, "There is attacking people, and there is attacking territory. "The reason why the Marquis of Yanghou attacked Wei ten times without inflicting any damage is not because Qin was weak and Wei strong, but because what he attacked were territories." "Territories are what a ruler loves very much." "A ruler is someone for whom subjects are willing to die happily." "To insult what the ruler loves and contend with those who are eager to die for him, therefore ten attacks failed to achieve victory." "When King Jin attacked Han and besieged Xing, I wish Your Majesty would not merely attack its territory but also target its people." "The king attacked Han and surrounded Xing by following Zhang Yi's advice." "Zhang Yi exerted much effort, and he would have ceded territory to redeem himself before the king, nearly cutting off land but not entirely from Han; " "if Zhang Yi's strength was insufficient, then the king would drive out Zhang Yi and seek someone even less capable than him." "In that case, what Your Majesty seeks from Han can be obtained through words alone,""


### Ying Hou Said: The People of Zheng Call Uncarved Jade Pu

The Ying Hou said: "The people of Zheng call uncarved jade pu, while the people of Zhou call an un-dried mouse pu." Zhou Huaipu passed by a merchant from Zheng and said: "Do you want to sell pu?" The Zheng merchant replied, "I do." [He] took out his pu and looked at it; it was a mouse. Therefore, he apologized and did not take it. Now, Pingyuan Jun considers himself virtuous and has gained a prominent reputation throughout the world; yet he submitted his sovereign Lord Fu Shaqiu to captivity and became his subject. The kings of the world still respect him, which means that the kings of the world are less wise than the merchant from Zheng; they are dazzled by reputation and do not know his true nature."


### Scholars of the World Form an Alliance and Gather in Zhao

The scholars of the world gathered together in Zhao and formed a coalition to attack Qin. Ying Hou, the prime minister of Qin, said: "Your Majesty need not be worried. Please allow me to have them dismissed." The scholars of the world hold no grudge against Qin, yet they gather together to attack it because they wish only to return home. Your Majesty, observe the dogs of Your Majesty: those lying down lie down, those standing up stand up, those walking walk, and those stopping stop; none fight with one another. But when a single bone is thrown to them, they all rise quickly and bite at it—why is that? Because there is a desire for contention among them." Thus, Tang Ju brought along music and gave them fifty gold pieces. He stayed in Wu'an, holding grand banquets where he met with others to drink, saying: "The people of Handan say who has come to take [this]." Therefore, those among them who were plotting could not be given anything; as for those who could be given something, they had already become his brothers. "If you and Qin are to calculate the merits of their actions, one need not inquire where the gold has gone; when the gold is exhausted, that means great merit has been achieved. Now I shall have someone load another fifty gold pieces to follow you." Tang Ju departed, and when he had traveled as far as Wu'an, the gold he distributed did not amount to three thousand pieces; yet already the scholars of the world were greatly fighting among themselves.


### He asked Ying Hou, "Have you captured Ma Fu?"

He asked Ying Hou, "Have you captured Ma Fu?" He said, "Yes." "Then are you about to besiege Handan?" He said, "Yes." "Sai will perish. The King of Qin will become emperor, and Wu'an Jun will serve as one of the Three Dukes." "Wu'an Jun has captured more than seventy cities for Qin through victories and conquests, annihilated Yanyang, Ying, and Hanzhong in the south, defeated the army of Ma Fu, and not a single soldier was lost. Even if Zhou Lüwang had such achievements, they would not surpass this." "When Sai perishes and the King of Qin becomes emperor, Wu'an Jun will be one of the Three Dukes. Can you place yourself beneath him?" "Even if you wish not to be beneath him, it is impossible." "Qin once attacked Han's Xing and was trapped in Shangdang; the people of Shangdang all returned to Sai." "For a long time, the people throughout the world have not been willing to be subjects of Qin." "Now, if we attack Sai, the northern territories will fall to Yan, the eastern ones to Qi, and the southern ones to Chu and Wei. How little would Qin actually gain?" "Therefore, it is better to take advantage of this situation and divide the land, thereby making Wu'an Jun's achievement appear as a great feat."


### Yinghou Loses Runan of Han

The Yinghou lost Ruzhou's Runan. King Zhao of Qin said to the Yinghou, "Have you lost a state, and is that your concern?" The Yinghou said, "I am not worried." The king asked, "Why is that?" He said, "There was a man from Liang named Dongmen Wu. When his son died, he did not grieve. His steward asked him, 'You love your son more than anyone in the world does; now that he has died and you do not grieve, why is that?'" Dongmen Wu said, "I was once without a son. When I had no son, I did not grieve; now that my son has died, it is as if I have gone back to being childless." "Why then should I grieve?"' "I, too, once had a state. When I had a state, I did not worry; now that Runan has been lost, it is as if I am returning to being without a state." "Why then should I worry?" King Qin did not believe this and told Meng Ao, "Nowadays, when I have one city under siege, my food tastes bland and I cannot rest comfortably on my mat. Now the Yinghou has lost territory yet claims to be untroubled—what is his true sentiment?" Meng Ao said, "I will find out his true feelings." Meng Ao then went to see the Yinghou and said, "Ao wishes to die." The Yinghou replied. "What do you mean?" He said, "The King of Qin reveres you; this is known throughout the world, and even more so within the state of Qin! Now Ao, having gained a position as the king's general in Qin, commands troops. As an official of Han, I have committed a grave offense by openly opposing and seizing territory from you. How can Ao still live?" "It is better for me to die." The Yinghou bowed to Meng Ao and said, "I wish to entrust matters to you." Meng Ao reported this back to King Zhao.


### Qin Attack Handan

The Qin forces attacked Handan, but failed to capture it after seventeen months. Zheng said to Wang Ji, "Why don't you bestow rewards upon the military officers?" Wang Ji replied, "I am loyal to King Wuyou. I do not heed others' words." Zhuang said, "That is not the case." A father toward his son has commands that must be carried out and others that must not be carried out. If he says, 'Discard your esteemed wife and sell your beloved concubine,' this is a command that must be obeyed; if he then says, 'Dare not think about it,' this is a command that should not be obeyed. The gatekeeper's wife said, "On that evening, some timid man brought in a certain scholar." The esteemed wife has already been discarded and the beloved concubine sold, yet one's heart still clings to them. To wish to teach others is something inherent in human nature. Now, although you are favored by the king, your relationship does not surpass that of father and son; your subordinates, though lowly, are no less esteemed than a gatekeeper's wife. Moreover, you have already saved the day when you acted on your own authority and treated your subordinates lightly. It is said, "Three men make a tiger; ten men can break an ax." "The masses' opinions shift easily, and without wings they still fly." Therefore it is said: "It would be better to bestow rewards upon the military officials and treat them with courtesy." Wang Ji did not listen. When the military officials were pushed to desperation, they indeed accused Wang Ji and Du Zhi of treason. The King of Qin was furious and wanted to execute Fan Jiu as well. Fan Jiu said, "I am a lowly man from the eastern borderlands who incurred offenses against Chu and Wei, and fled here for refuge. I have no support from the feudal lords, yet Your Majesty appointed me while I was a fugitive and entrusted me with official duties; this is well known throughout the world. Now, if someone who has been deceived or conspired with the accused were to be executed by Your Majesty's just judgment, it would reveal a mistake in your appointment and invite criticism from the feudal lords. I wish to request poison so that I may die, and Your Majesty would show kindness by burying me as a minister; thus, the king will not lose sight of my crime yet avoid the reputation of having made an erroneous appointment." The king said, "That is true." Thus he did not kill him but treated him kindly.


### Cai Ze Was Expelled from Zhao

Cai Ze was expelled from Zhao and went to Han and Wei, where he encountered having his cooking pots taken away on the road. He heard that Ying Hou appointed Zheng Anping and Wang Ji, both of whom were later found guilty of serious crimes. Shamed by this, Ying Hou then went west into Qin. Before meeting King Zhao, he had someone make a public declaration to provoke Ying Hou by saying: "The Yan guest Cai Ze is an outstanding and eloquent man of great talent in the world. Once he meets King Qin, the king will surely appoint him as chancellor and thus take your position." Ying Hou heard this and sent someone to summon Cai Ze. When Cai Ze entered, he merely paid a slight bow to Ying Hou, which made Ying Hou even more displeased. Upon seeing him again, Cai Ze acted with arrogance. Ying Hou then rebuked him and said: "You publicly declared that you would take my place as chancellor of Qin. Is there such a thing?" He replied, "Yes." Ying Hou said: "Please explain your reasoning." Cai Ze said, "Ah! How late you have been in recognizing this. The order of the four seasons shows that when a task is accomplished, one should step down. Is it not what all men desire to have strong hands and feet, as well as sharp ears, eyes, wisdom, and intelligence?" Ying Hou said: "Yes." Cai Ze continued, "To possess benevolence and uphold righteousness, to practice the Way and spread virtue throughout the world—when all under heaven cherish, rejoice in, and love such a person, wishing for him to be their king or ruler, is this not the time when wisdom and discernment are fulfilled?" Ying Hou said: "Yes." Cai Ze further said, "Wealth and honor, prominence and glory—when one brings order to all things so that each finds its proper place; When life is long and healthy, living out one's full years without premature death or injury; When all under heaven continue one's lineage, uphold one's achievements, and pass them on endlessly; when one's name and virtue remain pure, benefiting the world for a thousand generations, being praised without end—this is to share in the destiny of the world. Is this not an omen of the Way, and what sages call a most auspicious and virtuous matter?" Ying Hou said: "Yes." Ze said, "Take for example Shang Jun of Qin, Wu Qi of Chu, and the Grand Minister Zhong of Yue—how tragic were their ends. That is something one should not wish to emulate." Ying Hou realized that Cai Ze was trying to trap him with his arguments and replied, "What is it that cannot be done? Gongsun Yang served King Xiao of Qin with unwavering loyalty, devoting himself entirely to the public good without reservation. He enforced rewards and punishments faithfully to bring about order, exerted his wisdom and intelligence fully, presented sincere proposals, bore resentment and blame, betrayed old friends, captured Wei Gongzi Ang, and ultimately led the army of Qin to defeat enemy forces, capturing their generals and seizing thousands of li of territory. Wu Qi served King Dao of Chu, ensuring that his death did not harm the public interest; he allowed slander to be exposed rather than conceal loyalty. He spoke without seeking mere agreement and acted without seeking superficial acceptance. In practicing righteousness, he did not seek praise or fear criticism. He was determined to become a leader of a powerful state, never shying away from misfortune or danger. Grand Minister Zhong served King Goujian of Yue. When the ruler was in distress and disgrace, he remained completely loyal without wavering; even when the king seemed to have lost all hope, he did not abandon him but continued to exert his full abilities. Though achieving great merit, he showed no arrogance, and though wealthy and honored, he displayed neither pride nor idleness. These three men—Shang Yang, Wu Qi, and Grand Minister Zhong—are paragons of the highest righteousness and the most exemplary loyalty. Therefore, a true gentleman would sacrifice his life to establish a good name; where righteousness lies, even if one's body perishes, there is no regret or remorse. What could possibly be considered unacceptable?" Cai Ze said: "A wise ruler and virtuous ministers are the fortune of all under heaven; a discerning lord and loyal subjects are a nation's blessing; a kind father and filial son, a faithful husband and chaste wife—these are the blessings of a family. Therefore, although Bi Gan was loyal, he could not save Yin (Shang). Though Wu Zixu was wise, he could not preserve Wu; though Shen Sheng was filial, the state of Jin still fell into confusion. How can it be that there were loyal ministers and filial sons yet the country still perished in chaos? Because there was no wise ruler or virtuous father to heed their counsel. Therefore, when a ruler and father are slain or disgraced, the people grieve for his ministers and sons. To wait until death to establish loyalty and a good name is insufficient—this would mean Zuo Qiuming was not truly benevolent, Confucius not truly sage, and Guan Zhong not truly great." At this, Ying Hou praised him.

Cai Ze seized the opportunity and said, "Shang Jun, Wu Qi, and Grand Minister Zong were as ministers who gave their utmost loyalty and achieved great accomplishments—this is admirable. Hong Yao served King Wen, and the Duke of Zhou assisted King Cheng—was that not also loyalty? Judging by their conduct as ministers, which is more admirable: Shang Jun, Wu Qi, Grand Minister Zong, or Hong Yao and the Duke of Zhou?" Ying Hou said, "Shang Jun, Wu Qi and Grand Minister Zong are not as virtuous." Cai Ze said: "Then, compared to King Xiao of Qin, King Dao of Chu, and King Goujian of Yue, whose ruler is more kind, benevolent in appointing loyal men, and faithful to old friends?" Ying Hou said: "I do not know how they compare." Cai Ze said, "A ruler's closeness to loyal ministers cannot surpass that of King Xiao of Qin, Goujian of Yue, or Dao Wang of Chu. As a minister, one's duty is to correct disorder, endure hardship, overcome difficulties, expand territory and increase grain production, strengthen the state and enrich the people, empower the ruler, and extend authority across the land. Achievements that bring glory as far as ten thousand li away—none surpass those of Shang Jun, Wu Qi or Grand Minister Zong. Yet your salary and rank are as high, the wealth of your family exceeds that of these three men, yet you do not step down. I secretly fear for your safety. The saying goes: "When the sun reaches its zenith, it begins to set; when the moon is full, it starts to wane." When things reach their peak, they decline—this is a constant law of heaven; Advancing and retreating, expanding and contracting, changing and transforming—these are the normal ways of managing affairs. In ancient times, Duke Huan of Qi convened the feudal lords nine times and unified the realm. At the meeting in Kuichiu, he showed arrogance and pride, which led to rebellion by nine states. King Fuchai of Wu had no equal in the world; he looked down upon the feudal lords and bullied Qi and Jin, and thus ended up with his death and the destruction of his state. Xia Yu and Taishi Qi were so fierce in their shouts that they terrified entire armies, yet both died at the hands of ordinary men. All these cases occurred because they reached the height of power without heeding reason and principle. Shang Jun helped King Xiao of Qin standardize weights and measures, regulate the balance between military and agricultural priorities, break up land boundaries, and teach the people to farm and fight. Thus, when the army was active, territory expanded; when at rest, the state grew wealthy. Hence, the title "Wudi" (Mighty Emperor) of Qin was established over all under heaven, and feudal lords were appointed in Wei. After his achievements were complete, he was torn apart by chariots. Chu had a million soldiers with halberds, yet Bai Qi led an army of merely tens of thousands to fight against Chu. In one battle he captured Yan and Ying; in the second, he burned Yiling. To the south, he annexed Shu and Han; then crossing through the states of Yan and Wei, he attacked the powerful state of Zhao. In the north, he defeated Ma Fu's forces and slaughtered over 400,000 troops, so much blood flowed that it filled rivers, and the sound of battle roared like thunder—thus enabling Qin to achieve imperial status. After this, Zhao and Chu were cowed into submission and dared not attack Qin—this was the power Bai Qi wielded. The cities he personally subdued numbered more than seventy. After his achievements were completed, he was ordered to commit suicide at Duyou. Wu Qi for King Dao of Chu dismissed the incompetent and useless, abolished unnecessary offices, and reduced positions that were not urgent. He blocked private requests for favors, unified the customs of Chu, attacked Yangyue in the south and annexed Chen and Cai in the north. He shattered the horizontal alliances and scattered the vertical ones, leaving itinerant orators with no opportunity to speak. After his achievements were complete, Wu Qi was dismembered in death. Grand Minister Zong for King Goujian of Yue cleared wasteland and cultivated the land, ensured fertile fields produced grain, led men from all directions, united the efforts of both high and low, to defeat nearby Wu and achieve dominance. King Goujian eventually had him beaten to death with a staff. These four men, having achieved success yet not stepping down, brought disaster upon themselves in this way. This is what is meant by being faithful but unable to bend, and going forward without the ability to return. Fan Li understood this well; he withdrew from worldly affairs with ease and lived a long life as Tao Zhu (a wealthy man). Have you never observed the game of dice? Some wish to divide great stakes, while others desire to share in achievements. These are all matters you, as a ruler, have the authority to decide. Now, as chancellor of Qin, you have not left your seat in planning, nor stepped beyond the palace halls. Sitting alone, you control the feudal lords; your benefits extend to the Three Rivers region and strengthen Yiyang. You have conquered the perilous Yangchang Pass, blocked the mouth of Taihangshan, cut off the routes of Fan and Zhonghang, and built 栈道 (bridged roads) for a thousand li through Shu and Han, making all under heaven wish to submit to Qin as an ideal utopia. Qin's ambitions have been fulfilled; your achievements are at their peak. This is precisely the time for Qin to share its rewards! If you do not step back now, then you will be no different from Shang Jun, Bai Gong, Wu Qi, and Grand Minister Zhu. Why not return the chancellor's seal at this time, pass it on to a worthy man, and thereby gain the integrity of Boyi; you could remain as Ying Hou for life, be honored by generations with titles of nobility, and live a long life like the cypress or pine. Which is better: to end in disaster?" If this is the case, where would you stand, my lord?" Ying Hou said: "Well spoken." He then invited him in, seated him as a guest of honor.

A few days later, he entered court and told King Zhao of Qin: "There is a guest who has recently arrived from the east of Mount Tai named Cai Ze; he is an eloquent man. I have met many people, but none can match him—I am not as capable as he." King Zhao of Qin summoned Cai Ze for an audience. After speaking with him, the king was greatly pleased and appointed him as a Guest Minister (Keqing). Ying Hou then claimed illness and requested to return his chancellor's seal. King Zhao insisted on keeping Ying Hou in office, but Ying Hou declared himself seriously ill and was thus relieved of the position. King Zhao had just been impressed by Cai Ze's plans, so he appointed him as chancellor of Qin and in the east captured the Zhou royal house. After serving as chancellor to King Qin for several months, some people began to speak ill of Cai Ze. Fearing punishment, he claimed illness and returned his chancellor's seal, taking the title Gangcheng Jun (Lord Gangcheng). For more than ten years in Qin, there were King Zhao, King Xiaowen, and King Zhuangxiang. He eventually served the First Emperor (Qin Shi Huang). As an envoy of Qin to Yan, after three years, Yan sent Crown Prince Dan as a hostage to Qin.


## Qin 4

The state of Qin captured Hanzhong from Chu and fought another battle at Lantian, where it heavily defeated the forces of Chu. Chu would doubt whether Qin might not necessarily come to its aid, and now that the three states claim they will withdraw, Chu's response must be encouraged. The three states quickly attacked Chu, and Chu was bound to rush to Qin for urgent aid; the more Qin would dare not come out. This way we can separate Qin from attacking Chu, and the campaign will surely achieve success." Xue Gong said: "Good." So he sent a high-ranking envoy to Chu, and as expected, Chu responded with encouragement. ### Xue Gong Enters Wei and Sends Out Qi's Daughter

Xue Gong entered Wei and sent out Qi's daughter. Han Chun said to King Qin, "Why not take her as a wife? With Qi and Qin holding Hui over Wei, then Shangdang will belong to Qin." If Qi and Qin unite and support Fu Chu, once Fu Chu is established as ruler, his mother being in Qin means that Wei will already be a commandery of Qin. Nan wanted to use Qi and Qin to threaten Wei and trap Xue Gong, while Zuo wished to secure his younger brother. I request that Your Majesty take advantage of Nan and Zuo's situation. Wei, fearing this, would have to restore the previous arrangement; Fu Chu will certainly serve Qin for generations to come. Qi's daughter entered Wei and resented Xue Gong, but in the end she would serve Your Majesty through Qi."


### Three Kingdoms Attack Qin and Enter Hangu Pass

The Three Kingdoms attacked Qin and entered Hangu Pass. King of Qin said to Lou Huan, "The armies of the Three Kingdoms have advanced deeply; I wish to cede Hedong and make peace." Lou Huan replied, "Ceding Hedong would be a great loss; it would free the state from disaster, which is a great benefit." This is your responsibility as ruler and elder brother. "Why does Your Majesty not summon Gongzi Chi to consult with him?" The king summoned Gongzi Chi and asked him. He replied, "Whether we make peace or not, regret will follow." The king said, "Why is that?" Gongzi Chi replied, "If Your Majesty cedes Hedong to make peace, even though the Three Kingdoms withdraw, you will surely say: 'What a pity!' The Three Kingdoms are about to leave, and I only gave them three cities in exchange.'" This is the regret of making peace." The king said, "Between these regrets, I would rather lose three cities and feel regret than risk Xianyang and feel regret. I have decided to make peace." He finally sent Gongzi Chi with three cities to negotiate peace with the armies of the Three Kingdoms, and they withdrew.


### King Zhao of Qin Speaks to His Attendants

King Zhao of Qin said to those around him: "Today, between Han and Wei, which is stronger than at first?" They replied: "Not as strong." The king said, "Now, between Ru Er and Wei Qi, which is more virtuous than Mengchang and Mangmou?" They replied: "Not as virtuous." The king said, "With the virtue of Mengchang and Mangmou leading the strong armies of Han and Wei to attack Qin, they were still unable to do anything against me!" "Now with the incompetent Ru Er and Wei Qi leading the weak Han and Wei to attack Qin, their inability to do anything against me is also obvious!" Those around him all said: "Indeed so." Zhongqi pushed his zither aside and replied. The assessment of the world by three is wrong. In the past, during the time of the Three Jin states, the Zhi clan was the strongest. They destroyed Fan and Zhonghang, leading Han and Wei to besiege Zhao Xiangzi in Jinyang. They diverted the Jin River to flood Jinyang; only three planks of the city wall remained above water. Zhi Bo went out for an inspection of the waters, with Han Kangzi driving his chariot and Wei Huanzi riding as a passenger. Zhi Bo said: "At first, I did not know that water could destroy a nation; now I understand it. "The Fen River can be used to flood Anyi, and the Jiang River can be used to flood Pingyang." Duke Huanzi of Wei elbowed Duke Kangzi of Han, and Duke Kangzi stepped on the heels of Duke Huanzi. Their elbows and feet touched in the chariot, and thus the Zhi clan was divided. He died a violent death and his state perished, becoming a laughingstock to all under heaven. Now the strength of Qin cannot surpass that of Zhi Bo; Although Han and Wei are now weak, their virtue is still comparable to those under Jinyang. "This is precisely the time when they will be using their elbows and feet [to conspire], I hope Your Majesty does not take them lightly."


### Chu and Wei Fight at Xingshan Mountain

Chu and Wei fought at Xingshan. Wei needed Qin to take Shangluo in order to cut off Qin from Chu. Wei won the battle, and Chu was defeated at Nanyang. Qin demanded a bribe from Wei, but Wei did not give it. Ying Qian said to King Qin, "Your Majesty, why not tell King of Chu that the State of Wei promised me territory, but now after winning the battle, King Wei has betrayed me." "Why does Your Majesty not meet with me?" Wei, fearing the alliance of Qin and Chu, will certainly give land to Qin. This would mean that Wei has defeated Chu but lost territory to Qin; this would be Your Majesty using Wei's land as a virtue toward me, and those from Qin going to Chu will have ample resources. "If Wei is weak, if it does not give up its land, then Your Majesty attacks from the south and I cut off its western route, Wei will certainly be in danger." King Qin said, "Good." He informed Chu of this. King of Chu publicly declared that he would meet with Qin, and King Wei heard about it and was afraid, so he offered Shangluo to Qin.


### Chu Envoy Jing Li in Qin

The Chu envoy Jing Li was in Qin, accompanying King of Qin and King of Wei who met at the border. Chu was angry about Qin's alliance, and Zhou Zui said to King of Chu: "Wei requested not to meet with Chu but instead to form an alliance with Qin, so that is why Jing Li met them." "The reason why Qi did not join the alliance was because our small state treated them kindly when they met." King of Chu therefore did not punish Jing Li but showed gratitude toward Zhou and Qin.


### King of Chu Sends Jing Li to Qin

The King of Chu sent Jing Li to the State of Qin. A guest said to King Qin, "Jing Li is a person greatly loved by the King of Chu. Your Majesty might as well detain him in order to obtain territory." If the King of Chu agrees, then he can acquire land without resorting to military force; "If the King of Chu does not agree, then he can kill Jing Li and refuse to comply altogether. It would be better for him not to have Jing Li detained at all; this is a convenient strategy." King Qin then detained Jing Li. Jing Li sent someone to persuade King Qin, saying, "I see that Your Majesty's authority is slight in the eyes of all under heaven, and yet land cannot be obtained." "I have come as an envoy, and I heard that Qi and Wei are both about to cede territory in order to serve Qin. The reason for this is because Qin and Chu are regarded as brother states. "Now, if Your Majesty detains me, it will show to all under heaven that Chu no longer exists; how can Qi and Wei be more important than an isolated state?" "If Chu realizes that Qin is isolated, refuses to cede land, and instead forms alliances with the feudal lords for plans of its own, then surely the state will be in danger. It would be better to release me." King Qin then released him.


### Prince Qin Wants to See Dun Ruo

Prince Qin wanted to see Dun Ruo. Dun Ruo said, "According to my principles, I do not pay respects by bowing; if Your Majesty can allow me to come without bowing, that would suffice." "No, then I will not see him either." Prince Qin agreed to this. At that time, Dun Zi said, "In the world, there are those who have the reality but not the name, those who lack the reality yet possess the name, and those who neither have the name nor the reality. "Do you know this, Your Majesty?" The king said, "I do not know." Dun Zi said, "Those who have the reality but lack the name are the merchants. They do not possess the power of plowing and sowing with hoes, yet they have the reality of accumulated grain; these are those who have the substance but lack the title." Those without the reality but possessing the name are the farmers. They plow when the ground thaws, toil under the sun weeding, yet have no accumulated grain in reality; these are those without the substance but with the title." Those who possess neither the name nor the reality—you, Your Majesty, are precisely such a case. You have already been established as a ruler of ten thousand chariots, yet you lack the reputation for filial piety; you govern over thousands of li in territory but do not embody the reality of filial conduct." Prince Qin became greatly angered and indignant.

Dun Ruo said, "There are six warring states east of the mountains. Your authority is not concealed by the eastern mountain regions but is overshadowed by your mother. I secretly consider this unacceptable for Your Majesty." Prince Qin asked, "Is it possible to unify all the states east of the mountains?" Dun Zi said, "Han is the throat of the world; Wei is the chest and abdomen of the world. If Your Majesty provides me with ten thousand gold pieces to travel, I will go to Han and Wei. By winning over their ministers of state and bringing them into the service of Qin, then Han and Wei will follow." "Once Han and Wei are secured in our favor, the whole world can be brought under Your Majesty's control." Prince Qin said, "My state is poor; I fear it cannot provide that." Dun Zi said, "The world has never been without affairs; there is either a vertical alliance or a horizontal one. If the horizontal alliances are formed, then Qin will become an emperor; if the vertical alliance is formed, then Chu will be king. If Qin becomes emperor, then all under heaven will respectfully support it; if Chu becomes king, even though Your Majesty has ten thousand gold pieces, you cannot use them for personal gain." Prince Qin said, "Well said." He then provided ten thousand gold pieces and sent him east to Han and Wei to bring their generals and ministers into service. Bi traveled through Yan and Zhao, and killed Li Mu. When the King of Qi came to court, the four states would certainly follow—this was Dun Zi's strategy.


### Twentieth Year of King Qingxiangwang's Reign

In the twentieth year of King Qingxiangwang's reign, Qin Baiqi captured Xiling of Chu. Some sources say that Yanying and Yiling were also captured, and the tombs of previous kings were burned. The king moved northeast and took refuge in the city of Chen. Chu thus became weakened and was looked down upon by Qin. At this time, Baiqi once again led troops to launch an attack. There was a man from Chu named Huang Xie, who traveled and studied extensively and had broad knowledge; King Xiang considered him eloquent, so he sent him as an envoy to Qin. He persuaded King Zhao, "Among all the states under heaven, none are stronger than Qin and Chu. Now I have heard that Your Majesty intends to attack Chu; this is like two tigers fighting while a weak dog reaps the benefit. It would be better to maintain good relations with Chu." I beg to explain my reasoning: I have heard it said, "When things reach their extreme, they turn back; winter and summer are examples of this." "When accumulation reaches its peak, danger follows; a pile of chess pieces is an example of this." "Now the great state's territory covers half of the world, with two distant regions; since the beginning of mankind, there has never been a chariot force of ten thousand vehicles with such an expanse." The late Emperor Wenwang and Zhuangwang, as well as Your Majesty yourself, have for three generations not allowed the state of Qi to connect with your territory, thus cutting off the key alliances of the combined forces. Now Your Majesty has sent three envoys, Sheng Qiao, to oversee affairs in Han, and Cheng Qiao has entered Yan from the north. This means that without using armor or extending your authority, Your Majesty has ceded a territory of one hundred li; indeed, Your Majesty can be called capable.

Your Majesty also raised troops to attack Wei, blocked the gates of Daliang, captured He Nei, and took Yan, Suanzao, Xu, and Tao Ren. The armies of Chu and Yan flew like clouds but dared not resist; Your Majesty's achievements have been numerous indeed. Your Majesty declared a two-year respite for the people, and then restored them; you also captured Pu, Yan, and Shouyuan to face the forces at Renping Bing. The cities of Xiao Huang and Junyang were encircled, and thus Wei submitted. Your Majesty also ceded the area north of Pu and Mo to Yan, cutting off the vital link between Qi and Qin, and severing the backbone of Chu and Wei. The five alliances and six gatherings of the world dared not come to their rescue; indeed, Your Majesty's authority is formidable. If Your Majesty can maintain your achievements and uphold your authority, restrain the desire for war, and instead cultivate the precepts of benevolence and righteousness to prevent future troubles, then even the three sage kings would not surpass you by one, nor would the five hegemons exceed you by two. "If Your Majesty burdens your people with excessive conscriptions. And relies on the strength of weapons and armor, completely destroying Wei's authority, yet still intends to subdue all the lords under heaven by force alone, I fear that future troubles will arise." The Book of Songs says: "All things have a beginning; few reach their end." The Book of Changes says: "A fox wets its tail." This means that it is easy to begin, but difficult to bring things to a successful conclusion. Why do I know this? The House of Zhi saw the benefit in attacking Zhao, but did not foresee the disaster at Yuci; Wu saw the advantage in attacking Qi, yet failed to anticipate its defeat at Gansui. These two states were not without great achievements; they placed benefits before them, yet overlooked the dangers that followed. Wu trusted Yue and joined forces to attack Qi; after defeating the people of Qi at Ailing, Wu was later captured by King Yue at Sanjiang Pu. The House of Zhi trusted Han and Wei, joined forces to attack Zhao, captured the city of Jinye, and had already achieved victory for several days. However, Han and Wei turned against them and killed Zhibo Yao on the platform at Zao Tai. Now Your Majesty is envious of Chu's failure to be destroyed, yet you go so far as to undermine the strong alliance between Chu and Wei. I have considered this matter on behalf of Your Majesty and do not recommend it. The Book of Songs says: "Great achievements in warfare are far from the home, yet they remain untouched." From this we can see, the state of Chu is a source of support; a neighboring state is an enemy. The Book of Songs: "Others may have their thoughts, but I can fathom them." "Quickly darting hares are caught by dogs." Now, Your Majesty halfway trusts the kindness of Han and Wei toward you; this is exactly like Wu trusting Yue.

I have heard that enemies must not be underestimated, and opportunities must not be missed. I fear that Han and Wei are using humble words to conceal their intentions, while in reality they are deceiving Your Majesty's great state. Why is this so? Your Majesty has no accumulated virtue of generations with Han and Wei, yet harbors long-standing grievances. For a hundred generations, fathers and sons, brothers and relatives from Han and Wei have died in succession at the hands of Qin. Their homeland was ravaged, their ancestral temples destroyed, their family shrines defiled; abdomens were carved open and jaws broken, heads separated from bodies, bones exposed in fields and marshes, skulls shattered and corpses strewn across the borders, lying in sight of one another; Fathers, sons, elders, and the weak were captured as prisoners, following each other along the roads; The ghosts and gods had no offerings to eat, the people could barely survive, families were scattered, and many fled into servitude as slaves and concubines, filling the land. The survival of Han and Wei is a concern for Qin's ancestral temple. Now, Your Majesty's attack on Chu—does it not represent a mistake! On the day when Your Majesty attacks Chu, then where will the troops come from? Will Your Majesty borrow a route through the hostile states of Han and Wei? On the day your troops march out, Your Majesty fears they may not return; this means you are providing military strength to your enemies, Han and Wei. If Your Majesty does not borrow a route through the enemy states of Han and Wei, then you must attack Yang and Yourang. Following the paths to Yang and Yourang, these are all vast rivers and deep valleys, uninhabitable land covered by mountains and forests; even if Your Majesty possesses them, it will not be considered a gain of territory. This means that Your Majesty would have the reputation of destroying Chu without achieving any actual territorial gains. "Moreover, on the day Your Majesty attacks Chu, four states will surely rise up to respond to you. If Qin and Chu become entangled in conflict without resolution, the House of Wei will send troops to attack Liu, Fangyu, Zhi, Huling, Dang, Xiao, and Xiang; therefore, Song must be completely lost. The people of Qi will face southward, and the area north of Si River will surely rise in response. These are all flat, well-connected regions with fertile land, yet Your Majesty leaves them to be attacked alone. Your Majesty's destruction of Chu will only strengthen Han and Wei in the central regions and embolden Qi; the strength of Han and Wei would then be sufficient to rival Qin. Qi has the Si River as its southern border, faces the sea in the east, and relies on the Yellow River to the north, with no threats from behind; among all the states of the world, none is stronger than Qi. If Qi and Wei gain territory and preserve their advantages, yet treat the subordinates with caution and care, after one year they may not yet be able to declare themselves emperors, but it would still be sufficient to prevent Your Majesty from becoming emperor. With Your Majesty's vast territory, numerous people, and strong military forces, to launch a single campaign that cedes land to Chu, bows to Han and Wei, and allows the imperial title to be returned to Qi—this would be a miscalculation on Your Majesty's part. "I have considered matters for Your Majesty; there is no better course of action than maintaining good relations with Chu. If Qin and Chu unite as one, facing Han together, Han will surely surrender its head in submission. Your Majesty is protected by the dangers of the Shandong region to your west, and secured by the advantages of the Yellow River's bends to your east; Han will certainly become a spectator waiting for an opportunity. If this is the case, Your Majesty will hold ten times the territory of Zheng; the House of Liang will be chilled with fear. The cities of Xu and Yancheng will be encircled, while Shangcai and Zhaoling will cease to communicate. In this way, Wei would become a mere marquis within the pass. If Your Majesty maintains good relations with Chu, then the two great powers within the pass will cede their territory to Qi. The western territories of Qi can be taken by simply extending one's hands. This would mean that Your Majesty's territory spans both eastern and western seas, cutting off all other states from the center of the world. In this case, Yan and Zhao would be nothing without Qi and Chu; likewise, Qi and Chu would also be nothing without Yan and Zhao. Only then can you threaten Yan and Zhao while holding Qi and Chu in check; with these four states, they will submit without the need for further punishment."


### One Advises King Qin on Behalf of the Six States

One person advised King Qin of the Six States saying: "A vast territory is not sufficient for security, and a large population is not sufficient for strength." If a vast territory ensured safety, and a large population ensured strength, then the descendants of Jie and Zhou would still be alive. In the past, the state of Zhao was also once powerful. He asked, "How strong was Zhao?" With its left hand it subdued Qi, with its right hand it subdued Wei. It crushed a thousand-chariot state, two states, and the thousand-chariot state of Song. They built Gangping; Wei had no eastern frontier. The gathering of fodder, grazing, firewood, and forage was so controlled that no one dared to look toward the eastern gate. At that time, Wei was more perilous than a pile of eggs. Scholars from around the world gathered and plotted: "Shall we return their tribute and pay homage to the ruler of Handan?" Thus, whenever there was talk in the world of attacking Handan, none failed to prepare for a campaign. When Wei attacked Handan, it retreated and became the Fenzhe Encounter. It rode in summer chariots, claimed to be King Xia, was honored as Son of Heaven by the morning, and all under heaven followed. When Tai Gong of Qi heard about this, he raised an army to attack Wei; its territory was divided in two and the state faced great peril. King Liang personally carried a hostage and held up jade, requesting to become a subject of Lord Chen; only then did the world release Wei. King Weiwang of Ying heard about this and could not sleep or eat. He led the people of all under heaven to meet Shen Jue on the banks of the Si River, where he decisively defeated him. The people of Zhao heard about it and arrived at Zhisang; the people of Yan heard about it and came to Gedao. Gedao was cut off, and Pingji was completely isolated. Qi, having suffered a defeat and failed in its strategies, sent Chen Mao to lay down his sword and bow, surrendering to the south while listening for judgment. It then went west to persuade Zhao, north to persuade Yan, and internally explained to its people; thus, all under heaven released Qi. Thus, by accumulating thinness into thickness and gathering little into much, they equated the words of King Weiwang of Ying with those between Ji Zhou. Could I possibly think that King Weiwang of Ying was governing in decline and plotting disorder, leading to this situation? Ying became strong and faced all the feudal lords under heaven; thus, the world was willing to attack it!"


## Qin Wu


### To King Qin

He said to King Qin, "I secretly feel puzzled by Your Majesty's light regard for Qi and ease toward Chu, and your lowly treatment of Han." I have heard that a king whose army wins is not arrogant, and a hegemon who makes alliances is not resentful. Victorious yet not arrogant, thus he can subdue the world; Allied yet not resentful, thus he can command his neighbors. Now Your Majesty extends virtue to Wei and Zhao, yet lightly loses Qi—this is arrogance; Having conquered Yiyang in battle, yet showing no concern for the alliance with Chu—is this not resentment? Arrogance and resentment are not the deeds of a hegemon. I secretly fear for Your Majesty, and thus do not approve."

The "Shi Jing" says: "All things have their beginning; few can bring them to a successful end." Therefore, what the former kings valued most was only the beginning and the ending. How do we know this to be so? In the past, Zhi Bo Yao devastated Fan and Zhonghang, besieged and pressured Jinyang, and in the end was laughed at by the three families. King Fuchai of Wu exiled Yue to Kuaiji, defeated Qi at Ailing, and attended the Huangchi assembly, where he showed no courtesy toward Song. As a result, he was captured by Goujian and died in Gansui. The ruler of Liang defeated Chu and Qi, controlled the armies of Zhao and Han, drove twelve feudal lords to pay homage to the Son of Heaven at Mengjin. Later his son died, and he himself was captured by Qin in a commoner's hat. The three were not without merit; they could begin but could not bring things to a successful conclusion.

Now Your Majesty has captured Yiyang, devastated the Three Rivers, and made the scholars of all under heaven dare not speak. ### Pu Yang native Lü Buwei did business in Handan
