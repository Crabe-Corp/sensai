# Zhao Ce


## Zhao 1


### Zhi Bo Follows Han and Wei Armies to Attack Zhao

Zhi Bo followed the armies of Han and Wei to attack Zhao, besieging Jinyang and flooding it; the water rose to within three planks of the city walls. Xie Ci said to Zhi Bo, "The rulers of Han and Wei must be plotting rebellion now." Zhi Bo asked, "On what basis do you know this?" Xie Ci said, "I know it by observing their human affairs. Following the armies of Han and Wei in attacking Zhao, once Zhao falls, the disaster will surely reach Han and Wei as well." Now they have agreed that after defeating Zhao, its territory will be divided equally among three. Now the city is nearly submerged, with only three planks above water; pestles and stoves are growing frogs, people and horses are eating each other, and the city's surrender is imminent. Yet the rulers of Han and Wei show no joy but instead have worried expressions—how can this not be a sign of rebellion?"

The next day, Zhi Bo informed the rulers of Han and Wei, saying, "Xie Ci has said that you are about to rebel." The rulers of Han and Wei replied, "After defeating Zhao and dividing its territory among three, the city is now about to fall. Though the three families may be foolish, they would not abandon a favorable opportunity before them, break an oath of trust, and engage in dangerous and unachievable actions; this situation is clearly evident. This was Xie Ci's plan for Zhao, to make you doubt the loyalty of our two rulers and thus ease the attack on Zhao. Now if you listen to this slanderous minister's words and alienate the alliance between our two rulers, we regret it for your sake." They hurriedly left. Xie Ci said to Zhi Boh, "Why did you inform the rulers of Han and Wei about my words?" Zhi Bo asked, "How could you know that?" He replied, "The rulers of Han and Wei looked at me intently and hurried away quickly." Knowing that his words would not be heeded, Xie Ci requested to be sent as an envoy to Qi, and Zhi Bo dispatched him. The rulers of Han and Wei indeed rebelled.


### Zhi Bo Led Zhao, Han and Wei to Attack Fan Zhonghang Clan

Zhi Bo led Zhao, Han, and Wei to attack Fan and Zhonghang clans, and annihilated them. After several years of peace, he sent someone to request land from the State of Han. Han Kangzi wanted not to give it, but Duan Gui advised him, saying: "You cannot do that." Zhi Bo's character is such that he loves profit and is fierce in retaliation. If we do not give him the land when he requests it, he will certainly launch military action against Han. You should give it to him. If we give it to him, he will grow accustomed to this and then request land from other states. If those states refuse, they will inevitably face his military force; In that case, Han can avoid immediate trouble and await the change in circumstances." Kangzi said: "Good." He sent an envoy to present one town with ten thousand households to Zhi Bo. Zhi Bo was pleased, and then sent someone else to request land from the state of Wei. Lord Xuanzi of Wei wanted not to give it. Zhao Jia advised, saying: "He requested land from Han, and Han gave it to him." "Now he is requesting land from Wei. If Wei does not give it to him, then internally we will be strengthening ourselves and externally angering Zhi Bo." "Therefore, he must certainly direct his military forces against Wei!" "It is better to give it to him." Xuanzi said: "Agreed." He therefore sent an envoy to present Zhi Bo with one town of ten thousand households. Zhi Bo was pleased and then sent someone to Zhao, requesting the land of Cai and Gaolang; Lord Xiangzi of Zhao refused to give it. Zhi Bo therefore secretly allied with Han and Wei in order to attack Zhao.

Lord Xiangzi of Zhao summoned Zhang Mengtan and told him: "Zhi Bo's nature is such that he appears friendly on the surface but is actually distant. He has sent envoys three times to Han and Wei, yet I refused their requests. It is certain now that he will turn his military forces against me." "Now what can I do to remain at peace?" Zhang Mengtan said: "Dong Anyu was a capable minister of Jianzhu. He governed Jinyang for generations, and Yin Duo followed his example; the remaining policies and teachings still remain. Your Majesty should settle in Jinyang." The lord said: "Agreed." He then sent Yanling Sheng to lead the chariots and cavalry ahead to Jinyang, and the lord followed him. lando When they arrived, he inspected the city walls, examined the treasuries, and viewed the granaries. He summoned Zhang Mengtan and said: "Our city walls are already complete, our treasuries sufficient for use, and our granaries well stocked. But what shall we do about a lack of arrows?" Zhang Mengtan said: "I have heard that when Dongzi governed Jinyang, the walls of the palace were all built with Di Hao and Ku Chu plants, reaching over a zhang in height. Your Majesty may now take them down for use." They then took them down and tested them; their strength was such that even the best bamboo arrows could not surpass it. The lord said: "That is sufficient." "But what about our copper?" Zhang Mengtan said: "I have heard that when Dongzi administered Jinyang, the pillars of the palace rooms were all made with refined copper. Please take them down and use them; there will then be more than enough copper." The lord said: "Good." Orders were established, and preparations for defense were complete.

The armies of the three states launched an attack on Jinyang city, and a battle ensued. After three months they could not capture it, so they spread out their troops to encircle the city and diverted the Jin River waters to flood it. The siege of Jinyang lasted three years; within the city, people lived in nests and cooked with suspended pots. Their supplies were nearly exhausted, and their soldiers became sickly and weak. Xiangzi said to Zhang Mengtan: "Our food supplies are running low, the city's defenses have been exhausted, and our officials and soldiers are suffering. I can no longer hold out. I want to surrender the city. What do you think?" Zhang Mengtan said: "I have heard it said, 'If one cannot preserve what is lost or bring stability in times of danger, then there is no value in being a wise man.'" "Your Majesty should abandon this plan and never speak of it again." "I shall go to see the lords of Han and Wei." Xiangzi said: "Agreed."

Zhang Mengtan then secretly met with the lords of Han and Wei and said: "I have heard that when lips are lost, teeth feel cold. Now Zhi Bo is leading the two states to attack Zhao; if Zhao falls, you will be next." The two lords said: "We know this to be true." "Zhi Bo's nature, however, is coarse and he has few close allies. If our plan is discovered before it is carried out, the disaster will surely come upon us. What can we do about this?" Zhang Mengtan said: "The plan will leave your mouths and enter my ears; no one else will know about it." The two lords then secretly agreed with Zhang Mengtan on a plan involving the three armies, setting a date. At night, they sent him into Jinyang. Zhang Mengtan reported this to Xiangzi, who bowed twice in gratitude.

While Zhang Mengtan was going out to pay homage to Zhi Bo and then leaving, he encountered Zhiguo outside the 辕门 (yuanmen). Zhiguo went in to see Zhi Bo and said: "The two lords may be about to change their allegiance." Zhi Bo asked: "What is it like?" Zhiguo replied: "I met Zhang Mengtan outside the 辕门, and his demeanor was proud, and his conduct lofty." Zhi Bo said: "Not so." "I have made a careful agreement with the two lords. After defeating Zhao, we will divide its territory into three parts. I trust them personally and they certainly will not betray me. Forget what you saw and do not speak of it." Zhiguo left, met with the two lords, and then returned to advise Zhi Bo: "The expressions of the two lords have changed; they must be turning against you. It would be better now to kill them." Zhi Bo said: "Our troops have been besieging Jinyang for three years now, and day or night we are about to capture it and enjoy its benefits. Could they possibly have other intentions?" "It must not be so. Be careful and do not mention this again." Zhiguo said: "If you do not kill them, then you should at least become closer to them." Zhi Bo asked: "How can I grow closer to them?" Zhiguo said: "Lord Xuanzi of Wei has a strategist named Zhao Jia, and Lord Kangzi of Han has a strategist named Duan Gui. These are both men who can influence their lords' decisions." "You should make an agreement with the two lords that, upon defeating Zhao, you will each grant these two men a county of ten thousand households. If this is done, then the minds of the two lords can remain unchanged, and Your Majesty will achieve what he desires." Zhi Bo said: "After defeating Zhao and dividing its territory into three parts, to further grant each of these two men a county with ten thousand households would mean I receive less. That is not acceptable." Seeing that his advice was not heeded by the lord, and realizing his words were ignored, Zhiguo left. He changed his surname to Fu and departed without being seen again.

Zhang Mengtan heard about this and went in to see Xiangzi, saying: "I met Zhiguo outside the yuanmen. His gaze showed suspicion of me. After entering to see Zhi Bo, he left and changed his surname." "If we do not attack tonight, it will be too late later on." Xiangzi said: "Agreed." He sent Zhang Mengtan to meet with the lords of Han and Wei, setting a night appointment. They killed the officials guarding the levee and released the water to flood Zhi Bo's army. Zhi Bo's troops, trying to save themselves from the floodwaters, became disorganized. The armies of Han and Wei attacked from both flanks while Lord Xiangzi led his soldiers in a frontal assault, resulting in a decisive defeat for Zhi Bo's forces and the capture of Zhi Bo himself.

Zhi Bo died, his state was destroyed, and his territory divided; he became a laughingstock to the world. This was due to his insatiable greed. Failure to heed Zhiguo's advice was also the reason for his downfall. The Zhi clan was completely annihilated, and only the Fu clan survived. "


### Zhang Mengtan Consolidates the Zhao Clan

Zhang Mengtan, having consolidated the Zhao clan, expanded his territory and issued 500 decrees. He then followed a simple path to inform Xiangzi, saying: "In former times, the ruler of the previous state said: 'The reason why 500 can bring about dominion over all under heaven is due to restraint; two sovereigns must have the power to control their ministers, and one must not allow ministers to control the sovereign." "Therefore, those who are honored as marquises should not be placed in the position of prime minister; from generals and above, they should not serve as close attendants." "Now my name is renowned and my person respected, my authority great and the masses obedient. I wish to relinquish merit and fame, abandon power and influence, in order to distance myself from the people." Xiangzi replied with a look of regret: "Why is that?" "I have heard that those who assist the sovereign gain renown, those with great achievements are respected, and those entrusted with state affairs hold heavy authority. It is through personal loyalty and trustworthiness that one earns obedience from the masses." "This is how the former sages were able to unite the state and ensure peace for the nation!" "Why, then, do you say such a thing?" Zhang Mengtan replied: "What the lord has said is the beauty of successful achievement. "What I have spoken of, however, is the way to preserve the state." "I observe past achievements and hear of ancient times. Though the beauty of all under heaven may be the same, there has never been a case where the power between sovereign and minister is equal and harmony can still prevail." "Remembering past events serves as a teacher for future affairs." "If the lord does not take this to heart, then my strength will be insufficient." He looked deeply moved and resolute. Xiangzi dismissed him. After lying in seclusion for three days, he sent someone to say: "Regarding the administration of Jinyang, what would it be like if subordinates did not obey orders?" He replied: "Death and disgrace." Zhang Mengtan said: "The Left 司马 (Left Sima) was entrusted by the state to ensure peace for the nation. He did not avoid death in order to fulfill his loyalty. Your lordship should carry this out." The lord said: "You may proceed with your task." He then granted his approval. lou Zhang Mengtan thus reduced his wealth and fame, surrendered territory and responsibilities to relinquish power and status, and went to cultivate the fields at Fupin Qiu. Therefore it is said: The conduct of a virtuous person is the governance of an enlightened ruler.

After tilling the land for three years, Han, Wei, Qi, and Yan gathered at Fupin to plot against Zhao. Xiangzi went to see Zhang Mengtan and told him: "In former times, when the lands of the Zhī clan were divided among the Zhao clan, we gained ten more cities. Now, the feudal lords are plotting against us; what should we do about it?" Zhang Mengtan said: "Your lordship, please carry your sword and allow me to serve you in governing the state. Place me at the ancestral temple, appoint officials as ministers, and I shall try to devise a plan." The lord said: "Agreed." Zhang Mengtan then set out. His wife went to Chu, his eldest son to Han, the second son to Wei, and the youngest son to Qi. The four states became suspicious and their plot failed.


### Jin Biyang's Grandson Yu Rang

Jin Biyang's grandson Yu Rang first served the families of Fan and Zhonghang but was not satisfied, so he left them to join Zhibo. Zhibo favored him. When the three Jin states divided the Zhī clan, Zhao Xiangzi harbored the deepest resentment toward Zhibo, and he used his head as a drinking vessel. Yu Rang fled into the mountains and said, "Alas! A gentleman dies for one who recognizes his worth; a woman adorns herself for one who loves her. I must avenge the Zhī clan's enemies." He then changed his name and appearance, disguised himself as a criminal laborer, entered the palace to clean the latrine, intending to assassinate Zhao Xiangzi. When Zhao Xiangzi went to the latrine, he felt a sudden unease and arrested the cleaner for questioning. It turned out to be Yu Rang. He brandished his weapon and said, "I wish to avenge Zhī Bo!" Zhao Xiangzi's attendants wanted to kill him. Zhao Xiangzi said, "He is a man of righteousness; I shall merely be cautious and avoid him. Moreover, Zhī Bo is already dead with no descendants, yet his minister still comes to avenge him. He is indeed a virtuous man in the world." He finally released him. Yu Rang then smeared his body with soot to make it look like leprosy, shaved off his beard and eyebrows, self-mutilated to alter his appearance, and went out as a beggar. His wife did not recognize him and said, "His appearance does not resemble my husband at all, yet how similar his voice is to that of my husband!" He then swallowed charcoal to make himself mute, altering his voice. His friend said to him, "Your way is very difficult and will achieve nothing. One might say you have determination, but one cannot call it wisdom. With your talent, if you serve Zhao Xiangzi well, he will surely favor and trust you; you could easily achieve what you desire by being close to him, and your goal would certainly succeed." Yu Rang smiled and replied, "To avenge an old lord by betraying the new one is to create great disorder in the principle of loyalty between ruler and subject; nothing could be worse than this. What I am doing now is to demonstrate the righteousness between lord and minister, not for ease or convenience. Moreover, if one accepts a position of service to another and then seeks to kill him, that is serving the lord with divided loyalty. My actions may be difficult, but they are meant to shame future generations of ministers who harbor two minds."

After a short while, when Zhao Xiangzi was about to go out, Yu Rang lay in ambush beneath the cart he would pass. When Zhao Xiangzi reached the bridge and his horse became frightened, he said, "This must be Yu Rang." He sent someone to investigate, and it was indeed Yu Rang. At this, Zhao Xiangzi confronted Yu Rang in person and said, "Did you not once serve the families of Fan and Zhonghang?" When Zhibo destroyed the families of Fan and Zhōngháng, you did not avenge them, but instead pledged your service to Zhibo. Now that Zhibo is dead, why do you seek vengeance for him so deeply?" Yu Rang said, "I served the families of Fan and Zhòngháng. They treated me as an ordinary person, so I repaid them in kind; Zhibo treated me like a national hero, and thus I must repay him with the loyalty of a national hero." Zhao Xiangzi sighed deeply and wept, saying, "Alas, Master Yu! Your loyalty to Zhī Bo has already earned you a name. I will spare your life; that should be sufficient." You must decide for yourself, but I shall not kill you." He ordered his soldiers to surround him. Yu Rang said, "I have heard that a wise ruler does not suppress the righteousness of his subjects, and a loyal minister will not cherish life at the expense of fame. Your Majesty has already shown me clemency; no one in the world fails to praise your virtue. Today's matter, I willingly accept punishment for it, but I wish only to strike Your Majesty's robe with my sword. If I may do that, even in death I shall have no regrets. This is not what I dare hope for, but I must express the sincerity of my heart." Moved by Yu Rang's righteousness, Zhao Xiangzi ordered a messenger to present him with his robe. Yu Rang drew his sword and leaped three times, crying out to heaven as he struck the robe, saying, "Now I can avenge Zhī Bo." He then fell upon his sword and died. On the day of his death, all the scholars in the State of Zhao who heard about it wept bitterly.

### Su Qin Persuades Li Dui

Su Qin said to Li Dui, "Su Qin of Chengxuan Li in Luoyang, whose family is poor and parents are old, has no worn-out carriage or weak horse. He travels with a mulberry wheel, a wicker chest covered with straw, and thin ropes for binding his luggage. Carrying books on his back and a satchel over his shoulder, he endures dust and dew, braving frost and mist, crossing the Zhang and He rivers. His feet are wrapped in thick calluses, and he travels more than a hundred li each day before finding shelter. Now I have arrived at your outer gate, wishing to present myself before you and speak of affairs throughout the world." Li Dui said, "If Master wishes to see me with words from spirits, that is acceptable. But if it concerns matters of ordinary people, I already know them all." Su Qin replied, "I have indeed come to see you with words from spirits, not with the words of ordinary people." Li Dui received him. Su Qin said, "When I came today in the evening, I arrived after the outer gate had closed. I found no place to lay a mat and spent the night staying at someone's field, with a large thicket nearby." At midnight, a piece of earth clod and a wooden splinter quarreled, saying, "You are not as good as I am. I am made from the soil." "If I were to encounter a violent wind or heavy rain and be ruined, I would return to the earth." "But you are not the root of the tree; you are merely one of its branches." "If you were to encounter a violent wind and heavy rain, you would be washed into the Zhang or He rivers, flow eastward to the sea, drifting aimlessly without end." "I secretly believe that the earth clod is superior." "Now, you have killed Lord Fu and exterminated his family. Your position in the world is more precarious than a pile of eggs." "If you listen to my plan, you will live; if you do not listen to my plan, you shall die." Li Dui said, "Master may go to your lodging now. Please come back and see me again tomorrow." Su Qin left.

Li Dui's attendant said to Li Dui, "I secretly observed your conversation with Su Gong. His eloquence surpasses yours, and his breadth of knowledge exceeds yours. Would you be able to follow Su Gong's plan?" Li Dui said, "No, I cannot." The attendant said, "Since you are unable to do so, I wish you would firmly block your ears and not listen to his talk." The next day he saw him again; they talked all day before Su Qin left. The attendant went out to see Su Qin off, and Su Qin said to the attendant, "Yesterday my talk was rough and you were moved by it; today it was refined yet you showed no reaction. Why is that?" The attendant said, "Your plan is grand and your vision lofty, but my lord cannot employ it." "That is why I advised you to block your ears and not hear any talk." "Nevertheless, if Master comes again tomorrow, I will request funds for you so that you may be well provided for." The next day he came and they talked with clasped hands. Li Dui gave Su Qin the pearl of Mingyue, the He Shi jade, a sable fur coat, and one hundred yi of gold. With these resources, Su Qin was able to proceed westward into Qin.


### Zhao Takes Control of the Empire and Launches a Campaign Against Qi

Zhao Shou took over the empire and then launched a campaign against Qi. Su Qin submitted a memorial to Qi on behalf of Zhao Wang, saying: "I have heard that the virtuous rulers of old did not impose their moral conduct upon all under heaven; nor did they spread teachings and kindness to all the people. Nor was it appropriate for them to offer timely sacrifices to ghosts and gods." Sweet dew fell, winds and rains arrived at the proper time, farmers climbed to their fields, grain harvests were abundant, and the common people rejoiced; yet a virtuous ruler would dislike it. Now your achievements are not repeatedly inflicted upon the state of Qin, and accumulated resentment and hatred have not been deeply provoked against Han. I secretly heard the discussions of high ministers and lower officials, who all said that Your Majesty previously held a biased view, believing that Qin loved Zhao but hated Han. I secretly observe the matter and think: How could Qin possibly love Zhao while hating Han? They wish to destroy Han and swallow the land of the two Zhou states, so they use Han as bait, first making a declaration before all under heaven, hoping that neighboring states will hear it and observe. They are afraid their plan may not succeed, so they send out troops to feign a display of force toward Zhao and Wei. They fear that all under heaven will become alarmed and aware, so they slightly involve Han to divide their attention. They fear the world may doubt them, so they send a hostage as proof of good faith. They proclaim virtue to their allies and actually launch attacks on Zheng and Han. I secretly observe the map, and I believe that Qin's strategy must be like this.

"The strategies of the eloquent men all say that if Han loses the Three Rivers region and Wei loses the Jin state, before the markets and courts have even ended, disaster will reach Zhao. Moreover, there are cases where situations differ but dangers are the same, as well as cases where situations are the same but dangers differ. In the past, after a prolonged attack by the Chu people, Zhongshan perished. Now Yan has occupied all of Qi's northern territory, stretching from Shachou to the border of Julu for three hundred li; From Hanguan Pass to Yuzhong is fifteen hundred li. If Qin completely occupies Shangdang of Han and Wei, then the land bordering the capital city and neighboring states would be seven hundred li. If Qin stations its three armies with powerful crossbows at Yangtang, then the distance to Handan would be only one hundred and twenty li. Moreover, if Qin uses its three armies to attack Your Majesty's Shangdang region and encircles it from the north, then the western part of Guozhu will no longer belong to you. Now, if one crosses Guozhu and blocks Changshan for defense, the three-hundred-li route connects to Yan's Tang and Qu'ni. In that case, neither the steeds of Dai nor the foals of Hu will come eastward, nor will jade from Kunshan emerge. These three treasures would no longer be yours either. Now, by aligning with the powerful state of Qin for a prolonged attack on Qi, I fear that disaster will arise precisely from this. In the past, the kings of the Five States once formed a horizontal alliance to plan an attack on Zhao, dividing Zhao's territory into three parts, recording it in Panyu and entrusting it to Chouzuo. The armies of the Five States were already prepared for a set day to march out, when Qi sent its western forces to block Qin, compelling Qin to cancel orders and don plain mourning robes in submission. It returned Wen, Zhi, and Gaoping to Wei, and San Gong and Shqing to Zhao—this was Your Majesty's wise judgment. The state of Qi treating Zhao with courtesy is appropriate for the highest level of diplomatic relations; lords Now, however, it has resorted to punishment and launched an attack. I fear that in future generations, those who would serve as kings will not dare be certain of their own success. Now if Your Majesty seizes Qi, all under heaven will surely regard you as righteous. Qi has embraced its state and served Your Majesty; all under heaven will certainly respect you. In that case, if Qi is righteous, Your Majesty will gain the support of all under heaven; if Qi becomes tyrannical, Your Majesty can restrain it with the power of all under heaven. Thus, the fate of this generation lies in your hands alone. I wish Your Majesty would carefully deliberate with your close ministers and officials, thoroughly plan ahead, and maturely consider these matters before they occur."

### King of Qin Addresses Gongzi Ta

The King of Qin said to Gongzi Ta: "Last year's events at Xiao Xia, the State of Han was in the center vanguard, and attacked Qin together with the feudal lords." The State of Han shared a border with Qin; its territory did not cover thousands of li. Its shifting positions could not be predicted. Recently, Qin and Chu fought at Lan Tian. Han sent its elite troops to assist Qin, but when Qin suffered an unfavorable battle outcome, it turned against the State of Chu instead. It did not firmly uphold its alliances, but followed only what was convenient. Han's presence within our territory is a disease in our vital organs. I am going to attack it. What do you think?" Gongzi Ta said, "If the King sends troops against Han, Han will certainly be afraid. If they are afraid, we can obtain deep concessions without even fighting." The king said, "Good." He then mobilized troops, one army stationed at Yingyang and another at Taihang.

Han was afraid and sent Yangcheng Jun to apologize to Qin, offering the territory of Hedang as a gesture of peace. Ling Yang told the governor of Shangdang, Jin Tou: "Qin has mobilized two armies to threaten Han. Han can no longer hold on." Now the king is ordering Han to raise troops and cede Shangdang to Qin as a peace offering. Yang has conveyed this message to you, the governor; please comply accordingly." Jin Tou said: "There is a saying: 'The knowledge of carrying a jar lies in not losing the vessel.' "The king has given an order, but as your subordinate governor, even if the king and you suspect me, I still have my doubts. I request to mobilize all our defending forces to resist Qin. If we cannot hold out until the end, then I shall die for it." Han Yang hurried to report this to the king. The king said, "I have already promised Ying Hou. If I do not give it now, that would be deceiving him." He then sent Feng Ting to replace Jin Tou.

Feng Ting held Shangdang for thirty days, and secretly sent someone to King Zhao saying: "Han can no longer defend Shangdang and is about to hand it over to Qin. Its people all do not wish to be under Qin rule but desire to become part of Zhao." "Now there are seventeen walled cities, and we wish to submit them to Your Majesty. It is entirely up to the King's judgment." King Zhao was pleased and summoned Pingyang Jun, telling him: "Han can no longer defend Shangdong and is about to give it to Qin. Its officials and people do not wish to serve Qin but all desire to belong to Zhao." Now Feng Ting has sent envoys to offer it to me. What should I do?" Zhao Bao replied, "I have heard that sages consider unexpected gains as great misfortunes." The king said: "If people cherish my righteousness, how can this be considered an unexpected gain?" He replied, "Qin has been gradually devouring the lands of Han, cutting off communication in the middle. Therefore, they assume that Shangdang will fall into their hands without resistance." Moreover, the reason why Han is offering Shangdang to Zhao is to transfer its misfortune onto us. Qin has endured the labor of conquering, while Zhao reaps the benefit. Even a strong power cannot obtain something from a weak one without effort; how then can a small and weak state gain advantage over a powerful one? Now, if Your Majesty takes it, could this be considered justified?" Moreover, Qin uses oxen for plowing and has waterways to transport provisions. Its elite soldiers are stationed in the best territories, with strict orders and efficient governance; it cannot be confronted in battle. "Your Majesty should carefully consider this!" The king became greatly enraged and said, "Deploying a force of one million men, waging war for years upon years, yet not even a single city has been captured. Now we can obtain seventeen cities without fighting; why should we not do it?" Zhao Bao left.

The king summoned Zhao Sheng and Zhao Yu, telling them: "Han can no longer defend Shangtang. Now its governor has offered it to me, including seventeen walled cities." The two men replied: "We have waged war for over a year and not captured even one city. Now we can obtain cities without fighting; this is a great advantage." He then sent Zhao Sheng to accept the territory.

Zhao Sheng arrived and said: "Our king has sent his envoy, Minister Sheng. ### Su Qin Went from Yan to Zhao and Began the Vertical Alliance

Su Qin went from Yan to Zhao, initiating the alliance of vertical unity. He advised King Zhao, saying: "All the high officials and ministers under heaven, as well as common scholars, have long held Your Majesty's virtue and righteousness in great esteem; they all wish to offer their teachings and loyal advice before you." Although this is so, Lord Fengyang is jealous; therefore, Your Majesty cannot take charge of affairs. For this reason, foreign guests and traveling scholars dare not offer their wholehearted loyalty before you. Now that Lord Fengyang has passed away, Your Majesty can finally associate closely with the scholars and people. Therefore, I dare to offer my humble advice and demonstrate my loyal sincerity. For Your Majesty's consideration, there is no better plan than to pacify the people and maintain peace; I request that you refrain from taking any action. The foundation of pacifying the people lies in choosing good alliances. If an alliance is chosen wisely, then the people will be at peace; if it is not chosen wisely, the people cannot remain peaceful. Please allow me to speak of external dangers: Qi and Qin are two adversaries, and because of them, the people cannot be at peace; relying on Qin to attack Qi, the people still cannot be at peace; relying on Qi to attack Qin, the people also cannot be at peace. Therefore, plotting against another ruler and attacking another state often involves fabricating words to sever relations between people. I hope Your Majesty will be cautious not to utter such things from your mouth.

"Please dismiss those around you, for what I am about to say is different—nothing but yin and yang." If Your Majesty sincerely listens to me, Yan will certainly present you with lands of felt and furs, dogs, and horses; Qi will certainly offer the coastal areas rich in fish and salt; Chu will certainly give you the land of tangerines, oranges, and Yunmeng. Han and Wei can both be made to present fiefdoms for sacrificial use, and your noble relatives, fathers, and elder brothers may all receive marquisates. Offering land and tangible benefits is what the five hegemons of old sought by suffering military defeats and capturing their generals; granting marquisates to noble relatives is what Tang and Wu contended over, even resorting to exile or killing. Now Your Majesty can achieve both without effort; this is why I wish for you, O King. If Your Majesty allies with Qin, then Qin will certainly weaken Han and Wei; if you ally with Qi, then Qi will certainly weaken Chu and Wei. When Wei is weakened, it will cede the area beyond the Yellow River; when Han is weakened, it will offer Yiyang as a tribute. Once Yiyang is offered, the Shangjun region becomes cut off; once territory beyond the Yellow River is ceded, the route becomes impassable. When Chu is weakened, it has no support left. These three strategies cannot be considered without careful deliberation. If Qin takes the Zhi Road, Nanyang will be unsettled; if it threatens Han and encircles Zhou, Zhao will naturally weaken. If it occupies Wei and captures Qi, then Qi must come to court in submission. Once Qin has achieved its ambitions in the east of Mount Taihang, it will surely raise its army and turn toward Zhao. If Qin's troops cross the Yellow River and pass beyond the Zhang River, occupying Fanwu, then a battle will certainly take place beneath Handan. This is why I am concerned for Your Majesty.

"At present, among the states east of Mount Taihang (Shandong), none are stronger than Zhao. Zhao controls a territory of two thousand square li, has dozens of thousands of armed soldiers, a thousand chariots, ten thousand horses, and grain reserves sufficient to last for several decades; to the west it has Changshan, to the south the Yellow River and Zhang River, to the east the Qinghe, and to the north Yan State. Testing against weak states, Zhao is more than sufficient; it need not fear them. Moreover, among all the states under heaven, Qin fears and regards Zhao as its greatest threat. Yet why does Qin dare not raise arms to attack Zhao? Why is this so? It fears the possibility of Han and Wei discussing or acting against it from behind. Therefore, Han and Wei are Zhao's southern shield. When Qin attacks Han and Wei, it is different. There are no famous mountains or great rivers to serve as barriers; instead, Qin gradually devours them like silkworms eating leaves. Soon it will approach their capital cities and block them off. Han and Wei cannot resist Qin and must submit as vassals. Once Han and Wei become Qin's subjects, there will be no barrier between Qin and Zhao; disaster will fall upon Zhao. This is why I am worried for Your Majesty.

"Min Wen, by dividing the responsibilities of three men, Shun had no territory even as small as a chi in measure yet still came to rule over all under heaven. Yu had not even a gathering of one hundred people and yet became the ruler of the feudal lords. Tang and Wu each had no more than three thousand soldiers, no more than three hundred chariots, yet they became Son of Heaven. This was because they truly followed the right way. Therefore, a wise ruler examines the strength and weakness of enemy states from outside, and measures within the number and quality of his soldiers. He does not need to wait until two armies face each other before he can discern the key points of victory or defeat, survival or destruction; such insights are already clear in his mind. How could he be swayed by the words of ordinary people and make decisions blindly!

"I have secretly examined the maps of all under heaven. The combined territory of the feudal lords is five times that of Qin, and their total number of soldiers is ten times that of Qin's. If the six states unite as one force, facing west to confront Qin with bows drawn, then Qin will surely be defeated. Now they are being crushed by Qin and instead face west in submission, becoming Qin's vassals. To break others versus being broken by others; to make subjects of others versus becoming a subject oneself—how can these be spoken of in the same breath! Those who advocate horizontal alliances all wish to carve up the lands of the feudal lords and make a deal with Qin. If an agreement is made with Qin, then one can enjoy high towers and beautiful palaces, listen to the music of yu and se, savor the harmony of five flavors, have a grand hall in front and a long courtyard behind, be surrounded by charming beauties who smile gracefully—yet ultimately face disaster from Qin without sharing in its burdens. Therefore, advocates of horizontal alliances work day and night to use the power of Qin to intimidate the feudal lords, seeking to carve up their lands. I hope Your Majesty will carefully consider this matter.

"I have heard that a wise ruler cuts off doubts and removes slander, eliminates the traces of malicious rumors, and blocks the doors to factionalism. Therefore, plans for honoring the sovereign, expanding territory, and strengthening the military can be presented by ministers before him. I am now able to offer my loyal advice in your presence. Therefore, I secretly advise Your Majesty that there is no better course of action than to unite Han, Wei, Qi, Chu, Yan, and Zhao—the six states—into a vertical alliance. Together they can confront Qin in defiance. Let the generals and ministers of all under heaven meet by the banks of the Huai River, exchange hostages, slaughter white horses, and swear an oath. If Qin attacks Han or Wei, then Chu will cut off its rear; Qi will send elite troops to support them; Zhao will cross the Yellow and Zhang rivers; Yan will defend Yunzhong. If Qin attacks Qi, then Chu will cut its rear; Han will defend Chenggao; Wei will block the Wudao Pass; Zhao will cross the Yellow, Zhang rivers and Bo Guan Pass; Yan will send elite troops to assist. If Qin attacks Yan, then Zhao will defend Shangceng Mountain; Chu's army will station at Wuguan Pass; Qi will cross the Bohai Sea; Han and Wei will send elite troops to provide support. If Qin attacks Zhao, then Han's army will station at Yiyang; Chu's forces will be stationed at Wuguan Pass; Wei's troops will occupy the area beyond the Yellow River (He Wai); Qi will cross Bohai Sea; Yan will send its elite soldiers to assist. ### Qin Attacks Zhao

The State of Qin attacked the State of Zhao. Su Zi said to King Qin, "I have heard that a wise ruler treats his people by widely discussing and employing their skills; therefore, officials are not lacking in duties and labor is not exhausting." Regarding previous statements, he listens extensively and uses them at appropriate times; thus, affairs do not fail and evils do not become prominent. I wish Your Majesty would examine what I have presented and apply it for the benefit of the present time. I have heard that one who carries a precious treasure does not travel at night; one who undertakes great achievements does not treat the enemy lightly. Therefore, the virtuous bear heavy responsibilities and act with humility, while the wise achieve great accomplishments and speak in a manner that is agreeable. Thus, the people do not resent their leaders, and the world does not envy their achievements. I have heard: in a state a hundred times more powerful, the people do not wish to be behind; those whose achievements surpass their age, the sovereign does not need to act again; the people who have exhausted their strength, a benevolent ruler will not employ; seeking to obtain yet returning to tranquility is the rule of a sage sovereign; achieving great success while allowing the people to rest is the way of employing troops. Now, mobilizing troops repeatedly without ceasing, exhausting their strength yet not stopping, the anger of Zhao will surely be directed at its own territory. Is Zhao about to survive? However, it is a country with four wheels [i.e., a balanced and stable state], so even if we now capture Handan, it will not be in the long-term interest of our state. If one acquires extensive territory without cultivating it, and has a people who are weak yet not allowed to rest, while imposing severe punishments upon them, then even if they submit, they will not remain at peace. The saying goes: "When a state wins battles yet faces danger, it is because the burdens are unrelenting." "When achievements are great but authority is weak, territory will not be retained." Therefore, matters of excessive burden cannot be passed from father to son; unending demands cannot be imposed by a ruler upon his ministers. Therefore, he who understands the subtle as evident becomes strong; he who pays attention to allowing the people to rest will become a hegemon by employing such wisdom; and he who comprehends that lightness is actually weightiness shall become a king."

King Qin said, "If I halt my troops and allow the people to rest, then all under heaven will surely form an alliance against me, in order to resist Qin." Su Zi said, "I have reasons to know that the world cannot form an alliance to oppose Qin. I consider Tian Dan and Ru Er to be greatly overrated." How could it be that Tian Dan and Ru Er are so greatly overestimated? The rulers of all under heaven have also been mistaken! To consider the exhausted Qi, the weary Chu, the depleted Wei, and the unpredictable Zhao, and to wish to exhaust Qin and break Han—this is what I consider extreme foolishness. Qi under King Wei and King Xuan were rulers of the world's virtue; they had broad moral influence and extensive territory, a wealthy state with well-employed people, and generals who were brave with strong armies. King Xuan employed them, later enriching Han and intimidating Wei, to attack the south against Chu and strike westward at Qin. The Qi forces were trapped on the Yaxia Pass, and for ten years they seized territory. Though the people of Qin withdrew far away and did not submit, Qi became a place of emptiness and disorder. Why was it that the army of Qi was defeated, while Han and Wei barely survived? It is because they attacked Chu and struck Qin, and then suffered the consequences. Now, wealth is not a remnant of the virtue of King Wei or King Xuan; elite troops are not stored in the arsenals of rich Han and strong Wei; nor do generals possess the wisdom of Tian Dan or Sima. To gather together the shattered Qi, exhausted Chu, depleted Wei, and unpredictable Zhao in order to exhaust Qin and weaken Han—I consider this extremely mistaken. I believe that such an alliance cannot succeed at all. A guest raised a challenge, saying, "Now I have a concern about the world." The scholars of legalism all say, 'a white horse is not a horse.' Even though the white horse is indeed a horse, it is precisely because there exists such a thing as a white horse. This is my concern."

"In the past, the people of Qin sent troops to attack Huai, subdued its people, and three states joined in alliance with them. Zhao She and Bao Ning were generals; four men from Chu arose to join the alliance. When they approached Huai without rescuing it, the people of Qin departed and did not follow them. Is it that the three states hated Qin but loved Huai? Or did they forget their hatred of Huai and instead love Qin? To attack without rescuing, to leave without following—this is why the armies of the three states were trapped, and it was due to the abilities of Zhao She and Bao Ning. Therefore, they lost territory and suffered defeat at the hands of Qi. Tian Dan, leading the elite forces of Qi, marched freely within the central regions for fourteen years. The repeated attacks dared not deploy troops to strike Qin or weaken Han, yet they rushed about within their own borders—does this mean that even if an alliance were formed, it would still fail?" Thus King Qin withdrew his troops and did not act against the static states; the feudal lords rested, all under heaven was at peace, and for twenty-nine years no state attacked another.

### King Wuling Staying in Pingzhou

King Wuling was staying in Pingzhou, and Fei Yi sat in attendance. Therefore, a virtuous ruler remains tranquil while providing the people with moral instruction and convenient governance; thus, his fame spreads eastward, honoring the achievements of previous generations. As a minister, one should demonstrate the virtue of humility and courtesy in times of adversity, and contribute to benefiting the people and serving the sovereign when circumstances improve. These two principles constitute the proper relationship between ruler and minister. Now I wish to continue King Xiang's legacy, open up the lands of the Hu and Di peoples, yet no one in this generation understands it. If an enemy is weak, little effort yields great success; thus, the labor of the people can be minimized while enjoying achievements comparable to those of past generations. Those who accomplish feats for future generations must inevitably bear the burden of defying outdated customs; those with foresight and unique understanding will certainly face fear and suspicion from the common people. Now I intend to teach the people through Hu-style dress, horseback riding, and archery, yet surely the world will criticize me."

Fei Yi said, "I have heard that indecision in matters leads to no achievement, and hesitation in action brings no reputation. Now that Your Majesty has resolved to bear the burden of defying old customs, you should not hesitate due to the opinions of the world. Those who discuss the highest virtue do not conform to vulgar standards; those who accomplish great feats do not seek consensus from the masses. In ancient times, Shun danced among the Youmiao people, and Yu went bare-chested into a land of nakedness. They did not do so to indulge desires or for personal pleasure, but to demonstrate virtue and achieve great accomplishments. The foolish are confused by completed events, while the wise foresee things before they arise. Your Majesty should proceed with this plan." The king said, "I am not doubtful about adopting Hu-style dress; I fear that the world will laugh at me. The joy of a madman is pitied by the wise; the laughter of fools causes sorrow to the virtuous. If there are those in this world who follow me, then the achievements from adopting Hu-style dress may be immeasurable. Even if the whole world mocks me, I will surely possess the lands of the Hu people and Zhongshan."

The king then adopted the Hu-style dress. He sent Wangsun Xie to inform Gongzi Cheng, "I have adopted Hu-style dress and will wear it when I appear at court. I also wish for you, my uncle, to adopt it as well. In the family, one obeys one's parents; in the state, one follows the ruler—this is a common practice from ancient to modern times; a son does not defy his parent, and a minister does not oppose his sovereign—this is the universal principle of past kings. Now I have issued an order to change our dress, yet you, my uncle, refuse to comply; I fear the world will criticize this. The administration of a state follows fixed principles, and benefiting the people is fundamental; governing has its standards, and ensuring that commands are obeyed is most important. Therefore, the virtue of a wise ruler lies in guiding the lowly, while effective governance depends on earning the trust of the noble. Adopting Hu-style dress is not for indulging desires or pursuing personal pleasure. It has a purpose, and its achievements will have their limits. Only after the task is accomplished and success achieved can virtue become evident. Now I fear that you, my uncle, will defy the principles of governance and support Gongshi's arguments. Moreover, I have heard that actions beneficial to the state are free from wrongdoing, and those who act in accordance with noble relatives do not incur blame. Therefore, I wish for you, Uncle Gongshi, to uphold righteousness so that the achievement of adopting Hu-style dress can be realized. Wangsun Xie then paid a visit to his uncle and requested him to adopt the new attire."

Gongzi Cheng bowed twice and replied, "I have indeed heard of Your Majesty's adoption of Hu-style dress. I am not eloquent, and I have been ill in bed; thus, I was unable to come forward earlier. Now that the king has given orders, I dare to devote my humble loyalty. I have heard that the Central Kingdom is where wisdom and intelligence reside, where resources of all kinds gather, where sages teach, where benevolence and righteousness are practiced, where poetry, books, rites, and music are employed, where talents and skills are tested, where people from distant lands come to observe and learn, and where the Huaiyi peoples follow virtuous conduct. Now Your Majesty abandons the West [i.e., traditional Chinese culture] and adopts the dress of distant lands, abandoning ancient teachings and altering time-honored ways. This goes against people's hearts, opposes scholars, and distances us from the Central Kingdom. I hope Your Majesty will carefully consider this."

The envoy reported back to the king. The king said, "I have indeed heard of my uncle's illness." He then went to Gongshi Cheng's residence and personally requested, "Clothing is meant for practical use; rites are established to facilitate affairs. He entered slowly, hurriedly approached, and upon reaching her, apologized himself, saying, "Your old servant has been ill in his feet and could not walk quickly; I have not seen you for a long time. I secretly excused myself, but was afraid that there might be something wrong with Your Majesty's noble body, so I wished to see you, Empress Dowager." The empress dowager said, "This old woman relies on a palanquin to move around." He asked, "Has your daily food and drink not declined?" She said, "I rely only on porridge." He said, "Your old servant recently has had no desire to eat, so I have forced myself to walk, three or four li each day; this has slightly improved my appetite and harmonized my body." The empress dowager said, "This old woman is unable [to do that]." The empress dowager's expression somewhat softened.

Zuo Shi Gong said, "Your old servant's humble son Shu Qi is the youngest and not talented." But as I am old and declining, I secretly love and cherish him. I wish to have him appointed to fill a position among the black-clothed attendants so that he may guard the royal officials; I dare to present this request before my death." The empress dowager: "I respectfully agree. How old is he?" He replied, "Fifteen years old." Though young, I wish to entrust him before my body is cast into the ditches and ravines." The empress dowager asked, "Do men also love and cherish their youngest sons?" He replied, "Even more than women." The empress dowager smiled and said, "Women are much more so." He replied, "Your old servant secretly believes that Your Majesty's love for Yan Hou is greater than your love for Chang'an Jun." She said, "You are wrong; I love Chang'an Jun much more." Zuo Shi Gong said, "Parents who love their children will plan for them with long-term consideration. When you sent Yan Hou away, you held her heels and wept for her, feeling sorrow at how far she was going; this was indeed pitiable. After she had left, it was not that you did not think of her; in your sacrifices, you always made offerings for her welfare. You would pray, "May she never be sent back." Is this not a long-term plan? Is it not because you have descendants who will succeed to the throne?" The empress dowager said, "Indeed." Zuo Shi Gong asked, "Now, going back three generations before that, up to the time when Zhao was established as a state, among the descendants of its rulers who were made marquises, are there any whose lineages still continue?" She said, "None." He asked, "Not only Zhao, but are there any among the feudal lords whose lineages still exist?" She said, "This old woman has not heard of such." "This is because for those whose fates were recent, the calamity reached themselves; for those further back, it reached their descendants. Is it that the descendants of sovereigns are necessarily not virtuous? It is because they hold high positions without merit, receive generous stipends without labor, and yet possess many important instruments of power. Now you have elevated Chang'an Jun to a high position, granted him fertile land, given him many important instruments of power, yet did not wait for him to achieve merit for the state. One day when your majesty passes away, how will Chang'an Jun be able to rely on himself in Zhao? Your old servant thought Your Majesty's plan for Chang'an Jun was short-sighted, hence I considered your love for him less than that for Yan Hou." The empress dowager said: "Agreed. Do as you wish with him." Thus, for Chang'an Jun, one hundred chariots were prepared and he was sent to Qi as a hostage; then the troops of Qi set out.

Zi Yi heard of this and said, "Even the children of a sovereign, who are blood relatives, cannot rely on high rank without merit or generous stipends without labor to maintain their precious status; how much less can common ministers do so?"


### Qin sends Wang Jian to attack Zhao

The state of Qin sent Wang Jian to attack Zhao, and Zhao sent Li Mu and Sima Shang to resist him. Li Mu repeatedly defeated and drove away the Qin army, killing the Qin general Huanyi. Wang Jian, resenting this, gave a great deal of gold to Gao Kai, a favorite minister of the Zhao king and others, to act as a spy, saying: "Li Mu and Sima Shang intend to collude with Qin against Zhao in order to receive generous fiefs from Qin." The Zhao king suspected them, and sent Zhao Cong and Yan Wei as replacements for the generals, ordering Li Mu to be executed and Sima Shang dismissed from office. Three months later, Wang Jian immediately launched an attack and heavily defeated Zhao, killing the Zhao army and capturing King Qian of Zhao and his general Yan Wei, thus annihilating the state of Zhao.
