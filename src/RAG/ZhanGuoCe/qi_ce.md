# Qi Ce


## Qi 1


### Prince Wei of Chu Defeats an Enemy at Xuzhou

Prince Wei of Chu defeated an enemy at Xuzhou and wanted to expel Yingzi from Qi. Yingzi was afraid, so Zhang Chou said to King Wei: "Your Majesty defeated the enemy at Xuzhou, but Panzi is not being used. Panzi has made contributions to the state, and the people support him." Yingzi is not virtuous, yet Shen Ge is being used. Shen Ge is a minister who neither the high officials nor the common people support, so Your Majesty defeated him." Now if Yingzi is driven out, Panzi will certainly be used. He will reorganize his soldiers and confront Your Majesty in battle; this would surely be inconvenient for you." King Wei of Chu therefore did not drive him out.


### Qi's general Feng Tianying at Xue

Qi's general Feng Tianying was stationed at Xue. King Chu heard of this and became very angry, intending to attack Qi. Qi Wang had the intention to withdraw. Gongsun Han said, "Whether the sealing of Tianying is successful or not does not lie with Qi, but will also depend on Chu. "Fan persuaded King Chu to desire that the enfeoffment of Gong would be even more so than Qi." Yingzi said, "I wish to entrust it to you." Gongsun Han told King Chu, "Lu and Song serve Chu while Qi does not, because Qi is powerful whereas Lu and Song are small. Why would Your Majesty benefit only from the smallness of Lu and Song, yet not resent Qi's power?" "Qi ceding territory to enfeoff Tianying is precisely how it weakens itself. "I wish Your Majesty would not stop [the action]." King Chu said, "Well said." Therefore, he did not stop.


### Prince Jingguo Was Going to Build a City Wall around Xue

Jingguo jun was going to build a city wall around Xue, and many of his guests advised him against it. Prince Jingguo told the attendant: "Do not admit any guest." A man from Qi requested and said, "I will make a request in only three words!" "If I add one more word, please boil me alive." Prince Jingguo therefore received him. The guest hurried forward and said, "A great fish in the sea." Then he turned around and ran away. Prince Jingguo said, "There is a guest here." The guest said, "I, a humble servant, dare not take death as a joke." Prince Jingguo said, "No, please say it again." He replied, "Have you not heard of the great fish?" The net cannot catch it, and the hook cannot pull it. But when it is tossed about and loses its water, then even ants and centipedes can triumph over it. "Now Qi is also your water." "As long as you have the support of Qi, why do you need Xue?" "Even if you were to build a wall around Xue so high it reaches the sky, it would still be of no use." Prince Jingguo said, "Well said." He then stopped the construction of the wall around Xue.


### Jingguo Jun Advises King Qi

Jingguo Jun said to King Qi: "The reports from the five officials must be heard daily and reviewed frequently." King replied, "I have heard five explanations and am already tired of them." Now with Jingguo Jun.


### Jingguo Jun and His Friendship with Qi Maobian

Jingguo Jun was good friends with Qi Maobian. Qi Maobian had many flaws in his character, and his disciples did not like him. Shi Wei cited evidence to Jingguo Jun, but Jingguo Jun would not listen; therefore, Shi Wei resigned and left. Mengchang Jun also secretly advised him, but Jingguo jun became very angry and said, "Destroy your kind and ruin my family. If there is anything that can satisfy Qi Maobian, I will not hesitate to do it for him." Therefore, he gave him lodging in the upper guesthouse and ordered his eldest son to drive, providing meals morning and evening. Several years later, King Weiwang died, and King Xuanwang ascended the throne. Jingguo Jun's relations were greatly strained with King Xuanwang, so he bid farewell and went to Xue, where he stayed together with Qi Maobian. Not long after, Qi Maobian bade farewell and departed, requesting an audience with King Xuanwang. Jingguo Jun said, "The king dislikes Ying very much; if you go, you will surely be killed." Qi Maobian said, "I do not seek to live in the first place. I must go." Jingguo Jun could not stop him. Qi Maobian went to the state of Qi. When King Xuanwuang heard about this, he concealed his anger in preparation for receiving him. Qi Maobian met with King Xuanwang; the king said, "You are the man Jingguo Jun listens to and loves!" Qi Maobian replied, "There is affection, but no obedience. When the king was still a crown prince, I told Jingguo Jun: 'The Crown Prince's appearance is unkind; he looks past his eyebrows like a pig and behaves in such a way that one can trust him to rebel. It would be better to depose the Crown Prince and instead install Weiji's infant son, Jiaoshi." Jingguo Jun wept and said: "No, I cannot do it. I am not willing to bear it." If you had listened to me and done as I advised, you would certainly have avoided the troubles of today. This is one instance. When he arrived at Xue, Zhaoyang requested to exchange several times the amount of land for Xue; Qi Maobian again said, "You must listen to him." Jingguo Jun replied: "I received Xue from the previous king. Though it is now disliked by the present king, how could I betray what was entrusted to me by my predecessor!" Moreover, the temple of the previous king is located in Xue. How could I possibly hand over the temple of my predecessor to the state of Chu!" Again, he refused to listen to Qi Maobian. This is the second instance." King Xuanwang sighed deeply, his expression visibly moved. He said: "Jingguo Jun has been loyal to me in this way!" I was young and knew nothing of these matters. Will any guest be willing to bring Jingguo Jun back for me?" Qi Maobian replied: "I respectfully accept the order." Jingguo Jun wore King Weiwang's robes and donned the sword of dance; King Xuanwang personally met Jingguo Jun at the outskirts, gazing toward him and weeping. When Jingguo Jun arrived, he requested to be appointed as prime minister. Jingguo Jun declined the offer but eventually accepted it out of necessity. Seven days later, he claimed illness and strongly requested to resign. Jingguo Jun repeatedly refused but was not allowed to do so; three days later, the request was finally granted. At that time, Jingguo Jun could truly be said to have been capable of understanding people! Because he was able to understand others well, even when people criticized him, it did not discourage him. This is why Qi Maobian was willing to risk his life and seek out difficulties for the sake of others.


### Disaster of Handan

The disaster at Handan: Zhao sought aid from Qi. Tian Hou summoned his ministers and deliberated, saying, "Should we rescue Zhao or not?" Zou Zi said, "It would be better not to rescue them." Duangan Lun said, "If we do not come to their aid, then it will be disadvantageous for us." Tian Hou asked, "Why is that?" "If the Wei state annexes Handan, what benefit would that bring to Qi!" Tian Hou said, "Well spoken." He then mobilized his troops and declared, "The army will be stationed in the outskirts of Handan." Duangan Lun said, "What I consider beneficial or not is not this." łó Rescuing Handan and stationing the army in its outskirts means that Zhao will not be captured, yet Wei remains intact. Therefore, it would be better to attack Xiangling in the south to exhaust Wei; once Handan is captured and we take advantage of Wei's exhaustion, Zhao will fall and Wei will grow weak." Tian Hou said, "Well spoken." He then mobilized his forces and launched a southern attack on Xiangling. In the seventh month, Handan was captured. Qi then took advantage of Wei's weakened state and decisively defeated them at Guiling.


### Crisis of Nanliang

The Nanliang crisis arose, and the Han family requested aid from Qi. Dian Hou summoned his ministers to deliberate, saying, "Which is more advantageous: rescuing them early or rescuing them late?" Zhang Gai replied, "If we rescue them later, the Han will likely be defeated and fall into Wei's hands. It is better to rescue them early." Tian Chen Si said, "No, that is not possible." The armies of Han and Wei have not yet been exhausted, so if we come to their rescue, we will be substituting for Han and facing the army of Wei. In the end, we would still be at the mercy of Han. Moreover, Wei has the intention to destroy Han; when Han sees that it is about to perish, it will certainly appeal eastward to Qi for help. If we secretly cultivate ties with Han's allies and later take advantage of Wei's exhaustion, then our state can grow stronger, benefits can be gained, and our prestige can be enhanced." Dian Hou said, "Well said." He then secretly informed the Han envoy and sent him away. Han, believing it had exclusive support from the state of Qi, fought five battles but lost all five. Han then appealed eastward to Qi again, and Qi accordingly mobilized its forces to attack Wei, decisively defeating them at Maling. After Wei defeated Han and weakened it, the rulers of Han and Wei came northward to pay homage to Tian Ying, bowing before Lord Tian.


### Cheng Hou Zou Ji as Chancellor of Qi

Cheng Hou Zou Ji was prime minister of Qi, and Tian Ji was general; they did not get along well with each other. Gongsun Han said to Zou Ji, "Why don't you advise the king to launch a campaign against Wei?" If we win, it will be your strategy, and you can receive credit for the achievement; if we do not win in battle, Tian Ji will not advance further, and if he fights without dying, he can be punished for his disobedience." Zou Ji thought this was a good idea, so he advised the king and sent Tian Ji to attack Wei. Tian Ji won three battles in succession. Zou Ji informed Gongsun Han of this, so Gongsun Han sent someone with ten gold pieces to go and consult a fortune-teller at the market, saying, "I am a man of Tian Ji; I have won three battles in a row, my reputation and prestige are known throughout the world, now I want to undertake an important matter—will it be auspicious or not?" After the fortune-teller came out, someone was sent to arrest the person who had consulted him, and his statements were also verified before the king. Tian Ji then fled.


### Tian Ji as a General of Qi

Tian Ji was a general of Qi, who defeated Liang's Crown Prince Shen and captured Pang Juan. Sunzi said to Tian Ji, "General, may I suggest something important?" Tian Ji said, "How so?" Sunzi said, "General, you must not return to Qi without your troops. Let them grow weary first and weaken their defenses against the main force. The main force is like a route for overrunning, where soup ladles strike the cart wheels as they pass by each other. If you let them grow weary first and weaken their defense against the main force, then one can match ten, ten can match a hundred, and a hundred can match a thousand. Then you should position your army with Mount Taishan behind, the Ji River to the left, and Tian Tang to the right. Marching through Gaowan, send light chariots and swift cavalry to charge at Yongmen. If so, then the ruler of Qi can be corrected, and Cheng Hou can be driven away. Otherwise, General, you will not be able to enter Qi anymore." Tian Ji did not listen and indeed failed to return to Qi.


### Tian Ji Flees from Qi and Goes to Chu

Tian Ji fled from Qi and went to Chu, and Zou Ji took his place as prime minister. Qi was afraid that Tian Ji might use the power of Chu to regain influence in Qi, so Du He said: "I will request to be sent to keep him in Chu." He told the King of Chu, "Zou Ji's reason for not being friendly toward Chu is that he fears Tian Ji will use the power of Chu to recover influence in Qi. The king would be better off granting Tian Ji a fief in the southern part of the Yangtze River, to show that Tian Ji will not return to Qi. In this way, Zou Ji will treat Chu with great respect from Qi. Tian Ji is a fugitive, yet he receives a fief; he will surely be grateful to the king. If he returns to Qi, he will certainly serve Chu on behalf of Qi. This is the way to use both Tian Ji and Zou Ji." Chu indeed granted him a fief in the southern region of the Yangtze.


### Zou Ji Serves King Xuan

Zou Ji served King Xuanwang, and many officials were employed under him, which made King Xuanwang unhappy. Yan Shou was noble but had few subordinates; the king was pleased with this. Zou Ji said to King Xuan: "I have heard that having one filial son is not as good as having five filial sons. Now, how many people has Shou recommended?" King Xuan therefore considered Yan Shou to be obstructive and closed-minded.


### Zou Ji was over eight chi tall

Zou Ji was more than eight chi tall and had a graceful and handsome appearance. In the morning, when he put on his clothes and cap and looked in the mirror, he said to his wife, "Who is more beautiful between me and Xugong of the northern part of the city?" His wife replied, "You are much more handsome; how could Xugong compare with you!" Xugong of the northern part of town is one of the most beautiful men in the State of Qi. Zou Ji did not believe this and asked his concubine again, saying, "Who is more handsome between me and Xugong?" The concubine replied, "How could Xugong compare to you!" The next day a guest came from outside. While sitting and chatting with him, Zou Ji asked the guest, "Between me and Xugong, who is more handsome?" The guest replied, "Xugong is not as beautiful as you!" The next day, Xugong came. Zou Ji looked at him carefully and thought he was not as handsome as himself; Looking in the mirror and examining himself, he found that he was far less handsome. At dusk, while lying down to think about it, he said, "My wife thinks I am beautiful because she favors me; "My concubine thinks I am beautiful out of fear for me; "the guest who thinks I am handsome wants something from me." Therefore, he went to court and saw King Wei, saying, "I truly know that I am not as handsome as Xugong. My wife favors me, my concubine fears me, and my guest wants something from me; all of them think I am more beautiful than Xugong." "Now the State of Qi has a territory of one thousand li and 120 walled cities; the palace ladies and attendants on the left and right all favor you, Your Majesty; "the ministers in court all fear you; "within the four borders, no one does not want something from you. "Therefore, it is evident that Your Majesty is greatly deceived!" The king said, "Well spoken." He then issued an order: "All ministers, officials and common people who can point out my faults to my face will receive the highest reward; "those who submit written advice to me will receive a medium reward; "and those who criticize and discuss my faults in the marketplace or court, so that I hear of them, will receive a minor reward." When this order was first issued, ministers came forward to offer advice and the palace gates were as busy as a market. Several months later, they occasionally offered advice from time to time. After a full year, even if someone wanted to speak out, there was nothing left to advise on. lords Yan, Zhao, Han, and Wei heard of this and all paid homage to Qi. This is what is called winning a battle within the court.


### Qin Borrows Passage through Han and Wei to Attack Qi

The State of Qin requested passage through Han and Wei to attack Qi, so King Wei of Qi sent Zhang Zi as general to oppose them. While negotiating a truce with the Qin forces, they set up camp together, and envoys frequently traveled back and forth between them. Zhang Zi changed his insignia to mix in with the Qin army. A scout reported that Zhang Zi had entered Qin as a spy, but King Wei did not respond. After a short while, the scout again reported that Zhang Zi had surrendered Qi's troops to Qin, but King Wei still did not respond. This happened three times. An official requested, "Reports of Zhang Zi's defeat come from different people but with the same words. "Why does Your Majesty not dispatch a general to attack them?" The king said, "It is clearly evident that he has not betrayed me. Why should I attack him!" After a short time, news came that Qi's army had won a great victory and the Qin forces suffered a major defeat. Thus, King of Qin paid homage to the western frontier minister and apologized to Qi. Those around him asked, "How did you know this?" He said, "Zhang Zi's mother Qi was wronged by her husband. Her husband killed her and buried her under the horse 栈." "Our envoy, General Zhang Zi, was encouraged by me and said: 'With your strength, if you return with the entire army intact, I will certainly give a proper burial to General's mother.'" Zhang Zi replied, "I am not unable to provide a new burial for my former concubine. My mother Qi wronged my father. My father died without teaching me. To receive a new burial for one's mother without having received the father's instruction is to deceive the deceased father. Therefore, I dare not do so." "As a son who does not deceive his deceased father, how could he be a minister who deceives his living ruler?"


### Chu Will Launch an Attack on Qi

The State of Chu was about to attack Qi, and Lu showed affection toward it, which troubled King Qi. Zhang Gai said, "I request permission to make Lu remain neutral." He then went on behalf of Qi to meet the ruler of Lu. The ruler of Lu asked, "Is King Qi afraid?" He replied, "That is not something I know. I have come to console you." The ruler of Lu asked, "Why do you console me?" He replied, "Your plan is mistaken." "You do not side with the victor but with the vanquished—why is that?" The ruler of Lu said, "Do you think which of Qi and Chu will prevail?" He replied, "Even ghosts do not know." "If that is so, then why are you consoling me?" He said, "Qi and Chu are evenly matched; it makes no difference whether or not Lu is involved." lington You would certainly be better off if you could command the people and unite the descendants of the two states! Chu achieved a great victory over Qi, its elite soldiers and chosen troops were certainly killed, but its remaining forces would still be sufficient to contend with the world; If Qi had won, its elite soldiers and selected troops would have also perished. Yet if you were to lead the people of Lu and join forces with the survivors after a victory, your virtue would be great indeed, and the kindness shown to you would also be very significant." The ruler of Lu found this reasoning convincing and personally withdrew his troops.


### Qin Attacks Wei

Qin attacked Wei, and Chen Zhen united the Three Jin states and went east to tell King Qi: "In ancient times, when kings waged war, it was in order to correct the world and establish achievements and fame for future generations. Now, the mutual enmity among Qi, Chu, Yan, Zhao, Han, and Liang is so intense that it is insufficient to establish achievements or fame; rather, it merely strengthens Qin while weakening themselves. This is not a wise strategy for the states east of the mountains (Shandong). The one capable of endangering Shandong is the powerful state of Qin. Not worrying about the powerful Qin, but instead exhausting and weakening each other until both sides submit their states to Qin—this is why I consider it a danger for Shandong. The world has been carved up by Qin, yet Qin never had to exert any effort; The world has been boiled for Qin like a stew, yet Qin never provided even a single piece of firewood. How wise is Qin and how foolish are the Shandong states! I hope Your Majesty will take this into careful consideration. In ancient times, the Five Emperors, Three Kings, and Five Hegemons waged wars to punish those who violated moral conduct. Nowadays, Qin's war against the world is different; it must reverse everything, and rulers will inevitably face death or humiliation while their people will be captured and enslaved. Now, the people of Han and Liang have never dried their eyes from suffering, yet the people of Qi alone do not—this is not because Qi is closer to or more favored by Qin while Han and Liang are distant; it is simply that Qi is farther away from Qin whereas Han and Liang are nearer. Now, Qi will soon be close [to the same fate]! Now Qin intends to attack Liang's Jiang and Anyi. If Qin captures Jiang and Anyi, it can then descend eastward along the Yellow River, controlling both sides of the river and launching an eastern attack on Qi. With Qi surrounded by the sea to its south, it would find itself isolated from Chu, Han, and Liang in the south, as well as Yan and Zhao in the north. In such a situation, Qi will have no way to devise strategies for survival. I hope Your Majesty carefully considers this! Now the Three Jin states have already united, and they have once again formed a brotherly alliance. If they now dispatch elite troops to defend Liang's Jiang and Anyi, this would be a strategy for ten thousand generations. If Qi does not urgently send its elite forces to join the Three Jin states, it will surely face troubles in the future. When the Three Jin states unite, Qin will certainly dare not attack Liang; instead, it must turn south to attack Chu. If Chu and Qin become embroiled in conflict, the Three Jin states will be angry that Qi did not support them, and they must then launch an eastern attack on Qi. This is what I mean when I say Qi will certainly face great troubles; it would be better to urgently send troops to join the Three Jin states." King Qi respectfully accepted the advice, and indeed sent his troops to unite with the Three Jin states.


### Su Qin for Zhao Unites Vertical Alliances: Persuading King Xuan of Qi

Su Qin, acting for Zhao to form an alliance of vertical unity, advised King Xuan of Qi, saying: "To the south of Qi lies Taishan, to the east Langya, to the west Qinghe, and to the north Bohai. This is what is called a country surrounded on four sides by natural barriers." Qi's territory spans two thousand li, with hundreds of thousands of armored soldiers and grain stored in great quantities like hills and mountains. The chariots of Qi are excellent, and its five types of troops move as swiftly as a spearhead or an arrow, fight with the force of thunder and lightning, and disband like wind and rain. Even if there is military service, they have never crossed Mount Tai to the south, crossed Qinghe to the west, or traversed Bohai to the north. Linzi has 70,000 households. I dare to estimate that each household has at least three men of military age; multiplying seven by three gives twenty-one. Therefore, without needing to mobilize from distant counties, the number of soldiers in Linzi alone would already be 210,000. The people of Linzi are very wealthy and prosperous; none among them is without the skills to play the yu (a type of flute), strum the se (zither), strike the zu (an ancient stringed instrument), or play the qin (another zither-like instrument); they all enjoy cockfighting, dog racing, playing Liubo (an ancient board game), and cuju (ancient Chinese football). The roads of Linzi are crowded with so many carriages that they bump into each other, and the people press shoulder to shoulder. Their robes form a curtain when joined together, their sleeves create a canopy when raised, and their sweat falls like rain. Every household is prosperous and wealthy, with its members proud and ambitious. Given King Your Majesty's virtue and the strength of Qi, no other state in the world can match it. Now you are kowtowing to Qin from the west; I secretly feel ashamed for King Your Majesty. Moreover, the reason why Han and Wei fear Qin is because they share a border with it. When armies meet in battle, within no more than ten days the outcome—victory or defeat, survival or destruction—is decided. If Han and Wei win against Qin, their armies will be half destroyed, and they will be unable to defend their four borders; if they lose the battle, destruction inevitably follows. Therefore, Han and Wei place great weight on avoiding war with Qin but lightly accept becoming its vassals. Now, if Qin attacks Qi, it is not the same. It must cross the territories of Han and Wei, reach Weiyang and Jindao, pass through the perilous terrain of Kangfu, where chariots cannot travel side by side and horses cannot move abreast; a hundred men defending the pass can prevent even a thousand from passing. Even if Qin wishes to advance deeply, it must look back in fear, worried that Han and Wei will attack its rear. Therefore, Qin hesitates with suspicion and false threats, leaping forward but daring not to advance further; it is already clear that Qin cannot harm Qi. To fail to thoroughly assess that Qin cannot do anything against us, yet still wish to submit to Qin in the west, is a grave miscalculation by your ministers. Now we can avoid the reputation of being Qin's vassal, yet still possess the strength of a powerful state. I sincerely hope Your Majesty will pause and reconsider this matter." King Qi said, "I am not wise. Now Your Lordship has instructed me according to King Zhao's teachings; I respectfully offer up the state and its people in obedience."


### Zhang Yi for Qin Advocates a Horizontal Alliance with King Qi

Zhang Yi, for Qin, advocated a horizontal alliance with King Qi, saying: "Among the powerful states of the world, none surpasses Qi. Among ministers and high officials whose fathers and elder brothers are numerous, wealthy, and happy, none surpasses Qi." Yet those who advise Your Majesty consider only temporary advantages without regard for the benefits of a thousand years. Those who advocate for alliances with Your Majesty will certainly say: "To the west, Qi has a powerful Zhao; to the south, there are Han and Wei. It is a country backed by the sea, possessing vast territory and numerous people, strong troops and brave soldiers. Even if there were a hundred Qins, they would not know what to do with us!" Your Majesty hears their arguments but does not examine the true reality. Those who advocate alliances form cliques and collude, none of whom do not regard an alliance as advantageous. I have heard that Qi and Lu fought three wars, in which Lu won all three. Yet the state became endangered, followed by its eventual downfall. Although it had the reputation of victory, it actually experienced the reality of destruction. Why was this so? Qi was large while Lu was small. Now, Zhao's relationship with Qin is just like that of Qi to Lu. Qin and Zhao fought on the banks of the Hezhang River; in two battles, Zhao defeated Qin twice; they fought again beneath Fanwu, where Zhao also won both times. After four battles, Zhao lost hundreds of thousands of soldiers, and Handan was barely preserved. Although it gained the reputation of defeating Qin, its state had already been destroyed! Why was this so? Because Qin was strong and Zhao was weak. Now, Qin and Chu have married their sons and daughters to each other, becoming like brothers in a relationship of states; Han offered Yiyang, Wei ceded the area outside the Yellow River, Zhao paid homage at Mianchi and ceded Hejian to serve Qin. lineno If Your Majesty does not serve Qin, then Qin will drive Han and Wei to attack the southern territories of Qi. Zhao will cross the Yellow River and pass through the frontier passes, pointing toward Guan, where Linzi and Jimo will no longer belong to Your Majesty. Once the state is attacked in a single day, even if you wish to serve Qin afterward, it will be too late. Therefore, I hope Your Majesty carefully considers this." King Qi said: "Qi is a remote and obscure state, secluded by the East Sea; I have never heard of long-term benefits for the state. Now that you, my honored guest, have kindly instructed me, please allow me to serve Qin with our state and its people." He offered three hundred li of land rich in fish and salt to Qin.

### Han and Qi as Allied States

Han and Qi were allied states. Zhang Yi led Qin and Wei to attack Han. King Qi said, "Han is my allied state. Qin attacks it; I will rescue it." Tian Chensi said, "The king's plan is mistaken. It would be better to listen to them. Zikui gave his state to Zi Zhi; the common people did not support him, and the feudal lords did not ally with him. "Qin attacks Han; Chu and Zhao will certainly come to its rescue, which means all under heaven is giving Yan to me." The king said, "Good." He then accepted the Han envoy and sent him off. Han, believing it had secured an alliance with Qi, proceeded to fight against Qin. Chu and Zhao indeed quickly mobilized troops to rescue Han, so Qi took the opportunity to launch an attack on Yan, capturing the entire state of Yan in thirty days.


### Zhang Yi Serves King Hui of Qin

Zhang Yi served King Hui of Qin. After King Hui died, King Wu took the throne. The king's attendants resented Zhang Yi and said, "Yi was disloyal to the former king." Before they had finished speaking, envoys from Qi arrived again. Zhang Yi heard about this and said to King Wu, "I have a foolish plan; I wish to present it to Your Majesty." The king asked, "What is it?" He said, "For the sake of the state, there will be a great upheaval in the east; only then can Your Majesty obtain more territory." "Now King Qi intensely hates Zhang Yi. Wherever Yi is, he will surely raise an army to attack him." "Therefore, I wish to request that this unworthy body of mine be sent to Liang. Qi will surely raise an army and attack it." "The armies of Qi and Liang will be engaged under the city walls, unable to withdraw. Your Majesty may then take advantage of this opportunity to attack Han, enter the Three Rivers region, send troops through Hangu Pass without resistance, and approach Zhou. The ritual vessels will surely come out, allowing you to hold the Son of Heaven hostage, examine maps and records, and thus achieve a great imperial cause." The king said, "Good." He then prepared thirty chariots and sent Zhang Yi to Liang. As expected, Qi raised an army to attack Liang. King Liang was greatly alarmed. Zhang Yi said, "Your Majesty need not worry; please allow me to have the Qi army withdraw." He then sent his retainer Feng Xi to Chu, using this as a pretext to go to Qi. After completing the matters concerning Qi and Chu, Feng Xi said to King Qi, "Your Majesty intensely hates Zhang Yi; nevertheless, it was generous of you to entrust Yi to King Qin." King Qi said, "I intensely hate Yi. Wherever Yi is located, I will surely raise an army to attack it. Why would I entrust him?" Feng Xi replied, "This is precisely why Your Majesty entrusted Yi." "When Yi left Qin, he made an agreement with King Qin: 'For the king's benefit, there will be a major upheaval in the east, and only then can Your Majesty acquire more territory.'" "King Qi intensely hates Yi. Wherever Yi resides, he will surely raise an army against it." "Therefore, Yi requested to send his unworthy self to Liang, and Qi will certainly raise an army to attack Liang." "The armies of Liang and Qi will be locked in battle under the city walls and unable to withdraw. The king can then take advantage of this situation to attack Han, enter the three rivers region, send troops through the Hangu Pass without encountering resistance, approach Zhou, and the ritual vessels will certainly emerge. By holding the Son of Heaven hostage and examining maps and records, Your Majesty would achieve an imperial cause.'" King Qin believed this to be true, and provided thirty chariots to send Zhang Yi to Liang. And indeed, Qi attacked it. This means Your Majesty has exhausted your own strength to attack an allied state, expanded the number of neighboring enemies against yourself, and given Zhang Yi credibility with King Qin. "This is what I mean by entrusting Zhang Yi." The king said, "Good." And so he stopped.


### Xi Shou Fought for Liang Against Qi at Chengkuang But Was Not Victorious

Xi Shou fought for Liang against Qi at Chengkuang but was not victorious. Zhang Yi said to King Liang, "You did not follow my advice and endangered the state." King Liang therefore made Zhang Yi his prime minister. Zhang Yi formed a lateral alliance between Qin, Liang, and Qi to maintain close relations. Xi Shou wanted to defeat Zhang Yi and said to Duke Wei, "Yan has no grudge against Zhang Yi; it is merely that our approaches for the state differ. You must certainly support Yan." Duke Wei informed Zhang Yi, who agreed. As a result, they sat together in front of Duke Wei. Xi Shou knelt and crawled forward to offer Zhang Yi a wish for longevity lasting a thousand autumns. The next day, Master Zhang departed, and Xi Shou escorted him all the way to the border of Qi. King Qi heard about this and was angry with Zhang Yi, saying, "Yan is my enemy, yet Zhang Yi traveled with him. This must mean that he has conspired with Yan to sell out our state." Recently, Zhongshan mobilized all its forces to meet Yan and Zhao, fighting a southern battle at Changzi and defeating the State of Zhao; it fought in the north at Zhongshan, defeated the Yan army, and killed its general. Zhongshan was a small state with only a thousand chariots, yet it opposed two great states each with ten thousand chariots. After two battles, both victories were achieved in the north—this is an excellent example of military strategy. Yet its state was soon destroyed and its ruler and ministers fled to Qi; why did this happen? Because it did not restrain itself from the dangers of war and attack. From this we can see that the failure of waging war and attacks is evident in past events.

"Those in this age who are considered skilled at using military force—those who fight continuously and always win, whose defenses cannot be breached, and whom the world praises as excellent—if one state alone gains such power and preserves it, that is not beneficial to other states. I have heard that in a great victory through war, many soldiers die and the military grows weaker; a defense that cannot be breached exhausts its people and leaves city walls vulnerable. When soldiers die abroad, the populace is devastated at home, and city walls are exposed along the frontier—this is not a joy for kingship. Now, the target of a goose (or aim) is not at fault to anyone; when one draws a bow or crossbow and shoots it, hitting it is good, missing causes shame. Regardless of age, rank, or status, all people unite in wanting to pierce it—why is that? Because they dislike the target appearing difficult for others to hit. Now, if one constantly wins through war and holds a fortress that cannot be captured, this is not only making it seem difficult for others but also harming them. Therefore, the world will inevitably become its enemy. A ruler who exhausts his soldiers and exposes his state to hostility from all under heaven is not one a wise sovereign would emulate; Relying on strong military power only to weaken it later means that careful ministers will not serve. A wise ruler with discerning ministers can achieve the submission of feudal lords without moving a single soldier, and through courteous words and generous gifts, tribute will arrive. Therefore, when a wise ruler engages in warfare, his troops do not leave the barracks yet the enemy state is defeated; siege towers are never deployed yet border cities surrender. The soldiers and people remain unaware, yet the king's great achievements arrive nonetheless. The actions of a wise ruler are characterized by using few resources, taking long time spans, yet yielding enduring benefits. Hence it is said: if military action is taken as a last resort, then the feudal lords can be made to serve and obey.

"What I have heard is that the way of waging war without a proper teacher—though one may have an army of a million, it would be as ineffective as those in the hall." Even if there are generals like Helü and Wu Qi, they can be captured within one's own home; a city a thousand zhang in height can be taken between the cups and platters of a banquet; a hundred chi long siege ram can be broken on one's own mat or couch. Therefore, while the music of bells, drums, yu flutes, and se zithers continues without interruption, territory can be expanded and ambitions fulfilled; the laughter of entertainers, acrobats, and midgets need not cease—feudal lords can be brought together on the same day. Therefore, a name that matches heaven and earth is not considered too lofty; benefits that control within the seas are not considered excessive. Therefore, those who excel in establishing a king's legacy cause the world to labor while they themselves remain at ease; they bring chaos to the world yet find their own peace. When feudal lords have no completed plans, then one's state has no lingering worries. How do we know this is so? When ease and governance are in my hands, while toil and chaos fall upon the world—that is the way of kingship. When sharp weapons come, we resist them; when dangers arise, we confront them. By ensuring the feudal lords cannot complete their plans, one's state will have no lingering worries. How do I know this to be true? In the past, King Wei controlled a territory of a thousand li, had thirty-six hundred thousand armed soldiers. Though strong and having captured Handan, he encircled Dingyang to the west; then led twelve feudal lords in paying homage to the Son of Heaven, plotting against Qin from the west. King Qin was terrified, unable to sleep on his mat or enjoy his food. He ordered within the borders that all walls be reinforced with battlements and war equipment prepared; defenses were fully readied, and commanders for elite soldiers appointed, in anticipation of Wei's forces. Wei Yang advised King Qin, "The House of Wei has achieved great success and its commands are heeded throughout the world. With twelve feudal lords paying homage to the Son of Heaven and many allies, its support must be numerous." Therefore, if we use only one Qin state to oppose mighty Wei, I fear it will not suffice. "Why does Your Majesty not send me to see King Wei? Then I shall certainly cause Wei to retreat northward." King Qin agreed. Wei Yang met with King Wei and said, "Your Majesty's achievements are great; your commands are followed throughout the world. Now, among the twelve feudal lords following Your Majesty, they are not Song and Wei, but Zou, Lu, Chen, and Cai. These have long been states that Your Majesty can whip and command; they are insufficient for ruling all under heaven." "Your Majesty would be better off first taking Yan to the north, then attacking Qi in the east; then Zhao will certainly follow. If we take Qin to the west and attack Chu in the south, then Han will surely follow." "If Your Majesty harbors the intent to attack Qi and Chu while pursuing the ambition of leading all under heaven, then your path to kingship will become evident." "Therefore, Your Majesty would be better off first establishing royal authority, and then planning against Qi and Chu." King Wei was pleased with Wei Yang's words, so he personally expanded the royal palace, adopted red-dyed ceremonial robes and pillars, raised nine streamers, and followed a seven-star standard. This was the position of the Son of Heaven himself, yet King Wei occupied it. At this time, Qi and Chu became enraged; feudal lords flocked to Qi, who then attacked Wei, killing its crown prince and annihilating an army of ten thousand. King Wei was greatly frightened; he walked barefoot, ordered his troops to remain in the state, and retreated eastward to Qi. Only then did all under heaven cease their attacks against him. At that time, King Qin sat idly by and accepted the territory beyond Xihe without showing any virtue toward King Wei. Therefore, it is said that when Wei Yang first advised King Qin on strategy, the plan was made without leaving one's seat, discussed at a banquet table; the scheme was decided in the hall, and yet Hui's general would already be captured by Qi. Before siege towers were even deployed, the territory beyond Xihe had already fallen into Qin's hands. This is what I mean by comparing it to the hall, capturing a general within one's home, seizing a city between cups of wine, and breaking an enemy's attack on one's mat."


## Qi 6

### Wangsun Jia at Fifteen Serves King Min

Wangsun Jia was fifteen years old and served King Min. The king fled, and the place where the king had gone was unknown. ### Yan Attacks Qi and Captures More Than Seventy Cities

Yan attacked Qi and captured more than seventy cities, but Ju and Jimo could not be taken. Tian Dan of Qi broke the Yan forces from Jiemo and killed Qi Jie. Initially, when the Yan general captured Lucheng, someone slandered him. The Yan general feared punishment and so remained in defense of Lucheng, daring not to return. Lu Lian then wrote a letter, tied it to an arrow, and shot it into the city, sending it to the Yan general: "I have heard that wise men do not defy opportunity while abandoning profit, brave warriors do not fear death while destroying their reputation, and loyal ministers do not put themselves first but place their ruler last. Now you act out of a moment's anger without considering that the Yan king will be left without ministers, which is not loyalty; you kill yourself and lose Lucheng while your authority remains untrusted in Qi, which is not bravery; your achievements are wasted and your name destroyed, leaving no legacy for future generations to praise—this is not wisdom. Therefore, the wise man does not dwell on past calculations, and the brave warrior does not fear death. Now, life and death, honor and disgrace, high status and low rank—these are matters of the moment. I hope you will carefully consider this and not act like the common crowd." Moreover, while Chu attacked Nanyang and Wei attacked Pinglu, Qi had no intention of advancing to the south. To hold out in Lucheng together for a year of hardship means I see that you will certainly be unable to succeed. Qi must decide its fate at Lucheng; you have no other options left. That Yan state is in great chaos, its ruler and ministers have made miscalculations, the upper and lower ranks are confused. Li Fu, with a force of one million men, suffered five defeats abroad; this powerful state was encircled by Zhao, lost territory, and its ruler became trapped, becoming an object of ridicule to all under heaven—have you heard of this? Now the Yan king is cold-hearted and stands alone, his ministers are not to be relied upon; the state is exhausted with many disasters, and the people's hearts have nowhere to turn. Now you are using the weary people of Lucheng to resist the full might of Qi's army, and after a year it remains unresolved—this is the defense of Mozi; eating human flesh and boiling bones, yet the soldiers have no thought of retreating—this is an army like that of Sun Bin or Wu Qi. This ability will surely be seen by all under heaven! Therefore, for your sake, it would be better to disband the army and rest the soldiers, preserve your chariots and armor, return and report to King Yan; King Yan will surely be pleased. The soldiers and people see you as they would a father or mother, and your associates will raise their arms in support and speak of you in the world—your achievements will surely be clear. Above, assist the lonely ruler to control the ministers; below, nurture the people and provide resources for envoys. Correcting the state and reforming customs throughout the world, you can establish your achievements and reputation. Perhaps you should abandon Yan and leave this world behind, traveling east to Qi? Please request a fief with fixed boundaries, wealth comparable to Tao and Wei, allowing your descendants to be called "solitary" or "alone," coexisting with Qi for generations—this is also a plan. Both options bring fame and substantial rewards; I hope you will carefully deliberate and choose one wisely. Moreover, I have heard that those who adhere to minor virtues cannot achieve great authority, and those who resent small humiliations cannot establish a glorious name. In the past, Guan Zhong shot Duke Huan of Qi in the hook of his belt—this was an act of usurpation; he abandoned Gongzi Jiu and did not die for him—this was cowardice; he was bound in stocks and shackles, a disgrace to his person. These three actions—by village standards he would not be accepted, by the world's rulers he would not be made a minister. Had Guan Zhong remained in poverty and obscurity, imprisoned without release, shamed and unseen, spending his life in hardship until death, he would have been nothing more than a man of ignoble conduct. Yet Guanzi, despite these three faults, held the government of Qi and unified the world under one rule. He convened the feudal lords nine times and became the leader among the Five Hegemons; his name was high above all others, shining even to neighboring states. Cao Mo served as a general for Duke Lu and suffered three defeats in three battles, losing a thousand li of territory. Had Cao Zi's feet never left the battlefield, had his plans disregarded future consequences, and if he had always chosen death over life, then he would have been no more than a defeated general captured by the enemy. Cao Zi, as a defeated general taken prisoner, was not brave; his achievements were wasted and his name destroyed, leaving no reputation for future generations to praise—not wise. Therefore, he abandoned the shame of three defeats, withdrew and planned with Duke Lu—Cao Zi considered this to be his fortune. Duke Huan of Qi ruled over all under heaven and received homage from the feudal lords. With just a single sword, Cao Zi confronted Duke Huan on the sacrificial altar, his expression unchanging and his words and tone not disrespectful. The losses from three battles were reversed in a single day; the world was shaken and astonished, his authority extended to Wu and Chu, and his name passed down through later generations. These two men, Guanzi and Caozi, were not incapable of observing minor virtues or dying for small humiliations; they considered that to die and be cut off from the world without establishing achievements or reputation would not be wisdom. Thus, their accomplishments rival those of the Three Kings, and their fame endures as long as heaven and earth endure. You should consider this carefully!" The Yan general said, "I respectfully accept your words!" He then disbanded his troops and withdrew to Doudu. Thus, relieving the siege of Qi's state and saving the people from death—this was Zhong Lian's persuasion.

Looking around and seeing no one nearby, there was a pearl-dropper beneath the rock; Xiangwang called out to him and asked, "Did you hear my words?" He replied, "I did." The king said: "I am not his equal." Diao Bo said: "Certainly, I have long known that Your Majesty is not his equal. As for the lower one, who is comparable to Duke Huan of Qi?" ### Assassination of King Minwang of Qi

When Qi Minwang was assassinated, his son Fa Zhang changed his name and surname, becoming a hired laborer in the family of Tai Shi in Ju. Tai Shi Jiao's daughter found Fa Zhang's appearance and demeanor extraordinary, believing him to be no ordinary person. She pitied him and often secretly provided him with clothes and food, eventually becoming intimate with him. People in Ju who had been officials of Qi gathered together to search for the son of King Minwang, intending to install him as ruler. Fa Zhang then revealed his identity in Ju. They jointly established Fa Zhang as King Xiangwang. After King Xiangwang was enthroned, he took Tai Shi's daughter as his queen, and they had a son named Jian. Tai Shi Jiao said, "A woman who marries without my consent has no strategy; she is not of my lineage and has disgraced our family." He refused to see her for the rest of his life. Queen Dowager Junwang was virtuous and did not let the fact that she had never been seen by her father affect the filial rites of a daughter. After King Xiangwang died, his son Jian was installed as king of Qi. Queen Dowager Junwang treated Qin with caution and maintained trust with the feudal lords, which is why King Jian was able to rule for more than forty years without facing military conflict. Qin Shi Huang once sent an envoy to present Queen Dowager Junwang with a set of linked jade rings, saying, "Qi is known for its wisdom; can you solve this puzzle?" Queen Dowager Junwang showed the rings to her ministers, but none of them knew how to solve it. Queen Dowager Junwang took out a mallet and smashed the rings, then apologized to the Qin envoy, saying, "I have carefully solved it." When Queen Dowager Junwang fell ill and was about to die, she admonished Jian, saying, "Among the ministers, those who can be relied upon are a certain person." Jian said, "Please write it down." Queen Dowager Junwang said, "Good." She took a brush and writing tablet to record the words. Queen Dowager Junwang said, "An old woman like me is already gone!" After Queen Dowager Junwang died, King Jian's wife, Hou Sheng, became the regent of Qi. She accepted much gold and jade from Qin spies and sent guests to Qin; they all used altered words to advise the king to pay homage to Qin, without maintaining preparations for war or attack.

### Qi Uses Naojun's Rebellion
