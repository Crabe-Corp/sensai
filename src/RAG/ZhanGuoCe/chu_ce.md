# Chu Ce


## Chu 1


### Qi and Chu Become Embroiled in Conflict

Qi and Chu became embroiled in conflict, and Song requested to remain neutral. Qi pressed hard against Song, and Song agreed to it. Zi Xiang, on behalf of Chu, said to King of Song: "Chu lost Song because of its leniency; it will now emulate Qi's aggressive approach." Qi gained Song through aggression, and later will always be aggressive. This means following Qi to attack Chu; it may not necessarily be beneficial. If Qi defeats Chu, its momentum will inevitably threaten Song; If it does not win, then it is using a weak Song to provoke a powerful Chu. And allowing two ten-thousand chariot states [Qi and Chu] to constantly pursue their desires through aggression will surely put the state in danger."


### Five States Alliance to Attack Qi

Five states made an alliance to attack Qi. Zhao Yang said to the King of Chu, "The five states, having defeated Qi and Qin, will certainly plan to attack Chu in the south." The king asked, "If so, then what should we do?" He replied, "The House of Han is a vassal state; it likes profit and dislikes hardship. It likes profit, so we can entice it with benefits; It dislikes hardship, so we can intimidate it. If we offer them generous bribes for profit, their hearts will certainly be enticed. If I deploy all my troops to confront them, they will surely fear me." They will fear our military strength and be enticed by our benefits; the alliance of the five states can certainly be defeated. "After breaking their agreement, we may not even have to give them territory," he said. The King of Chu said, "Good." He then ordered the Grand Official to go to Han and meet Gongzhong, saying, "The matter of Niulan and the disaster at Maling were events witnessed by His Majesty himself. "If Your Majesty does not wish to use the five states in a military campaign, I request to offer you five walled cities and to mobilize all of Chu's forces to assist Qi." After Qi turned against Zhao and Wei, and if Chu indeed did not give them land, then the alliance of the five states would be in trouble.


### King Jingxuan Asks His Ministers

King Jingxuan asked his ministers, "I have heard that the northern people fear Zhao Xixu; indeed, how is it really?" None of the ministers answered. Jiang Yi replied, "A tiger seeks out all kinds of beasts to eat and catches a fox. The fox said, "You dare not eat me. Heaven's Emperor has appointed me as the leader of all beasts; if you now eat me, it would be going against Heaven's Emperor's command. If you do not believe me, I will go ahead of you first; follow behind me and see whether the beasts dare to run away when they see me?"' The tiger believed this to be true, so he followed along with the fox. The beasts saw them and all fled. The tiger did not realize that the beasts were fleeing out of fear of himself, but thought they feared the fox instead. Now Your Majesty's territory spans five thousand li, with a hundred thousand armed soldiers exclusively under Zhao Xixu's command; thus the northern people's fear of Xi Xu is actually their fear of Your Majesty's armed forces, just as all beasts fear the tiger."


### Zhao Xixu and Peng Chengjun Debate Before the King

Zhao Xixu and Pengcheng Jun debated before the king, who summoned Jiang Yi and asked him about it. Jiang Yi said: "Both men's words are good; I dare not speak of what follows." "This is called considering the virtuous."


### Crisis at Handan

The Handan crisis: Zhao Xixu said to King of Chu, "Your Majesty would be better off not rescuing Zhao and instead strengthening Wei." When Wei becomes strong, its incursions into Zhao will certainly go deeper. Zhao would be unable to resist, and thus must firmly defend itself; this would result in harm to both sides." Jing She said, "That is not the case. Zhao Xixu does not know this." When Wei attacks Zhao, it fears that Chu will attack its rear. Now if we do not rescue Zhao, and Zhao appears to be on the verge of collapse, Wei will have no worries about Chu. This would mean that both Chu and Wei are exploiting Zhao, and the harm will certainly be great! How could this result in mutual disadvantage?" Moreover, if Wei sends troops to deeply encroach on Zhao, and Zhao sees the signs of its own destruction while realizing that Chu will not come to its aid, it is certain that Zhao will ally with Wei in order to plot against Chu. Therefore, Your Majesty would be better off sending out a small force as support for Zhao. Zhao relying on Chu's strength will certainly fight against Wei. Wei, angered by Zhao's resistance and realizing that the aid from Chu is not formidable, will certainly not abandon its attack on Zhao. Zhao and Wei exhausting each other, while Qi and Qin respond to Chu's initiative, then Wei can be defeated." Chu therefore dispatched Jing She to raise troops in order to rescue Zhao. Handan fell, and Chu captured the area between Sui and Hui.


### Jiang Yin Yu E Zhao Xixu Yu Chu Wang

Jiang Yin yu e Zhao Xixu yu Chu Wang, er li bu neng, gu wei Liang Shanyang jun qingsheng yu Chu. King of Chu said, "Agreed." Zhao Xixu said, "Shanyang Jun has no merit in the State of Chu; he should not be granted a fief." Jiang Yin thus gained Shanyang Jun and conspired with him to further harm Zhao Xixu.


### Wei Family Resents Zhao Xixu Toward King of Chu

The Wei family resented Zhao Xixu toward King of Chu, and King of Chu informed Lord Zhao. Lord Zhao said: "I have always followed orders in the morning and evening, but the Wei family has inserted themselves between my lord and his ministers. I am greatly afraid." "It is not that I fear the Wei family!" "To reveal the relationship between my lord and his ministers, and to gain trust from all under heaven, this is a person who has become very close to hardship." "If one does not find it difficult to act outwardly, how could they forget the inner motivation?" "There is no day on which I will avoid being punished." The king said: "I understand this. What are you afraid of, Master?"


### Jiang Yi Hates Zhao Xixu

Jiang Yi hated Zhao Xixu and said to King of Chu: "There is a person who considers his dog as having virtue and loves it. His dog once urinated into the well. A neighbor saw that the dog had urinated in the well and wanted to go in and tell him about it. The dog hated this, so it bit him while standing at the door. The neighbor was afraid of the dog and thus could not go in to tell him." During the Handan crisis, Chu advanced its troops toward Daliang and captured it. Zhao Xixu took the treasures of Wei, which were known to the ruler of Wei, so Zhao Xixu often hated my being seen by the king."


### Jiang Yi Wishes to Harm Zhao Xixu in Chu

Jiang Yi wanted to harm Zhao Xixu in Chu and said to King of Chu, "When those below form cliques, the ruler above is endangered." "When those below are divided and quarrel among themselves, the ruler above remains secure." Do you also know this, Your Majesty? I hope His Majesty will not forget. Moreover, there are people who like to publicize others' virtues; what would Your Majesty think of such a person?" The king said, "This is a man of virtue. One should be close to him." Jiang Yi asked, "What about someone who likes to publicize others' vices? What would Your Majesty think of that person?" The king said, "This is a petty person. One should keep away from him." Jiang Yi said, "Then if there are cases of a son killing his father or a minister assassinating his ruler, and Your Majesty remains forever unaware, why is that? "It is because Your Majesty likes to hear about others' virtues but dislikes hearing about their vices." The king said, "Well spoken. I wish to hear both the good and the bad."


### Jiang Yi Advises Anling Jun

Jiang Yi said to Anling Jun, "You have no land even as small as a chi, nor any blood relatives. Yet you hold an exalted position and receive generous emoluments. All the people of the state see you and without exception bow respectfully and submit themselves to your authority. Why is this?" He said, "It is merely because the king has made an excessive appointment." "No," he replied. "Otherwise, you would not have achieved this position." Jiang Yi said, "Those who make friends through wealth will see their friendships end when the wealth is gone; those who make friends through beauty will find love wane when the bloom fades. Therefore, a favored concubine does not sleep on an old mat, and a beloved minister does not avoid the xuan carriage. "Now you hold the power of the State of Chu, yet you have no means to deeply bind yourself to the king. I secretly consider this a danger for you." Anling Jun said, "Then what should be done?" "I hope you will certainly request to die with the king and offer your life as a sacrifice. If you do this, you are sure to remain highly esteemed in the State of Chu." He said, "I shall follow your instruction carefully." Three years passed without a word being spoken. Jiang Yi met him again and said, "What I advised you to do has not yet borne fruit up to now. If you have not put my strategy into practice, I shall no longer dare to appear before you again." Anling Jun said, "I would never forget your words, but an opportunity has not yet presented itself." At this time, King of Chu toured Yunmeng. He traveled with a procession of a thousand chariots drawn by four horses each; the banners and flags obscured the sun. The wild fires blazing in the fields rose like colorful clouds, while the roars of rhinoceroses and tigers echoed like thunder. Suddenly, a fierce rhinoceros crashed through the chariot wheels toward the king. The king personally drew his bow and shot it; with one arrow, the beast fell dead. The king pulled out a flagpole to press down the rhinoceros's head and looked up to the sky, laughing and saying, "How joyful this outing is today! Who will share in such joy after I am gone for ten thousand years or a thousand autumns?" Anling Jun wept with tears streaming down his face and said, "When I enter the palace, I sleep on a woven mat; when I go out, I accompany you in your chariot. After Your Majesty has passed away for ten thousand years or a thousand generations, I wish to offer my life and test it in the netherworld among worms and ants. How could I possibly enjoy such pleasures again?" The king was greatly pleased, so he enfeoffed Tan as Anling Jun. When the gentlemen heard of this, they said, "Jiang Yi can truly be called a man skilled in planning, and Anling Jun can truly be considered one who knows the right time to act."


### Jiang Yi as an Envoy of Wei to Chu

Jiang Yi was an envoy of Wei to Chu. He said to King of Chu, "When I entered your territory, I heard that the custom in Chu is not to conceal others' virtues and not to speak ill of others. Is this truly the case?" The king said, "It is indeed true." Jiang Yi said, "Then was the rebellion of Bai Gong not able to proceed?" "If it is truly like this, then our crimes are forgiven." King of Chu said, "Why do you say that?" Jiang Yi said, "Zhou Hou served as prime minister of Chu. He was very powerful and made all the decisions, yet those around him all said 'there is no one else,' speaking with one voice."


### A Man from Ying with a Case Unresolved for Three Years

A man from Ying had a case that remained unresolved for three years in court, so the magistrate requested his house to determine his guilt by divination. The guest therefore told Zhao Xixu: "I wish to visit the residence of a certain family from Ying." Zhao Xixu said, "The certain family from Ying is not guilty, so their house cannot be taken." The guest took his leave. Zhao Xixu soon regretted it, and therefore said to the guest: "Xixu has been entrusted with official duties by you; why did you then treat Xixu as an old man?" The guest replied, "It was not because of age." He said, "When you said that I could not obtain it, your expression showed a sense of certainty. If it were not for old age, how else could this be?"


### Cheng Hun Chu Zhou

Cheng Hun chu Zhou, san ren ou xing, nan you yu Chu, zhi yu Xincheng. Chong Hun went out from Zhou, three people happened to be traveling, they traveled south to Chu, and arrived at Xincheng. Cheng Hun said to his magistrates: "Zheng and Wei are soft states of Chu; and Qin is a strong enemy of Chu. Zheng and Wei are weak, yet Chu responds to them with Shangliang; Yiyang is large, yet Chu surrounds it with the weak Xincheng. Pufan and Pingyang are a hundred li apart; the Qin people attacked them overnight, and Anyi did not know; Xincheng and Shangliang are five hundred li apart; the Qin people attack them overnight, yet Shangliang also does not know. Now, what border towns rely on is not Jiangnan Si shang. Therefore, why does King Chu not make Xincheng the main commandery? The border towns would greatly benefit from it." The Marquis of Xincheng was very pleased, so he prepared four-horse carriages and five hundred jin of gold for Chu. Cheng Hun received them, and thus established southern relations with Chu; King Chu indeed made Xincheng the main commandery.


### Han Gongshu Possesses Qi and Wei

Han Gongshu had Qi and Wei, while the crown prince had Chu and Qin to contend for the state. Zheng Shen served as a envoy of Chu to Han, falsely offering Chengxin and Yangren to the crown prince. King Chu was angry and intended to punish him for it. He replied, "I falsely gave them away, but I did so for the sake of the state. I obtained Chengxin and Yangren for the crown prince to contend with Gongshu over the state and succeeded in doing so. Qi and Wei will certainly attack Han. When the House of Han is in dire straits, it will surely place its fate at the mercy of Chu; how dare they then seek Chengxin and Yangren? "The crown prince may not prevail, but he will not perish. Now he is about to arrive with his fortune reversed, how dare I speak of territory?" King Chu said, "Well said." Thus he was not punished.


### Chu Duhe advises King Chu to attack Zhao

Chu Duhe advised King Chu to attack Zhao. The king was about to grant him the rank of Wudafu, and ordered him to proceed privately. Chen Zhen said to King Chu: "Duhe will not be able to obtain Zhao, and the rank of Wudafu cannot be reclaimed; it would be a reward without merit." If he obtains Zhao but the king does not bestow additional rewards, this will show no virtue. The king would be better off sending him with ten chariots; if the matter succeeds, grant him the rank of Wudadafu." The king said, "Good," and so sent him with ten chariots. Duhe was angry and did not proceed. Chen Zhen said to the king: "He will not be able to obtain Zhao."


### King of Chu Asks Fan Huan

The King of Chu asked Fan Huan, "I wish to appoint a prime minister in Qin; who is suitable?" Fan Huan replied, "I am not capable enough to know that." The king said, "Would Gan Mao be suitable as my prime minister?" Fan Huan replied, "No." The king said, "Why is that?" He replied, "Shi Ju was a gatekeeper of Shangcai. In major matters he did not serve his lord well, and in minor matters he did not manage his household well; yet he was known throughout the world for being strict and upright. Gan Mao served him faithfully." Therefore, although King Hui was wise and King Wu was perceptive, Zhang Yi, who loved to slander others, was served by Gan Mao. Gan Mao obtained ten official positions without committing any crime; indeed, Gan Mao is a truly virtuous man. However, he cannot be the prime minister of Qin. If Qin has a virtuous prime minister, it will not be beneficial to the State of Chu. Moreover, Your Majesty once used Hua in Yue and accepted Juzhang. During the difficult period of Mei, chaos arose in Yue; therefore, Chu observed to the south at Laihu and expanded its territory eastward along the Yangtze River. The reason Your Majesty's achievements have been able to reach this extent is because Yue was in chaos while Chu remained well governed. Now, Your Majesty has already used this strategy against Yue but forgotten it regarding Qin; I believe Your Majesty is greatly hasty in forgetting. If the king wishes to appoint a prime minister for Qin? Then Gongsun Hao would be suitable. Gongsun Hao is a relative of the King of Qin. When young, he shared clothes with him; when grown, they traveled together in the same carriage. He wears the king's robes while attending to affairs of state—truly, he is already a prime minister for King Qin. If you appoint him as your prime minister, it would be a great advantage for the State of Chu."


### Su Qin for Zhao Forms a Vertical Alliance: Persuading King Wei of Chu

Su Qin was serving Zhao by forming an alliance of vertical unity and persuaded King Wei of Chu, saying: "Chu is a powerful state in the world. Your Majesty is the virtuous king of the world. The western part of Chu's territory includes Qianzhong and Wu Jun, the eastern part has Xia Zhou and Haiyang, the southern part has Dongting and Cangwu, and the northern part has Fenxing Pass and Xunyang. The land spans five thousand li, with a million armored soldiers, a thousand chariots, ten thousand cavalry horses, and grain reserves sufficient for ten years; this is the foundation for becoming a hegemon or overlord. With Chu's strength and Your Majesty's virtue, no one in the world can rival you. Now, however, if you wish to bow westward and serve Qin, then all the feudal lords will inevitably face south and pay homage beneath Zhangtai. Qin's greatest threat to the world is none other than Chu; when Chu grows strong, Qin becomes weak, and when Chu is weak, Qin grows strong. Their positions cannot coexist. Therefore, for the sake of Your Majesty's state, there is no better plan than to form an alliance and isolate Qin. If Your Majesty does not join the alliance, Qin will certainly launch two armies: one army will emerge from Wuguan Pass; one army will descend into Qianzhong. If this happens, then Yan and Ying will be in danger. I have heard that one should manage affairs before they become chaotic, and take action before problems arise; if one waits until a disaster occurs to worry about it, then it will be too late. Therefore, I hope Your Majesty will make plans without delay. If Your Majesty sincerely listens to my advice, I will request that the states east of the mountains present their seasonal tributes in accordance with Your Majesty's enlightened decrees, entrusting their ancestral temples and state affairs to you, training soldiers and sharpening weapons, all at your command. If Your Majesty truly heeds my humble plan, then the finest music and beautiful women from Han, Wei, Qi, Yan, Zhao, and Wei will surely fill your harem. The fine horses and Tuota from Zhao and Dai will certainly fill the outer stables. Therefore, if the vertical alliance is formed, Chu shall become king; if the horizontal coalition succeeds, Qin shall become emperor. Now abandoning the cause of hegemony and overlordship while acquiring the reputation of serving others, I secretly consider this unwise for Your Majesty. Qin is a state like tigers and wolves; it harbors ambitions to swallow the entire world. Qin is the enemy of all under heaven, and those who advocate horizontal alliances all wish to carve up the feudal states' territories to serve Qin; this is what is called nurturing an enemy and serving a foe. To be a minister who carves up his ruler's territory, and to conduct foreign diplomacy with the fierce tiger-wolf Qin in order to encroach upon the world, yet when disaster from Qin finally comes, one pays no heed to its calamity. To externally rely on the might of powerful Qin and internally coerce one's ruler to demand territorial concessions is an act of great treachery and disloyalty, none more severe than this. Therefore, if the alliance is formed, the feudal lords will cede territory to serve Chu; if the horizontal coalition is formed, then Chu will cede its own land to serve Qin. These two strategies are vastly different, differing by a factor of ten thousand million. Between these two options, which one will Your Majesty choose? Therefore, my humble state of Zhao's king has sent me to offer this humble plan and present a clear agreement; it is at Your Majesty's command."

King Chu said: "My state lies westward in direct border with Qin, and Qin has the intention to conquer Ba and Shu and annex Hanzhong. Qin is a country of tigers and wolves; it cannot be trusted or relied upon. And Han and Wei are pressured by the threat of Qin; they cannot be trusted to make deep plans, for I fear that they might betray us and fall into the hands of Qin. Thus, before our plan is even revealed, the state will already be in peril. I have assessed my own strength; if I were to confront Qin with Chu alone, I see no prospect of victory. Internally, even consulting with the ministers is not enough to rely upon. I lie awake in bed without rest, eat food without relish; my heart is restless like a banner fluttering in the wind, with no clear direction or resolution. Now you wish to unify the world, bring peace to the feudal lords, and preserve endangered states; I will respectfully offer my ancestral temple and state in support of this alliance."


### Zhang Yi for Qin Breaks the Alliance and Promotes Horizontal Union

Zhang Yi, in order to break the alliance and promote the strategy of horizontal union for Qin, advised King of Chu, saying: "The territory of Qin occupies half of the world; its military strength is equal to that of four states. Surrounded by mountains and rivers, it is enclosed on all sides as a strong defense." There are more than one hundred thousand valiant warriors, a thousand chariots, ten thousand cavalry horses, and grain piled up like hills and mountains. With clear laws and regulations, the soldiers are willing to face hardships and die for their cause. The ruler is strict and wise; the generals are knowledgeable and martial. Even without deploying troops or weapons, they can roll up the perilous terrain of Changshan Mountain, break the backbone of the world, and those who resist submission will perish first. Moreover, forming an alliance is no different from driving a flock of sheep to attack a fierce tiger. The disparity between the tiger and the sheep is obvious. Now, Your Majesty does not ally with the fierce tiger but with a flock of sheep; I secretly think that Your Majesty's strategy is mistaken. Among all the powerful states in the world, it is either Qin or Chu; if not Qin, then Chu. The two states are evenly matched and in fierce competition; their positions cannot coexist. If Your Majesty does not side with Qin, then Qin will deploy its troops and occupy Yiyang, cutting off the upper territories of Han. They will advance to Hedong below, capture Chenggao, and Han must inevitably submit to Qin as a vassal. Once Han submits, Wei will follow like the wind. If Qin attacks Chu from the west and Han and Wei attack it from the north, how can your state avoid danger? Moreover, forming an alliance means gathering weak states to attack the strongest. To attack a strong state with weakness, without assessing the enemy and engaging in reckless warfare, while the country is poor yet frequently raises armies—this is a path to peril and destruction. I have heard that if one's military strength is inferior, one should not challenge the enemy to battle; if one's grain supply is insufficient, one should not engage in prolonged warfare. Those who advocate for the alliance merely use eloquent and empty words, exaggerating the ruler's virtue and righteousness, speaking only of benefits while concealing dangers. When disaster suddenly strikes Chu, it will be too late to take action; therefore, I hope Your Majesty will carefully consider this matter. To the west of Qin lies Bashu; with fleets and accumulated grain, they depart from Wenshan, follow the Yangtze River downstream, reaching Ying for more than 3,000 li. Each barge carries fifty soldiers and provisions for three months; drifting downstream, they can travel more than 300 li in a single day; although the distance is great, it does not require the toil of horses' sweat, and within ten days they can reach Hangu Pass; Once Hangu Pass is alarmed, then from Jingling to the east, all cities will be fortified and defended. The regions of Qianzhong and Wu Jun will no longer belong to Your Majesty. If Qin mobilizes its troops through Wuguan Pass, attacks from the south, then the northern territories will be cut off. The attack of Qin's army on Chu will bring danger within three months. Yet, Chu relies on the aid from other states, which would arrive after half a year; such is their situation that they cannot possibly reach in time. To rely on the rescue of weak states and forget the calamity from powerful Qin—this is why I consider it a great concern for Your Majesty. Moreover, Your Majesty once fought five battles with the Wu people and won three times before they were annihilated; their troops have been completely exhausted. There are only partial defenders of Xincheng, and the common people suffer greatly. I have heard that attacking a great power is easily perilous, and when the people are exhausted, they will resent their rulers. To pursue an achievement that is inherently dangerous while opposing the will of powerful Qin—I secretly fear for Your Majesty's safety. Moreover, the reason why Qin has not deployed troops through Hangu Pass for fifteen years to attack the feudal states is because they have secret plans and ambitions to swallow the entire world. Chu once had a conflict with Qin, fighting in Hanzhong. The Chu forces were defeated, and more than seventy marquises and jade-holders died; thus, they lost Hanzhong. King of Chu was greatly enraged and raised an army to attack Qin. They fought in Lantian, but were again repelled. This is what is called a fierce struggle between two tigers. When Qin and Chu exhaust each other, Han and Wei can remain intact and strike from behind—there is no strategy more advantageous than this. Therefore, I hope Your Majesty carefully considers it. If Qin sends troops to attack Wei and Yangjin, it will surely open the gates of the world's ambition. If Your Majesty mobilizes all his forces to attack Song, within a few months Song can be captured. Once Song is taken and the army turns eastward, then the twelve feudal lords of Si Shang will all belong to Your Majesty. Among the people in the world who were trusted for forming alliances and maintaining firm friendships was Su Qin, who was enfeoffed as Lord Wu'an and served as prime minister of Yan. He secretly conspired with King of Yan to destroy Qi and divide its territory between them. He then pretended to commit a crime, fled from Yan into Qi, and the King of Qi accepted him and appointed him as prime minister. After two years this was discovered; King of Qi became furious and had Su Qin torn apart by chariots in the marketplace. Trying to manage the world and unify all feudal lords with a deceitful, treacherous person like Su Qin—how it is clearly impossible. Now, between Qin and Chu, their territories are adjacent; they have long been neighboring states. If Your Majesty sincerely listens to my advice, I will request that the Crown Prince of Qin be sent as a hostage to Chu, and the Crown Prince of Chu be sent in return to Qin. I shall also offer a daughter of Qin as Your Majesty's concubine for menial service, present ten thousand households' capital city as a fief for her use, so that our states may forever remain like brothers, never attacking each other throughout our lifetimes. I believe there is no better plan than this one. Therefore, the humble state of Qin, under its King, has sent me as an envoy to present a letter to Your Majesty beneath the shade of your chariot. Please decide on this matter."

King of Chu said: "Chu is a remote and humble state, situated upon the East Sea. I am young and inexperienced in long-term strategies for my country. Now, your esteemed guest has kindly instructed me on clear policies; I have heard them and will respectfully follow your advice for the sake of my country." He then sent a hundred carriages as envoys, presenting to King of Qin the rhinoceros from Jihai and the night-glowing jade.


### Zhang Yi as Prime Minister of Qin

Zhang Yi, serving as prime minister of Qin, said to Zhao Ju: "Does Chu have any other gains if it loses Yanyang, Ying, and Hanzhong?" He replied: "No." Zhang Yi said: "Does Chu have anyone else who can be compared to Zhao Ju and Chen Zhen?" He replied: "There is no one else comparable." Zhang Yi said: "Tell the king of Chu to expel Zhao Ju and Chen Zhen, and I will request the return of Yanyang, Ying, and Hanzhong." Zhao Ju returned to report this to King of Chu, who was pleased with it. Someone said to Zhao Ju: "How unwise King of Chu is in discerning those who contend for reputation!" Han sought the official Teng Ji as prime minister, but Zhou did not agree; Wei requested Qi Mu Hui to be its prime minister and Zhou also refused. Why is that?" "Zhou treats us as a county under its control." "Now Chu is a powerful state with ten thousand chariots; " "Your Majesty is the most virtuous ruler in all under heaven." "If Zhang Yi says to expel you and Chen Zhen, and the king listens to him, then Chu would be acting less wisely than Zhou, and Zhang Yi's influence would surpass even that of the kings of Han and Wei." "Moreover, what Zhang Yi does brings glory and reputation to Qin, while his desire for honor and wealth is directed toward Wei." "If he wants to launch an attack on Wei, he must certainly campaign southward against Chu." "Therefore, in waging war there is a method: externally severing alliances and internally driving out their strategists." "Chen Zhen is a native of Xia, well-versed in the affairs of the Three Jin states. Therefore, if he is expelled, Chu will have no strategists left." "Now you are able to command the people of Chu; therefore, if you too are driven out, then the people of Chu will be without a leader." "This is what is called an internal attack, yet His Majesty fails to perceive it." "Why don't you present your minister before the king now and request that he be sent as an envoy to Qi, ensuring that diplomatic ties remain unbroken?" "If relations with Qi are not severed, Zhang Yi will hear of it and his demand for the return of Yanyang, Jing, and Hanzhong must certainly be delayed." "This would mean that Lord Zhao Ju's words are not to be trusted, and the king will surely look down on him."


### King Wei Asks Moao Zihua

King Wei asked Moao Zihua, "Since the time of my late ancestor King Wenwang up to myself, has there been anyone who did not seek promotion through rank, nor was motivated by salary, but instead worried about the state?" Moao Zihua replied, "As for me, I am not qualified to know that." The king said, "If not from you, Master, where else could I hear about it?" Moao Zihua replied: "What is it that the king wishes to ask?" There are those who maintain integrity in their rank, remain poor themselves, and worry about the state; there are those who hold high ranks, enjoy generous salaries, yet still worry about the state; there are those who cut their throats and tear open their bellies, closing their eyes for one last time without looking back even after ten thousand years, not knowing what benefit they have brought, yet still worrying about the state; there are those who weary their bodies and trouble their minds, yet worry about the state; there are also those who do not seek promotion through rank nor are motivated by salary, yet still worry about the state." The king said: "Master, what exactly do you mean by these words?"

Moao Zihua replied: "In the past, Lingyi Ziwen wore dark silk clothes to court and a deer hide coat at home; he stood in court before dawn and returned for meals only after dusk; he did not plan for the evening, having no savings even for one month. Therefore, those who maintain integrity in rank, remain poor themselves yet worry about the state are none other than Lingyi Ziwen. In the past, Yegong Zigao was personally honored with commendation and wealth as a minister of state; he resolved the disaster of Bai Gong and stabilized affairs in the State of Chu; he restored the achievements of previous kings to cover beyond Fangcheng, where four borders were not violated and his name was never humiliated by other feudal lords. At that time, no one in the world dared to direct their troops southward. Yegong Zigao, who received six hundred zhen of farmland as his stipend, is the one who held a high rank and enjoyed generous salary yet still worried about the state. In the past, Wu fought against Chu at Baiju, where soldiers from both sides clashed between the two chariots. Moao Daxin held his charioteer's hand, looked back and sighed deeply, saying: "Alas, my son! The time for the fall of the State of Chu has come!" "I will go deep into Wu's army. If I can strike down one man, or seize another, it would be for the sake of Daxin; perhaps then our state may yet have a chance to survive?"' Therefore, those who cut their throats, tear open their bellies, close their eyes for the last time and never look back even after ten thousand years without knowing what benefit they bring yet still worry about the state—none other than Moao Daxin. She selected clothes, ornaments, and pleasant things according to what he liked and provided them; palaces, sleeping quarters, and furnishings were chosen according to his preferences. She loved her more than the king did. The king said, "What women use to serve their husbands is beauty; and jealousy is a natural emotion." "Now Lady Zheng Xiu knows that I like the new favorite, and she loves her more than I do. This is how filial sons serve their parents, and how loyal ministers serve their rulers." Zheng Xiu, knowing that the king thought she was not jealous, therefore said to the new favorite, "The king loves your beauty very much. "Although that may be," Zheng Xiu said to her, "I dislike your nose." "You must see the king; then you must cover your nose." The new favorite went to see the king and thus covered her nose. The king said to Zheng Xiu, "When the new favorite sees me, she covers her nose—why is that?" Zheng Xiu said, "I know." The king said, "Even if it is unpleasant, you must tell me." Zheng Xiu said, "It seems she dislikes the smell of Your Majesty's stench." The king said, "How bold!" He ordered her nose to be cut off and did not allow disobedience.


### Queen of Chu Dies

The queen of the State of Chu died, and no new queen had been appointed yet. He said to Zhao Yu, "Why didn't you request that a new queen be appointed?" Zhao Yu replied, "The king would not listen; this is to know hardship and have relations severed by the queen." "Therefore, do not buy five pairs of earrings, but have one pair made well and present it to the king. Tomorrow, observe which well-made earring is worn; then request that the woman wearing it be appointed queen."


### Zhuang Xin Advises King Xiang of Chu

Zhuang Xin said to King Xiang of Chu, "Your Majesty has Zou Hou on your left and Xiahou on your right. You are followed by Yancheng Jun and Shouling Jun in a palanquin procession. You indulge exclusively in licentiousness and extravagance without attending to state affairs; the capital Yingdu is surely in danger." King Xiang said, "Are you old and confused, Master?" "Will you take this as an ill omen for the state of Chu?" Zhuang Xin said, "I truly see it as inevitable; I do not dare to regard it as an ill omen for the country." "If Your Majesty continues to favor these four men without decline, the state of Chu will surely perish." "I shall go into exile in Zhao and remain there for a while to observe what happens." Zhuang Xin went to Zhao and stayed there for five months. As expected, the Qin forces captured Yancheng, Yingdu, Wu, Shangcai, and Chen territories; King Xiang was driven into exile in Chengyang. At that time, he sent someone to prepare the horse-drawn carriage and summoned Zhuang Xin from Zhao. Zhuang Xin said, "Yes." When Zhuang Xin arrived, King Xiang said, "I failed to follow your advice. Now the situation has come to this; what can we do about it?" Zhuang Xin replied, "I have heard a saying from the common people: 'To see the rabbit and then look for the dog is not too late; to lose sheep and mend the pen is not too late either.'" "I have heard that in ancient times, King Tang and King Wu brought prosperity with a hundred li of territory, while Jie and Zhou perished despite ruling over the whole world." "Now although the state of Chu is small, if we cut short its weaknesses and extend its strengths, it still spans thousands of li. Is this merely a hundred li?" "Does Your Majesty not see the dragonfly? It has six legs and four wings, flying between heaven and earth. It bends down to peck at mosquitoes and horseflies for food, and looks up to drink the sweet dew. It considers itself free from danger, with no conflicts with people. It does not know that a five-foot-tall child is just about to prepare lead glue and silk thread, fasten it four ren above the ground, and then drop down for ants to eat. "The dragonfly is but a small creature; the yellow sparrow does this as well." "It pecks at white grains below and perches on lush trees above, flapping its wings proudly, believing itself free from danger and in no conflict with people." "It does not know that the young noblemen, holding a crossbow in their left hand and a bullet in their right, are about to shoot it from ten ren above, using its kind as targets." "By day it roams among lush trees; by night it is hunted for sour and salty delicacies. In the blink of an eye, it falls into the hands of these noblemen." "The yellow sparrow is but a small creature; this also applies to the yellow crane." "It flies over rivers and seas, lingers in vast marshes, bends down to peck carp and eels, looks up to bite water plants, flaps its six wings to ride the clear wind, soaring high with a drifting flight. It believes itself free from danger, in no conflict with people." "It does not know that the archers are just preparing their bows and arrows, tightening their silk nooses, ready to shoot it from a hundred ren above." "With their sharp stones and bowstrings, they pull the fine threads of the noose, and in an instant, the yellow crane is brought down by the wind." "Therefore, it roams over rivers by day and becomes a delicacy in cauldrons by night." "The yellow crane is but a small example; the affairs of Lord Cai Shenghou are no different." "He traveled south to the high banks, north to Mount Wushan; drank from Ruxi Stream and ate fish from Xiang River. With a young concubine on his left and a favored female attendant on his right, he galloped through Gaizhi without concerning himself with state affairs." "He did not know that Zi Fa had just received orders from King Xuan, binding him with red silk threads to present him." "The affairs of Lord Cai Shenghhou are but a small example; Your Majesty's situation is no different." "With Zou Hou on the left and Xiahou on the right, followed by Yancheng Jun and Shaoling Jun, you eat the grain of fiefdoms, wear gold from Fangfu, and gallop through Yunmeng without concerning yourself with the state or the world." "You do not know that Rang Hou has just received orders from King Qin, who is now filling the interior of Mian and expelling you to its exterior." King Xiang heard this and his complexion changed; his body trembled with fear. Thus, he immediately gave him the jade seal and appointed him as Yangling Jun, granting him the territory north of the Huai River.


### Qi Ming Persuades Zhuohua to Attack Qin

Qi Ming said to Zhuohua, "Attack Qin," but Zhuohua did not listen. Qi Ming told Zhuohua, "My coming here is to seek an alliance for Chuli Ji." "Ming has persuaded the ministers of Chu to attack Qin, and they have all accepted Ming's arguments. Only you, Your Majesty, do not accept it. I now have an excuse with which to report back to Chuli Zi." Zhuohua therefore valued him highly.


### Someone Advises Huang Qi

Someone said to Huang Qi, "Everyone says that you are not on good terms with Fu Zhi." "Have you not heard of Lao Laizi's teaching to Confucius about serving the ruler?" He showed him how strong his teeth were, but by sixty they had all worn down completely. "Now that Fu Zhi is capable and you deliberately do not get along with him, this will exhaust both of you." The proverb says: "When you see your lord's carriage, step down; when you see a staff [being carried], rise up." "Now, the king favors Fu Zhi, yet you do not get along with him; this is not being a loyal minister," said he.


### Changsha Disaster

The Changsha disaster: The Prince of Chu, Heng, was held as a hostage in Qi. After the King of Chu died, Xue Gong returned the Crown Prince Heng to him and thus joined forces with troops from Han and Wei to attack the eastern states. The crown prince was afraid. Zhao Gai said, "It would be better to have Qu Shu make peace with Qi by offering the new eastern states in order to provoke Qin. Qin, fearing that Qi will defeat the eastern states and thus establish its authority throughout the world, will certainly come to our rescue." The crown prince said, "Good." He immediately ordered Qu Shu to make peace with Qi by ceding the eastern states. The King of Qin, upon hearing this, was alarmed and ordered Xin Rong to inform Chu: "Do not cede the eastern states to Qi. I will send troops with you."


### Someone Presented an Elixir of Immortality to King Jing

Someone presented an elixir of immortality to King Jing, and a court official took it into the palace. A mid-ranking archer asked, "Is it edible?" He said, "Yes." The man then seized the elixir and ate it. King Jing became angry and sent someone to kill the mid-ranking archer. The mid-ranking archer had someone plead with the king, saying, "I asked the court official, and he said it was edible, so I ate it. Therefore, I am innocent, and the guilt lies with the court official." Moreover, a guest presented an elixir of immortality; if I ate it and the king kills me for that, then it was actually a death potion. The king has killed an innocent subject and demonstrated to others how they can deceive him." King Jing then refrained from killing the man.


### A Guest Advises Chunshen Jun

A guest advised Chunshen Jun, saying: "Tang used Bo, King Wu of Zhou used Hao; both possessed the world with no more than a hundred li." Now Sun Zi is a virtuous man of the world. If you, my lord, grant him authority over an area of a hundred li, I secretly think it would be inconvenient for you, my lord. What do you think?" Chunshen Jun said: "Well spoken." Thus, he sent someone to apologize to Sun Zi. Sun Zi left for Zhao, and the state of Zhao appointed him as Shangqing (a high-ranking official). Another guest advised Chunshen Jun again, saying: "In the past, Yi Yin left Xia and entered Yin; the Yin king prevailed while the Xia perished. Guan Zhong left Lu and went to Qi; Lu became weak while Qi grew strong." The place where a virtuous person resides, its ruler has never failed to be respected, and the state has never failed to gain honor. Now Sun Zi is a sage of the world. Why does your lordship dismiss him?" Chunshen Jun replied again: "Well spoken." Thus, he sent someone to invite Sun Zi from Zhao. Sun Zi wrote a letter in reply saying: "A leper showing pity for the king—this is an expression of disrespect. Although so, it cannot be examined carefully enough." This is said on behalf of a ruler who was killed by coercion or regicide. When a ruler is young and conceited about his talents, without methods or strategies to detect treachery, then high-ranking ministers will dominate state affairs for their own benefit and carry out executions against the ruler. Therefore, they assassinate virtuous and capable individuals while installing weak and immature ones; they depose legitimate heirs in favor of those who are unjust. The Spring and Autumn Annals warns: "Prince Wei of Chu was on a diplomatic mission to Zheng. Before he had left the border, he heard that the king was ill, so he returned under the pretense of inquiring about his condition. He then strangled the king with a ribbon from his hat, killed him, and seized power for himself." The wife of Cui Zhu of Qi was beautiful, so Duke Zhuang had relations with her. Cui Zhu led his faction loyal to the ruler and attacked him. Duke Zhuang requested to divide the state with him, but Cui Zhu refused; he wanted to commit suicide in the ancestral temple, but Cui Zhu did not permit it. Duke Zhuang fled out and jumped over the outer wall, but was shot in his thigh. He was then killed, and his younger brother Duke Jing was installed as ruler." In more recent times: Li Dui in Zhao starved the king, Zhu Fu, at Shachu, and after a hundred days killed him; Nao Chi in Qi pulled out King Min's tendons, hung them on the beam of his ancestral temple, and he died after a night. A leper, though suffering from sores and swellings, when compared to previous generations, has not yet reached the point of strangling with a ribbon or shooting in the thigh; compared to more recent times, it does not reach the level of pulling out tendons and starving to death. A ruler who is killed by coercion or regicide suffers mental anxiety and physical hardship far greater than a leper. From this, it can be seen that calling a leper pitiable to the king is acceptable." He then composed an ode: "Precious treasures and Sui pearls are unknown, not worn as ornaments. Hui cloth and silk are indistinguishable, their differences unrecognized. A beauty like Shu Zishē in the 闾 (Lü) district remains unknown, with no matchmaker to know of her. Momu seeks her out and is greatly pleased by her. Taking the blind for sighted, the deaf for hearing, right as wrong, good fortune as misfortune. Alas, O Heaven above, why must things be so alike!" The poem says: "Heaven is most divine; do not bring upon yourself disease."


### World United in a Coalition

The world united in a coalition. Zhao sent Wei Jia to see the Lord Chunshen of Chu and asked, "Do you have a general?" He replied, "I do. I want to appoint Linwu Jun as my general." Wei Jia said, "When I was young, I liked archery. May I use an analogy from archery to explain? Is that acceptable?" Lord Chunshen replied, "Yes." Jia said, "On a previous occasion, Geng Lai was with King Wei at the top of Jing Tai. Looking up, he saw birds flying." Geng Lai said to King Wei, "I will draw my bow and shoot without firing an arrow, yet a bird will fall." King Wei said, "Then can archery be so effective?" Geng Lai replied, "Yes." After a while, a goose came from the east. Geng Lai shot without firing an arrow and brought it down. King Wei said, "Then can archery really be this effective?" Geng Lai replied, "This is because of its injury." King Wei asked, "How did you know this?" He replied, "It flew slowly and cried sorrowfully. lodash Flying slowly indicates the pain from an old wound; Its sorrowful cry shows it has been separated from its flock for a long time. Therefore, although the old wound has not healed completely, its frightened heart is still unsettled." "When it heard the sound of the bowstring, it pulled up and flew high, causing the old wound to rupture and fall." "Now Linwu Jun has previously suffered injuries at the hands of Qin. He cannot be appointed as a general to resist Qin."


### Han Ming Visits Chunshen Jun

Han Ming visited Chunshen Jun and waited for three months before being granted an audience. After their conversation ended, Chunshen Jun was greatly pleased with him. Han Ming wanted to speak again, but Chunshen Jun said, "I have already understood you, Master; please rest now." Han Ming hesitated and said, "I would like to ask Your Lordship a question but am afraid it might be too bold. If I do not examine whether Your Lordship is as sage-like as Yao, who else could be?" Chunshen Jun said, "You are mistaken, Master; how can I possibly compare to Yao?" Han Ming said, "Then in your estimation, with whom am I comparable, to Shun or not?" Chunshen Jun replied, "You are indeed like Shun." Han Ming said, "Not quite. I would like to explain fully for Your Lordship. Your virtue is indeed not as great as Yao's, and my ability does not measure up to Shun's. When a virtuous man like Shun served the sage ruler Yao, it took three years before they truly understood each other. Now Your Lordship understands me in a short time; this would mean that you are more sage than Yao and I am more virtuous than Shun." Chunshen Jun said, "Well spoken." He summoned the gate official and ordered that Han Shi Sheng be recorded in the guest register, to be received once every five days. Han Ming said, "Have you also heard of Ji?" Ji was an old horse who pulled a salt cart up the Taihang Mountains. Its hooves were split and knees broken, its tail was soaked and the flesh around it 溃烂 (ruined), saliva dripped to the ground, white sweat flowed freely, and on the middle slope it hesitated, dragging the yoke but unable to proceed upward. When Bole encountered this scene, he got off his carriage, clasped the horse and wept, then removed his hemp robe to cover it. At this, Ji lowered its head and snorted, then raised its head and neighed, the sound reaching up to heaven, like a melody from gold and stone. Why was that? Because it recognized Bole as one who truly understood it. Now I, unworthy as I am, have been trapped in the local administration, dwelling in a remote alley and mired in vulgar customs for a long time. Your Lordship alone has not thought to cleanse and promote me, allowing me to raise my voice in service to you, like Ji on Mount Liang?"


### King Kao Lie of Chu Had No Heir

King Kao Lie of Chu had no sons. Chunshen Jun was worried about this and sought out women who were suitable for bearing children, introducing many of them, but in the end there were still no sons. Li Yuan of Zhao took his younger sister and wanted to present her to King Chu, but upon learning that she was not suitable for bearing children, he feared she would again fail to gain favor. Li Yuan sought employment with Chunshen Jun as a retainer. Afterward, he paid an audience and returned home, thus missing the appointed time. When Li Yuan returned for an audience, Chunshen Jun asked about his situation. He replied, "The King of Qi sent envoys to seek my younger sister; I drank with the envoy, hence missing the deadline." Chunshen Jun asked, "Have they already made a formal proposal?" He replied, "Not yet." Chunshen Jun said, "Might I have the opportunity to see her?" He replied, "Yes." Thus, Li Yuan presented his younger sister, and she was favored by Chunshen Jun. Knowing that she had become pregnant, Li Yuan then conspired with her. Li Yuan's younger sister took the opportunity to advise Chunshen Jun, saying, "King Chu holds you in such high favor and esteem that even his brothers cannot compare. Now, for more than twenty years you have served as prime minister to King Chu, yet the king has no son. After he passes away, his brothers will be placed on the throne instead." Once King Chu is replaced by another ruler, each new king will naturally favor those he was close to before. How can you then continue to enjoy lasting favor and privilege?" Would this not be the case? You have held power for a long time and have offended many of the king's brothers with your conduct. If they are truly placed on the throne, disaster will soon befall you. How then can you preserve your position as prime minister and the fiefdom of Jiangdong?" Now I myself know that I am pregnant, but no one else is aware. My time in your favor has not been long; if you genuinely value me and present me to King Chu, the king will surely take a liking to me. If by Heaven's grace I bear a son, then your child will become the king. You would then be able to obtain all the fiefs of Chu; how does this compare with facing unforeseen punishment?" Chunshen Jun greatly approved of her words. He then took Li Yuan's younger sister out to a secluded residence and informed King Chu about her. King Chu summoned her in, and favored her. She soon gave birth to a son, who was made crown prince, and Li Yuan's younger sister was established as queen consort. King Chu held Li Yuan in high esteem, and thus Li Yuan gained influence over affairs of state. After Li Yuan had his younger sister installed as queen and their son made crown prince, he feared that Chunshen Jun might reveal the secret or become increasingly arrogant. Therefore, he secretly maintained a group of assassins, intending to kill Chunshen Jun to cover up the matter; however, many people in the state already knew about this.

Chunshen Jun served as prime minister of Chu for twenty-five years when King Kao Lie fell ill. Zhu Ying said to Chunshen Jun, "In this world there are unexpected blessings, and also unexpected misfortunes. Now you find yourself in an age of unforeseen events, serving a ruler who brings unforeseen circumstances—how could there not also be people who bring unforeseen consequences?" Chunshen Jun asked: "What do you mean by 'unexpected blessings'?" Zhu Ying replied, "You have served as prime minister of Chu for more than twenty years. Though your title is that of chancellor, in reality you are the ruler of Chu. All five of his sons served as chancellors for vassal states. Now the king is gravely ill and will die soon. The crown prince is weak and unable to rise from his illness, yet you serve as regent for a young ruler. If you then seize power and govern in place of him, it would be akin to Yi Yin or Zhou Gong. When the king grows older and returns power to him—if you do not, then you should face south as a sovereign ruler and thereby possess the state of Chu. This is what is meant by an unexpected blessing." Chunshen Jun asked: "What do you mean by 'an unexpected misfortune'?" Zhu Ying replied, "Li Yuan does not govern the state; he is the king's maternal uncle. He holds no military command, yet for a long time now he has secretly maintained assassins. When King Chu dies, Li Yuan will surely enter first and seize control of the situation, cutting off your escape routes. He will then take power and kill you to cover up his involvement." This is what is meant by 'an unexpected misfortune.'" Chunshen Jun asked: "What do you mean by a person who brings unforeseen consequences?" Zhu Ying replied: "You first appointed me as a Langzhong. When the king dies, Li Yuan will enter first; I request to strike him in the chest and kill him for you. This is what is meant by a person who brings unexpected consequences." Chunshen Jun said: "Master, leave it be. Do not mention it again. "Li Yuan is a weak man; I also treat him kindly—how could things reach this point?" Zhu Ying, fearing for his safety, fled away. Seventeen days later, King Kao Lie of Chu died. As expected, Li Yuan entered first and stationed his assassins inside the Jimen Gate. Chunshen Jun arrived afterward and stopped at the Jimen Gate. Li Yuan's assassins attacked Chunshen Jun from both sides, beheaded him, and threw his head outside the Jimen Gate. He then ordered officials to completely exterminate the entire family of Chunshen Jun. The daughter of Li Yuan, who had originally been favored by Chunshen Jun and became pregnant before being presented to the king, gave birth to a son who was later established as King You of Chu. In this year, Emperor Qin Shi Huang had already reigned for nine years. Lao Ai also caused chaos in the state of Qin. When he was discovered, Lao Ai and his three generations were exterminated, and Lu Buwei was deposed.


### Yu Qing Advises Chunshen Jun

Yu Qing said to Chunshen Jun, "I have heard from the Spring and Autumn Annals that when in safety one should think of danger; when in danger, one must consider safety." Now King Chu is growing old, and your fiefdom cannot fail to be established at an early date. For the lord's consideration of a fief, there is none better than distancing from Chu. King Xiao of Qin enfeoffed Shang Jun; after King Xiao's death, he could not avoid killing him eventually. King Hui of Qin enfeoffed Ran Zi; after King Hui's death, the new king took it away from him. Gongsun Yang was a meritorious subject; Ran Zi was a relative. Yet they could not escape being deprived and killed because their fiefs were too close [to the capital]. Tai Gongwang was enfeoffed in Qi, and Shao Gong Shi was enfeoffed in Yan because their fiefs were distant from the royal court. "Now Yan has committed great crimes and Zhao is deeply angry, so you would be better off marching north to show virtue toward Zhao, taking advantage of the chaos in Yan, thereby securing your fief. This is a once-in-a-century opportunity." The lord said, "Those who have spoken of attacking Yan are either Qi or Wei. Wei and Qi recently hold grudges against Chu; even if the lord wishes to attack Yan, by which route could he proceed?" He replied, "Please allow King Wei to do so." The lord asked, "How would that be?" He replied, "I will go to Wei and take measures to establish trust." He then said to King Wei, "Chu is indeed powerful, invincible in the world, and yet it will attack Yan." King Wei said, "Earlier, you said Chu was invincible in the world; now you say it will attack Yan. Why is that?" He replied, "Nowadays, a horse may indeed have great strength; but if one says it can carry a thousand jin, that is not the case. Why?" Because carrying a thousand jin is beyond a horse's capacity. Now it may be true to say that Chu is powerful, but if it were to cross Zhao and Wei and fight a battle in Yan, would that not exceed what is feasible for Chu?" If something beyond Chu's capacity is done by Chu, it will only weaken Chu. Weakening Chu would demonstrate strength to Wei; which of these would be more advantageous for Your Majesty?"
