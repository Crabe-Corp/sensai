# Zhongshan Ce


## Zhongshan


### Wei Wenhou Wants to Destroy Zhongshan

The Wei Wenhou wanted to destroy Zhongshan. Chang Zhuangtan said to Zhao Xiangzi, "If the State of Wei annexes Zhongshan, it will certainly eliminate the State of Zhao. "Why don't you request Gongzi Qing to take her as his official wife and then bestow a fief upon her in Zhongshan? This would mean the state of Zhongshan is reestablished."


### Xi Shou Establishes Five Kings

Xi Shou established five kings, and Zhongshan later held on. Qi said to Zhao and Wei: "I am ashamed to be ranked as a king alongside Zhongshan. I wish to join with your great states in attacking it, so that we may depose its king." Zhongshan heard of this and was greatly alarmed. Zhongshan summoned Zhang Deng and told him, "I am about to become king. Qi said to Zhao and Wei that he is ashamed of being ranked as a king together with me and wants to attack me." He feared the loss of his state, not merely the loss of kingship. "No one but you can save me." Deng replied, "If my lord will provide me with many carriages and generous gifts, I shall go to see Tian Ying." The ruler of Zhongshan sent him to Qi. Seeing Tian Ying, he said, "I have heard that you wish to depose the king of Zhongshan and will attack him together with Zhao and Wei. This is a mistake." Given how small Zhongshan is, if three states attack it, even though Zhongshan may be forced to abandon kingship, it will still have no choice but to comply. Moreover, if Zhongshan is afraid, it will certainly depose its king and strive to submit to Zhao and Wei. This would be as though you are driving sheep for Zhao and Wei, which is not in the interest of Qi. How much better it would be if Zhongshan were to depose its king and serve Qi instead?"

Tian Ying said, "What should I do then?" Zhang Deng said, "Now you should summon Zhongshan, meet with it, and promise to recognize its king. Zhongshan will certainly be pleased and sever ties with Zhao and Wei." Zhao and Wei, angry at this, will attack Zhongshan. In desperation, Zhongshan will find it difficult to maintain its kingship; then Zhongshan must certainly be frightened into deposing its king in order to serve you." They fear the loss of their state. If you cause them to lose their kingship and thus their state, it is better than driving sheep for Zhao and Wei." Tian Ying said, "Agreed." Zhang Chou said, "No, that is not possible. I have heard it said that those who share the same desires hate each other, while those who share the same worries become close allies. Now five states have mutually recognized one another as kings, but Fanhai is not among them. This means that all of them desire to become kings, yet their concern lies with Fanhai. Now if you summon Zhongshan and meet with it while promising to recognize its kingship, this would be taking away from the five states and adding strength to Fanhai. Granting kingship to Zhongshan will block the four other states, causing them to feel cold-hearted. They must first grant it kingship and then have their own states fall under Qin's influence. This would mean you are facing Zhongshan directly while losing the four other states. Moreover, Zhang Deng's character is such that he has long been adept at subtly recommending the ruler of Zhongshan; it would be difficult to trust him as a source of benefit."

Tian Ying did not listen. As expected, he summoned the ruler of Zhongshan and promised to recognize him as king. Zhang Deng then told Zhao and Wei, "Qi wants to attack Hedong. How do you know this? Qi is extremely ashamed of being on equal footing with Zhongshan as a king. Now, by summoning Zhongshan and meeting with it while promising kingship, he intends to use its troops. Wouldn't it be better for a great state like yours to grant it the title of king first and thus prevent this meeting?" Zhao and Wei agreed, and indeed granted kingship to Zhongshan while growing closer to it. As a result, the state of Zhongshan truly severed ties with Qi and followed Zhao and Wei instead.


### Zhongshan and Yan and Zhao Become Kings

Zhongshan was made king by Yan and Zhao, but Qi did not allow envoys from Zhongshan to pass through the Guan Pass. They said: "I am a state with ten thousand chariots, while Zhongshan is a state with only one thousand chariots; how can it be on equal terms with me?" They wanted to cede Pingyi as a bribe to Yan and Zhao, and send troops to attack Zhongshan.

Lan Zhu jun was worried about this. Zhang Deng said to Lan Zhu jun, "What are you afraid of from Qi?" Lan Zhu jun said, "Qi is strong, a state with ten thousand chariots. It considers it a disgrace that Zhongshan should be on equal terms in name, and does not hesitate to cede territory as bribes to Yan and Zhao and send troops to attack Zhongsan." "Yan and Zhao love positions and are greedy for land. I fear they will not support us firmly." "The worst case is that the state may be endangered, or at least the king may be deposed. How can I not be worried?" Zhang Deng said, "Please allow me to send envoys to the states of Yan and Zhao so that they may support Zhongshan and thus complete its kingship, thereby securing stability." "Would you like me to do this?" Lan Zhu jun said, "This is exactly what I want." He said, "Please let me pretend you are the King of Qi while I, Zhang Deng, try to persuade you." "If it is acceptable, then we will proceed with this plan." Lan Zhu jun said, "I would like to hear your reasoning."

Zhang Deng said: "The reason why the King of Qi is not reluctant to cede territory as a bribe to Yan and Zhao, and send troops to attackzhongshan, is that in reality he wants to depose the king of Zhongshan." "The King said: 'That's right.'" "If so, then the King will suffer losses and face danger." Ceding territory as a bribe to Yan and Zhao is to strengthen one's enemies; sending troops to attack Zhongshan would be the first to provoke trouble. If the King carries out both actions, he may not necessarily achieve his goal of eliminating Zhongshan. "If the King follows my strategy, no territory will be lost and no troops need to be used; yet Zhongshan can still be deposed." "The King would certainly ask: 'What is your strategy?'" Lan Zhu jun said, "Then what is your strategy?" Zhang Deng said: "The King should send a high-ranking envoy to inform the ruler of Zhongshan, saying: 'I have closed my borders and not allowed your envoys through because Zhongshan has become king solely with Yan and Zhao without informing me. That is why I have blocked you.'" "If the King of Zhongshan were to come in person, wearing jade ornaments and appear before me, then I would also support him." Zhongshan fears that Yan and Zhao may not support it firmly. Now, Qi's message says, "I will also assist the King," so Zhongshan must certainly flee from Yan and Zhao to meet with the King of Qi in person. When Yan and Zhao hear this, they will be angry and cut off relations with Zhongshan; the King of Qi will also sever ties. Thus, Zhongshan will become isolated, and an isolated state cannot help but lose its kingship. "If you use this reasoning to persuade the King of Qi, would he listen?" Lan Zhu jun said: "In that case, he must certainly listen. This is how to depose Zhongshan; where then would its kingship remain?" Zhang Deng said: "This is why the King can preserve his position." With this message from Qi, Zhongshan should inform Yan and Zhao but not go in person. This will help build closer relations with Yan and Zhao. Yan and Zhao would certainly say: "The reason why Qi wants to cede Pingyi to bribe us is not because it wishes to depose the King of Zhongshan; it merely wants to alienate us from Zhongshan and make itself appear as a benefactor." Even if there were a hundred Pingyis, Yan and Zhao would certainly not accept them." Lan Zhu jun said: "Well said." They sent Zhang Deng to Qi, and as expected, the King of Qi used exactly this message. łó Zhongshan then informed Yan and Zhao but did not go in person; as a result, both Yan and Zhao supported Zhongshan and recognized its king. Thus the matter was settled.


### Sima Xi Sends an Envoy to Zhao

Sima Xi sent an envoy to Zhao and requested for himself the position of prime minister of Zhongshan. Gongsun Hong secretly learned about it. The ruler of Zhongshan went out, and Sima Xi drove the chariot, while Gongsun Hong rode as a passenger on the right side. Hong said, "As a subject, to invoke the might of a great state for one's own sake in seeking the position of prime minister—how would this be regarded by our lord?" The ruler said, "I have eaten his flesh and will not share it with others." Sima Xi kowtowed on the crossbar of the chariot and said, "I know that death is upon me!" The ruler asked, "Why?" "I have committed a crime deserving punishment." The ruler said, "Go on; I understand now." After a short while, an envoy from Zhao arrived to request Sima Xi as prime minister. The ruler of Zhongshan greatly suspected Gongsun Hong, and Gongsun Hong fled out.


### Sima Xi Three Times Prime Minister of Zhongshan

Sima Xi san xiang dongzhu ji, yin jian nan zhi. Tian Jian said to Sima Xi: "The Zhao envoy has come here; can we not mention Yin Jian's virtue alone?" Zhao will surely request him. If you grant it, then you, sir, will have no internal difficulties. If you do not give it to Zhao, you, sir, should advise the ruler to appoint him as a formal wife. The virtue of Yin Jian toward the public is boundless." Indeed, when Zhao made a request, the governor's office granted it. Sima Xi said: "If you do not grant it to Zhao, King Zhao will surely be greatly angered; When he is greatly angered, your position will certainly become perilous. "However, by appointing her as a wife, there would be no one who cannot request another's wife and then blames others."


### Yin Ji and Jiang Ji Compete to Become Empress

Yin Ji and Jiang Ji competed to become empress. Sima Xi said to Yin Ji's father: "If the matter succeeds, you will have land and subjects; if it fails, I fear you may lose your life." "Rather than trying to make it succeed, why not go and see the minister?" Yin Ji's father kowtowed and said: "You are indeed right. How can one foresee whether a matter will succeed or not?" Sima Xi immediately submitted a memorial to the Prince of Zhongshan, saying: "I have heard that a weak Zhao means a strong Zhongshan. The Prince of Zhongshan was pleased and received him, saying: "I would like to hear your explanation about how Zhao can be weakened and Zhongshan strengthened." Sima Xi said: "I wish to go to Zhao, observe its geographical hazards and obstacles, the wealth or poverty of its people, whether its rulers and ministers are virtuous or unworthy, and use these observations as resources for assessing our adversary. These matters cannot be detailed in advance." The Prince of Zhongshan sent him off.

He met with the King of Zhao and said: "I have heard that Zhao is renowned throughout the world for its musical talent, a place where beautiful women are born. Now that I have arrived at your border and entered the capital to observe the people's songs, customs, appearances, and complexions, I find none of them particularly beautiful or attractive." "Given how much traveling I have done and the many places I have visited, I have never seen anyone like Yin Ji of Zhongshan." Those who do not know her would simply regard her as a deity; no amount of eloquence could match her. Her appearance and complexion surpass all others in the country. "As for her eyebrows, eyes, nose, and facial features—her beauty is like a crescent moon. She is the descendant of an emperor; she is not merely a concubine of a feudal lord." The King of Zhao was swayed and greatly pleased, saying: "I would like to request her for myself. What do you think?" Sima Xi said: "I secretly observed her beauty, and could not help but speak of it. If you wish to request for her, that is not something I dare to discuss; I only hope the king will keep this matter secret."

Sima Xi took his leave and returned to report to the Prince of Zhongshan: "The King of Zhao is not a virtuous ruler. He does not value virtue and morality, but instead favors music, beauty, and sensual pleasures; he does not cherish benevolence and righteousness, but rather values bravery and strength. "I have heard that he wishes to request the so-called Yin Ji." The Prince of Zhongshan turned a stern face and was displeased. Sima Xi said: "Zhao is a powerful state, so it is certain that they will make the request. If Your Highness refuses to give her up, then our state's safety and stability are in danger; but if we do give her to them, we will be laughed at by the feudal lords." The Prince of Zhongshan said: "Then what should we do?" Sima Xi said: "Your Highness should appoint her as empress, to cut off the King of Zhao's intentions. No one in the world has ever dared to request another ruler's empress." Even if he wishes to make such a request, neighboring states would not agree to it." The Prince of Zhongshan then appointed her as empress; the King of Zhao made no further requests.


### Zhu Fu Wants to Attack Zhongshan

Zhu Fu wanted to attack Zhongshan and sent Li Ci to observe it. Li Ci said, "It can be attacked." "If you do not attack it, I fear that others will precede us in doing so." Zhu Fu asked, "Why is that?" He replied, "The ruler of Zhongshan has given his carriage and umbrella to those who come in the morning from poor lanes and narrow alleys; there are seventy such families." Zhu Fu said, "That is a virtuous ruler. How can he be attacked?" Li Ci said, "Not necessarily." "Promoting scholars causes the people to pursue fame and neglect fundamentals; "honoring the virtuous causes farmers to become lazy and soldiers timid." "If a state under such circumstances does not perish, there has never been one like it."


### Ruler of Zhongshan Hosts a Banquet for Scholars from Du

The Zhongshan jun hosted a banquet for the scholars of Du, and the official Sima Ziqi was present there. The lamb stew was not distributed to everyone, so Sima Ziqi became angry and went to the state of Chu, persuading King of Chu to attack Zhongshan. As a result, the ruler of Zhongshan fled. Two men carried halberds and followed behind him. The ruler of Zhongshan looked back at the two men and asked, "What are you doing?" The two men replied, "Our father once suffered from hunger and was about to die, but Your Majesty gave him a pot of food and cakes. My father, on the verge of death, said: 'If Zhongshan encounters trouble, you must die for it.'" Therefore, we have come to die for Your Majesty." The ruler of Zhongshan sighed and looked up, saying, "It is not the number that matters in times of need; nor does resentment depend on its depth; what truly hurts the heart is the wound it causes. "The state of Wu lost its country over a single bowl of lamb stew, while I gained two loyal men through one pot of food."


### Yue Yang as a General of Wei

Yue Yang was a general of Wei. He attacked Zhongshan. His son was at that time in the central region, and the ruler of Zhongshan boiled him, made a soup, and presented it to Yue Yang. Yue Yang ate it. Through the ages, people have praised him: Yue Yang ate his son to demonstrate his own resolve, clearly harming his father in order to pursue the law.


### King Zhao After Pacifying the People and Preparing His Troops

King Zhao, after pacifying the people and preparing his troops, once again wished to attack Zhao. Wu'an Jun said, "It is not advisable." The king said, "Previously, when the state was exhausted and its people starving, you did not consider the strength of the common people but requested more military rations in order to destroy Zhao. Now I have pacified the people in order to train soldiers, accumulated provisions, and the stipends for the three armies are double what they were before. Yet you say 'It is not advisable.' What is your reasoning?"

Wu'an Jun said, "In the matter of Changping, the Qin army achieved a great victory and the Zhao army suffered a major defeat; the people of Qin rejoiced, while the people of Zhao were fearful. The deceased among the Qin people were given lavish burials, those who had lost family members received generous support, and those who labored were feasted together. Food and gifts were provided abundantly to squander their wealth; the dead among the Zhao people could not be properly buried, and the injured received no treatment. They wept for one another in sorrow, united in their hardships, worked diligently on their fields, and thus generated wealth. Now that Your Majesty is mobilizing the army, even though it is double its former size, I estimate that Zhao's defenses have also increased tenfold. Since Changping, Zhao's officials and ministers have been anxious and vigilant, rising early for court sessions and retiring late. They have used humble words and generous gifts to send princesses in marriage in all directions, forming alliances with Qin, Yan, and Wei, and maintaining good relations with Qi and Chu. With united efforts and careful planning, they have focused solely on preparing against Qin. Their state is internally strong, and their alliances externally solidified. At the present time, Zhao cannot be attacked."

Wu'an Jun said, "At that time, King Chu relied on the size of his state and neglected governance. His ministers envied one another for their achievements, flatterers held power, capable officials were dismissed or sidelined, the people's hearts turned away, city walls remained unrepaired, and there were neither competent officials nor adequate defenses. Therefore, I was able to lead my troops deep into their territory, capturing twice as many cities and towns. We destroyed the bridges and burned our boats to concentrate the people's resolve, looted the countryside, and thereby secured provisions for the army. At that time, the soldiers in Qin's central forces regarded the army as their home and the generals as their parents. They did not need agreements to be loyal or plans to be trustworthy; united of heart and purpose, they achieved success together, never retreating even at the cost of death. The people of Chu, fighting on their own land, all thought about their families; they were scattered and disorganized, with no will to fight. That is why we were able to achieve success. In the Battle of Yique, Han was isolated and relied on Wei, unwilling to commit its forces first. Wei relied on Han's strength and wished to use it as a vanguard. The two armies had differing interests in seeking advantageous positions, so I was able to deploy decoy forces to await Han's formation and concentrate my troops for a sharp strike at Wei when they were unprepared. After the Wei army was defeated, the Han forces naturally collapsed; we pursued the retreating enemy in victory and thus achieved success. All of these were based on calculating advantages and assessing circumstances, following natural principles—what divine power was there involved! Now that Qin has defeated Zhao's army at Changping, it did not take the opportunity to strike while they were shaken and fearful in order to annihilate them. Instead, fearing them, we released our grip on them, allowing Zhao to cultivate crops and accumulate more resources, raise orphans and children, increasing its population, repair weapons and armor to strengthen itself further, and reinforce city walls and moats to enhance its defenses. The ruler humbled himself to inspire his ministers, while the ministers in turn showed consideration for their soldiers' lives. As for figures like Pingyuan Jun, they all ordered their wives and concubines to mend clothes among the ranks of soldiers. leading The people and officials were united in purpose, with ruler and subjects working together with one mind—just like Goujian during his time of hardship at Kuaiji. If we attack them under such circumstances, Zhao will surely hold out in strong defense. Provoking their army to battle, they will certainly refuse to come out. Laying siege to their capital city would surely not be successful. Attacking their outlying cities will certainly not result in capturing them. Looting the countryside will surely yield nothing of value. If our troops go out without achieving success, the feudal lords will begin to harbor doubts, and external relief forces will inevitably arrive. I see only the dangers, not any benefits. Moreover, I am ill and unable to march."

Ying Hou was ashamed and withdrew, then reported this to the king. The king said, "Without Bai Qi, could I not have destroyed Zhao?" He then sent additional forces and instead ordered Wang He to replace Wang Ling in attacking Zhao. For eight or nine months, Handan was besieged; many were killed or wounded, yet the city could not be captured. lodash Sui Wang sent light and agile troops to attack their rear, and Qin suffered repeated defeats. Wu'an Jun said, "You did not listen to my strategy; now what has happened?" The king, upon hearing this, became angry and summoned Wu'an Jun. He forced him to rise and said, "Although you are ill, I want you to lie in bed and still serve as my general." If there is success, it will fulfill my wish, and I shall bestow greater honors upon you. If you do not go, I shall hold a grudge against you." Wu'an Jun kowtowed and said, "I know that if I go even though it may result in no success, at least I will be spared blame. But if I do not go, although I would bear no guilt, I cannot avoid punishment." Nonetheless, I only hope Your Majesty will consider my humble strategy: release Zhao and allow its people to recover in order to deal with the shifting circumstances among the feudal lords. Calm their fears and strike at their arrogance; punish the unjust to issue commands to the feudal lords. Thus, the whole world can be stabilized—why must Zhao necessarily come first?" This is what is meant by bending to one minister's advice in order to conquer the entire world. If Your Majesty does not consider my humble advice and insists on seeking satisfaction against Zhao, thus incurring guilt upon me, this would be what is called defeating one minister while the whole world bends to you. What is the value of conquering a single subject's will, compared with commanding the might of all under heaven? I have heard that a ruler loves his state, and a loyal minister loves his reputation. A destroyed state cannot be restored, and dead soldiers cannot come back to life. I would rather accept severe punishment and die than endure the disgrace of being a commander who brings shame upon the army. I hope Your Majesty will reflect on this." The king did not respond and left.
