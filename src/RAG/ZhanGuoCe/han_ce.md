# Han Ce


## Han 1


### Three Jin States Have Defeated the Zhi Clan

The Three Jin states had already defeated the Zhi clan and were about to divide its territory. Duan Gui said to King of Han, "When dividing the land, you must take Chenggao." King of Han said, "Chenggao is a place with stones and rapids; I have no use for it." Duan Gui said, "That is not the case. I have heard that a thickness of one li can affect the power over a thousand li—this is the advantage of terrain." A multitude of literati can destroy three armies, if they are unexpected. olocation If the king heeds my words, then Han will surely take Zheng." The king said, "Well said." They indeed took Chenggao. When Han eventually captured Zheng, it indeed began from Chenggao.


### Dacheng Wu Comes from Zhao

Dacheng Wu came from Zhao and said to Shen Buhai in the state of Han, "You value me highly in Zhao through your influence in Han. I will reciprocate by valuing you highly in Han through my influence in Zhao. Thus, you would have two Hans, and I would have two Zhaos."


### Siege of Handan by Wei

When Wei besieged Handan, Shen Buhai first met with King of Han, but he did not know what the king desired. He was afraid that if he spoke, his words might not coincide with the king's wishes. The king heard Shenzi say: "With whom should I ally myself?" He replied, "This is a matter of vital importance to safety and danger, and a major issue for the state. I respectfully request time to ponder deeply and think carefully about it." He then secretly told Zhao Zhuo and Han Chao, "You are both eloquent men of the state. As subjects, one merely needs to speak words that can certainly be used, and to give complete loyalty." The two men each presented their proposals on the matter to the king. Shenzi subtly observed what pleased the king and spoke accordingly, and the king was greatly pleased with him.


### Shen Zi Requests to Serve under His Cousin's Official Post

Shen Zi requested to serve under his cousin's official post, but Zhao Hou did not approve it. Shen Zi showed a resentful expression. Zhao Hou said, "This is not what I meant by learning from you." "Should I listen to the visitor's request and thus neglect your teachings?" "How could I abandon the methods you taught me and disregard the visitor's petition?" "You still taught me to follow merit, and consider rank in order." "Now that there is a request made, whom should I listen to?" Shen Zi then left his residence and asked for forgiveness, saying, "You are indeed the kind of ruler one would follow!"


### Su Qin for Chu Unites the Vertical Alliance: Persuading King Han

Su Qin, acting for the state of Chu to form a vertical alliance, addressed King Han and said: "Han has in the north the strongholds of Gong, Luo, and Chenggao; in the west the pass at Yiyang and Changban; in the east the regions of Wan, Rang, and Weishui; and in the south Mount Xing. The territory spans a thousand li, with tens of thousands of armed soldiers." The powerful crossbows and strong bows that dominate the world all originate from Han. Xi Zi, Shaofu Shili, Ju Lai, and others are archers capable of shooting beyond six hundred bu. Han soldiers can leap forward to shoot, firing a hundred arrows without pause; those at a distance hit the chest, and those nearby strike the heart. The swords and halberds of Han soldiers are all forged in Ming Shan, Tang Xi, Mo Yang, and He Bo Bo. Deng Shi, Wan Feng, Long Yuan, and Da E are all so sharp they can sever the legs of horses and oxen on land or strike swans and geese in water; when facing an enemy, they can cut through armor. Armors, shields, shoes, helmets, iron cloaks, leather gauntlets, and cuirasses are all fully equipped. With the valor of Han soldiers, clad in strong armor, wielding powerful crossbows and sharp swords, one man can match a hundred; this is not an exaggeration. Given Han's strength and Your Majesty's virtue, yet you wish to submit westward to Qin, acknowledge yourself as its eastern vassal, build imperial palaces, accept the insignia of rank, perform ancestral rites in spring and autumn, and bow submissively. To disgrace one's state and be laughed at by all under heaven, there is nothing worse than this. Therefore, I hope Your Majesty will carefully consider this matter. If Your Majesty serves Qin, then Qin will certainly demand Yiyang and Chenggao. If you cede these now, next year they will ask for more territory to be surrendered. If you give in, there will soon be no land left to offer; if you refuse, then you abandon previous concessions and invite further disaster. Moreover, Your Majesty's territory is limited, while Qin's demands are endless. To use a finite amount of land to resist boundless demands is nothing more than inviting resentment and buying disaster; even without battle, the territory will already have been reduced. I have heard a proverb from the common people: "Better to be the mouth of a chicken than the behind of an ox." Now, if Your Majesty bows westward and serves Qin as its vassal, how is this different from being the behind of an ox? Your Majesty's virtue and your command over Han's powerful army, yet to bear the name of being behind an ox—this I secretly consider a shame for Your Majesty."

King Han became visibly angry, his face reddening; he raised his arm and gripped his sword, gazing heavenward and sighing deeply: "Even if I die, I will never serve Qin. Now that my lord has conveyed the King of Chu's instructions to me, I shall respectfully offer up our state and join the alliance."


### Zhang Yi for Qin Advocates the Horizontal Alliance to King of Han

Zhang Yi, on behalf of Qin and advocating for the Horizontal Alliance, said to King of Han: "The territory of Han is perilous and mountainous; it produces five grains, but not wheat but rather beans; the food consumed by the people consists mostly of bean porridge and guisou soup; if there is one year without a harvest, the people will not refuse bran or chaff; its territory does not exceed nine hundred li, and it lacks provisions for two years. Estimating the King's troops, even if all were gathered they would not exceed thirty thousand, and among them are servants and attendants who must be supported; after accounting for those stationed to defend border outposts, watchtowers, and frontier fortifications, the actual number of combat-ready soldiers would amount to no more than twenty thousand. Qin has more than a million armored soldiers, a thousand chariots, ten thousand cavalrymen, and countless warriors as fierce as tigers, who charge forward bareheaded, their faces set in grim determination, brandishing halberds with great vigor—so numerous that they cannot be counted. The fine horses of Qin, the multitude of its armed forces, are so numerous that those who can leap forward and kick backward with a span of three xun between them cannot be numbered. The soldiers of the lands east of Mount Hua, clad in armor and wearing helmets to engage in battle, while Qin men discard their armor and run bare-chested toward the enemy, holding a severed head in one hand and carrying a live captive in the other. The soldiers of Qin compared to those of the lands east of Mount Huan are like Meng Ben compared to a timid man; when they fight with all their strength, it is as if Wu Huo were fighting against an infant. To deploy warriors as fierce as Meng Ben and Wu Huo to attack a weak, defiant state is no different from dropping a weight of one qianjun onto an egg laid by a bird—it will certainly be smashed without any chance of survival. The feudal lords do not realize the weakness of their armies or the scarcity of their food supplies, yet they listen to flattery and pleasant words, forming alliances through mutual deception. They all say: "If you follow my strategy, you can become strong enough to dominate the world." Those who disregard the long-term interests of their states and heed only momentary persuasion, thereby misleading their rulers, could not be more mistaken than this. If Your Majesty does not serve Qin, then Qin will deploy troops to occupy Yiyang and cut off the northern territories of Han; if it takes Chenggao and Yiyang to the east, then Hongtai Palace and Sanglin Park will no longer belong to Your Majesty. If Chenggao is blocked and the northern territories are cut off, then Your Majesty's state will be divided in two. To serve Qin first brings safety; to refuse service to Qin brings danger. To invite disaster in the hope of gaining blessings, to have shallow plans but deep desires, and to oppose Qin while aligning with Chu—though one may wish not to perish, it is impossible. Therefore, for Your Majesty's best interest, there is no better course of action than to serve Qin. What Qin desires most is nothing more than the weakening of Chu. And among those who can weaken Chu, none are better suited than Han. It is not because Han is stronger than Chu, but due to the geographical situation. If Your Majesty faces west and serves Qin in order to attack Chu on its behalf, as a vassal state, King of Qin will surely be pleased. To attack Chu and claim its territory for oneself, while shifting the burden of disaster onto others and pleasing Qin—there is no plan more advantageous than this. Therefore, King of Qin has sent his envoy to present a letter to Your Majesty's chief historian; the matter must be decided upon without delay."

Han said: "Your guest has kindly instructed me. I will request to be treated as a commandery or county, build the emperor's palace, hold sacrifices in spring and autumn, acknowledge myself as an eastern vassal, and cede Yiyang."


### King Xuan Addresses Ju Liu

King Xuan asked Ju Liu, "I wish to use both Gongzhong and Gongsu; would that be possible?" Ju Liu replied, "No, it is not possible. The Jin State used the Six Chancellors and the state was divided; King Jian of Qi employed Tian Cheng and Jian Zhi, yet King Jian was assassinated; the State of Wei used both Xi Shou and Zhang Yi, and as a result, territory beyond the Xhe River was lost. Now if Your Majesty employs both of them, the more powerful one will establish his faction within the court, while the weaker one will seek support from external forces. Ministers may either establish factions within to dominate their ruler, or form alliances externally to divide the land; in that case, Your Majesty's state will surely be in danger."


### Zhang Yi to King Qi

Zhang Yi said to King Qi, "Your Majesty would be better off supporting Han Peng and helping him drive Zhang Yi out of Wei." Wei then appointed Xi Shou as chancellor, and through the alliance with Qi and Wei, Han Peng was deposed; instead, Gongshu was made chancellor to launch a campaign against Qin. Gongzhong, upon hearing this, would certainly not enter Qi. "You will be supported in Wei; thus, you have nothing to fear."


### Chu Zhaoxian xiang Han

Chu Zhaoxian Xiang Han The Qin state was about to attack the State of Han, and thus Han deposed King Zhaoxian. King Zhaoxian sent someone to tell Gongshu, "It would be better for you to honor King Zhaoxian in order to strengthen the alliance with Chu. The Qin state will certainly say that Chu and Han have united."


### Qin Attacks Xing

The State of Qin attacked Xing, and the State of Han sent envoys to rush to the area of Nanyang. Qin had already dispatched troops, and again attacked Xing; as a result, Han ceded the territory of Nanyang. Qin accepted the land, yet continued to attack Xing. Chen Zhen said to King Qin: "The state sent envoys because its geographical position was inconvenient, and it ceded territory because the alliance was not close. "Now that Han has already ceded land yet the alliance remains distant, and although envoys have been sent, Qin's military attacks still do not cease. I fear that no state in the east of the Shan Mountains will be willing to send envoys or make territorial concessions to serve Your Majesty any longer." Moreover, although the king sought a hundred gold ingots from Sanchuan and could not obtain them, he requested a thousand gold ingots from Han, and they were ready in one day. "Now that the king attacks Han Shu, this action severs a superior alliance and strengthens only his private treasury. I secretly believe it is not in Your Majesty's interest."


### Five States Alliance to Attack Qin

Five states formed an alliance to attack Qin, with King of Chu serving as the leader of the coalition; they were unable to inflict damage on Qin, and their troops remained at Chenggao without engaging in battle. Wei Shun said to Shi Qiu Jun, "Once the five states disband, they will certainly attack Shi Qiu in order to recoup their military expenses. You provide for your subject, and I shall request on your behalf to stop the attacks from all under heaven against Shi Qiu." Shi Qiu Jun said, "Good." He then sent him off.


### Zheng Qiang Carries Eight Hundred Jin of Gold into Qin

Zheng Qiang carried eight hundred jin of gold into Qin and requested to attack Han. Ling Xiang said to Zheng Qiang, "You are requesting to attack a state that is allied with you by offering eight hundred jin of gold; Qin will certainly not listen to you." "You would be better off making King of Qin doubt Gong Shu." Zheng Qiang said, "How should I do that?" He said, "When Gong Shu attacked Chu, it was because Ji Se still existed; therefore, he declared war on Chu first." "Now King of Chu has already sent Ji Se with a hundred chariots to reside in Yangzhi, and ordered Zhao Xian to transfer and stay there with him. After more than ten days, the matter is already settled." And Ji Se is Gong Shu's enemy; and Zhao Xian is a man of Gong Shu. "When King of Qin hears about this, he will certainly suspect that Gong Shu is acting for Chu."


### Zheng Qiang's Expulsion of Zhang Yi from Qin

Zheng Qiang sent Zhang Yi to Qin and said, "The envoy of Yi must be going to Chu." Therefore, he told the Grand Minister, "You detained Zhang Yi's envoy. Qiang requested from Qin that they plot against Yi to the west." Therefore, he took advantage of this and made a request to King Qin, saying, "Zhang Yi sent someone to present the territory of Shangyong; therefore, I have dispatched an envoy to pay respects twice before King Qin." King Qin became angry, and Zhang Yi fled.


### Campaign at Yiyang

In the campaign at Yiyang, Yang Da said to Gongsun Xian: "Please allow me to lead fifty thousand troops to attack Xi Zhou. If we capture it, this will be like Gan Mao receiving the Nine Tripods." Otherwise, when Qin attacks Xi Zhou, all under heaven will resent it. They will certainly rush to rescue Han, and then Gan Mao's mission will fail."


### Qin Surrounds Yiyang

The Qin forces surrounded Yiyang, and You Teng said to Gong Zhong, "Why don't you offer Zhao's Lin, Lishi, and Qi as collateral for the promised territory? Then Lou Huan will certainly fail." Mobilize the armies of Han and Zhao to threaten Wei, and Lou Bi will certainly be defeated. If Han unites as one, Wei must betray Qin, and Gan Mao will certainly be defeated. Use Chengyang to support Di Qiang in Qi, and Chu will certainly defeat him. "Only when Qin is certain to be defeated, and Qin loses Wei, will Yiyang surely not fall."


### Gongzhong bears a grudge against Ganzhao due to the Yiyang incident

Gongzhong bore a grudge against Ganzhao because of the Yiyang affair. Later, Qin returned Wusui to Han, but afterwards, King of Qin firmly suspected that Ganzhao had used Wusui as a means to reconcile with Gongzhong. Du He, on behalf of Gongzhong, said to the King of Qin: "Mingye wishes to serve Your Majesty through Ganzhao." The King of Qin became greatly angry with Ganzhao, so Chuli Ji was very pleased with Du Liao.


### Qin and Han Fight at Zhuoze

The State of Qin and the State of Han fought at Zhuoze, and the House of Han was in dire straits. Gongzhong Ming said to King Han: "Allied states cannot be relied upon." "Now the mind of Qin desires to attack Chu. Your Majesty might as well take advantage of Zhang Yi to make peace with Qin, and bribe them with a famous capital city, and join forces with them to attack Chu." "This is a strategy of exchanging one for two." King Han said, "Good." He then prepared Gongzhong's departure, intending to go west and negotiate with Qin.

King Chu heard of this and was greatly alarmed; he summoned Chen Zhen and informed him. Chen Zhen said, "Qin has long desired to attack us. Now they have also obtained one of Han's famous capitals and are fully armed; Qin and Han joining forces to face southward—this is exactly what Qin has been praying for in their ancestral temple." "Now that they have already obtained it, Chu will certainly be attacked next." "If Your Majesty listens to me, prepare within the four borders by selecting an army, announce that we are rescuing Han, and send war chariots filling the roads; "Send envoys with many carriages and generous gifts to make it clear that King Han is relying on Your Majesty's rescue." "Even if Han cannot listen to us, Han will certainly be grateful to Your Majesty and will not come as a mere vassal." "This way, Qin and Han will not be in harmony. Even if their troops arrive, Chu will not suffer greatly." "If we can convince Han to break relations with Qin, Qin will certainly be very angry and harbor deep resentment toward Han. "Han receiving help from Chu will surely look down on Qin." "Looking down on Qin, Han's response to Qin will certainly lack respect." "This is how we can trap the armies of Qin and Han and thus free our country from disaster."

King Chu was greatly pleased, so he prepared within the four borders by selecting ten thousand troops, announced that they were rescuing Han, sent envoys with numerous carriages and generous gifts. He said to King Han: "Although our small state is insignificant, we have already mobilized all our forces." "We hope your great state will act freely against Qin. Our small country will sacrifice itself for Han."

King Han was greatly pleased and thus stopped Gongzhong from proceeding. Gongzhong said, "No, it is Qin who has given us the truth; those who have come to rescue us with empty promises are from Chu. "Relying on an empty promise and lightly cutting off relations with a powerful enemy like Qin will surely make us the laughingstock of all under heaven." "Moreover, Chu and Han are not brother states, nor have we long planned together to attack Qin. "Qin wants to attack Chu, and now Chu has mobilized its troops under the pretense of rescuing Han—this must be Chen Zhen's plan." "Moreover, Your Majesty has already sent envoys to inform Qin. If we do not proceed now, it will be an act of deception toward Qin." "To lightly invite the calamity of a powerful Qin and trust the strategy of which minister, Your Majesty will surely regret it." King Han did not listen, so he broke relations with Qin. Qin was indeed greatly enraged and raised an army to fight the House of Han at Namen; however, help from Chu did not arrive, and the House of Han suffered a major defeat.

The army of Han was not weak or diminished, nor were its people foolish; their troops were captured by Qin, and their wisdom mocked by Chu. This mistake in judgment stemmed from listening to Chen Zhen and failing to follow the strategy of Han Ming.


### Yan Shuai Sees Gongzhong

Yan Shuai saw Gongzhong, but Gongzhong did not see him. Yan Shuai said to the attendant of Gongzhong, "Gongzhong must think that I am a superficial person, so he does not wish to see me. Gongzhong likes concubines; I said he likes scholars; Gongzhong is stingy with wealth; I said he is generous; Gongzhong has no virtue; I said he values righteousness. "From now on, I will only speak the truth." The attendant informed Gongzhong of this, and Gongzhong immediately rose to see him.


### Han Gongzhong Advises Xiang Shou

Han Gongzhong said to Xiang Shou, "A captured bird will overturn a cart when it is tired." You defeated the State of Han and humiliated Gongzhong. Now that Gongzhong has recovered his country and resumed service to Qin, he believes for certain that he will be granted a fiefdom. Now you have reconciled with the State of Chu, and in the middle you bestowed the small position of Lingyi on someone with Guiyang as his fief. If Qin and Chu unite and attack Han again, Han will certainly be destroyed. Gongzhong himself would lead his private followers to fight against Qin; I hope you carefully consider this." Xiang Shou said, "I have united Qin and Chu not in order to confront Han. You go ahead and present my message to them."

Gongzhong said, "The relations between Qin and Han can be reconciled." He replied, "I would like to add something to you. The proverb says: 'What is valued is what makes it valuable.' Now, the king's affection for you, Lord Xi Gong, is not as strong as his affection for Gongsun Hao; his knowledge and ability in recognizing talent are not as good as those of Gan Mao. Now these two men have both been deprived of close involvement in affairs, yet you alone are entrusted by the king to make decisions for the state; this is because they have lost favor through some fault. Gongsun Hao was partial to Han, and Gan Mao was partial to Wei; therefore, the king does not trust them. Now Qin and Chu are vying for dominance, yet you are partial to Chu; this makes you follow the same path as Gongsun Hao and Gan Mao. How then is your situation different from theirs? Everyone says that Chu is fickle, yet you insist on it; this makes you appear self-important. You had better advise the king to prepare for their changes, be friendly with Han as a precaution; if you do this, there will be no misfortune. The House of Han first submitted its state to Gongsun Hao, and later entrusted the country to Gan Mao; thus, Han is your enemy. Now you say that being friendly with Han will prepare us against Chu; this means recommending an external person without avoiding one's own enemies.

Xiang Shou said, "My ministers wish for the alliance between Han and Qin." He replied, "Gan Mao promised Gongzhong the territory of Wusui and agreed to return the people from Yiyang. Now you merely order them to be collected; this will be very difficult." Xiang Zi said, "Then what should we do?" "Wusui will ultimately not be obtainable anymore." He replied, "Why does your lordship not have Qin request Yingchuan from Chu on behalf of Han? This is a territory that Han has entrusted to others. If your lordship seeks it and obtains it, this will demonstrate authority over Chu and allow you to bestow the territory as a favor upon Han. If your lordship seeks it but fails to obtain it, this means that the resentment between Han and Chu will not be resolved, and both states will turn toward Qin. Qin and Chu are vying for strength, yet your lordship passes through Chu to attack Han; this is beneficial to Qin." Xiang Zi said, "What should we do?" He replied, "This is a good course of action. Gan Mao wanted to use Wei to attack Qi, and Gongsun Hao wished to use Han to attack Qi. Now your lordship captures Yiyang as a feat of merit, unites Chu and Han to stabilize the situation, and punishes the offenses of Qi and Wei; this is why Gan Mao and Gongsun Hao have no further business."


### Someone Advises Gongzhong: "To Listen Means to Listen for the State"

Someone said to Gongzhong, "To listen means to listen for the state, not necessarily to listen to the head." Therefore, the sage listens to proverbs in the marketplace; I hope you will listen to my words, Your Lordship. You sought neutrality from Qin but failed to obtain it. You used Gong Sun Hao to oppose Gan Mao, and urged Qi's troops in order to dissuade Wei; both Chu and Zhao are your enemies. I fear the state will suffer from this, Your Lordship; I hope you will once again seek neutrality from Qin."

Gongzhong said, "How should I do that?" The reply was, "King Qin considers Gong Sun Hao to be aligned with you and thus does not listen to him. Gan Mao is not on good terms with you and therefore does not speak for you. Why don't you take advantage of Xingyuan to talk with King Qin?" "As a minister of King Qin, Xingyuan is loyal to you. I will request permission to speak on your behalf to King Qin: 'Which is more beneficial for Qin, the alliance or the separation between Qi and Wei?"' "Which is stronger for Qin, the division or the union of Qi and Wei?"' King Qin will surely say: "If Qi and Wei are separated, then Qin becomes stronger; if they unite, then Qin is weakened." If Qi and Wei are divided, then Qin becomes strong; if they unite, then Qin weakens." I will then say, "Now Your Majesty listens to Gong Sun Hao and unites the armies of Han and Qin with Qi to attack Wei. Wei dares not fight, surrenders territory, and joins forces with Qi; this weakens Qin. I consider Gong Sun Hao disloyal. If now Your Majesty listens to Gan Mao and uses the armies of Han and Qin to occupy Wei and attack Qi, Qi dares not fight and does not seek territorial concessions but unites with Wei; this also weakens Qin. I regard Gan Mao as disloyal." Therefore, it would be better for Your Majesty to order Han to remain neutral and attack Qi. King Qi will then declare that he will rescue Wei in order to strengthen himself; Qi and Wei cannot truly listen to each other, and after some time, they must inevitably go to war. If Your Majesty wishes, then you can show trust in Gong Sun Hao toward Qi, have Han take Nanyang for itself, exchange the Yigu Valley and return it; this is what King Hui desires. If Your Majesty wishes, you can show trust in Gan Mao toward Wei, and use the armies of Han and Qin to hold Wei so as to create a rift with Qi; this is what King Wu desires. I believe that ordering Han to remain neutral in order to strengthen Qi is the greatest urgency for Qin. Gong Sun Hao aligns with Qi and refuses to speak, while Gan Mao is weak and dares not pay homage; these two men are Your Majesty's greatest troubles. I hope Your Majesty will carefully consider this matter. "'"


### Han Gongzhongxiang Prime Minister

Han Gongzhongxiang The relations between Qi and Chu were friendly toward Qin. Qin and Wei met, and Qin, while maintaining good relations with Qi, severed ties with Qi in favor of Chu. The king sent Jing Li to Qin, and Li participated in the meeting between Qin and Wei. King of Chu was angry at Jing Li, fearing that Qi might suspect Chu's meeting as having secret dealings with Qin and Wei, and thus intended to punish Jing Li.

Someone said to King of Chu: "I congratulate Li on his participation in the meeting. The meeting between Qin and Wei was intended to unite Qi and Qin while severing ties with Chu from Qi. Now that Li participated in the meeting, Qi has no reason to believe that Wei is uniting with Qin against Chu. Moreover, Qi fears that Chu might have secret dealings with Qin and Wei; therefore, Qi will certainly value Chu more highly. Therefore, Li's participation in the meeting is a great asset to Your Majesty." If now Li had not participated in the meeting, Wei's intention to sever Qi from Chu would have been clear. Qi and Chu would believe it, and they would certainly look down on Your Majesty. Therefore, Your Majesty had better not punish Jing Li in order to show Qi that there is a relationship with Qin and Wei; then Qi will value Chu more highly, and also doubt the intentions of Qin and Wei toward Qi." The king said: "Agreed." Therefore, he did not punish Jing Li and instead elevated his rank.


### Wang Said: Earlier You Said "The World Is Without Virtue"

Wang said: "Earlier you said 'the world is without virtue.'" "Now you say, 'We are about to attack Yan,' what is the reason for this?" He replied: "Now, it is true that horses have much strength, but if one says they can withstand a thousand jin, this is not the case. Why is that?" "A thousand jin is not something a horse is capable of bearing." "Now, it is true that Chu is powerful, but if one speaks of crossing Yue, Zhao, and Wei to fight in Yan, would that be something Chu is capable of doing?" "Moreover, since it is not what Chu is capable of, yet Chu does it anyway, this will exhaust Chu." "To strengthen Chu or to exhaust Chu, which would be more beneficial for the king?"


### Someone Advises King Wei to Warn Within All Four Borders

Someone said to Wei Wang: "Your Majesty should warn within all four borders that those who follow Your Majesty, if the dikes are not completed within eleven days, shall be put to death." The king therefore took some of his leisure boats and attacked them. "I went to Chu on behalf of the king, but when I returned, the king's ministers rebelled, so I proceeded." Chunshen Jun heard about this and said to the envoy, "You have betrayed me; you will not see the king anymore. Within ten days, tens of thousands of troops have now crossed into Wei territory." The Qin envoy heard about this and reported it to King Qin. King Qin said to King Wei, "If a great state has an intention, it will certainly come here and be satisfied with this."


### Guan Yang Advises Chunshen

Guan Yang said to Chunshen, "Everyone considers Chu to be strong, but you have made it weak. As for me, I do not think so." The former ruler had never seen an attack in more than twenty years. Now Qin wants to send troops through the Mian'ai Pass, but we must not allow it; Borrowing a route through the two Zhou states and doubling back on Han to attack Chu would be unacceptable. Now it is different; Wei will perish in the morning or evening, and cannot protect its territories of Xu, Yanling, and Wu. It has already ceded them to Qin for a distance of 160 li. "This is what I have seen: the day when Qin and Chu will fight."


### Gongzhong Shu's Lack of Trust Among the Feudal Lords

Gongzhong Shu was not trusted by the feudal lords, and thus the feudal lords kept him in seclusion. The State of Nan Wei submitted to Chu, but King of Chu did not listen. Su Dai said on behalf of the King of Chu: "It would be better to agree and prepare for their possible rebellion. When Ming rebelled, he often relied on Zhao to defy Chu, and relied on Qi to defy Qin. Now that four states have kept him in seclusion, and he has nowhere to go, I am also worried about this." "This is precisely the time when he will act like Weisheng."


## Han 2


### Chu Besieges Yongshi for Five Months

Chu besieged Yongshi for five months. The Han envoy sent envoys to seek help from Qin, and the carriages of officials could be seen continuously on the roads; however, the Qin army did not descend below Xiao Pass. Han again sent Shang Jin as an envoy to Qin, who said to the King of Qin: "To Qin, Han is a treasure when it remains within your territory and a valuable ally when it moves out like wild geese in formation." Now Han has already been weakened, yet the Qin army still does not descend below Xiao Pass. I have heard that when lips are gone, the teeth feel cold; I hope Your Majesty will carefully consider this." The Queen Dowager Xuan said: "Many envoys have come, but only Shang Jin's words are correct." She summoned Shang Zi to enter. Queen Dowager Xuan said to Shang Zi: "When I served the late king, he placed his thigh upon my body, and though exhausted, I did not grow weary; he would place his whole body on top of mine, yet I never felt burdened. Why is that?" Because there was some benefit to me from it. Now, aiding Han—if the troops are not numerous and provisions not ample—will be insufficient to save Han. "To rescue Han from peril costs a thousand gold pieces each day; it would only be reasonable if I could gain some small benefit from it as well."

Shang Jin returned with the message to inform King Han, and King Han sent Zhang Cui. Zhang Cui pretended to be ill and only traveled one county per day. When Zhang Cui arrived, Gan Mao said: "Han is in dire straits; you have come despite being ill, Master." Zhang Cui replied: "Han is not yet in a crisis, but it will be soon." Gan Mao said: "Qin is a powerful state and the King knows everything; whether Han's situation is urgent or not, he knows it all." "Now you say it is not urgent. Is that acceptable?"
