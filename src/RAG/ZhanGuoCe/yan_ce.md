# Yan Ce


## Yan 1


### Su Qin Will Form an Alliance and Go North to Persuade Yan Wenhou

Su Qin was about to form an alliance, and he went north to persuade Yan Wenhou, saying: "Yan has in the east Chaoxian and Liaodong, in the north Linhu and Loufan, in the west Yunzhong and Jiuyuan, and in the south Hutuo and Yishui. The territory spans more than 2000 li, with hundreds of thousands of armored soldiers, 700 chariots, 6000 cavalry horses, and grain reserves sufficient for ten years. To the south there are the abundant resources of Jieshi and Yanmen, and to the north there is the advantage of jujube and chestnut production. Even if the people do not engage in farming, the fruits of jujubes and chestnuts would be sufficient to feed them. This is what is called a treasury of heaven. There is no country more at peace and free from trouble than Yan, where one does not see the worries of defeated armies or slain generals. Does Your Majesty know why this is so? The reason Yan has not been invaded or attacked by enemies is because Zhao serves as a southern shield. Between Qin and Zhao, there were five battles; Qin won twice while Zhao won three times. When Qin and Zhao exhaust each other, Your Majesty can control the entire state of Yan from behind them; this is why Yan has not encountered any difficulties. Moreover, if Qin were to attack Yan, it would have to cross Yunzhong and Jiuyuan and pass through Dai and Shanggu. The distance of the route alone spans thousands of li; even if they captured Yan's cities, according to Qin's calculations, they certainly could not hold them. It is clear that Qin cannot harm Yan. Now, when Zhao attacks Yan, upon issuing the order of mobilization, within no more than ten days, an army of hundreds of thousands will already be stationed at Dongyuan. Crossing the Hutuo River and wading through the Yishui, within no more than four or five days they will reach the capital. Therefore it is said that when Qin attacks Yan, the battle takes place a thousand li away; when Zhao attacks Yan, the battle occurs within a hundred li. To be unconcerned about a threat within a hundred li and instead worry about one a thousand li away is no better strategy than this. Therefore, I wish Your Majesty would form an alliance with Zhao against Qin; if the whole world becomes one, then your state will surely be free from danger."

Yan Wang said: "My country is small; to the west it is pressed by powerful Qin, and to the south it is close to Qi and Zhao. Qi and Zhao are powerful states; now, Your Lordship has kindly instructed me to follow your advice, to unite in an alliance for the safety of Yan. I will respectfully submit my state to this alliance." Thus, he provided Su Qin with carriages, horses, gold, and silk before sending him off to Zhao.


### Fengyang Jun Li Dui Was Very Unpleased With Su Qin

Fengyang jun Li Dui was very unpleased with Su Qin. While Su Qin was in Yan, Li Dui therefore told Fengyang jun: "If Qi and Yan are separated, then Zhao will be strong; if Qi and Yan unite, then Zhao will become weak." Now your alliance with Qi is not beneficial to Zhao. "I secretly consider it unwise for you."

Fengyang jun said, "Why should I ally Yan with Qi?" He replied: "The one who controls Yan is Su Zi. And Yan is a weak state; to the east it cannot match Qi, and to the west it cannot match Zhao. How could it possibly have no relations with Qi in the east or with Zhao in the west?" And you are very unfriendly toward Su Qin. Could Su Qin possibly protect weak Yan and isolate it from all other states in the world?" This would be driving Yan to ally with Qi. Moreover, Yan is a state that has survived the fall of its former dynasty; it establishes itself through authority, gains weight from external alliances, and pursues what is esteemed. Therefore, for your sake, if you treat Su Qin well it will be beneficial; even if you do not treat him well, still benefit from him, in order to sow doubt between Yan and Qi. If Yan and Qi are doubtful of each other, then Zhao will gain strength. "If King Qi doubts Su Qin, then you will have more resources." Fengyang jun looked up to him and said: "Good." Nan Liao's envoy then formed a friendship with Su Qin.


### Difficulty of Quan Zhi: Yan Fights Again but Does Not Win

The difficulty of Quan Zhi, Yan fought again but did not win, Zhao did not come to the rescue. Kuai Zi said to Duke Wen: "It would be better to offer land and request an alliance with Qi; Zhao will certainly come to our rescue." "If they do not come to our aid, we will have no choice but to submit." Duke Wen said: "Good." He ordered Guo Ren to offer land and request a peace negotiation with Qi. Zhao heard about it, so he sent troops to rescue Yan.


### During the Reign of Yan Wengong

During the reign of Yan Wengong, King Hui of Qin gave his daughter in marriage to the Crown Prince of Yan. After Wen Gong's death, Yi Wang ascended the throne. King Xuan of Qi took advantage of Yan's mourning period to attack it and captured ten cities.

Wu'an Jun Su Qin, on behalf of Yan, persuaded King Qi. He bowed twice to offer congratulations and then looked up to express condolences. King Qi lowered his halberd and stepped back, saying, "How quickly celebration and condolence follow each other!"

He replied, "The wise man's way of handling affairs is to turn misfortune into fortune and transform failure into achievement. Therefore, Duke Huan of Qi bore the disgrace of a woman and yet his reputation became even more respected; the Prime Minister of Han offered an offense and yet their relationship became even stronger. These are all examples of turning misfortune into fortune and transforming failure into achievement. If Your Majesty can listen to me, there is no better course than returning the ten Yan cities and using humble words to apologize to Qin. Qin will know that Your Majesty returned the Yan cities for its sake, and thus Qin will be grateful toward Your Majesty. Yan will gain ten cities without cause, so Yan will also be grateful to Your Majesty. This is abandoning a powerful enemy and establishing a close alliance. Moreover, if Yan and Qin both serve Qi alone, then Your Majesty's commands will be followed by all under heaven. This is for Your Majesty to use empty words to attach Qin and gain the whole world with ten cities. This would be the achievement of a hegemon or emperor. "This is what is meant by turning misfortune into fortune, and transforming failure into success."


### Someone slandered Su Qin to the King of Yan

Someone slandered Su Qin to King Yan, saying: "Wu'an Jun is a man in whom the world does not trust." "The king treated him with great courtesy as a ruler of ten thousand chariots, honored him before the court, showing that he is on equal footing with petty people throughout the world." Wu'an Jun came from Qi, but King Yan did not provide him with lodging. He said to King Yan: "I am a humble man from the eastern state of Zhou. I have personally rendered no slightest merit for Your Majesty, yet you welcomed me at the suburbs and honored me before the court." "Now I have served Your Majesty as an envoy, gained ten cities for you, and preserved the endangered Yan state. If you do not listen to me, people will surely say that I am untrustworthy, which would harm my reputation before the king." "If I were not killed, it would be a blessing for Your Majesty." "Suppose I were as trustworthy as Weisheng, as upright as Boyi, and as filial as Zeng Shen—three virtues held in the highest esteem throughout the world—and yet still served Your Majesty, would that not be acceptable?" King Yan said: "Yes." He said, "If that is the case, I will no longer serve Your Majesty."

Su Qin said: "Now, a man as filial as Zeng Shen would not stay away from the state of Qin for even one night according to righteousness; how could Your Majesty possibly send him on a mission to Qi?" "A man as upright as Boyi would not accept an unearned meal. He considered King Wu's righteousness to be tainted and thus refused to serve him, rejecting the rulership of Guzhu, and eventually starved to death on Shouyang Mountain." "A man with such integrity—how would he possibly be willing to walk thousands of miles on foot, serving a weak and endangered ruler of Yan?" "A man as trustworthy as Weisheng once made an appointment but when the other party did not arrive on time, he clung to a pillar of a bridge and died." "With such absolute faithfulness, how could one possibly display the might of Yan and Qin before Qi to achieve great merit?" "Moreover, integrity and conduct are for one's own sake, not for the benefit of others. These are strategies for self-preservation, not methods for advancement or conquest." "Moreover, the three sage kings succeeded one another in power; tyrannical rulers rose and fell successively. None of them relied on self-preservation alone." "Do you consider self-preservation to be acceptable, Your Majesty?" "Then Qi would not have expanded its territory beyond Yingqiu, and you would not have crossed the borders of Chu or even looked beyond your frontier cities." "Moreover, I have an elderly mother in Zhou. To leave my old mother and serve Your Majesty—abandoning the strategy of self-preservation for a plan of advancement—is something that naturally does not align with your wishes." "You are a ruler who values self-preservation, while I am an official who seeks advancement. This is precisely the case of being wronged by a ruler for one's loyalty and trustworthiness."

King Yan said: "What crime could there be in loyalty and faithfulness?" He replied, "Your Majesty does not understand." "A neighbor of mine had a relative who served as an official far away; his wife became involved with another man in secret." "When the husband was about to return, the other man worried about it." 藟 The lover said: "Don't worry. I have already prepared poisoned wine for him." Two days later, the husband arrived. The wife sent her concubine to offer a cup of wine. The concubine knew it was poisoned; if she served the wine, she would kill the husband; if she spoke out, she would be expelled by the wife. So she pretended to collapse and discarded the wine. The husband became very angry and beat her with a stick. "Therefore, the concubine's feigned collapse and rejection of the wine saved the husband above and preserved the wife below." "Though her loyalty was so great, she could not avoid being beaten—this is an example of suffering punishment for one's faithfulness and integrity." "My situation is precisely an unfortunate case similar to the concubine discarding the wine." "Moreover, my service to Your Majesty was for the sake of righteousness and national benefit. Now I have been punished; I fear that those who serve you in the future will dare not be certain of their own safety." "Moreover, when I negotiated with Qi, I never deceived them at all." "No one could have spoken to Qi more effectively than me. Even with the wisdom of Yao and Shun, they would not dare claim otherwise."


### Zhang Yi for Qin Breaks Up the Coalition and Promotes Horizontal Alliances: Advising King Yan

Zhang Yi, who broke up the coalition and promoted horizontal alliances for Qin, said to King Yan: "Your Majesty's closest ally is no one other than Zhao." In the past, King Zhao gave his elder sister as a wife to the king of Dai, but he wanted to annex Dai. He arranged to meet with the King of Dai at the Guozhu Pass. He then ordered craftsmen to make a golden ladle, lengthening its tail so that it could be used to strike people. While dining with the King of Dai, he secretly told the cooks: "When the wine is flowing and the music playing, serve a hot soup; then take advantage of the commotion to strike him down." At that moment, when the drinking was in full swing and the entertainment at its height, they brought forward the hot soup. The cooks presented the soup and took advantage of the confusion to strike him down; King Dai's brains were smeared on the ground. His elder sister, upon hearing this, sharpened a hairpin and stabbed herself with it. Therefore, to this day there is Mount Moji, whose story is known throughout the world.

"King Zhao's cruelty and lack of humanity are well known to Your Majesty." "And can King Zhao really be considered trustworthy?" Zhao raised an army to attack Yan, twice surrounding the capital and taking Your Majesty hostage; only after ceding ten cities did Your Majesty manage to retreat in order to apologize. Now King Zhao has already paid homage at Mianchi, offering the territory of Hejian as a vassal to Qin. If Your Majesty does not serve Qin, then Qin will deploy troops to Yunzhong and Jiuyuan, drive Zhao to attack Yan, and the Yi River and the Great Wall will no longer belong to Your Majesty. Moreover, now that Zhao is submitting to Qin, it is as if they were a commandery or county. They dare not recklessly raise an army for war. Now, if Your Majesty serves Qin, King Qin will surely be pleased, and Zhao will then dare not act recklessly. "This means having the powerful support of Qin in the west, and no threats from Qi or Zhao to the south. Therefore, I hope Your Majesty will carefully consider this."

King Yan said: "I am a petty ruler in a remote land. Even though I may be an adult man, I am as helpless as an infant; my words are not sufficient to seek justice, and my plans are inadequate for making decisions." "Now that you, a great guest, have kindly instructed me, I shall offer the state of Yan to serve Qin from the west and present five cities at the southern end of Changshan."


### Gong Ta as Yan's Envoy to Wei

Gong Ta was sent as an envoy to Wei by Yan, but Wei did not listen and detained him for several months. A guest asked King Wei, "Why do you refuse to heed the envoy from Yan?" He replied, "Because it is in chaos." The guest responded, "When Tang attacked Jie, he wanted the latter's state to be in chaos. Therefore, a great disorder allows one to seize its territory, and a minor disorder permits one to obtain its treasures." Now the envoy from Yan said: 'If matters are indeed worth heeding, even if all treasures and lands are offered, it would still be insignificant.' "Why then does Your Majesty not receive him?" King Wei was convinced and thus received the envoy from Yan, sending him off.


### Su Qin's Death and His Younger Brother Su Dai's Desire to Succeed Him

Su Qin died, and his younger brother Su Dai wished to succeed him. He therefore went north to visit King Kuai of Yan, saying: "I am a humble man from the eastern Zhou kingdom; I have secretly heard that Your Majesty's righteousness is very high and very proper. Though I am not intelligent, I dare to leave my plow and hoe behind in order to approach Your Majesty." When he arrived at Handan, what he heard about the city of Handan was even more impressive than what he had previously heard about the eastern Zhou. "I secretly resolved to pursue this aspiration, and thus I came all the way to Yan's court. Observing Your Majesty's ministers and officials, I realized that you are indeed a wise ruler of the world."

The king asked: "What do you mean by calling me a wise ruler of the world?" He replied, "I have heard that a wise ruler is one who earnestly wishes to hear about his faults and does not desire to hear only of his virtues. "I shall now point out Your Majesty's faults." Qi and Zhao are enemies of Your Majesty; Chu and Wei are your allied states. Now, Your Majesty supports one's enemies in order to attack one's allies; this is not a way to benefit Yan. If the king himself considers this matter carefully, he will realize that his strategy has been flawed. "To offer no advice in such a situation is not to be a loyal minister." The king said, "As for Qi and Zhao, I have never dared to wish to attack them."

He replied: "To have no intention of plotting against others yet cause others to suspect you is dangerous; to have the intent to plot against others and allow them to know it is clumsy; If a plan has not yet been revealed but becomes known outside, then one is in danger. "Now I have heard that Your Majesty is restless in your position, finds no satisfaction in food and drink, and constantly thinks of avenging Qi. You personally cut armor straps, saying 'There are great numbers (of enemies)'; your wife herself weaves armor straps, also saying 'There are great numbers.' Is this true?"

The king said: "If you have heard of it, I dare not conceal the truth. "I have harbored deep resentment and accumulated anger toward Qi for two years now, longing to take revenge." Qi is my enemy state; therefore, it is the one I wish to attack. The only problem is that our country is weak and lacks sufficient strength. "If you can lead Yan to contend with Qi, then I shall entrust the state entirely to you."

He replied: "Among all the warring states of the world there are seven in total, and Yan is the weakest among them; if we fight alone, we cannot succeed, but if we have an ally, then nothing will be impossible. If we attach ourselves to the south and align with Chu, then Chu becomes strong; if we attach to the west and ally with Qin, then Qin becomes strong; if we connect in the center with Han and Wei, then Han and Wei become strong. If the state to which we attach ourselves is made strong, this will certainly make Your Majesty powerful as well." Now King Qi is an experienced ruler who acts according to his own will. He has attacked the south against Chu for five years, and as a result, their stored resources have been exhausted. To the west they were trapped by Qin for three years, causing the people to grow weary and emaciated, and the soldiers to become exhausted and worn out. To the north they fought against Yan, destroyed three entire armies, captured two generals, and then used their remaining forces to move southward and conquer the powerful state of Song with 5000 chariots, surrounding twelve feudal lords. This is what their ruler desires to obtain, but the strength of their people has been completely drained—how could they possibly continue to achieve such things? Moreover, I have heard that frequent warfare exhausts the people, and prolonged military campaigns weaken the troops."

The king said: "I have heard that Qi has the clear Ji River and the muddy He River, which can serve as strong defenses; it also has the Great Wall and massive fortifications sufficient to serve as barriers. Is this truly so?" He replied: "If the timing of Heaven does not favor them, then even with the clear Ji and muddy He rivers, how could they be sufficient as defenses? If the people's strength is exhausted, then even having a Great Wall or massive fortifications would not be enough to serve as barriers. Moreover, in the past, the western bank of the Ji River was left unoccupied, which was done precisely to guard against Zhao; the forces along the northern He River were not mobilized, as they were meant to prepare for defense against Yan. Now both the western Ji and northern He regions have been completely surrendered; within their borders, resources are exhausted. A conceited ruler will certainly not be good at planning, and ministers of a doomed state are greedy for wealth. If Your Majesty truly does not hesitate to offer a beloved son or an uncle as a hostage, and use precious jewels, jade, silk, and other valuables to bribe those around the Qi court, they will grow grateful toward Yan and neglect their former conquest of Song. Then Qi can be destroyed."

The king said: "Shall I then entrust you with my destiny, as if it were decreed by Heaven?" He replied: "If internal unrest does not arise, the five hostile forces cannot be resisted. "If Your Majesty deals with external affairs and I handle matters within, this is precisely the situation that will lead to its downfall."


### After King Yan Kuai Was Enthroned

After King Yan Kuai was enthroned, Su Qin died in Qi. When Su Qin was in Yan, he shared hardships with the king's minister Zi Zhi, and Su Dai was on good terms with Zi Zhi. After Su Qin died, Qi Xuanwang again employed Su Dai.

In the third year of King Yan Kuai's reign, he allied with Chu and the Three Jin states to attack Qin, but failed to achieve victory and returned. Zi Zhi served as prime minister of Yan, wielding great power and authority. Su Dai was serving as an envoy from Qi to Yan when King Yan asked him, "What is Qi Xuanwang like?" He replied, "He will certainly not become a hegemon." King Yan asked, "Why?" He replied, "Because he does not trust his ministers." Su Dai wanted to advise King Yan to place great trust in Zi Zhi. Thus, King Yan placed great faith in Zi Zhi. Zi Zhi therefore sent Su Dai a hundred gold pieces and allowed him to act as he wished.

Lu Mao Shou said to King Yan, "It would be better for you to cede the state to Zi Zhi." People say Yao was a virtuous ruler because he offered the world to Xu You, who certainly would not accept it. Thus, Yao gained the reputation of ceding the world while actually retaining power over it. Now, Your Majesty offers your state to your prime minister Zi Zhi. lington Zi Zhi will surely not dare to accept it; thus, Your Majesty would be acting in the same way as Yao." King Yan therefore transferred full authority of the state to Zi Zhi, who thereby gained immense power.

Some said, "Yu appointed Yi as his successor and made Qi an official. When Yu grew old, he considered Qi unfit to rule the world and passed power on to Yi." Qi and his faction conspired against Gongyi and seized the throne from him. Thus, Yu's name was one of passing power to Yi, but in reality he allowed Qi to take it himself. Now Your Majesty says you have entrusted the state to Zi Zhi, yet all officials are loyal to the crown prince. This means that in name you have given power to Zi Zhi, but actually the crown prince holds authority." King Yan therefore collected all seals from officials of the rank of three hundred stones and presented them to Zi Zhi. Zi Zhi then sat facing south, assuming the duties of a king, while King Yan Kuai grew old and ceased to govern. He became merely a subject, with all state affairs decided by Zi Zhi.

In the third year of Zi Zhi's rule, Yan state fell into great chaos; the people were frightened and resentful. General Shi Bei and Crown Prince Ping plotted to attack Zi Zhi. Chu Zi said to Qi Xuanwang, "Take advantage of this opportunity to destroy them, and Yan will surely be broken." King Qi therefore sent a messenger to Crown Prince Ping, saying, "I have heard of the virtue of the crown prince; you are going to set aside private interests and establish public justice, rectify the relationship between ruler and minister, and restore proper order between father and son. My state is small and cannot take precedence." "Nevertheless, it is only you who can issue such commands."

Crown Prince Ping therefore gathered his faction and a large crowd. General Shi Bei surrounded the royal palace, attacking Zi Zhi but failing to succeed; General Shi Bei and the common people then turned around and attacked Crown Prince Ping. General Shi Bei died as a martyr, and the internal strife in Yan lasted for several months. Tens of thousands were killed; the people of Yan were frightened and resentful, and public support began to wane.

Meng Ke said to Qi Xuanwang: "Now is the time to attack Yan, a moment comparable to those of Wen and Wu; this opportunity must not be missed." King Qi therefore ordered Zhang Zi to lead the armies from the five capitals, along with troops from Beidi, to attack Yan. The soldiers did not fight, and city gates were left unbarred; King Yan Kuai died. Qi achieved a great victory over Yan, and Zi Zhi fled. In the second year [after these events], the people of Yan enthroned Gongzi Ping; this was King Zhao of Yan.


### Initially, Su Qin's Younger Brother Li, Relying on Yan Zi, Sought an Audience with King Qi

Initially, Su Qin's younger brother Li, relying on Yan Zi, sought an audience with King Qi of Qi. King Qi resented Su Qin and wanted to imprison Li, but Yan Zi apologized on his behalf and the matter was dropped. Thus he said: "Is King Qi really a hegemon?" He replied, "No, he cannot." He asked, "Why is that?" He replied, "Because he does not trust his ministers." Thus King Yan appointed Zi Zhi exclusively to a position of authority. Soon after, he remarked that the state of Yan was in great disorder. Qi attacked Yan and killed Wang Kui and Zi Zhi. Yan then installed King Zhao. Su Dai and Li thus dared not enter Yan, but both eventually returned to Qi, where they were treated kindly by the state of Qi.


### Su Dai Passes Through Wei

Su Dai passed through Wei, and Wei arrested Su Dai for Yan. Qi sent an envoy to King Wei, saying: "Qi requests to bestow the state of Song as a fief for Jingyang Jun, but Qin did not accept it." It is not that Qin considers it disadvantageous to have Qi and obtain the land of Song; rather, it does not trust King Qi and Su Zi. Now if Qi and Wei are so deeply at odds, then Qi will not deceive Qin. If Qin trusts Qi, and Qi and Qin unite, Jingyang Jun would obtain the land of Song, which is not beneficial to Wei. Therefore, Your Majesty had better send Su Zi eastward; Qin will certainly become suspicious and no longer trust Su Zi. "The situation where Qi and Qin are not in harmony, with no changes occurring elsewhere in the world, will lead to a formation for attacking Qi." Thus they released Su Dai to Song, and Song treated him well.


### King Zhao of Yan Assumes the Throne After the Fall of Yan

After King Zhao of Yan ascended the throne following the fall of Yan, he humbled himself and offered generous rewards to attract talented individuals, hoping to appoint them in order to avenge his grudge. Therefore, he went to visit Master Guowei and said: "Qi took advantage of the chaos in my isolated state and attacked and destroyed Yan. I am fully aware that Yan is small and weak, lacking sufficient strength to take revenge." Yet if I can obtain capable men with whom to govern the state and thus wash away the disgrace of my ancestors, that would be my wish. I dare to ask: how may one use a nation to avenge a grudge?"

Master Guowei replied: "An emperor deals with his teachers, a king with his friends, a hegemon with his ministers, and a ruler of a fallen state with his servants. If one bows his fingers in submission and serves another, bowing low to receive instruction, then those who surpass oneself by a hundredfold will come. If one hurries forward before others and rests last, asks questions first and remains silent afterward, then those who surpass oneself tenfold will come. If people rush ahead and one follows suit, then those equal to oneself will come. If one leans on a table with a cane, glances around imperiously giving orders, then only servants and laborers will come. If one behaves arrogantly, strikes out forcefully, and shouts and scolds loudly, then only common criminals and slaves will come. This is the ancient method of following the right path to attract capable men. If Your Majesty sincerely selects the virtuous people within the state and receives them at your court, when all under heaven hear that the King honors his virtuous ministers, then scholars from throughout the world will surely flock to Yan."

King Zhao said: "To whom should I pay homage in order for this to be possible?" Master Guowei said: "I have heard of an ancient ruler who offered a thousand gold pieces to find a thousand-li horse, but after three years he could not obtain one. A palace official said to the ruler: "Let me go and seek it for you." The ruler sent him off. After three months, he found a thousand-li horse, but the horse was already dead. He bought its head for five hundred gold pieces and returned to report to the ruler. The ruler became very angry and said: "What I sought was a living horse; what use is a dead one, and why should five hundred gold pieces be spent on it?" The palace official replied: "For a dead horse you are willing to pay five hundred gold, then how much more for a living one? All under heaven will believe Your Majesty is capable of buying horses; the horses will come now." Thus, within less than a year, three thousand-li horses arrived. Now if Your Majesty truly wishes to attract capable men, you should begin with me, Master Guowei; If I am given a position, then how much more those who are superior to me? Would they not come from afar, even thousands of li away?"

Thus King Zhao built a palace for Guowei and treated him as his teacher. Yue Yi came from Wei, Zou Yan from Qi, Ju Xin from Zhao; scholars vied to gather in Yan. King of Yan mourned the dead and comforted the living, sharing joys and hardships with his people. liğinde Twelve years later, the state of Yan became prosperous and wealthy; soldiers were happy, carefree, and eager for battle. Thus he appointed Yue Yi as supreme general, and together with Qin, Chu, and the three Jin states, they plotted to attack Qi. The Qi army was defeated, and King Min fled abroad. Yan's troops alone pursued the retreating forces as far as Linzi, seizing all of Qi's treasures and burning its palaces and ancestral temples. The only cities in Qi that did not fall were Ju and Jiimo.


### Qi Attacks Song: Song is in Distress

Qi attacked Song, and Song was in a hurry. Su Dai then sent a letter to King Zhao of Yan, saying: "Being listed among the great powers with ten thousand chariots yet sending hostages to Qi, one's name is humbled and authority is light. Qin and Qi assisted them in attacking Song, causing the people hardship and actual expense. Destroying Song, ravaging northern Huabei of Chu, enriching great Qi, making enemies of powerful states—this is a sign of national weakness. These three actions are all great defeats for the state, yet you carry them out, intending to eliminate dangers and gain trust from Qi. But Qi has not placed trust in you, and instead harbors even greater suspicion of Yan. Then your service to Qi is misplaced. The people are burdened and resources wasted, yet no achievement of even a foot or inch is gained. Destroying Song only enriches the enemy, and future generations will bear its calamity. You added northern Huabei of Song to Qi, strengthening a great power with ten thousand chariots; and now Qi annexes it, which is equivalent to increasing the strength of Qi by one. The northern Yi territory spans seven hundred li, and adding Lu and Wei to it—this is what is called a great power with ten thousand charioteers. If Qi annexes them, this would be increasing the strength of two Qis. Even with the strength of one Qi, Yan is still unable to resist; now you are facing three Qis against Yan, and the calamity will surely be great.

"Nevertheless, I have heard that wise men undertaking affairs can transform misfortune into fortune and turn failure into success. The people of Qi dyed plain cloth purple, yet sold it at ten times the price. King Goujian of Yue took refuge at Kuaiji, and later destroyed Wu to dominate the world. These are all examples of transforming misfortune into fortune, turning failure into achievement. Now, if Your Majesty wishes to transform misfortune into fortune and convert failure into success? Then there is no better course than to keep Qi at a distance while showing it great respect, sending envoys to make an alliance with the Zhou royal house, and burning all Qin diplomatic seals from around the world, making this agreement: 'The best strategy is to destroy Qin; the next best is to be its honored guest.' Qin would hold its guests in suspense, waiting for a chance to break them, and King of Qin must worry about this. For five generations, Qin has forged alliances with the feudal lords; now it is beneath Qi; lington King of Qin's ambition—if he can exhaust Qi, would not hesitate to achieve success by capturing a single capital city. Yet why does Your Majesty not send a commoner to persuade Qin with the argument that Qi is exhausted, saying to King of Qin: "Yan and Zhao destroyed Song, enriched Qi, and submitted to it beneath them—not because they benefited from it, but despite knowing it was disadvantageous, they were compelled by circumstances. Why is this so? Because they do not trust King of Qin. Now, why does Your Majesty not appoint someone trustworthy to receive Yan and Zhao? If Lord Jingyang or Lord Gaoling were to precede Yan and Zhao, and in the event of any change in Qin, they could be used as hostages—then Yan and Zhao would trust Qin. Qin becomes the Western Emperor, Zhao the Central Emperor, and Yan the Northern Emperor; they are established as the Three Emperors to command the feudal lords. If Han and Wei do not obey, then Qin will attack them. If Qi does not comply, Yan and Zhao will attack it. Who in the world would dare to disobey? When all under heaven submit and obey, we can then drive Han and Wei to attack Qi, saying that the sorrow is reclaiming Song's territory and returning northern Huabei of Chu. Reclaiming Song's land and restoring northern Huabei to Chu are benefits shared by Yan and Zhao. Establishing the Three Emperors is a common aspiration of Yan and Zhao. If they actually gain what they desire in benefits, and obtain the name of their aspirations, then Yan and Zhao abandoning Qi would be like discarding worn-out shoes. Now, if Your Majesty does not bring in Yan and Zhao, then the hegemony of Qi will surely succeed. Before reaching King Qin, King Qin was startled and sprang up, pulling away and tearing his sleeve. He drew his sword, but the sword was long and remained stuck in its scabbard. At that moment of urgency, the sword was lodged firmly, so it could not be drawn immediately. Jing Ke chased after King Qin, who ran around the pillar in retreat. The court officials were shocked and taken by surprise, completely losing their composure. According to Qin law, ministers attending in the palace could not carry weapons of any size. The attendants holding weapons were all stationed below the hall and could not ascend without an imperial decree. In the moment of crisis, it was too late to summon the guards below. Therefore, Jing Ke pursued King Qin, and in their panic they had no means to strike him down, so they merely rushed forward with bare hands to grapple with Jing Ke. At that time, the imperial physician Xia Wuju struck Jing Ke with the case he was carrying for medicine. As King Qin ran around the pillar, in a panic and not knowing what to do, those nearby shouted: "Your Majesty, draw your sword!" "Your Majesty, draw your sword!" King Qin then drew his sword and struck Jing Ke, severing his left thigh. Jing Ke fell to the ground; he then took up his dagger and lunged at King Qin, but missed and struck a pillar instead. King Qin struck Jing Ke again, wounding him in eight places. Jing Ke, realizing that his mission had failed, leaned against a pillar and laughed, sitting with legs spread wide in defiance as he cursed: "The reason the matter did not succeed was because I intended to take you alive and force an agreement from you, so as to fulfill my promise to Crown Prince Dan." After the attendants moved forward and beheaded Jing Ke, King Qin remained dazed for a long time. Then he assessed rewards for those who had contributed to the success of the matter and punishments for those guilty of crimes, with distinctions made among them. He bestowed two hundred jin of gold upon Xia Wuju, saying: "Wuju loves me and struck Jing Ke with his medicine case."

Thus, Qin became greatly enraged at Yan and dispatched more troops to Zhao, ordering General Wang Jian's army to attack Yan. In October they captured the city of Ji in Yan. King Xi of Yan, Crown Prince Dan and others all led their elite troops eastward to take refuge in Liaodong. The Qin general Li Xin pursued King Yan. In desperation, the king followed a plan suggested by Wang Jia of Dai and killed Crown Prince Dan, intending to offer him as tribute to Qin. Qin then advanced its troops once more to attack them. After five years, Qin finally destroyed the state of Yan and captured King Xi of Yan. Qin unified all under heaven.
