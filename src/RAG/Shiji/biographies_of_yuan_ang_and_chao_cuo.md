# Biographies of Yuan Ang and Chao Cuo

## 1

Yuan Ang was a native of Chu, courtesy name Si. His father had previously been a bandit and was later relocated to Chuling. During the reign of Empress Dowager Gao, Yuan Ang once served as a sheren (attendant) for Lu Lu. When Emperor Xiaowen ascended the throne, Yuan Ang's elder brother Kuai recommended Yuan Ang to serve as a Zhonglang (Middle Attendant).

## 2

Marquis of Jiang Hou served as prime minister. After the court session ended, he hurriedly left, appearing very pleased with himself. The emperor treated him with great respect and often personally saw him off. Yuan Ang stepped forward and said, "Your Majesty, what kind of man do you think the prime minister is?" The emperor replied, "A pillar of the state." Yuan Ang said, "Marquis of Jiang Hou is what is called a meritorious subject, not a pillar of the state. A true pillar of the state remains loyal when the sovereign is present and also when he is absent." During the time of Empress Dowager Lü, the Lü clan held power, arbitrarily appointing kings as prime ministers, and the Liu clan was nearly extinguished like a thread. At that time, Marquis of Jiang Hou held the position of Taiwei (commander-in-chief), in charge of military authority, yet he was unable to correct this situation. After Empress Dowager Lü died, the ministers jointly rebelled against the Lü clan. The Taiwei controlled the military and happened to achieve success in this matter; thus he was called a meritorious subject but not a true pillar of the state. The prime minister now shows an arrogant attitude toward you, Your Majesty. "Your Majesty's humility and yielding have caused the subject to be disrespectful; I secretly consider this unacceptable for Your Majesty." In later court sessions, the emperor became more solemn, and the prime minister grew increasingly respectful. Later, Marquis of Jiang Hou glared at Yuan Ang and said, "I was on good terms with your elder brother; now you have defamed me in court!" Yuan Ang then refused to apologize.

## 3

When Marquis of Jiang Hou was dismissed from his post and returned to his fief, the people of the state submitted a petition accusing him of rebellion. He was summoned and detained in the Qing Shi (a place for holding suspects), and none of the imperial clan's nobles dared speak on his behalf, except Yuan Ang, who publicly declared that Marquis of Jiang Hou was innocent. Marquis of Jiang Hou was released, and Yuan Ang had played a significant role in this. Thereafter, Marquis of Jiang Hou formed a close friendship with Yuan Ang.

## 4

When the Prince of Huainan Lai Wang visited court, he killed Pi Yang Hou and behaved with great arrogance in his residence. Yuan Ang advised, "If the feudal lords become overly arrogant, problems will surely arise; it would be appropriate to reduce their territories." The emperor did not follow this advice. Prince of Huainan Wang became even more arrogant and lawless. When the plot of Jipu Hou Chai Wu's crown prince to rebel was discovered, during the investigation it involved Prince of Huainan. Prince of Huainan was summoned, and the emperor then exiled him to Shu, sending him off in a covered carriage. At that time Yuan Ang was serving as Zhonglangjiang (Commander of the Middle Attendants), and he advised, "Your Majesty has long been indulgent toward Prince of Huainan, not restraining him slightly, which led to this situation. Now you are suddenly treating him harshly." "Prince of Huainan is a stubborn man. If he were to die suddenly on the road, as if struck down by mist and dew, Your Majesty would end up being accused of not having enough space in your vast empire to tolerate him, bearing the name of one who has killed his own brother. How could this be?" The emperor did not listen and proceeded with the order regardless.

## 5

When Prince of Huainan reached Yong, he fell ill and died. Upon hearing this news, the emperor stopped eating and wept bitterly with great sorrow. Yuan Ang entered, kowtowed, and requested to be punished for his crime. The emperor said, "This has happened because I did not follow your advice." Yuan Ang replied, "Your Majesty should forgive yourself. This is a past matter; how could it be regretted now!" "Moreover, Your Majesty has three virtuous deeds surpassing the world; this incident is not enough to tarnish your reputation." The emperor asked, "What are these three virtuous deeds that surpass the world?" Yuan Ang said, "When Your Majesty was in Dai, the Empress Dowager once fell ill. For three years, Your Majesty did not close your eyes to sleep, nor remove your clothes; no soup or medicine was administered unless it had first been tasted by Your Majesty." "Even Ceng Shen, a commoner, found this difficult to achieve. Now, as an emperor, Your Majesty personally practices such filial devotion; your virtue far surpasses that of Ceng Shen." "When the Lü clan was in power and ministers held absolute authority, yet Your Majesty traveled from Dai by six relays of carriages through perilous waters; even the bravery of Bin Yu could not match that of Your Majesty." "When Your Majesty arrived at Dai Di, you twice refused the imperial throne while facing westward and three times declined it again after turning south to face the court." "Xu You once declined the throne, yet Your Majesty has five times refused the imperial position; this surpasses Xu You by four instances." "Moreover, Your Majesty exiled Prince of Huainan in the hope that hardship would reform his character and lead him to repent. It was due to the negligence of the officials responsible for his protection that he fell ill and died." At this, the emperor was reassured and said, "Then what should be done?" Yuan Ang replied, "Prince of Huainan had three sons; their fates now rest in Your Majesty's hands." Thus Emperor Wen posthumously enfeoffed his three sons as kings. Yuan Ang thereby gained great prestige in the imperial court.

## 6

Yuan Ang often spoke with a sense of grand principle and boldness. The eunuch Zhao Tong, favored by the emperor on many occasions, often resented Yuan Ang; this troubled Yuan Ang greatly. Yuan Ang's nephew, Zhong, who served as a Changshi Qi (attendant horseman) and held an imperial token for authority, rode alongside him and advised Yuan Ang, saying, "You should fight with him in court, publicly humiliate him, so that his slander will be disregarded." When Emperor Xiaowen went out, Zhao Tong served as his companion rider. Yuan Ang knelt before the carriage and said, "I have heard that those who ride with the Son of Heaven in a six-foot chariot are all heroes and outstanding men from throughout the land." "Now, although Han may be lacking in talent, how can Your Majesty ride with a man who is but a leftover of execution tools!" At this, the emperor laughed and ordered Zhao Tong to descend from the carriage. Zhao Tong wept as he stepped down from the carriage.

## 7

Emperor Wen was ascending from Badingle, and wished to gallop westward down a steep slope. Yuan Ang rode alongside him, grasping the reins of the carriage. The emperor asked, "Is General afraid?" Yuan Ang replied, "I have heard that a son worth a thousand gold pieces does not sit near the eaves where rain might fall, and one worth a hundred gold pieces does not ride on the crossbar of a carriage. A sage ruler does not take risks in pursuit of good fortune." "Now, Your Majesty is driving six steeds at full speed down a steep hill. If the horses were to startle and the carriage overturn, even if Your Majesty considers his own life lightly, what would become of the High Temple and Empress Dowager?" The emperor then stopped.

## 8

The emperor visited Shanglin Garden, and Empress and Shen Fu Ren accompanied him. While in the palace, they often sat on the same mat together. When it came time to sit down, the officer in charge of arranging the seating laid out the mats; Yuan Ang pulled back Shen Fu Ren's mat. Shen Fu Ren became angry and refused to sit. The emperor was also angered, stood up, and went into the palace. Yuan Ang then stepped forward to advise, saying, "I have heard that when there is order between respect and subordination, harmony exists between superiors and inferiors." "Now that Your Majesty has already established an empress, Shen Fu Ren is merely a concubine. How can a concubine sit on equal terms with her mistress?" This is precisely why the distinction between respect and subordination has been lost. "Moreover, if Your Majesty favors her, you may bestow generous gifts upon her." What Your Majesty does for Shen Fu Ren is precisely what brings misfortune to her. "Has Your Majesty never heard of the 'human pig'?" At this, the emperor was appeased and summoned Shen Fu Ren to speak with her. Shen Fu Ren then gave Yuan Ang fifty catties of gold as a gift.

## 9

However, because Yuan Ang often gave forthright advice, he could not remain in court for long and was transferred to serve as Duwei (military commissioner) of Longxi. He treated his soldiers with kindness and compassion; the soldiers all vied to die for him. He was later promoted to prime minister of Qi. When he was transferred to serve as prime minister of Wu, before departing, Zhong said to Yuan Ang, "Prince of Wu has long been arrogant and his state is full of treachery." "If you now rashly wish to investigate and punish them, they will either report you to the throne or stab you with a sharp sword." "The south is low-lying and damp; if you can drink daily without concern, occasionally advise the prince to refrain from rebellion. That would be sufficient." "If you do this, you might just manage to escape danger." Yuan Ang followed Zhong's advice, and Prince of Wu treated Yuan Ang with great favor.

## 10

When Yuan Ang returned home on leave, he encountered Chancellor Shentu Jia along the road. He dismounted to pay respects, and the chancellor bowed from his carriage in return to Yuan Ang. Yuan Ang returned home, feeling ashamed of his subordinates, and went to the chancellor's residence to pay a visit, seeking an audience with the chancellor. The chancellor took a long time before receiving him. Yuan Ang then knelt and said, "I would like to speak in private." The chancellor said, "If what you have to say concerns official business, go to the relevant department and discuss it with the chief clerk and assistants; I will then submit a report." "If it is personal, however, I do not accept private conversations." Yuan Ang immediately knelt and said, "You are the prime minister. Do you think yourself more capable than Chen Ping or Marquis of Jiang Hou?" The chancellor replied, "I am not as capable." Yuan Ang said, "Good. Since you yourself admit to being less capable," "Chen Ping and Marquis of Jiang Hou assisted Emperor Gaozu in stabilizing the empire, served as generals and prime ministers, and eliminated the Lü clan to preserve the Liu dynasty; "you were merely a military officer in charge of archers, promoted to command a unit, and gradually accumulated merit until becoming the governor of Huaiyang. You have no achievements from brilliant strategies or victories in sieges or open battles." "Moreover, when Your Majesty came from Dai, every morning at court sessions, whenever officials submitted memorials, you never stopped the carriage but listened to their words. If what they said was not useful, it was set aside; if acceptable, it was adopted. You always praised them." "Why is that?" "It is to attract the virtuous scholars and officials of the realm." "Every day, Your Majesty hears what you have never heard before and understands what you did not know; your wisdom grows increasingly sage-like;" "but now you are closing the mouths of people throughout the land, causing them to remain silent and making yourself grow increasingly ignorant day by day." "To hold a foolish prime minister accountable before a sage ruler—your misfortune will not be far behind." The chancellor then bowed twice and said, "Jia is but an ignorant man who did not realize this. I am fortunate to receive your instruction, General." He invited Yuan Ang in and seated him together, treating him as a distinguished guest.

## 11

Yuan Ang had always disliked Chao Cuo; whenever he saw that Chao Cuo was seated in a place, Yuan Ang would leave. When Yuan Ang sat down, Chao Cuo also left: the two had never spoken together in the same hall. When Emperor Xiaowen died and Emperor Xiangjing ascended the throne, Chao Cuo became Yushi Daifu (Minister of Justice) and ordered officials to investigate Yuan Ang for accepting gifts from Prince of Wu. Yuan Ang was found guilty and sentenced accordingly; however, an imperial edict pardoned him and reduced his status to that of a commoner.

## 12

When Wu and Chu rebelled, upon hearing the news, Chao Cuo said to his officials: "Yuan Ang received a great deal of money from Prince Wu, concealed it deliberately, and claimed that he would not rebel." "Now they have indeed rebelled; I should request to punish Yuan Ang for knowing about their plans." The official replied, "Before the matter was uncovered, punishing him could cut off any leads. "Now that the army is heading west, what benefit would it bring to punish him?" lø Yuan Ang should not be suspected of having any conspiracy." Chao Cuo hesitated and had not yet made a decision. Someone informed Yuan Ang of the situation, and fearing for his safety, Yuan Ang met with Dou Ying at night. He explained to him why Wu had rebelled and expressed his wish to appear before the emperor in person to explain the circumstances orally. Dou Ying entered the palace and reported this to the emperor, who then summoned Yuan Ang for an audience. Chao Cuo was present before, but when Yuan Ang requested that the attendants be dismissed and a private conversation granted, Chao Cuo left. He remained deeply resentful. Yuan Ang fully explained the reasons why Wu rebelled, and because of Chao Cuo's actions, he suggested that only by swiftly executing Chao Cuo to apologize to Wu could the Wu forces be persuaded to withdraw. The details of this conversation are fully recorded in the account of events related to Wu. Yuan Ang was appointed as Taichang, and Dou Ying became Grand General. The two had always been on good terms with each other. When Wu rebelled, Eminent elders from the various mausoleums and virtuous officials in Chang'an vied to attach themselves to the two men, with hundreds of carriages following them daily.

## 13

After Chao Cuo had been executed, Yuan Ang, in his capacity as Taichang (Minister of Ceremonies), was sent on a mission to Wu. Prince Wu wanted to appoint him as a general, but Yuan Ang refused. Wu wished to kill him and dispatched one Duwei with 500 men to surround and guard Yuan Ang's forces in the camp. When Yuan Ang was still the prime minister of Wu, he once had a subordinate official who stole and loved one of his attendants. Yuan Ang knew about it but did not expose him, treating him as before. When someone informed the subordinate official that "the master knows you have an affair with his attendant," he fled and returned home. Yuan Ang personally chased after him, caught up to him, and then gave the attendant to him as a gift, restoring him once again as his subordinate official. When Yuan Ang was being guarded in Wu, this subordinate official happened to serve as the captain and military officer responsible for guarding Yuan Ang. He then took all of Yuan Ang's belongings and prepared two stones (about 120 liters) of pure strong wine. On a cold day, when the soldiers were hungry and thirsty, they drank the wine and became drunk; the soldiers at the southwest corner of the camp all lay down. At night, the military officer woke Yuan Ang up and said, "You may now leave. Prince Wu has ordered your execution tomorrow morning." Yuan Ang did not believe him and asked, "Who are you?" The military officer said, "I am the former subordinate official who once stole your attendant." Yuan Ang was greatly surprised and thanked him, saying; "You are fortunate to have family. I do not wish to burden you." The military officer replied, "You just go ahead; I will also flee and leave my family behind. How can this be a concern for you?" He then cut through the ropes, leading Yuan Ang along with the drunk soldiers through a tunnel to escape. The military officer parted from Yuan Ang, who took off the jade seal and kept it in his robe. He used a staff as support and walked on foot for about seven or eight li. At dawn, he encountered cavalry of Liang; they galloped away, and Yuan Ang returned to report.

## 14

After Wu and Chu were defeated, the emperor replaced Prince Pinglu Hou Li of the former Yuan Wang's descendants as King of Chu. Yuan Ang was appointed prime minister (xiang) of Chu. He once submitted a memorial with certain suggestions, but they were not adopted. While Yuan Ang was ill and had been dismissed from office, he stayed at home and mingled with the common people in his neighborhood. He walked about with them, betting on cockfights and dog races. Luoyang Ju Meng once visited Yuan Ang, who treated him kindly. A wealthy man from Anling said to Yuan Ang: "I have heard that Ju Meng is a reckless and lawless person. Why does General associate with him?" Yuan Ang replied, "Although Ju Meng is a reckless person, when his mother died, over a thousand carriages were sent by guests to attend her funeral. In this regard, he surpasses many others." Moreover, in times of crisis, such people can be of help to anyone. "In a moment of sudden crisis, if someone knocks on your door, they will not use kinship as an excuse or cite life and death to refuse. The people the world looks up to in such times are only Ji Xin and Ju Meng." "Now, you often travel with just a few attendants. In case of an emergency, would that be enough to rely on?" He scolded the wealthy man and refused any further contact with him. When other officials heard of this, they all praised Yuan Ang highly.

## 15

Although Yuan Ang was living at home, Emperor Xiangjing often sent people to consult him for advice and strategies. Prince Liang wanted to request the position of heir apparent; Yuan Ang advised against it, and later this matter was dropped. Because of this, Prince Liang resented Yuan Ang and once sent assassins to kill him. The assassins arrived in Guanzhong and inquired about Yuan Ang; all the people they asked praised him highly, with no one holding back their words. They then met Yuan Ang and said, "We were hired by Prince Liang with money to assassinate you. But you are a man of virtue; we cannot bring ourselves to kill you. "There have been more than ten groups sent to assassinate you before—be prepared!" Yuan Ang felt uneasy and, troubled by the strange occurrences at home, went to Baise's residence to consult a diviner. On his way back, assassins sent later by Prince Liang indeed ambushed and killed Yuan Ang outside the Guomen Gate of Anling.

## 16

Chao Cuo was a native of Yingchuan. He studied the doctrines of Shen Buhai and Shang Yang on legalism and names at the school of Zhang Huixian in Zhi, where he was a fellow student with Luoyang's Song Meng and Liu Li. He served as Taichang Zhangu due to his literary talents.

## 17

Chao Cuo was by nature sharp, upright, and severe in judgment. During the reign of Emperor Xiaowen, there was no one in the realm who studied the Book of Documents (Shangshu). It was said that Fu Sheng from Jinan, an old scholar who had once served as a doctor under the former Qin dynasty, specialized in this text. He was over ninety years old and too frail to be summoned; thus, the emperor issued an edict ordering Taichang to send officials to go to him and learn it. Taichang sent Chao Cuo to study the Book of Documents under Fu Sheng's tutelage. After returning, he submitted proposals on matters deemed beneficial and appropriate, citing passages from the text in his arguments. The emperor issued an edict appointing him as Tai Zi She Ren (attendant to the crown prince), Men Dafu, and Jia Ling. Because of his eloquence, he gained favor with the Crown Prince, who referred to him at home as "the brain in a bag." On several occasions during Emperor Xiaowen's reign, he submitted memorials discussing the reduction of feudal lords' power and proposing revisions to laws and decrees. He submitted dozens of memorials, but Emperor Xiaowen did not act on them; however, he was impressed by his talent and promoted him to Zhong Dafu. At that time, the Crown Prince favored Chao Cuo's strategies, while many of the great meritorious ministers such as Yuan Ang strongly disapproved of him.

## 18

When Emperor Xiangjing ascended to the throne, he appointed Chao Cuo as Neishi (Minister in Charge of Internal Affairs). Chao Cuo often requested private audiences to discuss matters, and the emperor always listened. His favor surpassed that of all nine senior ministers, and he revised many laws and decrees. Chancellor Shentu Jia was inwardly displeased but lacked the means to harm him effectively. The Neishi's office was located in the courtyard of the Temple of the Supreme Ancestor. Its gate faced east, which was inconvenient; thus, Chao Cuo dug two gates facing south and broke through part of the temple wall to connect them. Chancellor Shentu Jia heard about this, became greatly enraged, and intended to use this as an offense in a memorial requesting Chao Cuo's execution. Chao Cuo learned of this and immediately requested a private audience at night, fully explaining the situation to the emperor. When Chancellor Shentu Jia submitted his report on official business, he took the opportunity to accuse Chao Cuo of 擅自 (sui zan) digging through the temple wall to build a gate and requested that he be handed over to Tingwei for execution. The emperor said, "This is not the temple wall but merely a partition in the courtyard; it does not warrant legal punishment." Chancellor Shentu Jia apologized. After the court session ended, he angrily told his chief clerk: "I should have executed him first and then reported it. Instead, I requested permission first and was outwitted by this child. This is a grave mistake." Chancellor Shentu Jia soon fell ill and died. Because of this, Chao Cuo grew even more powerful and influential.

## 19

He was promoted to Yushi Daifu (Ministry of Justice) and requested the emperor to investigate the crimes and misconduct of the feudal lords, proposing to reduce their territories and reclaim their outlying commanderies. When the memorial was submitted, the emperor ordered the senior officials, marquises, and members of the imperial clan to gather for a discussion. None dared to oppose it, except Dou Ying, who argued against it; thus, a rift developed between him and Chao Cuo. Chao Cuo revised thirty chapters of the legal code, and all the feudal lords loudly protested against him. Chao Cuo's father heard about this and came from Yingchuan to him, saying: "The emperor has just ascended the throne. You are now in power and handling affairs, yet you encroach upon and reduce the territories of the feudal lords, separating their close relatives. Many people speak ill of you and harbor resentment toward you—why is that?" Chao Cuo replied, "That is indeed true. If we do not act this way, the Son of Heaven will not be respected, and the ancestral temples will remain in danger." Chao Cuo's father said, "The Liu clan may be secure, but the Chao family is in danger. I will leave you and return home!" He then drank poison and died, saying: "I cannot bear to witness disaster befall my family." Ten days later, the seven states of Wu and Chu indeed rebelled, using Chao Cuo's name as their pretext for rebellion. When Dou Ying and Yuan Ang advised the emperor, he ordered Chao Cuo to be executed in his court robes at Dongshi (Eastern Market).

## 20

After Chao Cuo's death, Ye Zha Puye Deng Gong served as a captain and was appointed general to fight against the armies of Wu and Chu. Upon his return, he submitted a report on military affairs and paid an audience with the emperor. The emperor asked him: "On your way back, did you hear that Chao Cuo had died—did the Wu and Chu forces cease their rebellion?" Deng Gong replied, "Prince Wu had been preparing for rebellion for decades. His anger over the reduction of his territory and using Chao Cuo's execution as a pretext does not mean that his true intent was against Chao Cuo himself. Moreover, I fear that scholars throughout the realm will now hold their tongues and dare no longer speak out!" The emperor asked, "Why is that?" Deng Gong said: "Chao Cuo was concerned that the feudal lords were too powerful and could not be controlled, so he requested to reduce their territories in order to strengthen the capital. This would have been a benefit for generations to come. Now, just as the plan was beginning to be implemented, he was suddenly executed. This silences loyal ministers within and provides an opportunity for revenge by the feudal lords outside. I secretly consider this unwise on Your Majesty's part." At this, Emperor Jing was silent for a long time and said: "Your words are well spoken. I too regret it." He then appointed Deng Gong as the Zhongwei of Chengyang.

## 21

Deng Gong was a native of Chenggu and known for his many ingenious strategies. During the Jianyuan period, when the emperor sought virtuous and capable men, the ministers recommended Deng Gong. At that time, Deng Gong had been dismissed from office but was then appointed directly to a position among the Nine Ministers. After one year, he again resigned on grounds of illness and returned home. His son Zhang became prominent among the nobles for his study of Huanglao philosophy.

## 22

Grand Historian said: Yuan Ang, although not particularly fond of learning, was good at adapting to circumstances. With a kind heart as his foundation, he acted with noble principles and spoke passionately. He encountered the early reign of Emperor Wendi, whose policies coincidentally aligned well with his own inclinations. At that time, circumstances changed; and when the issue of Wu and Chu arose, although his advice was accepted, it did not ultimately lead to success. His love for reputation and pride in his own virtue eventually led to his downfall through the very name he cherished. When Chao Cuo was a family official, he often made suggestions that were not adopted; Later, he seized power and made many changes on his own initiative. When the feudal lords rose in rebellion, instead of urgently trying to correct the situation and rescue the state, he sought private revenge. As a result, he lost his life. The saying goes: "To change ancient ways and disrupt established norms will either lead to death or ruin." Was this not exactly what happened to men like Chao Cuo!
