# Hereditary House of Chancellor Chen

## 1

Chen Chengxiang Ping was a native of Huyou Xiang, Yangwu. When he was young, his family was poor; he loved reading and had thirty mu of farmland, living alone with his elder brother Bo. Bo often tilled the fields, allowing Ping to travel and study. Ping was a tall man with handsome features. Someone once asked Chen Ping, "With poverty, what food have you eaten to become so fat?" His sister-in-law envied the fact that Ping did not attend to household affairs or property, saying, "He eats nothing but bran and husks." "To have a younger brother like this is worse than having none at all." Bo heard of it and drove his wife out, abandoning her.

## 2

When Ping grew up to the age where he could marry, wealthy families were unwilling to give him their daughters in marriage, and poor families also brought shame upon Ping. After some time, there was a wealthy man in Huyou named Zhang Fu; Zhang Fu's granddaughter had been married five times and each of her husbands died soon after, so no one dared to marry her. Ping wanted to marry her. When there was a funeral in the town, Ping, being poor, served as an assistant mourner, arriving early and leaving late to help out. Zhang Fu saw him at the funeral site and was particularly impressed by Ping's imposing presence, so Ping also stayed until last because of this. Fu followed Ping to his home, where he found it was a poor alley near the city wall; the door was made of worn-out mats, yet there were many tracks of carriages from respected elders outside the gate. Zhang Fu returned home and said to his son Zhong, "I wish to give my granddaughter in marriage to Chen Ping." Zhang Zhong said, "Ping is poor and does not take on responsibilities; the whole county mocks his actions. Why would you give your daughter to him?" Fu said, "Is there truly a person as handsome and fine as Chen Ping who remains poor and lowly forever?" In the end, he gave his daughter to him. Because Ping was poor, they borrowed money for the dowry and provided funds for wine and meat to welcome the bride into the family. Fu warned his granddaughter, "Do not because of poverty act carelessly toward others. Treat your elder brother Bo as you would a father and treat your sister-in-law as you would a mother." After Ping married Zhang's granddaughter, his resources became more abundant, and the scope of his travels expanded daily.

## 3

During a local community sacrifice in the village, Ping served as the butcher, dividing the meat very evenly. The elders said, "Excellent! This is how Chen Ruzi serves as a butcher!" Ping said, "Alas, if I were to govern the world, it would also be divided as evenly as this meat!"

## 4

Chen She rose up and became king of Chen. He sent Zhou Shi to conquer the territory of Wei, establishing Wei Jiu as King of Wei, where they fought against the Qin army at Linji. Chen Ping had already previously bid farewell to his elder brother Bo and joined a group of young men who went to serve King Wei Jiu at Linji. King Wei appointed him as Tai Pu (Minister of the Imperial Stables). When his advice to King Wei was not heeded, and someone slandered him, Chen Ping fled away.

## 5

After some time, Xiang Yu advanced to the He River area and Chen Ping went to submit to him, following him into battle against the Qin forces. Xiang Yu bestowed upon Ping the title of Qing (a rank of nobility). When Xiang Yu was made King of Pengcheng in the east, Han Wang returned to pacify the Three Qins and moved eastward, while the King of Yin rebelled against Chu. Xiang Yu then appointed Ping as Xinwu Jun, commanding the followers of King Wei Jiu who were in Chu to go and attack, subduing the King of Yin before returning. Xiang Wang sent Xiang Han to bestow upon Ping the title of Duwei and granted him twenty yi in gold. Not long after, Han Wang captured Yin (King). Xiang Wang was furious and intended to execute the generals and officials who had pacified Yin. Fearing execution, Chen Ping sealed up his gold and seal, sent an envoy to return them to Xiang Wang, while he himself traveled incognito with a staff and sword and fled. Crossing the river, the boatman saw a handsome man traveling alone and suspected him to be an escaped general, thinking there might be gold, jade, or treasures in his possession. He watched him closely, intending to kill Ping. Ping was afraid and so took off his clothes, exposing himself as he helped row the boat; realizing that he had nothing of value, the boatman stopped.

## 6

Ping then arrived at Xiuwu and surrendered to Han forces. Through Wei Wuzhi, he requested an audience with Han Wang, who summoned him in. At that time, Wanshi Jun Fen was the Zhong Juan (attendant) to Han Wang and received Ping's letter of introduction before leading him in for an audience. Ping and six others entered together, and were granted food. The king said, "Enough for now; go to your quarters." Ping said, "I have come here with a matter in mind. What I wish to say cannot be delayed until tomorrow." Thus Han Wang conversed with him and was pleased, asking, "What position did you hold in Chu?" He replied, "I was a Duwei." On that day, Han Wang appointed Ping as Duwei, making him his chariot companion and putting him in charge of the imperial guards. The generals all 哗然 (were greatly surprised), saying, "Our great king has gained a defector from Chu in one day; we do not yet know his abilities, and yet he is immediately allowed to ride with him, even being put in charge of supervising the experienced officers!" Han Wang heard this and became even more favorably disposed toward Ping. He then joined Han forces in their eastern campaign against Xiang Wang. When they reached Pengcheng, they were defeated by the Chu forces. They retreated and gathered scattered troops to Yingyang, appointing Ping as Ya Jiang (Deputy General), under the command of King Han Xin, stationed at Guangwu.

## 7

Jiang Hou, Guan Ying and others all slandered Chen Ping, saying, "Although Ping is a handsome man, he is like jade on the outside of a hat; there may not be much substance within. I have heard that when Ping lived at home, he seduced his sister-in-law; he was unwelcome in Wei and fled to Chu; after returning to Chu, he did not find favor there either, and then fled again to Han. Now Your Majesty has given him a high position and appointed him as the commander of the imperial guard. I have heard that Ping accepts bribes from the generals; those who give more gold receive favorable positions, while those who give less are given poor ones. Ping is a fickle and treacherous minister; I hope Your Majesty will be cautious." Han Wang suspected this and summoned Wei Wuzhi to reprimand him. Wei Wuzhi said, "What I spoke of was his ability; what Your Majesty inquired about is his conduct. Now, if someone has the virtue of Weisheng and Xiaoji but brings no benefit to the outcome of victory or defeat, how would Your Majesty have time to use such a person? While Chu and Han are at odds, I recommend strategists with ingenious plans; what matters is whether their strategies can truly benefit the state. Moreover, how could stealing from a sister-in-law or accepting bribes be sufficient grounds for suspicion?" Han Wang summoned Ping and reprimanded him, saying, "Sir, you served Wei but did not succeed, then went to serve Chu and left; now you follow me again. Is a man of integrity really so fickle?" Ping replied, "I served King Wei, but King Wei did not heed my advice, so I left and went to serve Xiang Wang. Xiang Wang could not trust people; those he entrusted and favored were either members of the Xiang clan or his wife's brothers. Even if there were outstanding strategists, they could not be employed, so I left Chu. I heard that Your Majesty is capable of employing people, so I returned to you." I came here with nothing but the clothes on my back; without accepting gold, I would have no means of support. If a loyal minister's plans are indeed worth considering, Your Majesty should employ them; if they are not useful, the gold is still here. I will seal it and return it to the government, then I would be willing to resign." Han Wang apologized and gave him generous rewards, appointing him as Zhong Wei of the Hu Jun (Commander of the Imperial Guard), overseeing all the generals. The generals then dared not speak further.

## 8

Later, the Chu forces launched a fierce attack, cutting off Han's supply route and besieging Han Wang within Yingyang city. After some time, Han Wang became greatly concerned and proposed to cede the area west of Yingyang in order to negotiate peace. Xiang Wang did not agree. Han Wang said to Chen Ping, "The world is in chaos; when will it ever be stabilized?" Chen Ping replied, "Xiang Wang's character is respectful and kind to people; many scholars of integrity and good manners have flocked to him. As for rewarding meritorious service with titles and fiefs, he is stingy about it; thus, scholars also refrain from attaching themselves to him because of this. Now Your Majesty is careless and lacks courtesy, so those scholars with integrity do not come; yet Your Majesty can generously bestow titles and fiefs, so many scholars who are dull-witted, greedy for profit, and shameless also come to Han. If both sides could each eliminate their respective weaknesses and adopt the other's strengths, then with a mere gesture of command, the world would be stabilized. Yet Your Majesty is arrogant and disrespectful toward people, thus unable to attract those of integrity. However, there are those in Chu who can be unsettled. Among Xiang Wang's most loyal and resolute ministers—Yafu, Zhongli Mie, Long Ju, Zhou Yin, and others—there are no more than a few such men. If Your Majesty could truly spend several thousand catties of gold to dispatch spies, sow discord among their ranks, and create doubts in their minds, Xiang Wang is a man who is suspicious by nature and believes slanders; he will surely turn on his own ministers and have them executed. Then Han can raise its troops to attack, and the defeat of Chu is certain." Han Wang thought this was a sound strategy and so issued four thousand catties of yellow gold to Chen Ping, allowing him full discretion in its use without questioning his expenditures.

## 9

Chen Ping then used much of the gold to send spies into the Chu army, publicly declaring that generals such as Zhongli Mie were serving under Xiang Wang and had achieved many victories; yet they still could not receive fiefdoms or be made kings. They wished to unite with Han forces to destroy the Xiang clan and divide their territory among themselves as kings. Xiang Yu indeed began to doubt Zhongli Mie and others. After Xiang Wang became suspicious, he sent envoys to Han. Han Wang prepared a lavish feast with the highest level of sacrificial offerings and invited them in. When he saw the envoys from Chu, Han Wang pretended to be greatly surprised and said, "I thought you were sent by Yafu, but it turns out you are from Xiang Wang!" He then had them taken away and replaced with a meager meal of coarse food to serve the envoys of Chu. The envoys from Chu returned and fully reported this to Xiang Wang. Xiang Wang indeed became greatly suspicious of Yafu. Yafu wanted to quickly capture Yingyang city, but Xiang Wang did not believe him and refused to listen. Yafu heard that Xiang Wang doubted him, so he became furious and said, "The great affairs of the world are already settled; Your Majesty may do as you please! I wish to resign and return home!" Before he could reach Pengcheng, a boil broke out on his back and he died. Chen Ping then sent out two thousand women at night through the eastern gate of Yingyang city. The Chu forces attacked them, and Chen Ping thus led Han Wang to escape by night through the western gate of the city. They then entered Guan Pass, gathered scattered troops, and moved eastward again.

## 10

The following year, the Marquis of Huaiyin defeated Qi, declared himself King of Qi, and sent envoys to inform Han Wang. Han Wang was furious and scolded him, but Chen Ping stepped on Han Wang's foot discreetly. lington Han Wang realized this as well, so he treated the envoys from Qi with great courtesy and had Zhang Zifang finally appoint Xin as King of Qi. Ping was granted the fiefdom of Huyou Xiang. By employing his ingenious strategies and plans, they eventually destroyed Chu. Chen Ping often served as the commander of the imperial guards accompanying Han Wang in subduing Yan Wang Zang Tu.

## 11

In the sixth year of the Han era, someone submitted a petition accusing King of Chu Han Xin of rebellion. Emperor Gao asked his generals, and they replied, "Quickly send troops to dig a pit for this scoundrel." Emperor Gao remained silent. He asked Chen Ping, who firmly declined and said, "What do the generals say?" The emperor fully informed him. Chen Ping said, "Has anyone who submitted a petition saying that Xin is rebelling actually witnessed it?" It was replied, "No one has." He asked, "Does Xin know about it?" It was replied, "No, he does not." Chen Ping said, "Your Majesty, whose elite troops are superior, ours or those of Chu?" The emperor replied, "They cannot be surpassed." Chen Ping asked, "Does Your Majesty have a general who can surpass Han Xin in military ability?" The emperor replied, "No one can match him." Chen Ping said, "Now our troops are not as elite as those of Chu, and our generals cannot match Han Xin. If we raise an army to attack him, this is merely driving him into battle. I secretly consider it dangerous for Your Majesty." The emperor asked, "Then what should be done?" Chen Ping said, "In ancient times, the Son of Heaven would tour and convene feudal lords." "There is Yunmeng in the south. Your Majesty may pretend to travel there, and summon the feudal lords to meet you at Chen." Chen is on the western border of Chu. When Xin hears that the Son of Heaven is touring leisurely, he will certainly come out to meet and pay homage without incident. "When he comes to pay homage, Your Majesty can then capture him. This is merely a matter for one strong man." Emperor Gao considered this reasonable and sent envoys to notify the feudal lords of their meeting at Chen, saying, "I will tour Yunmeng in the south." The emperor then proceeded accordingly. Before they reached Chen, King of Chu Xin indeed met them on the road outside the city. Emperor Gao had already prepared warriors in advance; when he saw Xin arrive, he immediately seized and bound him, placing him on a rear carriage. Xin cried out, "The world is already settled, so I was indeed destined to be boiled!" Emperor Gao turned and said to Xin, "Be quiet!" "You have clearly rebelled!" The warriors bound him with his hands behind his back. They then convened the feudal lords at Chen and completely pacified the territory of Chu. Upon returning to Luoyang, Emperor Gao pardoned Xin and reappointed him as the Marquis of Huaiyin, and together with meritorious ministers, he divided fiefs and sealed their ranks permanently.

## 12

At this time, he also granted Chen Ping a covenant tablet, ensuring that his title would never be extinguished through generations, as Hou of Huyou. Chen Ping declined and said, "This is not my merit." The emperor said, "I employed your strategies and plans to win battles and defeat the enemy. If this is not a meritorious act, then what is it?" Chen Ping said, "Without Wei Wu, how could I have been recommended?" The emperor said; "You may be called a man who does not forget his roots." He then rewarded Wei Wu again. The following year, as commander of the imperial guards, he accompanied the campaign against King Han Xin of Han, who had rebelled, in Dai. They finally reached Pingcheng but were surrounded by the Xiongnu and endured seven days without food. Emperor Gao employed Chen Ping's ingenious strategy, which involved sending envoys to the Xiongnu Chanyu and his wife Yizhi, thereby opening a path out of the encirclement. After Emperor Gao escaped, the details of the strategy were kept secret, and no one in later generations learned about them.

## 13

Emperor Gao passed south of Qu'ni and ascended the city walls, gazing at its large houses and saying, "What a magnificent county!" "I have traveled throughout the world, but I have seen only Luoyang and this place." He turned to ask the imperial censor, "How many households and people are there in Qu'ni?" The official replied, "At the beginning of the Qin dynasty, there were more than 30,000 households. In recent years, with frequent wars and many people fleeing or hiding, only 5,000 are visible now." The emperor then issued an edict to the imperial censor, reappointing Chen Ping as Hou of Quni, granting him full rights to its revenues and revoking his previous fiefdom at Huyou.

## 14

After that, he often served as commander of the imperial guards in campaigns against Chen Xi and Qing Bu. On six occasions, his ingenious strategies proved effective, each time earning him an increase in territory, for a total of six enlargements to his fief. Some of these strategies were kept quite secret and no one in later generations could learn about them.

## 15

After Emperor Gao defeated the army of Qing Bu and returned, he was wounded and traveled slowly back to Chang'an. King Yan Lu Wan rebelled, so the emperor sent Fan Kuai as Chancellor with an army to attack him. After he had departed, someone made a slanderous accusation against Fan Kuai. Emperor Gao was furious and said, "Kuai sees that I am ill and now hopes for my death." Following Chen Ping's plan, he summoned the Marquis of Jiang Hou Zhou Bo beneath his bed and gave him an imperial decree, saying, "Chen Ping, quickly ride in haste to bring Zhou Bo to replace Kuai as general. When you arrive at the army, immediately behead Kuai!" After the two had received the decree, they rode in haste but had not yet reached the army. Along the way, they discussed and said, "Fan Kuai is an old friend of the emperor and has many meritorious deeds. Moreover, he is the husband of Lü Xu, the sister of Empress Dowager Lü. He is both a relative and noble. The emperor wants to behead him out of anger, but may later regret it." "We should rather arrest him and present him before the emperor; let His Majesty decide his fate himself." Before reaching the army, they built an altar and summoned Fan Kuai with a token of authority. Fan Kuai accepted the decree, was immediately bound with his hands behind him and placed in a cage cart, sent by relay to Chang'an. They then ordered Marquis of Jiang Hou Zhou Bo to take command and lead troops to pacify the rebellious counties in Yan.

## 16

While traveling, Chen Ping heard of Emperor Gao's death and feared that Empress Dowager Lü or Lü Xu might spread slander and become angry. He therefore rode in haste to leave first. On the way, he encountered imperial envoys who ordered Chen Ping to join Guan Ying in garrisoning at Yingyang. Chen Ping accepted the decree and immediately rode back to the palace, where he mourned with great sorrow before presenting his report in front of the emperor's funeral bier. Empress Dowager Lü felt compassion for him and said, "You have labored enough; go out and rest." Chen Ping feared slander might follow, so he firmly requested to remain as a night guard within the palace. The empress dowager then appointed him as Langzhong Ling and said, "Teach and advise Xiao Hui." After this, the slander from Lü Xu could not proceed. When Fan Kuai arrived, he was pardoned and his title and fief were restored.

## 17

In the sixth year of Emperor Xiao Hui's reign, Chancellor Cao Can died. An Guo Hou Wang Ling was appointed as Right Chancellor and Chen Ping as Left Chancellor.

## 18

Wang Ling was originally from Pei. In his early days, he had been a local strongman of the county. When Emperor Gaozu was obscure and not yet famous, he treated Wang Ling as an elder brother. Wang Ling lacked literary refinement, was hot-tempered, and loved to speak bluntly. When Emperor Gaozu rose in Pei and advanced as far as Xianyang, Wang Ling had also gathered a band of several thousand men to reside in Nanyang, but refused to follow Duke Pei. When Han Wang returned to attack Xiang Ji, Wang Ling then submitted his troops to the Han forces. Xiang Yu took Wang Ling's mother and placed her in his camp; when envoys were sent to Wang Ling, they seated his mother facing east, hoping to lure him over. Wang Ling's mother privately gave the envoys a message and wept, saying, "Tell my son Ling to carefully serve Han Wang. Han Wang is an upright man; do not because of your old servant hold divided loyalties." "I will die to see the envoys off." She then committed suicide by drawing a sword. Xiang Wang was furious and boiled Wang Ling's mother to death. Wang Ling eventually followed Han Wang in pacifying the world. Because he was on good terms with Yong Chi, who was an enemy of Emperor Gao, and because Wang Ling originally had no intention of following Emperor Gao, his ennoblement came late; he was made the Marquis of An Guo.

## 19

After becoming Right Chancellor as Hou of An Guo, two years later Emperor Xiao Hui died. Empress Dowager Gao wanted to appoint the Lü clan as kings and asked Wang Ling, who replied, "No, it cannot be done." She then asked Chen Ping, and Chen Ping said, "Yes, it can be done." Empress Dowager Lü was angry. She pretended to promote Wang Ling to the position of Taifu (Grand Tutor) for the emperor, but in reality did not rely on him at all. Wang Ling was angry and claimed illness to resign from office. He closed his doors and never again attended court, dying seven years later.

## 20

After Wang Ling was dismissed as chancellor, Empress Dowager Lü transferred Chen Ping to the position of Right Chancellor and appointed Hou Shiyi (Marquis of Biyang) Shen Shi Qi as Left Chancellor. The Left Chancellor did not manage government affairs, but remained constantly in attendance at court.

## 21

Shiyi was also a native of Pei. When Han Wang suffered defeat at the western side of Pengcheng, Chu captured Tai Shang Huang and Lady Lü as hostages. At that time, Shen Shi Qi served as a retainer to Lady Lü. Later, he followed the campaign that defeated Xiang Ji and was ennobled as a marquis; he enjoyed favor with Empress Dowager Lü. When he became chancellor, he remained in the palace, and all officials relied on him to decide matters.

## 22

Lü Xu often recalled how Chen Ping had previously plotted for Emperor Gao to arrest Fan Kui, and she repeatedly slandered him by saying, "Chen Ping is not managing affairs as chancellor; he drinks strong wine every day and plays with women." When Chen Ping heard this, his behavior became even more excessive. Empress Dowager Lü heard about it and secretly rejoiced. She confronted Chen Ping in the presence of Lü Xu, saying, "A vulgar saying goes 'a daughter-in-law's words cannot be trusted'; it all depends on how you and I are." "Do not fear the slanders of Lü Xu."

## 23

Empress Dowager Lü established several members of the Lü clan as kings, and Chen Ping pretended to comply. After Empress Dowager Lü died, Chen Ping conspired with Grand Marshal Zhou Bo and ultimately eliminated the Lü clan, establishing Emperor Xiao Wen. This was originally Chen Ping's plan. Shen Shi Qi was dismissed from his position as chancellor.

## 24

When Emperor Xiao Wen ascended the throne, he considered that Grand Marshal Zhou Bo had personally used troops to eliminate the Lü clan and thus deserved great merit; Chen Ping wanted to yield his position of respect to Zhou Bo, so he claimed illness as an excuse. In the early years of Emperor Xiao Wen's reign, he was puzzled by Chen Ping's illness and inquired about it. Chen Ping said, "During Emperor Gaozu's time, Zhou Bo's achievements were not as great as mine." "And when the Lü clan was eliminated, my contribution was also less than Zhou Bo's." "I wish to yield the position of Right Chancellor to Zhou Bo." Thus, Emperor Xiao Wen appointed Marquis Jiao Hou Zhou Bo as Right Chancellor, ranking first in position; Chen Ping was transferred to the post of Left Chancellor, ranked second. He granted Chen Ping a thousand catties of gold and increased his fief by 3,000 households.

## 25

After a short while, when Emperor Xiao Wen had become more experienced and familiar with state affairs, he summoned the Right Chancellor Zhou Bo in court and asked, "How many cases are decided by the courts throughout the country in one year?" Zhou Bo replied, "I do not know." He asked again, "What is the annual revenue and expenditure of money and grain for the empire?" Zhou Bo once more replied that he did not know. He was drenched in sweat, his back soaked, ashamed to answer. At this point the emperor also asked Left Chancellor Chen Ping. Chen Ping said, "There are officials in charge of these matters." The emperor asked, "Who is this official?" Chen Ping replied, "If Your Majesty inquires about court cases, the responsibility lies with the Tingwei; if you ask about money and grain, it is the duty of the Neishi who administers grain." The emperor said, "If each matter has its own responsible official, then what exactly is it that you are in charge of?" Chen Ping replied with a bow, "Your Majesty, I am the chief minister! His Majesty does not know my incompetence and has allowed me to hold the position of prime minister in disgrace. The prime minister, above all, assists the Son of Heaven in regulating yin and yang, harmonizing the four seasons; below, he nurtures all things according to their proper conditions; externally, he pacifies the Four Barbarians and feudal lords; internally, he brings the people close and attached. He ensures that officials and gentlemen each perform their duties properly." Emperor Xiao Wen then praised him highly. The Right Chancellor was greatly ashamed and went out to scold Chen Ping, saying, "Did you not teach me the answer beforehand?" Chen Ping smiled and said, "Are you in your position but do not know what responsibilities it entails? And if His Majesty were to ask the number of thieves and bandits in Chang'an, would you dare make up an answer?" At this point, Marquis Jiao Hou realized that his abilities were far inferior to Chen Ping's. After a short time, Marquis Jiao Hou claimed illness and requested to be relieved of his chancellorship; Chen Ping then served alone as the sole chancellor.

## 26

In the second year of Emperor Xiao Wen's reign (178 BC), Chancellor Chen Ping died, posthumously honored with the title Hou Xian. His son Gong Hou Mai succeeded him as marquis. He died in the second year, and his son Jian Hou Hui succeeded him as marquis. In the 23rd year, he died, and his son He succeeded him as marquis. In the 23rd [year], He was executed in public for abducting a married woman, and the fiefdom was abolished.

## 27

Initially, Chen Ping said, "I have used many secret plots, which is what Daoism forbids. If my descendants are to be ruined in the future, it will already have happened; they can never rise again because of the hidden misfortunes I have brought." However, his great-great-grandson Chen Zhang, being a close relative of the Wei clan's nobility, wished to have the Tchen lineage re-ennobled; yet he was never granted this.

## 28

Grand Historian said: Chancellor Chen Ping, when young, originally favored the teachings of Huangdi and Laozi. At the time he was cutting meat on the sacrificial table, his thoughts had already been far-reaching. Amidst the turmoil and shifting allegiances between Chu and Wei, he ultimately submitted to Emperor Gao. He often devised ingenious strategies, rescuing the nation from entangled crises and alleviating national disasters. During the time of Empress Dowager Lü, many incidents occurred; yet Chen Ping ultimately managed to extricate himself, stabilized the ancestral temple, and ended his life with a glorious reputation. He was called an exemplary chancellor—was he not one who began well and ended well! Who but a man of wisdom and strategy could have measured up to this?
