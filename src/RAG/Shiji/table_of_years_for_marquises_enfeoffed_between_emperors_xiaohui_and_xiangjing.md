# Table of Years for Marquises Enfeoffed Between Emperors Xiaohui and Xiangjing

## 1

The Grand Historian, reading the section on the Marquis of Bianhou in the "Table of Titles," said: There is a reason for this indeed! The ruler of Changsha issued an official decree, commending his loyalty. In the past, when Emperor Gaozu pacified the empire, eight states were granted to non-royal relatives who had rendered meritorious service. By the time of Emperor Xiaohui, only Changjiang Quan was enfeoffed for five generations. After five generations, it became extinct due to having no descendants. Yet there were no transgressions; they fulfilled their duties as a vassal state faithfully. This is indeed credible. Therefore, the family's favor extended to collateral branches and common descendants, resulting in several individuals being enfeoffed as marquises without having rendered any personal service. Between Emperor Xiaohui and Emperor Xiaojing, over fifty years passed during which the imperial court posthumously honored meritorious subjects from Emperor Gaozu's time, as well as those who had followed from Dai, contributed to suppressing Wu and Chu, or were sons of feudal lords considered close as kin, or foreigners who had pledged allegiance. More than ninety individuals were thus enfeoffed. All these figures demonstrated loyalty from beginning to end; they were the most prominent exemplars of benevolence, righteousness, and achievement in their time.
