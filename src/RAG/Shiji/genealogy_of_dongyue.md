# Genealogy of Dongyue

## 1

The King of Minyue Wu Zhu and the Eastern Hai Wang Yao, their ancestors were all descendants of the King of Yue Goujian, surnamed Zou. After the Qin had unified the empire, they deposed them as chieftains and established the Minzhong Commandery in their territory. When the feudal lords rebelled against the Qin, Wu Zhu and Yao led the Yue people to submit to Wu Rui, the magistrate of Poliang, who was known as Pojun. They followed the feudal lords in overthrowing the Qin dynasty. At that time, Xiang Ji controlled authority; he did not make them kings, and therefore they did not submit to the Chu state. When the Han attacked Xiang Ji, Wu Zhu and Yao led their Yue people in assisting the Han. In the fifth year of the Han dynasty, Wu Zhu was reinstated as King of Minyue, ruling over his former territory in Minzhong and with his capital at Dongye. In the third year of Emperor Xiaohui's reign, they honored the contributions made by the Yue people during Emperor Gaozu's time. It was said that Minjun Yao had rendered many meritorious services and his people were willing to follow him; therefore, he was enfeoffed as King of Donghai, with his capital at Dongou. He became known in common parlance as the King of Dongou.

## 2

Several generations later, in the third year of Emperor Xiao Jing's reign, King Wu Liu Pi rebelled and wished to enlist Minyue's support. However, Minyue was unwilling to join him; only Dongou followed King Wu. After the defeat of Wu, Dongou accepted a reward from the Han and killed King Wu at Dantu; therefore, they were all spared punishment and returned to their state.

## 3

Wu Wang's son Ziju fled to Minyue and resented the fact that Dongou had killed his father. He often advised Minyue to attack Dongou. By the third year of Jianyuan, Minyue mobilized troops and besieged Dongou. loin With their supplies exhausted and in dire straits, they were about to surrender when they sent envoys to urgently appeal for help from the Son of Heaven. The Son of Heaven consulted Grand Marshal Tian Fen, who replied: "The Yue people attacking each other is a common occurrence. Moreover, they are often fickle and unreliable; it is not worth the trouble for the Central Kingdom to send troops to rescue them." "They have been abandoned and not subject since the Qin dynasty." At this, Zhong Dafu Zhuang Zhu interrogated Tian Fen, saying: "The only concern is whether we have the strength to rescue them and the virtue to protect them; If we truly can, why should we abandon them?" Moreover, the Qin abandoned Xianyang and yet it was a major city; how much more so for the Yue!" Now, a small state has come to urgently appeal to the Son of Heaven because it is in dire straits. If the Son of Heaven does not offer relief, where else should they turn for redress? Furthermore, how can you claim to be the ruler of myriad states?" The emperor said: "The Grand Marshal is not capable enough to deliberate on this matter. I have just ascended the throne and do not wish to issue tiger tokens to mobilize troops from commanderies and states." He then dispatched Zhuang Zhu with a token of authority to raise troops in Kuaiji. The governor of Kuaiji wished to refuse and not raise troops, so Zhuang Zhu beheaded a military officer, conveyed the emperor's intent, and thus mobilized the troops who crossed the sea by boat to rescue Dongou. Before they arrived, Minyue withdrew its forces. Dongou requested to relocate the entire state into the Central Kingdom, and so all their people came, settling in the region between the Yangtze and Huai rivers.

## 4

By the sixth year of Jianyuan, Minye attacked Nanyue. Nanyue adhered to the Son of Heaven's decree and dared not 擅自 mobilize troops for an attack but reported the matter instead. The emperor dispatched Daxing Wang Hui to depart from Yuzhang and Da Nong Han Anguo to leave Kuaiji, both appointed as generals. Before the troops had crossed the mountains, King Ying of Minye mobilized forces to resist at a perilous pass. His younger brother Yushan then conspired with the prime minister and his clan, saying: "The king launched an attack on Nanyue without authorization or request; therefore, the Son of Heaven has sent troops to punish him. Now the Han army is numerous and powerful. Even if we are fortunate enough to defeat them, more will come later until our state is ultimately destroyed. We should now kill the king to apologize to the Son of Heaven." If we do this, the Son of Heaven will listen and withdraw his troops; thus, our entire state can remain intact. If he does not listen, then we must fight with all our strength; if we do not win, we will flee into the sea." All said, "Good idea." They immediately assassinated the king and sent envoys carrying his head to Daxing. Daxing said: "The reason we came was to punish the king. Now that his head has arrived, he is offering atonement; without fighting, we have achieved our goal—there could be no greater benefit." He therefore decided to halt the military campaign on his own initiative and informed the Da Nong army, while sending envoys with the king's head to rush back and report to the Son of Heaven. An edict was issued to disband both generals' forces, stating: "Ying and others were the principal evildoers; only Wu Zhu's descendant Youjun Chou had no involvement in the plot." The emperor then dispatched a Langzhong to enfeoff Chou as Yue Youwang, to uphold the ancestral sacrifices of Minyue.

## 5

After Yushan had killed Ying, his authority was recognized throughout the state; many of its people submitted to him, and he secretly declared himself king. Youwang could not command his people or uphold righteousness. The Son of Heaven heard about this and considered Yushan not worth launching another military campaign against, saying: "Yushan frequently conspired with Ying to cause chaos, but later took the initiative in killing Ying; thus, our troops need not be burdened." He therefore enfeoffed Yushan as King of Dongyue, to coexist with Youwang.

## 6

By the fifth year of Yuanding, Nanyue rebelled. King Yushan of Dongyue submitted a memorial requesting to lead eight thousand soldiers under the command of Louchuan General to attack Lu Jia and others. When their troops arrived at Jieliang, they cited the rough sea conditions as an excuse for not proceeding and remained neutral, secretly sending envoys to Nanyue. And when the Han captured Panyu, Yushan did not arrive. At that time, Louchuan General Yang Pu sent envoys to submit a memorial requesting permission to lead his troops directly against Dongyue. The emperor said the soldiers were weary and refused, disbanding the forces and ordering all officers to station at Mei Ling in Yuzhang until further orders.

## 7

In the autumn of the sixth year of Yuanding, Yushan heard that Louchuan had requested to execute him and learned that Han troops were approaching his border. He therefore rebelled, mobilizing forces to resist the Han advance. He proclaimed Generals Zou Li and others as "Tunhan Generals," who entered Baisha, Wulin, and Mei Ling, killing three Han military commanders. At that time, the Han had dispatched Da Nong Zhang Cheng and former Shanzhou Hou Chi to command garrisons; they dared not attack but instead retreated to more convenient positions. All were executed for cowardice.

## 8

Yushan carved a "Wudi" seal and declared himself emperor, deceiving his people with false claims. The Son of Heaven dispatched Henghai General Han Yue to depart from Juzhang, sailing by sea from the east; Louchuan General Yang Pu departed from Wulin; Zhongwei Wang Wenshu departed from Mei Ling; Yue Hou served as Gechuan and Xia Sai General, departing from Ruoxia and Baisha. In winter of the first year of Yuanyoung, they all entered Dongyue. Dongyue had long mobilized forces to resist from perilous positions, sending Xunbei General to defend Wulin. He defeated several officers of the Louchuan army and killed senior officials. Louchuan General led Qiantang Yuan Zhonggu to behead Xunbei General and was enfeoffed as Hou of Yuer. The troops had not yet advanced.

## 9

Previously, Yue Yan Hou Wu Yang was in the Han court; the Han sent him back to advise Yushan, but Yushan refused to listen. When Henghai General arrived first, Yue Yan Hou Wu Yang led seven hundred people from his fief to rebel and attacked the Yue forces at Hanyang. Following Chengjian Hou Ao, along with his commander, they conspired with Youwang Jugu, saying: "Yushan is the principal evildoer who has taken control of us by force. Now that Han troops have arrived and are numerous, we should plan to kill Yushan and surrender ourselves to the Han generals; perhaps we may be fortunate enough to escape." They then jointly killed Yushan and surrendered their forces to Henghai General. Thus, Youwang Jugu was enfeoffed as Dongcheng Hou with a fief of ten thousand households; Chengjian Hou Ao was enfeoffed as Kailing Hou; Yue Yan Hou Wu Yang was enfeoffed as Beishi Hou; Henghai General Yue was enfeoffed as Andao Hou; Henghai Jiao Wei Fu was enfeoffed as Liaoying Hou. Fu, a son of Gong Wang of Chengyang, had previously been the Hai Chang Hou but lost his title due to legal violations. He originally followed the army without merit and was granted a marquisate solely because of his royal lineage. All other generals had achieved no notable success, so none were enfeoffed. Dongyue's general Duanjun abandoned his troops and surrendered when the Han forces arrived, and was enfeoffed as Wuxi Hou.

## 10

The Son of Heaven then declared that Dongyue was narrow and full of obstacles, while Minyue was fierce and often fickle. He issued an edict ordering military officials to lead the people from these regions to settle between the Yangtze and the Huai rivers. Thus, the land of Dongyue became empty.

## 11

Grand Historian said: "Although Yue was a barbarian land, did its ancestors ever truly render great virtue and merit to the people? How long they have endured!" For several generations, they often held positions as kings; Goujian alone was once called a hegemon. Yet Yushan committed great treason, destroyed the state and relocated its people. Even his descendants such as Youwang Jugu were still enfeoffed as marquises with ten thousand households. From this we can see that the Yue had long been hereditary dukes and marquises. This was probably due to the enduring legacy of Yu.
