# Biographies of Confucian Scholars

## 1

Tai Shigong said: Whenever I read the regulations on official appointments, and came to the part about widely encouraging scholars to enter government service, I could never help but set down my book and sigh. He said: Alas! When the Zhou dynasty declined, the poem "Guan Ju" was composed; when King You and King Li became weak, rites and music fell into disrepair, feudal lords acted with impunity, and political power lay in the hands of strong states. Therefore, Confucius lamented that the royal way had been abandoned and false doctrines prevailed; thus he compiled the Book of Songs and the Book of Documents, and restored rites and music. When he visited Qi and heard the Shao music, for three months he did not know the taste of meat. After returning from Wei to Lu, then the music was corrected, and the Ya and Song were each placed in their proper positions. The world was muddled and corrupt, so no one could use him; therefore, Confucius sought audiences with more than seventy rulers without success. He said, "If there were someone who would employ me, within a month I could bring about change." When he hunted west and captured a qilin, he said, "My way has come to an end." Therefore, based on historical records, he composed the Spring and Autumn Annals as a substitute for royal law; its words were subtle but its meanings broad, and many scholars of later generations recorded it.

## 2

After Confucius's death, the seventy disciples scattered and traveled among the feudal lords; some became teachers or high officials, while others served as friends and mentors to scholars and gentry, or chose to remain in seclusion. Therefore, Zilu stayed in Wei, Zizhang in Chen, Dantaiziyu in Chu, Zixia in Xhe, and Zigong finally died in Qi. People such as Tian Zifang, Duanguanmu, Wu Qi, and Qin Huili were all students of Zixia's school and became teachers to kings. At that time, only Duke Wenhou of Wei loved learning. Later, the decline continued until Emperor Qin Shi Huang, when all under heaven competed in warring states; Confucian learning had already declined. Yet between Qi and Lu, scholars alone did not abandon it. During the reigns of Kings Wei and Xuan, figures such as Mencius and Xun Qing all followed Master Confucius's teachings and refined them, thereby becoming prominent scholars in their time.

## 3

By the later years of the Qin dynasty, they burned books on poetry and history, buried scholars alive, and from then on, the Six Arts were lost. When Chen She became king, Confucian scholars in Lu carried ritual objects of the Kong family to submit themselves to King Chen. At that time, Kong Jia was appointed as a doctor by Chen She, and in the end died together with him. Chen She rose from the ranks of commoners, led a band of conscripted laborers, and within ten days became king of Chu; yet in less than half a year he perished. His cause was extremely insignificant and shallow—then why did scholars and gentlemen carry Confucius's ritual objects to submit themselves as his subjects? It was because the Qin dynasty burned their learning, and they harbored resentment that they took up arms under King Chen in anger.

## 4

When Emperor Gaozu of Han killed Xiang Yu and led his army to surround Lu, the Confucian scholars within Lu continued to lecture, recite, and practice rites and music; the sounds of stringed instruments and songs never ceased. Was this not a country influenced by sages' teachings, one that cherished rites and music? Therefore, Confucius said while in Chen: "Return! Return! My young disciples at home are wild and impetuous, yet they write beautifully in elegant style; I do not know how to refine them." The region between Qi and Lu has been known for its literary culture since ancient times, a natural disposition of the people. Therefore, after the rise of the Han dynasty, Confucian scholars were finally able to study and practice their classical learning, as well as observe grand archery ceremonies and local drinking rites. Shusun Tong formulated the Han dynasty's rituals and thus became Taichang. The scholars and disciples who jointly established them were all selected as leaders, and at this time there was a sigh of revival in learning. Yet warfare still continued; the empire had yet to be pacified, and there was no time or leisure for matters of schools and education. During the reigns of Emperor Xiaohui and Empress Lü, all chancellors and high officials were men who had gained merit through military service. During the reign of Emperor Xiaowen, some scholars were indeed summoned for appointment; however, Emperor Xiaowen himself favored legalist doctrines. By the time of Emperor Xiaojing, Confucian scholars were not appointed; and Empress Dowager Dou favored Huang-Lao Daoist teachings. Therefore, all the doctors held their posts in readiness for questions, but none advanced in rank.

## 5

When the current emperor ascended the throne, Zhao Wan and Wang Zang among others promoted Confucian learning, and the emperor also showed favor toward it. Thus he summoned upright, virtuous, and learned scholars of literature. After this, those who expounded the Book of Songs in Lu were Shen Peigong; in Qi, Yuan Gusheng; and in Yan, Han Taifu. Those who expounded the Book of Documents began with Fu Sheng of Jinan. lando Those who expounded rites began with Gaotangsheng of Lu. Those who expounded the Yijing began with Tiansheng of Zichuan. Those who expounded the Spring and Autumn Annals in Qi and Lu were Humusheng, while in Zhao it was Dong Zhongshu. After Empress Dowager Dou died, Marquis Wu'an Tianfen became chancellor and suppressed the Huang-Lao Daoist and legalist schools as well as other hundred schools of thought. He invited hundreds of literary Confucian scholars, and Gongsun Hong, a commoner who interpreted the Spring and Autumn Annals, rose to become one of the Three Dukes of the Son of Heaven and was enfeoffed as Marquis Pingjin. Scholars throughout the empire began to follow this trend.

## 6

Gongsun Hong, as an official in charge of education, lamented the stagnation and suppression of Confucian learning. He thus made a request: "The chancellor and the Grand Censor said: 'The decree states, "We have heard that guiding the people through rites and influencing them with music." Marriage is a great principle of human relationships. Now that rites have been abandoned and music has collapsed, I feel deeply sorrowful about it. Therefore, we should carefully invite upright and erudite men from all over the world to take their places in court. Let the officials of rites encourage learning, hold discussions on widely known knowledge and promote rites, setting an example for all under heaven. The Taichang deliberated: "Together with the doctors and their disciples, we should promote moral influence in villages and towns to cultivate more virtuous talents." Carefully deliberating with Taichang Zang, Doctor Ping and others, they said: "We have heard that in the three dynasties of Xia, Shang, and Zhou, there was education at the village level. In the Xia dynasty it was called Xiao, in the Shang dynasty Xu, and in the Zhou dynasty Xiang. To encourage virtue, they were honored in court; to punish evil deeds, they imposed criminal penalties. Therefore, the implementation of moral education should begin by establishing a model of virtue in the capital city and extend from within outward. Now, Your Majesty shines with the highest virtue and opens up great enlightenment. You match heaven and earth in your conduct, uphold human relationships, encourage learning and cultivate rites, honor moral influence and inspire virtuous men, thus setting an example for all directions—this is the foundation of peace and prosperity. In ancient times, when government and moral education were not yet fully established, rites were incomplete; we request that we revive them by building upon the existing offices. Establish an office for doctors with fifty students each and exempt their families from taxes and labor duties. The Taichang should select citizens aged eighteen or older who are of proper appearance and demeanor to be appointed as students of the doctors. In commanderies, states, counties, and towns, those who love literature, respect elders, uphold government and moral education, behave properly in their communities, and whose conduct does not contradict what they have learned should be recommended by magistrates and assistant officials to the local two-thousand-dan officials. These officials must carefully examine suitable candidates and submit them along with annual reports to the Taichang, where they may receive instruction as students. Once a year, all should be tested; those who can master one classical discipline or more will fill vacancies in the positions of literary scholars and historians. Those top students qualified to become Langzhong officials should be recorded and recommended by the Taichang. If there are outstanding talents of exceptional ability, they should immediately report their names. Those who do not pursue learning or are of low ability and cannot master even one discipline should be dismissed, and those responsible for recommending them inadequately will be punished. I have carefully examined the imperial edicts and laws in effect, which clearly define the distinctions between heaven and humanity, elucidate the meanings of past and present, are elegant in style, profound in explanation, and generous in their grace. Low-ranking officials with limited knowledge cannot fully understand or convey these decrees, thus failing to properly inform the people below. The administration of rites should be followed by the management of historical records; offices should be assigned based on literary and ritual competence, with promotions or delays depending on merit. We request that officials of rank equivalent to 200 dan or higher, as well as clerks of 100 dan who have mastered one discipline or more, be appointed to positions such as Left and Right Neishi and Da Xingcushi. Those of rank below 100 dan should be appointed to the positions of commandery governor's clerks: two each for central regions, and one for border commanderies. Priority should be given to those who have memorized the most texts; if there are not enough, then select historians to fill positions under officials of two-thousand dan rank, and literary scholars or historians for commandery posts as needed. We request that these regulations be officially recorded in the law. Other matters shall follow existing laws and decrees." The imperial decree said: "Approved." From this time onward, the chancellors, high officials, scholars, and clerks became increasingly filled with men of literary learning.

## 7

Shen Gong was a native of Lu. When Emperor Gaozu passed through Lu, Shen Gong followed his teacher and the disciples to present themselves before Emperor Gaozu at Nan Gong in Lu. During the reign of Empress Dowager Lü, Shen Gong studied in Chang'an and shared a teacher with Liu Ying. Later, when Ying became King of Chu, he ordered Shen Gong to serve as tutor to his son Wu, the crown prince. Wu did not like studying and resented Shen Gong. When King Ying died, Wu succeeded him as King of Chu and treated Shen Gong harshly. Shen Gong was ashamed by this treatment, returned to Lu, and withdrew from public life to teach at home. He never left his house for the rest of his life and refused all visitors, going only when summoned by imperial decree. More than a hundred students from distant regions came to study under him. Shen Gong taught exclusively using the Book of Songs, without transmitting any commentary (possibly uncertain). If there were uncertainties, he would leave them untransmitted.

## 8

Lanling Wang Zang had studied the Book of Songs and served as a tutor to Crown Prince Xiao Jingdi, but was later dismissed. When the current emperor first ascended the throne, Wang Zang submitted a memorial to serve as an imperial guard and was repeatedly promoted. Within one year he became Minister of the Imperial Guards (Langzhong Ling). Dai Zhao Wan had also studied the Book of Songs under Shen Gong, and Wan served as Grand Censor. Wan and Zang requested the Son of Heaven to build a Mingtang Hall for receiving feudal lords, but failed to accomplish this. They then spoke highly of their teacher Shen Gong. Thus the Son of Heaven sent envoys with silk, jade, and a specially prepared carriage drawn by four horses to welcome Shen Gong. Two of his disciples rode in light carriages as attendants. When he arrived, he was received by the Son of Heaven. The Son of Heaven asked about governance and disorder, but Shen Gong was already over eighty years old. He replied: "Good governance does not depend on many words; it depends only on how effectively one acts." At that time, the Son of Heaven favored literary eloquence. Upon hearing Shen Gong's reply, he fell silent. Nevertheless, since he had already been summoned, the emperor appointed him as Taizhong Dafu and housed him in the Lu guesthouse to deliberate on matters concerning the Mingtang Hall. Empress Dowager Dou Taihuang favored Laozi's teachings and disapproved of Confucianism. She used the mistakes of Zhao Wan and Wang Zang to reprimand the emperor, who then abandoned the Mingtang project and handed both men over to officials for punishment; later they all committed suicide. Shen Gong also fell ill and was dismissed from office, returning home, where he died several years later.

## 9

More than ten of his disciples became doctors: Kong Anguo served as governor of Linhuai, Zhou Ba as Neishi of Jiaoxi, Xia Kuan as Neishi of Chengyang, Dang Lushi as governor of Donghai, Miu Sheng of Lanling as Neishi of Changsha, Xu Yan as Zhongwei of Jiaoxi, and Que Men Qingji of Zou served as Neishi of Jiao dong. All of them governed their officials and people with integrity and were praised for their love of learning. Though the conduct of some students in the official schools was not fully exemplary, hundreds still rose to positions such as 大夫 (Da Fu), 郎中 (Lang Zhong), and 掌故 (Zhang Gu). Though interpretations of the Book of Songs varied, many were based on Shen Gong's teachings.

## 10

Yuangu Sheng, the Taifu of King Qinghe, was a native of Qi. He studied the Book of Songs and during Emperor Xiao Jing's reign served as a doctor. He debated with Huang Sheng before Emperor Jingdi. Huang Sheng said: "Tang and Wu were not divinely appointed rulers, but murderers." Yuangu Sheng replied: "That is incorrect. Jie and Zhou were cruel and chaotic, so the hearts of all under heaven turned to Tang and Wu. Tang and Wu followed the will of the people in punishing Jie and Zhou; the people of Jie and Zhou did not serve them but instead submitted to Tang and Wu. If Tang and Wu had no choice but to establish their rule, then how could it be said they were not divinely appointed?" Huang Sheng replied: "Even if a hat is worn out, it must still be placed on the head; even if shoes are new, they must still be worn on the feet. Why is this so? Because it represents the distinction between superior and inferior." Now, although Jie and Zhou lost their way, they were still sovereigns; Tang and Wu, though sage-like, were subjects. If a ruler behaves improperly, and subjects cannot speak out to correct the error in order to uphold the Son of Heaven, but instead take advantage of the mistake to kill him, usurp his position, and sit facing south as sovereigns, how can this be anything other than regicide?" Yuangu Sheng said: "If it must be as you say, then was it not wrong for Emperor Gaozu to replace the Qin and take the position of Son of Heaven?" At this, Emperor Jingdi said: "To eat meat but not horse liver does not mean one lacks a sense of taste; to say that scholars who do not claim Tang and Wu were divinely appointed are not foolish." Thus the debate was ended. After this, scholars dared not openly discuss whether a ruler had been divinely appointed or deposed by force.

## 11

Empress Dowager Dou favored the Book of Laozi and summoned Yuangu Sheng to ask about it. Gu said: "This is merely a book for common people." The empress dowager became angry and said: "How dare you compare it to the writings of Sikong Chengdan?" She then ordered Gu to enter a pigpen and slaughter a pig. Emperor Jingdi, knowing that the empress dowager was angry but that Gu had spoken truthfully and committed no crime, therefore gave Gu a sharp weapon. When Gu went down into the pigpen to slaughter the pig, he struck it right in the heart with one thrust, and the pig fell over immediately at his hand. The empress dowager remained silent, unable to find further grounds for punishment, and dismissed the matter. After a short while, Emperor Jingdi, recognizing Gu's integrity and uprightness, appointed him as Taifu of King Qinghe. After some time, he fell ill and was relieved of his post.

## 12

When the current emperor first ascended the throne, Gu was again summoned on account of his virtue and talent. Many sycophantic Confucian scholars resented and slandered Gu, saying "Gu is old," and he was dismissed and sent back home. At that time, Gu had already been over ninety years old. When Gu was summoned, Gongsun Hong of Xue was also summoned and cast sidelong glances at Gu. Gu said: "Master Gongsun, you must pursue upright scholarship and speak truthfully; do not distort learning to flatter the world!" From then on, all interpretations of the Book of Songs in Qi were based upon Yuangu Sheng's teachings. Many people from Qi who gained prominence and honor through their knowledge of the Book of Songs were all Gu's disciples.

## 13

Han Sheng was a native of Yan. During Emperor Xiao Wendi's reign, he served as a doctor; during Emperor Jingdi's reign, he was Taifu to King Changshan. Han Sheng expounded the meaning of the Book of Songs, producing several ten thousand words in internal and external commentaries. His interpretations differed somewhat from those between Qi and Lu, yet their ultimate principles were the same. Bian Sheng of Huainan received his teachings. From then on, those in Yan and Zhao who interpreted the Book of Songs followed Han Sheng's tradition. Han Sheng's grandson, Shang, became a doctor to the current emperor.

## 14

Fu Sheng was a native of Jinan. He had previously served as a doctor under the Qin dynasty. During Emperor Xiao Wendi's reign, there was a desire to find someone capable of studying the Book of Documents (Shangshu), but no one in the empire could be found. It was then learned that Fu Sheng had this ability and he was to be summoned. At that time, Fu Sheng was over ninety years old and too elderly to travel; thus, the emperor issued an order for Tai Chang to send Zhang Gu Chao Cuo to go and study with him. During the Qin dynasty's book-burning campaign, Fu Sheng had hidden them in a wall for safekeeping. Later, when large-scale warfare broke out and he was displaced, after the Han dynasty was established, Fu Sheng searched for his books but found that dozens of chapters had been lost; only twenty-nine remained. He then used these to teach in the regions between Qi and Lu. Scholars thus became somewhat capable of discussing the Book of Documents, and no great masters in Shandong failed to include it in their teaching.

## 15

Fu Sheng taught Zhang Sheng of Jinan and Ouyang Sheng, who in turn taught Er Kuan of Qiansheng. After Er Kuan had thoroughly studied the Book of Documents, he was recommended by his commandery for his literary talent and went to study under a doctor, receiving instruction from Kong An Guo. Er Kuan was poor and had no means of support, so he often served as a steward for his fellow students and occasionally took on odd jobs to earn money for food and clothing. Whenever he walked, he always carried the classics with him; whenever he stopped or rested, he would recite and study them. Through examinations ranked by merit, he was appointed as a clerk in the Office of the Minister of Justice (Tingwei). At that time, Zhang Tang was promoting education and appointed him as a clerk to handle memorials and legal cases. He used ancient laws to deliberate on difficult and important lawsuits and favored Er Kuan highly. Er Kuan was a warm and kind person, upright and wise, self-disciplined, skilled in writing books and memorials, quick with written words, but not eloquent when speaking. Zhang Tang regarded him as a man of virtue and often praised him highly. When Zhang Tang became Minister of Justice, he appointed Er Kuan as his clerk and recommended him to the Son of Heaven. The emperor summoned him for an audience and was pleased with him. Six years after Zhang Tang's death, Er Kuan rose to the position of Minister of Justice. Nine years later, he died while still in office. While Er Kuan held a position among the Three Dukes, his ability to remain in power for so long stemmed from his gentle and accommodating nature that allowed him to follow imperial will with ease; however, he offered no advice or remonstrance. Among officials, subordinates looked down on him and did not exert themselves for his service. Zhang Sheng also became a doctor. However, Fu Sheng's grandson was summoned for his ability to study the Book of Documents but could not clearly explain it.

## 16

After this, Lu Zhou Ba, Kong An Guo from Lu, and Jia Jia of Luoyang were somewhat capable of discussing matters related to the Book of Documents. The Kong family possessed an ancient text version of the Book of Documents, but Kong An Guo interpreted it using the current script. Through this, he established his own school of interpretation. He recovered more than ten lost chapters; thus, the number of passages in the Book of Documents increased significantly at this time.

## 17

Among scholars who discussed rites, Gao Tang Sheng from Lu was considered to have the most fundamental and authoritative interpretation. Originally, during Confucius's time, the rites were not fully recorded in a complete text; by the time of the Qin dynasty's book-burning, even more texts had been scattered and lost. To this day, only the Rites for Officials (Shi Li) remain, and Gao Tang Sheng was able to explain them.

## 18

Meanwhile, Xu Sheng from Lu excelled in performing ritual postures and movements. During Emperor Xiao Wendi's reign (r. 180–157 BCE), Xu Sheng served as a Rites Official Doctor due to his expertise in ritual postures. The tradition was passed down from father to son, reaching Xu Yan and Xu Xiang of the next generation. Xiang had a natural talent for performing ritual postures but could not fully understand the classical texts on rites; Yan was somewhat capable, though still not entirely proficient. Xiang served as a Rites Official of the Han dynasty and eventually became Neishi (governor) of Guangling. Yan, along with Gong Hu Manyi, Huan Sheng, and Dan Ci from the Xu family's disciples, had all once served as Rites Officials of the Han dynasty. Meanwhile, Xiao Fen of Xiaqiu served as governor of Huaiyang based on his knowledge of rites. After this, those who could discuss rites and perform ritual postures did so through the tradition of the Xu family.

## 19

Starting from Shang Ju of Lu, who received the Book of Changes (Yi Jing) from Confucius, after Confucius's death, Shang Ju transmitted it. It passed through six generations until reaching Tian He, courtesy name Zizhuang, a native of Qi, during the rise of the Han dynasty. Tian He passed it on to Wang Tongzi Zhong from Dongwu; Wang Tongzi Zhong in turn transmitted it to Yang He, a native of Zichuan. Yang He studied the Book of Changes and was summoned during the first year of Yuanguang (134 BCE). He rose to the rank of Zhongdaren. Ji Mo Cheng, a native of Qi, attained the position of Xiang (governor) in Chengyang through his study of the Book of Changes. Meng Dan from Guangchuan served as Men Daren to the Crown Prince due to his knowledge of the Book of Changes. Zhou Ba from Lu, Heng Hu from Ju, and Zhu Fu Yan from Linzi all attained the rank of Erqianshi (a high-ranking official position) through their study of the Book of Changes.  Nevertheless, those who truly wished to master the interpretation of the Yi Jing traced its foundation back to Yang He's school.

## 20

Dong Zhongshu was a native of Guangchuan. He studied the Spring and Autumn Annals, and during Emperor Jing's reign served as a Doctor. He lectured and recited while drawing a curtain to block out distractions. His disciples passed on his teachings in succession, some never having seen him in person. For three years, Dong Zhongshu did not step outside the courtyard of his residence; such was his level of dedication. He conducted himself with proper decorum and only acted in accordance with ritual propriety, and all scholars regarded him as a teacher to be revered. When the current emperor ascended the throne, Dong Zhongshu became Xiang (governor) of Jiangdu. Using the changes in calamities and anomalies described in the Spring and Autumn Annals to deduce how yin and yang forces interact, he argued that to summon rain one should close off all yang influences and release yin; conversely, to stop rain, the opposite approach was taken. When applied in a single state, these methods never failed to achieve their intended results. Later demoted to the rank of Zhong Daren and confined to his residence, he wrote a record on calamities and anomalies. At that time, when the Gao Temple in Liaodong was struck by disaster, Zhu Fu Yan, who resented Dong Zhongshu, submitted his book to the Son of Heaven.  The emperor summoned various scholars and showed them the book, which contained critical remarks. Lü Bushu, a disciple of Dong Zhongshu, did not recognize his teacher's writing and regarded it as the work of an ignorant person. The emperor then ordered that Dong Zhongshu be placed under arrest, sentenced to death, but later issued a decree pardoning him. From then on, Dong Zhongshu dared not speak of calamities and anomalies again.

## 21

Dong Zhongshu was an upright and honest man. At that time, the empire was focused on repelling the four foreign tribes, and although Gongsun Hong studied the Spring and Autumn Annals less thoroughly than Dong Zhongshu did, he flattered the times to gain influence and rose to the ranks of high officials. Dong Zhongshu regarded Gongsun Hong as a sycophant. Gongsun Hong resented this and said to the emperor: "Only Dong Zhongshu can be sent to negotiate with King Xi of Miao." King Xi of Jiaoxi had long heard of Dong Zhongshu's virtue and treated him well. Dong Zhongshu feared that he would eventually be punished for his actions, so he resigned from office and returned home in haste. Until his death, Dong Zhongshu never managed any property; instead, he devoted himself to studying, teaching, and writing books. Therefore, from the founding of the Han dynasty until the fifth generation, only Dong Zhongshu was renowned for his profound understanding of the Spring and Autumn Annals, which he interpreted according to the Gongyang School.

## 22

Huwu Sheng was a native of Qi. During the reign of Emperor Xiaojing, Huwu Sheng served as a Doctor (Bo Shi), and later retired from office due to old age, devoting himself to teaching. Many scholars in Qi who studied the Spring and Autumn Annals were students of Huwu Sheng; Gongsun Hong also received instruction from him to some extent.

## 23

Xiaqiu Jiang Sheng expounded the "Guliang Commentary on the Spring and Autumn Annals." After Gongsun Hong gained influence, he once compiled and compared their interpretations, but in the end, Dong Zhongshu prevailed.

## 24

Among Dong Zhongshu's disciples who became prominent were: Cui Da of Lanling, Yin Zhong of Guangchuan, and Lü Bushu of Wen. Cui Da rose to become the prime minister of the State of Liang. Lü Bushu rose to the position of Changshi, and was entrusted with a ceremonial staff to adjudicate cases in Huainan. In dealing with matters among the feudal lords, he made independent decisions without prior approval, basing his judgments on the principles of the Spring and Autumn Annals; the Son of Heaven found all his rulings just. Among his disciples who achieved prominence, some rose to the rank of Ming Daren (a high official title). Numerous others became Lang officials, Yezhe attendants, and Zhanggu record-keepers—counted in the hundreds. Moreover, Dong Zhongshu's son and grandson all attained high official positions through their scholarship.
