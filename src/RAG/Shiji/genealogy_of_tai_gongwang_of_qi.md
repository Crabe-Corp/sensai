# Genealogy of Tai Gongwang of Qi

## 1

Tai Gongwang Lü Shang was a native of Donghai Shang. His ancestors once served as the Four Peaks, assisting Yu in controlling floods and managing land with great achievements. During the transition between the Yu and Xia dynasties, he was granted a fief at Lü, or perhaps at Shen, and bore the surname Jiang. During the Xia and Shang dynasties, the fiefs of Shen and Lü were sometimes granted to collateral branches or descendants, or they became commoners; Lü Shang was a descendant from this lineage. Originally surnamed Jiang, he adopted the surname of his fief, hence the name Lü Shang.

## 2

Lü Shang had once been poor and destitute; in his old age, he fished and cast a hook to secretly approach the western lord of Zhou. The western lord was about to go hunting; he divined and received an omen, which said: "What you will capture is not a dragon, not a tiger, not a bear, nor a she-bear; what you will capture shall be the assistant to a sovereign or warlord." Thus, when the western lord of Zhou went hunting, he indeed encountered Tai Gong on the southern bank of the Wei River. After conversing with him, he was greatly pleased and said: "Since my late ancestor Duke Tai Gong said 'A sage will come to Zhou, and through this Zhou shall prosper.'" Are you truly that person? "My ancestor Tai Gong has been waiting for you for a long time." Therefore, he was given the title "Tai Gongwang," and was taken back with him; he was appointed as military advisor.

## 3

Some say that Tai Gong had broad knowledge, and once served Zhou Zhu. When Zhou Zhu proved to be a tyrant, he left him. After traveling and persuading various feudal lords without finding an opportunity, he finally went westward to join the western lord of Zhou. Some say that Lü Shang was a recluse who lived in seclusion by the sea. When the western lord of Zhou was imprisoned at Youli, Sanyi Sheng and Hong Yao, who had long known him, invited Lü Shang. Lü Shang also said, "I have heard that the western lord is virtuous and good at respecting and caring for elders; why not go there?" The three of them sought beautiful women and rare treasures for the western lord, presented them to Zhou Zhu in order to redeem him. The western lord was thus released and returned to his state. It is said that although the manner in which Lü Shang served Zhou varied, ultimately he became a teacher and advisor to Kings Wen and Wu.

## 4

After the western lord of Zhou, King Chang, was released from Youli and returned, he conspired in secret with Lü Shang to cultivate virtue and undermine the government of Shang; many of their actions involved military strategy and ingenious plans. Therefore, later generations discussing military affairs or the covert strategies of Zhou all regarded Tai Gong as the original planner. When the western lord's administration was just, and when he resolved the dispute between Yu and Rui, poets praised him as having received his mandate from heaven and called him King Wen. He attacked Chong, Mixu, and Quanyi, and greatly expanded the fief of Fengyi. When the world was divided into three parts, two of them submitted to Zhou; much credit for this was due to Tai Gong's strategies and plans.

## 5

After King Wen died, King Wu ascended the throne. In the ninth year, he wished to continue King Wen's cause and launched an eastern campaign to see whether feudal lords would gather in support. As the army marched, Master Shangfu stood on the left holding a yellow halberd and on the right grasping a white banner to give an oath, saying: "O dark rhinoceroses, O dark rhinoceroses! Assemble your people and prepare your boats; those who arrive late shall be executed!" Thus they proceeded to Mengjin. Eight hundred feudal lords gathered there without prior appointment. The feudal lords all said, "Zhou Zhu can be attacked." King Wu said, "Not yet." He withdrew the army and, together with Tai Gong, composed this great oath.

## 6

Two years later, Zhou Zhu killed Prince Bi Gan and imprisoned Ji Zi. King Wu was about to attack Zhou Zhu; he divined with tortoise shells, but the omens were inauspicious, and a sudden storm of wind and rain arrived. The ministers were all frightened; only Tai Gong strongly encouraged King Wu, so King Wu proceeded with the campaign. On the Jiazi day of the first month in the eleventh year, they swore an oath at Musye and launched a campaign against Shang Zhou. Zhou Zhu's army was defeated. lø Zhou Zhu fled in reverse, climbed onto Lütai, and was pursued and beheaded there. The next day, King Wu stood at the altar of the earth god; the ministers presented clear water, Wei Kangshu Feng spread colored mats, Master Shangfu led sacrificial animals, and Shi Yi composed blessings to inform the gods about the punishment for Zhou Zhu's crimes. They distributed the money from Lütai and released grain stored at Juqiao to relieve poor civilians. They built a tomb for Prince Bi Gan, and freed Ji Zi from imprisonment. They moved the Nine Tripods, reformed Zhou's administration, and began anew with all under heaven. Master Shangfu contributed many strategies.

## 7

Thus, after King Wu had pacified the Shang and became king of all under heaven, he enfeoffed Master Shangfu at Qi Yingqiu. On his way east to take possession of his fiefdom, he stopped overnight and traveled slowly. A traveler at the inn said: "I have heard that opportunity is hard to obtain but easy to lose. You sleep so soundly; you are probably not one who wishes to go and take up a fiefdom." Upon hearing this, Tai Gong got up in the night and set out; by dawn he had arrived at his state. The Marquis of Lai launched an attack to contest Yingqiu with him. Yingqiu bordered the state of Lai. The people of Lai were barbarians; taking advantage of Zhou Zhu's chaos and the early stability of the new Zhou regime, they had not yet been subdued in distant regions, so they contested Tai Gong for his fief.

## 8

Upon arriving at his state, Tai Gong reformed the administration; he followed local customs and simplified rituals. He promoted commerce and handicrafts, facilitated benefits from fishery and salt production, so that many people flocked to Qi, making it a great power. When King Cheng of Zhou was young, the Dukes Guan and Cai rebelled, and the Huai Yi people defected from Zhou. Thus, Zhao Kanggong was sent to command Tai Gong: "From east to the sea, west to the Yellow River, south to Muling, north to Wudi, the Five Dukes and Nine Regional Lords shall indeed be authorized to campaign against them." From this point on, Qi gained the right to launch military campaigns and became a great power. Its capital was Yingqiu.

## 9

About more than a hundred years after Tai Gong's death, his son Duke Ding Lü Ji ascended to power. After Duke Ding died, his son Yigong De succeeded him. After Yigong died, his son Guigong Cimu succeeded him. After Guigong died, his son Aigong Buchen ascended to the throne.

## 10

During Aigong's reign, Ji Hou slandered him before Zhou; as a result, the Zhou court boiled Aigong to death and installed his younger brother Jing, who became known as Hugong. Hugong moved the capital to Bofeng during the reign of King Yi of Zhou.

## 11

Aigong's younger half-brother, Shan, resented Hugong; thus, he and his faction led the people of Yingqiu to launch a surprise attack, killed Hugong, and declared himself ruler. He became known as Duke Xian. In the first year of Duke Xian's reign, he expelled all Hugong's sons and moved the capital from Bofeng to Linzi.

## 12

In the ninth year, Duke Xian died; his son Wugong Shou succeeded him. In the ninth year of Wugong's reign, King Li of Zhou fled and took refuge in Zhi. In the tenth year, the royal court was in chaos; senior ministers administered government affairs, a period known as "Gonghe." In the twenty-fourth year, King Xuan of Zhou first ascended to the throne.

## 13

In the twenty-sixth year, Wugong died; his son Ligong Wuji succeeded him. Ligong was tyrannical and cruel, so Hugong's descendants returned to Qi; the people of Qi wished to install them, and thus they attacked and killed Ligong. Hugong's son also died in battle. The people of Qi then installed Ligong's son Chi as ruler, who became Duke Wen; seventy men who had killed Ligong were executed.

## 14

Duke Wen died in the twelfth year of his reign; his son Chenggong Tuo succeeded him. Chenggong died in the ninth year of his reign, and his son Zhuanggong Gou ascended to the throne.

## 15

In the twenty-fourth year of Zhuanggong's reign, Quanyi killed King You of Zhou; the Zhou dynasty moved its capital eastward to Luoyi. The state of Qin began to be ranked among the feudal lords. In the fifty-sixth year, Jin assassinated its ruler Duke Zhao Hou.

## 16

In the sixty-fourth year, Zhuanggong died; his son Lügong Lupu succeeded him.

## 17

In the ninth year of Lügong's reign, Duke Yin of Lu first ascended to the throne.  In the nineteenth year, Duke Huan of Lu assassinated his elder brother Duke Yin and declared himself ruler.

## 18

In the twenty-fifth year, northern Rong forces attacked Qi. Zheng sent Crown Prince Hu to come and rescue Qi; Qi wanted to give him a wife in marriage. Hu said, "Zheng is small while Qi is large; I am not their match." He thus declined the offer.

## 19

In the thirty-second year, Lügong's younger half-brother Yizhong Nian died. His son was Gongsun Wuzhi; Lügong loved him and ordered that his rank, ceremonial attire, and provisions be equal to those of the crown prince.

## 20

In the thirty-third year, Lügong died; the crown prince Zhuer ascended the throne and became Duke Xiang.

## 21

In the first year of Duke Xiang's reign, when he was still a crown prince, he had once fought with Wuzhi. After ascending to power, he reduced Wuzhi's rank and ceremonial attire; thus, Wuzhi became resentful.

## 22

In the fourth year, Duke Huan of Lu and his wife went to Qi. Duke Xiang of Qi had previously secretly been intimate with Lady Lu. Lady Lu was Duke Xiang's younger sister; she had been married to Duke Huan of Lu since the time of Lügong, and when Duke Huan came to Qi, Duke Xiang resumed his affair with her. Duke Huan learned about this, became angry at his wife, and she informed Duke Xiang of Qi.  Duke Xiang of Qi hosted a banquet with Duke Lu, got him drunk, and ordered the strongman Pengsheng to carry Duke Lu onto his chariot. Then he pulled and killed Duke Huan; when Duke Huan stepped down from the chariot, he was already dead. The people of Lu considered this an insult, and so Duke Xiang of Qi killed Pengsheng as a gesture of apology to Lu.

## 23

In the eighth year, Qi attacked Ji; the people of Ji abandoned their fief and fled.

## 24

In the twelfth year, originally, Duke Xiang had sent Liancheng and Guanzhipu to garrison Kuichou; they were to go at harvest time and be replaced after another harvest. After one year of service, when the replacement was due at the next harvest season, Duke Xiang did not send a relief force. Someone requested that they be replaced; the duke refused to grant permission. Thus, these two men became angry and conspired with Gongsun Wuzhi to launch a rebellion. Liancheng had an unmarried younger female relative in the palace of Duke Xiang, who was not favored. He sent her to spy on Duke Xiang and said, "If the plot succeeds, I will make you Wuzhi's wife." In winter, the twelfth month, Duke Xiang went on a tour to Gufen and then proceeded to hunt at Peiqiu. He saw a wild boar; one of his attendants said, "Pengsheng."  The duke became angry, shot it, and the pig stood up and cried out. The duke was frightened, fell from his chariot, injured his foot, and lost a shoe. Upon returning, he flogged the servant responsible for his shoes, Fu, three hundred times. Fu left the palace. Meanwhile, Wuzhi, Liancheng, Guanzhipu and others heard that the duke was injured, so they immediately led their followers to attack the palace. They encountered Fu, the servant in charge of shoes. Fu said, "Do not yet enter and alarm the palace; it will not be easy to enter if they are alarmed." Wuzhi did not believe him, so Fu showed him his wounds, and then Wuzhi believed him. They waited outside the palace and ordered Fu to enter first. Fu entered first, then hid Duke Xiang behind a door. After some time, Wuzhi and others became anxious and so entered the palace. Fu then turned to fight with those inside the palace and the duke's favored ministers against Wuzhi et al., but they were not victorious, and all died. Wuzhi entered the palace but could not find the duke. Someone saw a foot between the door panels, looked inside, and it was Duke Xiang. They then assassinated him, and Wuzhi declared himself ruler of Qi.

## 25

In spring of the first year of Duke Huan's reign, Duke Wu Zhi of Qi toured Youlin. The people of Youlin had previously harbored resentment toward Wu Zhi. When he went there for a tour, the people of Youlin attacked and killed Wu Zhi, informing the officials of Qi: "Wu Zhi assassinated Duke Xiang to seize power; as loyal subjects, we have carried out due punishment." "Please only choose the rightful heir among the marquises and obey your orders."

## 26

Originally, when Duke Xiang got drunk and killed Duke Huan of Lu, he had an affair with his wife. He executed people unjustly, was lustful toward women, repeatedly insulted senior ministers, and his younger brothers feared that disaster would reach them; thus, the next younger brother Jiu fled to Lu. His mother was a woman from Lu. Guan Zhong and Zhao Hu served as his tutors. The next younger brother Xiao Bai fled to Ju, where Bao Shu served as his tutor. Xiao Bai's mother was a woman from Wei and was favored by Duke Li. Since childhood, Xiao Bai had been close to the virtuous official Gao Xi. When the people of Youlin killed Wu Zhi and discussed selecting a new ruler, Gao and Guo secretly summoned Xiao Bai from Ju beforehand. Lu heard of Wu Zhi's death and also mobilized troops to escort Gongzi Jiu, while sending Guan Zhong separately with an army to block the road to Ju. Guan Zhong shot and hit Xiao Bai's belt hook. Xiao Bai pretended to be dead, and Guan Zhong sent someone quickly to report the news to Lu. The Lu forces escorting Jiu moved even more slowly, taking six days to reach Qi. By then, Xiao Bai had already entered the country; Gao Xi installed him as ruler, and he became Duke Huan.

## 27

Duke Huan was struck in the belt hook, pretended to be dead to mislead Guan Zhong. Afterwards, he was loaded into a warm chariot and driven away at speed; with support from Gao Xi and Guo Nei, he managed to enter Qi first and establish himself as ruler before mobilizing troops to resist Lu. In autumn, they fought the people of Lu at Qianshi; the Lu army was defeated and fled, while the Qi forces blocked their retreat route. Qi sent a letter to Lu saying: "Jiu is your brother; we cannot bear to execute him. Please have Lu kill him yourselves." Zhao Hu and Guan Zhong are our enemies; please allow us to capture them so we may take satisfaction in mincing them into meat paste. Otherwise, we will besiege Lu." The people of Lu were worried and so killed Jiu at Shengdu. Zhao Hu committed suicide, while Guan Zhong requested to be imprisoned. After Duke Huan was installed as ruler, he mobilized troops to attack Lu, with the intention of killing Guan Zhong in mind. Bao Shuya said: "I was fortunate to accompany Your Majesty, and you have finally succeeded as ruler. Your Majesty's position is already exalted; I cannot add anything further to your honor. If Your Majesty wishes to govern Qi, Gao Xi and Shuya are sufficient. But if Your Majesty aspires to become a hegemon or even the Son of Heaven's overlord, no one but Guan Yiwu will suffice. Guan Yiwu is in a country that values him highly; he cannot be lost to us." Thus, Duke Huan followed his advice. He pretended to summon Guan Zhong with the intent of taking revenge, but in reality he wanted to employ him. Guan Zhong knew this and so requested to go. Bao Shuya went out to receive Guan Zhong, and upon reaching Tangfu, he removed his shackles. After purification rites, he presented himself before Duke Huan. Duke Huan honored him with generous gifts and appointed him as a high official, entrusting him with political affairs.

## 28

After Duke Huan gained Guan Zhong, he worked with Bao Shuya, Xi Peng, and Gao Xi to reform the administration of Qi. They consolidated the military forces of five families, established policies on trade in fish and salt to provide for the poor, rewarded virtuous and capable individuals, and thus all the people of Qi were pleased.

## 29

In the second year, Qi attacked and destroyed Tan; Duke Tan fled to Ju. Originally, when Duke Huan was in exile, he passed through Tan, but the people of Tan treated him with no courtesy; thus, he attacked them.

## 30

In the fifth year, Qi attacked Lu, and Lu's army was defeated. Duke Zhuang of Lu requested to offer Su Yi as a tribute in exchange for peace, and Duke Huan agreed; they met at Ke and concluded an alliance. As Lu was about to conclude the oath, Cao Mei seized Duke Huan with a dagger on the altar and said: "Return the territories that Qi has invaded from Lu!" Duke Huan agreed. Soon after, Cao Mei put down his dagger, faced north, and returned to the position of a subject. Duke Huan regretted this later and wanted to withhold the land from Lu and kill Cao Mei. Guan Zhong said: "To have promised under coercion and then break the promise by killing him would only bring a small sense of satisfaction, but it will betray our credibility to the feudal lords and lose support from all under heaven. That is not acceptable." Thus, they gave back to Lu the three territories that had been lost in previous defeats, as promised to Cao Mei. The feudal lords heard of this and all came to trust Qi and wished to submit to it. In the seventh year, the feudal lords convened with Duke Huan at Zhen, and from then on, Duke Huan began to establish his hegemony.

## 31

In the fourteenth year, Teng Li's son Wan, known as Jing Zhong, fled to Qi. Duke Huan of Qi wanted to appoint him as an official, but he declined; thus they appointed him instead as the head of the artisans. He was the ancestor of Tian Chengzi Chang.

## 32

In the twenty-third year, Shan Rong attacked Yan, and Yan urgently appealed to Qi for help. Duke Huan of Qi came to rescue Yan, then proceeded to attack Shan Rong, reaching Guzhu before returning. Yan Zhuanggong thus escorted Duke Huan into the territory of Qi. Duke Huan said: "Unless it is the Son of Heaven, feudal lords do not escort each other beyond their borders. I cannot be disrespectful to Yan." Thus, he divided the territory along the ditch up to where Yan's ruler had escorted him and returned it to Yan. He ordered the Yan ruler to restore the policies of Zhao Gong, pay tribute to Zhou as in the time of Cheng and Kang. The feudal lords heard about this and all followed Qi.

## 33

In the twenty-seventh year, Duke Min of Lu's mother was Aijiang, a younger sister of Duke Huan. Aijiang had an affair with Gongzi Qingfu of Lu; Qingfu assassinated Duke Min. Aijiang wanted to install Qingfu as ruler, but the people of Lu instead reestablished Duke Li as king. Duke Huan summoned Aijiang and had her killed.

## 34

In the twenty-eighth year, Duke Wen of Wei faced a Rong invasion and urgently appealed to Qi for assistance. Qi led the feudal lords in building the city at Chuqiu and reestablished the ruler of Wei there.

## 35

In the twenty-ninth year, Duke Huan was playing with his wife Cai Ji on a boat. Cai Ji, who was skilled in water, rocked the boat, alarming Duke Huan. He tried to stop her but she did not cease; he left the boat and became angry, returning Cai Ji home without divorcing her. Cai was also angry and married off his daughter. Duke Huan heard of this and became furious, raising an army to launch a campaign.

## 36

In spring of the thirtieth year, Duke Huan of Qi led the feudal lords in attacking Cai, which collapsed. They then proceeded to attack Chu. King Cheng of Chu raised an army and asked: "Why have you trespassed onto my land?" Guan Zhong replied: "In the past, Zhao Kanggong commanded our ancestor Duke Tai Gong saying: 'For the Five Lords and Nine Heirs, if they truly rebel, you may campaign against them to assist and support the Zhou dynasty.'" "He granted our ancestor sovereignty over territory extending east to the sea, west to the Yellow River, south to Muling, and north to Wudi. "Chu has failed to present its tribute of bamboo leaves for sacrificial wine, leaving the king's sacrifices incomplete; therefore, we have come to hold them accountable." "And King Zhao went on a southern campaign and never returned, so we are here to inquire about it." King Chu said: "It is true that tribute has not been submitted; this is my fault. How dare I fail to provide it!" "As for King Zhao's southern campaign and his failure to return, you should inquire about that by the riverside." The Qi army advanced and stationed at Xing. In summer, King Chu sent Qu Wan to lead troops in defense against Qi; the Qi army withdrew and encamped at Zhaoling. Duke Huan showed off his forces before Qu Wan with pride. Qu Wan said: "If you lead by virtue, then it is possible; "If not, then Chu will take Fangcheng as its wall and the rivers Jiang and Han as ditches. How could you possibly advance?" He then concluded an alliance with Qu Wan and departed. Passing through Chen, the ruler of Chen, Yuan Taochu, deceived Qi by ordering them to take an eastern route; this was discovered. In autumn, Qi attacked Chen. In this year, Jin killed Crown Prince Shen Sheng.

## 37

In summer of the thirty-fifth year, [the ruler] convened feudal lords at Kuichiu. King Xiang of Zhou sent Zai Kong to bestow Duke Huan with sacrificial meat from Wen and Wu, red bows and arrows, and a grand carriage, ordering him not to bow. Duke Huan wanted to accept the order, but Guan Zhong said, "It is not acceptable," so he descended from his chariot and bowed to receive the gifts. In autumn, [he] convened feudal lords again at Kuichiu, showing an even more arrogant attitude. The Zhou dynasty sent Zai Kong to attend the meeting. Some feudal lords had begun to rebel. Duke of Jin was ill and arrived late, meeting Zai Kong. Zai Kong said: "The Duke of Qi is arrogant now; you should not go." He followed the advice. In this year, Duke Xian of Jin died; Li Ke killed Xi Qi and Zhuo Zi. Duke Mu of Qin brought in his daughter as the wife of Gongzi Yiwu, who then became ruler of Jin. Duke Huan thus launched a campaign to quell the chaos in Jin, reaching Gaoliang, and sent Xi Peng to install the new ruler of Jin before returning.

## 38

At this time, the Zhou royal house was weak; only Qi, Chu, Qin, and Jin were powerful. Jin had initially participated in the assembly, but after Duke Xian's death, chaos erupted within the state. Duke Mu of Qin remained distant and did not participate in alliances among the Central States. King Cheng of Chu initially subdued the Jing Man people, allowing them to be governed by their own chieftains. Only Qi participated in alliances among the Central States, and Duke Huan was able to promote his virtue; therefore, feudal lords gathered as guests. At this time, Duke Huan declared: "I have led southern campaigns to Zhaoling and gazed upon Xiong Mountain; "I have campaigned northward against the Shan Rong, Lizhi, and Gu Zhu; "westward against Daxia, crossing the Shala Sands; "I tied up my horses and suspended my chariot to ascend Taixing Mountain, reaching Beier Mountain before returning." No feudal lord dared to defy me. "I have convened three military assemblies and six chariot processions, uniting the feudal lords nine times to restore order in the world." "In previous dynasties of Xia, Shang, and Zhou when they received their mandate from heaven, what was different about this?" "I wish to perform the Fengshan ceremony at Mount Tai and hold a sacrificial rite on Liangfu Mountain." Guan Zhong strongly advised against it, but he did not listen; So they explained to Duke Huan that rare and exotic treasures from distant lands had to be presented before the Fengshan ceremony could take place, and thus Duke Huan abandoned the plan.

## 39

In the thirty-eighth year, King Xiang's younger brother Dai conspired with the Rong and Di tribes to attack Zhou. Qi sent Guan Zhong to mediate peace between the Rong people and the Zhou dynasty. The Zhou court wished to bestow on Guan Zhong the rites of a high-ranking minister, but Guan Zhong kowtowed and said: "I am merely an attendant minister; how could I possibly accept such honors!" After three refusals, he finally accepted the rites of a lower-ranking minister to appear before them. In the thirty-ninth year, King Xiang's younger brother Dai fled to Qi. Qi sent Zhong Sun to request an audience with the king on behalf of Dai and apologized for his actions. King Xiang was angry and refused to listen.

## 40

In the forty-first year, Duke Mu of Qin captured Duke Hui of Jin but later released him back. In this year, Guan Zhong and Xi Peng both died. Guan Zhong was ill; Duke Huan asked him: "Among the ministers, who is suitable to be prime minister?" Guan Zhong said: "No one knows me better than you, my lord." The duke asked: "What about Yiya?" He replied: "To kill one's son to please the ruler is against human nature; he cannot be trusted." The duke asked: "What about Kai Fang?" He replied: "To abandon one's parents to serve the ruler is against human feelings; he should not be trusted." The duke asked: "What about Shudao?" He replied: "To castrate himself to serve the ruler is against human feeling; he cannot be trusted." After Guan Zhong's death, Duke Huan did not follow his advice and eventually employed the three men, who then seized power.

## 41

In the forty-second year, the Rong attacked Zhou; Zhou urgently requested assistance from Qi, and Qi ordered feudal lords to dispatch troops for Zhou's defense. In this year, Gongzi Chonger of Jin arrived in Qi; Duke Huan married his daughter to him.

## 42

The forty-third year. Initially, Duke Huan of Qi had three wives: Wang Ji, Xu Ji, and Cai Ji; none bore him children. Duke Huan favored concubines and had many favorites, six of whom were treated like a wife; the eldest was Wei Ji, who bore Wugui; the younger Wei Ji, who gave birth to Duke Yuan of Hui; Zheng Ji, mother of Duke Zhao the Filial; Ge Ying, mother of Duke Pan the Illustrious; Mi Ji, mother of King Shangren the Yi; Song Huazi, mother of Gongzi Yong. Duke Huan and Guan Zhong entrusted Duke Zhao the Filial to Duke Xiang of Song, appointing him as crown prince. Yong Wu was favored by Gong Ji of Wei and, through the eunuch Shudao, made generous gifts to Duke Huan; he also gained favor. Duke Huan agreed to make Wugui his heir. After Guan Zhong died, the five sons of Duke Huan all vied for succession. In winter, on the Yihai day of the tenth month, Duke Huan of Qi died. Yiya entered and, together with Shudao, used their influence over the concubines to kill the officials and installed Gongzi Wugui as ruler. Crown Prince Zhao fled to Song.

## 43

While Duke Huan was ill, the five sons each formed factions and vied for power. After Duke Huan died, they turned on one another in battle; as a result, the palace was empty and no one dared to place his body into a coffin. Duke Huan's corpse lay on the bed for sixty-seven days until maggots crawled out of the palace gates. On the Yihai day of December, Wugui was enthroned; only then was a coffin prepared for Duke Huan's remains. On the Xinsi night, his body was embalmed and placed in the coffin.

## 44

Duke Huan had more than ten sons, but among them, five succeeded to the throne: Wugui reigned for three months before dying without a posthumous name; then came Duke Zhao the Filial; then Duke Pan the Illustrious. then King Shangren the Yi. then Duke Yuan of Hui. In the first year of Duke Zhao's reign, March, Duke Xiang of Song led an army of feudal lords to escort Crown Prince Zhao and attacked Qi. The people of Qi were terrified and killed their ruler Wugui. The people of Qi intended to install Crown Prince Zhao, but the followers of the four other sons attacked him. The crown prince fled to Song, and thus Song engaged in battle with the four sons' faction from Qi. In May, Song defeated the forces of the four sons of Qi and installed Crown Prince Zhao as ruler; he became Duke Zhao the Filial. Song had come to enforce this because Duke Huan and Guan Zhong had entrusted him with the crown prince's succession. Due to the chaos, it was not until August that Duke Huan of Qi was finally buried.

## 45

In spring of the sixth year, Qi attacked Song because they had not allied with Qi. In summer, Duke Xiang of Song died. In the seventh year, Duke Wen of Jin ascended to the throne.

## 46

In the tenth year, Duke Zhao the Filial died; his younger brother Pan, with the help of Gongzi Kaifang of Wei, killed the son of Duke Zhao and ascended to power as Duke Pan the Illustrious.  Duke Pan the Illustrious was a son of Duke Huan, and his mother was Ge Ying.

## 47

In the first year of Duke Pan's reign, Duke Wen of Jin defeated Chu at Chengpu and convened feudal lords at Jiantu for an assembly. He paid homage to Zhou, and the Son of Heaven granted Jin the title of Hegemon. In the sixth year, Di forces invaded Qi. Duke Wen of Jin died. The Qin army was defeated at Xiao Pass. In the twelfth year, Duke Mu of Qin died.

## 48

In May of the nineteenth year, Duke Pan the Illustrious died; his son She was installed as ruler of Qi. She's mother had not been favored by Duke Pan the Illustrious, and thus the people did not fear him. Shang Ren, a younger brother of Duke Pan the Illustrious who had competed for the throne after Duke Huan's death but failed to obtain it, secretly associated with virtuous scholars and gained the affection of the common people, who were pleased. When Duke Pan died and his son She ascended the throne, who was young and weak, they conspired with the masses to assassinate King She of Qi on the tenth day of the tenth month at the tomb site. Shang Ren then declared himself ruler, becoming Duke Yi. Duke Yi was a son of Duke Huan; his mother was Mi Ji.

## 49

In spring of the fourth year of Duke Yi's reign, initially, when Duke Yi was still a prince, he once hunted with Bing Rong's father and lost in their competition for game. After ascending to the throne, he amputated Bing Rong's father's feet and made Bing Rong his servant. Yong Zhi's wife was beautiful; the duke took her into the palace, and assigned Yong Zhi as a side rider. In May, Duke Yi went for an outing at Shenchi, where the two men bathed and played around. Yong Zhi said, "You with amputated feet!" Bing Rong replied, "You who stole my wife!" Both men were deeply hurt by these words and thus became resentful. They plotted to accompany the duke on a trip into the bamboo grove, where they assassinated Duke Yi while he was in his carriage and then abandoned him in the bamboo grove before fleeing.

## 50

Duke Yi's rule had been arrogant, and thus the people did not support him. The people of Qi deposed his son and welcomed Gongzi Yuan from Wei, installed him as ruler; he became Duke Hui. Duke Hui was a son of Duke Huan. His mother, a woman from Wei named Shao Wei Ji, had fled the chaos in Qi and thus remained in Wei.

## 51

In the second year of Duke Hui's reign, a tall Di warrior came; Prince Chengfu attacked and killed him, burying his body at the northern gate. Zhao Chuan of Jin assassinated his ruler Duke Ling.

## 52

In the tenth year, Duke Hui died; his son Wu Ye ascended to the throne as Duke Qing. Initially, Cui Zhu was favored by Duke Hui; after Duke Hui's death, the Gao and Guo families feared his encroachment and expelled him. Cui Zhu fled to Wei.

## 53

In the first year of Duke Qing's reign, King Zhuang of Chu was powerful and launched a campaign against Chen; in the second year, he besieged Zheng. Lord Zheng Bo surrendered, but later King Zhuang restored him as ruler of Zheng.

## 54

In spring of the sixth year, Jin sent Xi Ke to Qi; Qi arranged for its queen to observe him from behind a curtain. As Xi Ke ascended, the lady laughed at him. Xi Ke said, "If I do not take revenge for this, I shall never cross the Yellow River again!" After returning, he requested to attack Qi; Duke of Jin did not agree. When envoys from Qi arrived in Jin, Xi Ke seized four Qi envoys within the Yellow River region and killed them. The eighth year. Jin attacked Qi, and Qi sent Gongzi Qiang as a hostage to Jin; the Jin army then withdrew. In spring of the tenth year, Qi launched an attack on Lu and Wei. The ministers of Lu and Wei went to Jin to request military aid, all through Xi Ke's influence. Jin appointed Xi Ke as the commander of the central army with 800 chariots, Shi Xie as the commander of the upper army, and Luan Shu as the commander of the lower army to rescue Lu and Wei and attack Qi. On the Renshen day of the sixth month, they engaged the forces of Duke of Qi at Milv Ji below. On the Guichou day, they formed their ranks at Longpang; Chou Fu served as the right-hand man of Duke Qing of Qi. Duke Qing said, "Charge forward and break through the Jin army to have a meal together." He shot and wounded Xi Ke, whose blood flowed down to his shoes. Xi Ke wanted to return to the camp, but his charioteer said, "I have just entered battle and been wounded twice. I dare not mention my injuries for fear of alarming the soldiers; please endure it, sir." So they continued to fight. During the battle, Qi was in dire straits; Chou Fu feared that Duke Qing would be captured and so exchanged places with him. Duke Qing became the right-hand man. The chariot got entangled in a tree and stopped. The Jin junior general Han Jue crouched before the chariot of Duke Qing, saying, "My lord sent me to rescue Lu and Wei," taunting him. Chou Fu instructed Duke Qing to get down from the chariot for a drink, thereby allowing him to escape and retreat into his own army. Xi Ke of Jin wanted to kill Chou Fu. Chou Fu said, "If I die in place of my lord and am punished for it, then no future subjects will be loyal to their lords." Xi Ke spared him, and thus Chou Fu was able to escape back to Qi. At this time, the Jin army pursued Qi forces all the way to Malei. Duke of Qi offered valuable treasures as a token of apology, but it was refused; they demanded the woman who had laughed at Xi Ke, Xiao Tong Shuzi, and ordered Qi to plow its fields eastward. The reply was: "Shuzi is the mother of Duke of Qi." "The mother of the Duke of Qi is just like the mother of the Duke of Jin; how could you treat her?" "And if you start a war in the name of righteousness but then act with violence afterward, is that acceptable?" At this, they agreed and ordered Qi to return the territories it had invaded from Lu and Wei.

## 55

In the eleventh year, Jin first established six high-ranking officials (Six Ministers), rewarding those who had distinguished themselves in the Battle of Guan. Duke Qing of Qi paid homage to Jin, wishing to elevate Duke Jing of Jin as the Son of Heaven. However, Duke Jing did not dare accept it and thus returned. Upon returning, Duke Qing reduced the hunting grounds, lowered taxes and levies, supported orphans, visited the sick, distributed stored supplies to relieve the people's distress; the people were greatly pleased. He treated the feudal lords with generous gifts. Until Duke Qing's death, the common people remained loyal to him and no feudal lord dared to violate Qi.

## 56

In the seventeenth year, Duke Qing died; his son Huan ascended the throne as Duke Ling.

## 57

In the ninth year of Duke Ling's reign, Luan Shu of Jin assassinated their ruler Duke Li. In the tenth year, King Dao of Jin attacked Qi; Qi sent Gongzi Guang as a hostage to Jin. In the nineteenth year, they appointed Zi Guang as crown prince; Gao Hou served as his tutor and ordered him to convene an alliance with feudal lords at Zhongli. In the twenty-seventh year, Jin sent Xianzi of Zhongxing to attack Qi. The Qi army was defeated; Duke Ling fled into Linzi. Yan Ying tried to stop Duke Ling, but the duke did not listen. He said, "You too have lost your courage!" The Jin army then besieged Linzi; the defenders of Linzi did not dare to come out, and so the Jin forces burned parts of the outer city before withdrawing.

## 58

In the twenty-eighth year, initially, Duke Ling married a woman from Lu, who bore him a son named Guang, whom he designated as crown prince. Zhong Ji and Rong Ji. Rong Ji was favored; Zhong Ji bore a son named Ya, who she entrusted to Rong Ji's care. Rong Ji requested that the child be made crown prince, and the duke agreed. Zhong Ji said, "No, it cannot be done." "Prince Guang's position as heir has already been announced among the feudal lords; to now discard him without cause will surely lead you, my lord, to regret it." The duke said, "It is up to me." Thus he deposed Crown Prince Guang and appointed Gao Hou as tutor for Ya, making him the new crown prince. When Duke Ling fell ill, Cui Zhu welcomed back the former Crown Prince Guang and installed him as ruler; he was known as Duke Zhuang. Duke Zhuang killed Rong Ji. On the Renchen day of the fifth month, Duke Ling died; Duke Zhuang ascended to the throne and seized Crown Prince Ya at Goudou Qiu, killing him. In August, Cui Zhu killed Gao Hou. Jin heard of the chaos in Qi and launched an attack against it, reaching Gaotang.

## 59

In the third year of Duke Zhuang's reign, Luan Ying, a minister of Jin, fled to Qi; Duke Zhuang received him as an honored guest. Yan Ying and Tian Wenzhi advised against it, but the duke would not listen. In the fourth year, Duke Zhuang of Qi sent Luan Ying to secretly enter Quwo in Jin as an inside supporter, with troops following him. They ascended Taihang Mountain and entered Mengmen Pass. Luan Ying was defeated; the Qi forces retreated but captured Chaoge.

## 60

In the sixth year, initially, Tang Gong's wife was beautiful. After Tang Gong died, Cui Zhu took her as his wife. Duke Zhuang had an affair with her and often visited the Cui family, even giving away Cui Zhu's official hat to others. A servant said, "That is not acceptable." Cui Zhu became angry and, taking advantage of his campaign against Jin, sought to conspire with Jin to attack Qi but failed to find an opportunity. Duke Zhuang once beat a eunuch named Jia Ju, who later served again and used the opportunity to inform Cui Zhu about Duke Zhuang's movements in order to take revenge. In May, Marquis Ju paid homage to Qi; Qi hosted a banquet for him on the Jiaxu day. Cui Zhu claimed illness and did not attend official duties. On the Yitai day, the duke inquired about Cui Zhu's illness and then proceeded to visit Cui Zhu's wife. Cui Zhu's wife entered the room, locked the door with Cui Zhu inside and did not come out; Duke Zhuang hugged a pillar and sang. The eunuch Jia Ju blocked the duke's attendants and entered, locking the door; Cui Zhu's followers armed with weapons rose up from within. The duke climbed onto a platform to plead for mercy, but it was refused; he requested an oath of allegiance, which was also denied. He then asked to commit suicide in the ancestral temple, but this too was refused. They all said, "Your subject Zhu is ill and unable to obey orders. He is close to the duke's palace. The attendants vied to pursue those who had committed adultery, and did not know of two orders." The duke climbed over a wall but was shot in the thigh. He fell back down and was thus assassinated. Yan Ying stood outside Cui Zhu's gate and said, "If the lord dies for the state, I will die with him; if he flees for the sake of the state, I shall flee with him. If he dies or flees for his own selfish reasons, then unless it is for someone intimately close to him, who would dare take responsibility!" After the gate opened and he entered, Yan Ying placed his head on the duke's corpse and wept, then exited after three jumps. People told Cui Zhu, "You must kill him." Cui Zhu said, "He is the people's hope; sparing him will win us their support."

## 61

On the Dingchou day, Cui Zhu installed Chu Jiu, Duke Zhuang's half-brother, as ruler; this was King Jing. King Jing's mother was the daughter of Lu Shusun Xuanbo. After King Jing ascended the throne, he appointed Cui Zhu as Right Chancellor and Qing Feng as Left Chancellor. The two chancellors feared unrest would arise, so they made an oath with the people: "Those who do not support Cui and Qing shall die!" Yan Zi looked up to heaven and said, "What I will not do is follow anyone except those who are loyal to the lord and beneficial to the state!" He refused to take the oath. Qing Feng wanted to kill Yan Zi, but Cui Zhu said, "He is a loyal minister; spare him." The Qi Grand Historian recorded, "Cui Zhu assassinated Duke Zhuang," and Cui Zhu killed him. His younger brother wrote the same record again, and Cui Zhu had him killed as well. The youngest brother recorded it once more, and this time Cui Zhu let him go.

## 62

In the first year of King Jing's reign, initially, Cui Zhu had two sons, Cheng and Qiang. After his wife died, he took a woman from Dongguo as his concubine, who bore him a son named Ming. The woman from Dongguo had her stepson Wujiu and her younger brother Yan serve as ministers for the Cui family. When Cheng committed a crime, the two ministers quickly dealt with him and declared Ming as heir apparent. Cheng requested to retire from the Cui clan, and Cui Zhu agreed; however, the two ministers refused to listen, saying, "Cui is a fief of the family's ancestral line; it cannot be given up." Cheng and Qiang became angry and reported this to Qing Feng. Qing Feng had a grudge against Cui Zhu and wanted his downfall. Cheng and Qiang killed Wujiu and Yan at Cui Zhu's home, and the entire family fled in panic. Cui Zhu was furious but had no one to rely on; he sent a eunuch as his charioteer and went to see Qing Feng. Qing Feng said, "I will kill them for you." He sent Lu Puwei, Cui Zhu's enemy, to attack the Cui clan. Cheng and Qiang were killed, and the entire Cui family was exterminated; Cui Zhu's wife committed suicide. Cui Zhu had nowhere to return, so he also committed suicide. Qing Feng became the prime minister and seized full power.

## 63

In the tenth month of the third year, Qing Feng went out hunting. Initially, after killing Cui Zhu, Qing Feng became even more arrogant; he was fond of drinking and hunting and no longer paid attention to government affairs. Qing She held power but had already developed internal discord. Tian Wenzhi said to Huanzi, "Disorder is about to arise." The Tian, Bao, Gao, and Luan clans conspired together against the Qing clan. Qing She mobilized his troops to surround Qing Feng's palace, but the four families' followers jointly attacked and defeated him. When Qing Feng returned, he could not enter and fled to Lu. Qi people reproached Lu, so Feng fled to Wu. Wu gave him Zhufang, where he gathered his clan and settled; they became wealthier than when they were in Qi. That autumn, the people of Qi moved Duke Zhuang's remains for reburial and publicly humiliated Cui Zhu's corpse in the marketplace to appease the populace.

## 64

In the ninth year, King Jing sent Yan Ying to Jin, where he privately told Shu Xiang, "The governance of Qi will ultimately return to the Tian clan. Although the Tian clan lacks great virtue, they have used public authority for private gain and shown kindness to the people; thus, the people love them." In the twelfth year, King Jing went to Jin, met Duke Ping, and wanted to join forces with him to attack Yan. In the eighteenth year, the king once again visited Jin and saw Duke Zhao. In the twenty-sixth year, he hunted in the outskirts of Lu and took the opportunity to enter Lu, where he visited with Yan Ying to inquire about Lu's rituals. In the thirty-first year, Duke Zhao of Lu fled to Qi after escaping from a crisis caused by the Ji clan. Qi wanted to grant him a fief of one thousand she, but Zi Jia stopped Duke Zhao. As a result, Duke Zhao requested Qi to attack Lu and take Yun for his residence.

## 65

In the thirty-second year, a comet appeared. King Jing was seated in Baisui Hall and sighed, saying, "How grand! Who could ever possess this?" All the ministers wept, but Yan Zi laughed; the king became angry. Yan Zi said, "I was laughing at how excessively flatter the ministers are." King Jing said, "A comet has appeared in the northeast, over Qi's celestial territory; I am worried about it." Yan Zi said, "Your Majesty builds high terraces and deep pools, levies taxes as if never satisfied, and imposes punishments so harshly that people fear them. If such is the case, a malevolent star will appear; what need is there to fear a comet?" The king asked, "Can it be averted?" Yan Zi said, "If gods can be summoned by prayers, they can also be driven away through rituals. The people's grievances and resentment number in the tens of thousands; yet Your Majesty orders one person to avert it, how can that possibly overcome so many voices?" At this time, King Jing was fond of building palaces and collecting dogs and horses; he lived in luxury, imposed heavy taxes, and enforced harsh punishments. Hence, Yan Zi used these words to advise him.

## 66

In the forty-second year, King Helu of Wu attacked Chu and entered Ying.

## 67

In the forty-seventh year, Yang Hu of Lu attacked his ruler but failed; he fled to Qi and requested Qi to attack Lu. Bao Zi advised King Jing, so Yang Hu was imprisoned. Yang Hu managed to escape and fled to Jin.

## 68

In the forty-eighth year, Qi met with Duke Ding of Lu in a friendly conference at Jiagu. Li Chu said, "Confucius understands ritual but is timid; I suggest we have the Lai people perform music and use it as an opportunity to seize Duke Lu. This way, we can achieve our goal." King Jing feared that Confucius serving as prime minister of Lu would become a threat to Qi's dominance, so he followed Li Chu's plan. During the meeting, when Lai musicians were brought in to perform, Confucius ascended the steps and ordered officials to arrest and execute the Lai performers. He then used ritual propriety to reprimand King Jing. King Jing was ashamed, so he returned the territories of Lu that Qi had occupied as an apology and withdrew from the meeting. In this year, Yan Ying died.

## 69

In the fifty-fifth year, Fan and Zhonghang rebelled against their ruler in Jin; as Jin attacked them fiercely, they came to request grain. Tian Qi wanted to incite rebellion and build support among the traitorous ministers. He persuaded King Jing, saying, "Fan and Zhonghang have repeatedly shown kindness to Qi; we cannot fail to come to their aid." He then sent Tian Qi to offer assistance and provided them with grain.

## 70

In the summer of the fifty-eighth year, King Jing's wife Yantong lost her legitimate son. King Jing's favored concubine Rui Ji gave birth to a son named Tu. However, Tu was young, and his mother was of low status with poor conduct; the ministers feared he would become heir, so they proposed selecting among the king's sons the one who was older and more virtuous as crown prince. King Jing was old and disliked discussions about succession. He also favored Tu's mother and wanted to make her son the heir, but hesitated to voice this publicly. So he told the ministers, "Let us just enjoy ourselves; what is there to worry about? Will the state ever lack a ruler?" In autumn, King Jing fell ill and ordered Guo Huizi and Gao Zhaozi to name his youngest son Tu as crown prince. He also expelled the other royal princes and exiled them to Lai. After King Jing died, Crown Prince Tu ascended the throne; this was Yan Ruzi. In winter, before he had been buried, the exiled royal princes feared punishment and all fled abroad. Tu's half-brothers Gongzi Shou, Ju, Qian fled to Wei, while Gongzi Ku and Yangsheng fled to Lu. The people of Lai sang, "When Duke Jing died, we were not allowed to bury him; when the three armies acted, we had no say in the plan. O commander, o commander, who are you taking sides with?"

## 71

In the first year of Yan Ruzi's reign, spring, Tian Qi pretended to serve Gao and Guo. Every time he attended court, he rode as a side rider and said, "You have gained the ruler's favor; all the ministers feel threatened and want to conspire rebellion." He also told the other ministers, "Gao Zhaozi is formidable. We should act before he does." The ministers followed his suggestion. In the sixth month, Tian Qi and Bao Mu led the ministers with troops into the palace to attack Gao Zhaozi. Zhaozi heard of this and joined Guo Huizi in rescuing the ruler. The king's forces were defeated, and Tian Qi's followers pursued them. Guo Huizi fled to Ju, whereupon they turned back and killed Gao Zhaozi. Yanyu fled to Lu. In August, Qi Bingyizi. After defeating the two prime ministers, Tian Qi sent someone to Lu to summon Gongzi Yangsheng. Yangsheng arrived in Qi and was secretly hidden at Tian Qi's home. On the Wusi day of the tenth month, Tian Qi invited the ministers and said, "The mother of Chang has a sacrifice with fish and beans; I hope you will all come to join me for a banquet." During the banquet, Tian Qi placed Yangsheng in a large sack and put it at the center of the gathering. He then opened the sack to reveal Yangsheng and declared, "This is the ruler of Qi!" The ministers all prostrated themselves in homage. They were about to swear an oath with the ministers and establish him as ruler, but Bao Mu was drunk. Qi falsely accused the ministers by saying, "I conspired with Bao Mu to jointly install Yangsheng." Bao Mu angrily said, "Have you forgotten King Jing's command?" The ministers looked at each other and seemed to regret their actions. Yangsheng stepped forward, kowtowed, and said, "If it is acceptable, then establish me; otherwise, I will stop here." Afraid that a disaster might arise, Bao Mu said again, "They are all King Jing's sons; why should this not be acceptable!" Thus they swore an oath and installed Yangsheng as ruler. This was Duke Dao. Duke Dao entered the palace and ordered someone to move Yan Ruzi to Tai, where he was killed in his tent. He also drove out Ruzi's mother, Ruizi. Ruizi had always been of low status and her son was young, so he held no power; the people of the state looked down on him.

## 72

In the first year of Duke Dao's reign, Qi attacked Lu and captured Huan and Chan. Originally, when Yangsheng was in exile in Lu, Ji Kangzi had given his sister to him in marriage. When he returned and ascended the throne, he sent someone to bring her back. Ji Ji had an affair with Ji Fanghou and revealed this situation, so Lu dared not send her back. Therefore, Qi attacked Lu to finally bring Ji Ji back. Because of Ji Ji's favored status, Qi returned the occupied lands to Lu.

## 73

Baozi had a conflict with Duke Dao and did not get along well with him. In the fourth year, Wu and Lu attacked southern Qi. Baozi assassinated Duke Dao and reported to Wu. King Fuchai of Wu wept outside the military gate for three days, intending to enter from the sea to punish Qi. The Qi forces defeated them, and so the Wu army withdrew. Jin's Zhao Mang attacked Qi, reaching Lai before withdrawing. The people of Qi jointly installed Duke Dao's son Ren as ruler; this was Duke Jian.

## 74

In the spring of the fourth year of Duke Jian's reign, originally when Duke Jian and his father Yangsheng were in exile in Lu together, Jianzhi was favored by them. After ascending the throne, he appointed him to hold power. Tian Chengzi feared him and frequently looked around in court. Yu Yang said to Duke Jian, "The Tians and the Jians cannot coexist; you must choose between them." He did not listen. Zi Wo at night, Tian Ni killed a man; they encountered each other and captured him to bring inside. The Tians were just reconciled, so they had the prisoner pretend to be ill and sent wine to the guard. After the guard became drunk, they killed him and escaped. Zi Wo made an oath with all the Tians at Chen Zong. Originally, Tian Bao wanted to become a retainer of Zi Wo; he sent Gongsun to speak with Bao, but the matter was stopped because Bao had a funeral. Later, he finally became his retainer and gained favor with Zi Wo. Zi Wo said to him, "I will drive out all the Tians and establish you as ruler; is that acceptable?" He replied, "I am already far removed from the Tians." Moreover, those who oppose us are no more than a few people; why drive out everyone!" He then informed the Tians. Zi Xing said, "If he gains power over the ruler and we do not act first, there will certainly be disaster for you." Zi Xing stayed in the palace.

## 75

In summer, on the Renshen day of the fifth month, Chengzi's brothers arrived at court with four chariots. Zi Wo was in a tent and came out to meet them; then they entered together and shut the door. Eunuchs guarded them, but Zi Xing killed the eunuchs. The ruler was drinking with his concubines at Tantai when Chengzi moved him to the sleeping quarters. The ruler took up a halberd and was about to attack them, when Tai Shi Ziyu said, "It is not that we are unwilling; we are going to eliminate the threat." Chengzi left and stayed at Ku, but upon hearing that the ruler was still angry and about to leave, he said, "Where is there no ruler!" Zi Xing drew his sword and said, "Hesitation is the thief of opportunity. Who among us does not belong to the Tian clan?" "If anyone does not kill Zi Wo, may he suffer as our entire clan suffers." Thus the ruler stopped. Zi Wo returned, gathered his followers to attack Weiwu and the main gate, but failed in both attempts; he then fled. The Tians pursued him. A native of Fengqiu captured Zi Wo and reported it, killing him at Guoguan. Chengzi was about to kill Dalu Zifang, but Tian Ni pleaded on his behalf and he was spared. Using the ruler's authority, they took a carriage from the road and exited through Yongmen Gate. Tian Bao offered him a carriage, but he refused it, saying, "Ni pleaded for me, and Bao gave me the carriage; I would be showing favoritism." "To serve Zi Wo yet show partiality toward his enemy, how could I face the scholars of Lu and Wei?"

## 76

On the Gengchen day, Tian Chang captured Duke Jian at Xuzhou. The ruler said, "If I had followed Yu Yang's advice earlier, I would not have come to this." On the Jiawu day, Tian Chang assassinated Duke Jian at Xuzhou.  Tian Chang then installed Duke Jian's younger brother Kuang as ruler; this was Duke Ping. After Duke Ping ascended the throne, Tian Chang served as his prime minister and monopolized Qi's government affairs. He carved out Anping and all lands to its east in Qi as fiefs for the Tian clan.

## 77

In the eighth year of Duke Ping's reign, Yue destroyed Wu. He died in the twenty-fifth year and was succeeded by his son JI, who became Duke Xuan.

## 78

In the fifty-first year of Duke Xuan's reign he died; his son Kānggōng Dai succeeded him as ruler. Tian Hui rebelled at Linqiu. In the second year of Duke Kang's reign, Han, Wei, and Zhao were first officially recognized as feudal lords. In the nineteenth year, Tian He, a great-grandson of Tian Chang, was first enfeoffed as a feudal lord and moved Duke Kang to the seacoast.

## 79

In the twenty-sixth year, Duke Kang died; thus the Lu lineage was extinguished from its ancestral rites. The Tian clan finally possessed the state of Qi and became King Weiwang, who was powerful throughout the world.

## 80

Grand Historian said: When I visited Qi, from Mount Tai to Langya, the land stretched northward to the sea, with fertile soil spanning two thousand li. Its people were broad-minded and open but often concealed their knowledge; this was part of their nature. With the wisdom of Tai Gong, who laid the foundation for the state, and with Duke Huan's prosperity, who cultivated good governance to convene alliances among feudal lords and was honored as a hegemon—was this not entirely fitting? How grand it is! Truly the style of a great state!
