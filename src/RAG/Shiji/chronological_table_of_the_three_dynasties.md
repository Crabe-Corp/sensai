# Chronological Table of the Three Dynasties

## 1

The Grand Historian said: The records of the Five Emperors and the Three Dynasties are already ancient. Before Yin, the genealogies of feudal lords cannot be traced; since Zhou, they can be recorded in some detail. Confucius arranged the Spring and Autumn Annals according to historical records, recording the first year, correcting dates, months, and days; indeed, it is very detailed. As for arranging the Book of Documents, it is brief, without years or months; some have them to some extent, but most are missing and cannot be recorded. Therefore, when in doubt, he transmitted the doubt; this shows his caution.

## 2

I have read genealogical records, and since Huangdi, they all include years. Examining their historical records, genealogies, and the transmission of the Five Virtues from beginning to end, all ancient texts are different and inconsistent. Master Confucius's failure to arrange years and months is not without reason! Therefore, I compiled a chronological table from Huangdi since the Five Emperors' genealogies and the Book of Documents to the Regency Period.

## 3

From the Yellow Emperor down to the Regency Era, this is the Chronological Table of Generations.

## 4

The table begins with the Yellow Emperor and ends at the Regency Period, compiling generations from the Five Emperors genealogies and the annals of the Book of Documents.

## 5

Zhang Fuzi asked Master Chu: "The Odes mention that Qi and Houji were both born without a father. Now, according to all the biographies, they all say there was a father, and each of these fathers is a son of Huangdi. Could this not contradict the Odes?"

## 6

Master Chu replied: "Not necessarily so. The Odes say that Qi was born from an egg and Houji came into being through the footprint of a human, which is meant to show their possession of heavenly mandate and sincere devotion. Ghosts and spirits cannot come into being by themselves; they require humans to give birth, so how could there be a birth without a father! One statement says there is a father and one says there is no father. To transmit certainty as certainty, and doubt as doubt, therefore both accounts are mentioned. Yao knew that Qi and Ji were all virtuous men, born by the will of Heaven, so he enfeoffed Qi with a territory seventy li in size. After more than ten generations it reached Tang, who became king over the world. Yao knew that the descendants of Houji would later become kings, so he further enfeoffed them with a territory one hundred li in size. In subsequent generations, spanning nearly a thousand years, it reached King Wen who gained dominion over the world. The Commentary on the Odes says: "Tang's ancestor was Qi, born without a father." Qi's mother and her sisters were bathing in the Xuanqiu River when a swallow carried an egg and dropped it. Qi's mother obtained it, held it in her mouth, mistakenly swallowed it, and thus gave birth to Qi. Qi was born virtuous; Yao appointed him as Minister of Instruction and gave his family the surname Zi Shi. "Zi" means "zi"; "Zi" means increase and magnification. The poets praised and eulogized it, saying: "The Shang dynasty's foundation is vast; the Mandate of Heaven descended with the black bird, which gave birth to Shang." "Shang" means simplicity; it was also known as Yin. King Wen's ancestor was Houji, who was also born without a father. Houji's mother was Jiang Yuan, who went out and saw the footprint of a great man. She stepped on it with her foot, felt something within her body, and thus gave birth to Houji. Jiang Yuan thought the child had no father, considered it lowly, and abandoned it in the middle of a road; cattle and sheep avoided treading on it. She took it to the mountains, where mountain dwellers raised it. She abandoned it again in a large marshland, where birds covered and fed it with their wings. Jiang Yuan found this strange; thus she realized that the child was destined by Heaven, so she took him back and raised him to grow up. Yao knew of his virtue and talent, appointed him as the Grand Minister of Agriculture, and gave his family the surname Ji Shi. "Ji" means origin. The poets praised and eulogize it, saying: "In the beginning of the people's birth," deeply cultivated and perfected, thus explaining the origin of Houji.' Confucius said: "In ancient times, Yao appointed Qi as Zi Shi because there was to be Tang. He gave Houji the surname Ji Shi because there would be King Wen." Great King appointed Jili, a clear omen of Heaven. Taibo went to Wu and thus the line of Yuan was born.' The Mandate of Heaven is hard to explain; only a sage can perceive it. Shun, Yu, Qi, and Houji were all descendants of Huangdi. Huangdi received the Mandate of Heaven and ruled over the world; his virtue and grace deeply benefited future generations, so his descendants were again established as Son of Heaven. This was Heaven's reward for virtue. People do not know this; they think these rulers simply arose from commoners and ordinary men. How could an ordinary man without any cause suddenly rise to become king over the world? It is because of the Mandate of Heaven."

## 7

"Among Huangdi's descendants, which kings ruled over the world for such a long time?"

## 8

It is said: "The transmission says that the emperor of the world, who pleads for redemption and saves the lives of his subjects among ten thousand common people, will have blessings lasting ten thousand generations." This refers to Huangdi. When the Five Policies are clear, rites and righteousness are cultivated; when one raises an army to wage war in accordance with the timing of Heaven and achieves success, he becomes king and receives blessings for a thousand generations. The King of Shu is a descendant of Huangdi; to this day, he resides five thousand li southwest of the Han dynasty and regularly comes to pay homage, offering tribute to the Han. Is it not because his ancestors had virtue whose grace continues to flow through later generations? How can morality and virtue be neglected! Rulers who wish to become kings should raise their sights and observe these examples. The great general of the Han, Huo Zimeng, whose name was Guang, was also a descendant of Huangdi. This can be spoken to those with broad knowledge and far-sightedness; it is indeed difficult to explain to those with shallow understanding. Why do I say this? In ancient times, feudal lords took their surnames from the names of their states. Huo was a state name. King Wu enfeoffed his younger brother Shuchu in Huo. Later, Duke Xianggong of Jin destroyed the ruler of Huo; descendants became commoners and moved back and forth to live in Pingyang. Pingyang is located in Hedong, which was Jin territory and later divided into the state of Wei. According to the Odes, it can also be considered part of the Zhou dynasty era. The Zhou dynasty began with Houji, who was born without a father. According to the genealogical traditions of the Three Dynasties, Houji had a father named Gaoxin; Gaoxin was a great-grandson of Huangdi. The Records of the Beginning and End of Huangdi say: "More than a hundred years after the rise of Han, there was a man neither short nor tall who came from the village of Baoyan, held power over the world, at that time an infant ruler wished to ride in a carriage." General Huo was originally from Pingyang Baoyan. When I served as a Lang, I met an official named Kaogong at the Qiting pavilion; he told me this. How remarkable it is!"
