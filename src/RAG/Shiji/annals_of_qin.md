# Annals of Qin

## 1

The ancestors of the Qin traced back to a descendant of Emperor Zhuanxu named Nüxiu. Nüxiu was weaving when a black bird fell and dropped an egg; Nüxiu swallowed it, and gave birth to a son named Daye. Daye married the daughter of Shaodian, who was named Nühua. Nühua gave birth to Dafei, who assisted Yu in controlling floods and managing water and land. After the task was completed, the emperor bestowed on him a black jade tablet. Yu accepted it and said: "It is not my ability alone that accomplished this; Dafei also served as an assistant." Emperor Shun said: "Ah, you Fei, for assisting Yu's achievements, I shall bestow upon you a black flag. Your descendants will greatly flourish." He then married a woman of the Yao clan, the daughter of Yu. Dafei bowed and accepted it; he assisted Shun in taming birds and beasts, many of which became domesticated. He was thus known as Baiyi. Shun bestowed upon him the surname Ying.

## 2

łoż Dafei had two sons: one was named Dalian, who became the ancestor of the Niaosu clan; the other was named Ruomu, who became the ancestor of the Fei clan. His great-great-grandson was Feichang; his descendants were either in China or among the Yi and Di peoples. During the time of Jie, ruler of Xia, Feichang left Xia and joined Shang; he served as a charioteer for Tang and helped defeat Jie at Mingtiao. Dalian's great-great-grandson was Mengxi, Zhongyan; he had a bird-like body but spoke human language. Emperor Taipu heard of this and divined whether to appoint him as a charioteer; the result was auspicious, so he appointed him as his charioteer and married him to one of his daughters. From Emperor Taipu onward, Zhongyan's descendants continued to achieve great accomplishments and assisted the Yin state; thus, many of the Ying clan became prominent and eventually became feudal lords.

## 3

His great-great-grandson named Zhongyu lived among the western Rong tribes and was in charge of protecting the western frontier. He gave birth to Feilian. Feilian had a son named Elai. Elai was strong and powerful, while Feilian was fast; father and son both served Yin Zhou with their talents and strength. When King Wu of Zhou attacked Zhou, he also killed Elai. At that time, Feilian was quarrying stone in the north for Zhou and returned without anything to report. He built an altar on Huotaishan Mountain to make a sacrifice; there he found a stone coffin with an inscription stating: "The Emperor commanded Chu Fu not to be involved in the chaos of Yin, and bestowed upon you this stone coffin as the Hua clan." After his death, he was buried on Huotaishan Mountain. Feilian also had a son named Jisheng. Jisheng gave birth to Mengzeng. Mengzeng was favored by King Cheng of Zhou; he was known as Zaigao Lang. Zaigao Lang had a son named Hengfu, and Hengfu had a son named Zao Fu. Zaofu was favored by King Miao of Zhou for his skill in driving chariots. He obtained a team of four fine horses: Ji, Wenli, Huailou, and Luer, and accompanied the king on a western tour; he enjoyed it so much that he forgot to return. When Xu Yanwang rebelled, Zaofu drove the chariot for King Miao and galloped back to Zhou at full speed, covering a thousand li in one day to quell the rebellion. King Miao granted Zaofu the city of Zhao as his fief, and from then on, Zaofu's descendants bore the surname Zhao. From Feilian to Jisheng down five generations until Zaofu, they lived separately in Zhao. Zhao Cui was a descendant of them. Elaige was the son of Feilian, who died young. He had a son named Nufang. Nufang gave birth to Panggao, who in turn had a son named Taiji; Taiji had a son named Daluo, and Daluo had a son named Feizi. Because of Zaofu's favor, they were all granted the city of Zhao and took the surname Zhao.

## 4

Feizi lived in Quanqiu; he loved horses and livestock and was good at breeding and raising them. The people of Quanqiu spoke highly of him to King Xiaowang of Zhou, who summoned Feizi and appointed him to oversee horse breeding between the Qian and Wei rivers; as a result, the horses multiplied greatly. King Xiaowang wanted to appoint him as the legitimate heir of Daluo. Shen Hou's daughter was married to Daluo and gave birth to a son, Cheng, who became his legitimate heir. Shen Hou then said to King Xiaowang: "In the past, my ancestor's daughter of Lishan married Rong Xuxuan and gave birth to Zhongyu. Because of their kinship, they returned to Zhou and protected the western frontier; because of this, the western frontier remained peaceful and harmonious. Now I have once again married my daughter to Daluo, giving birth to his legitimate son Cheng. By marrying into the family of Daluo repeatedly, the western Rong all submitted; this is why we became kings." "Your Majesty should consider this." Thus King Xiaowang said: "In the past, Boyi was in charge of breeding livestock for Shun; because his livestock multiplied greatly, he received land and was granted the surname Ying. Now his descendants have also bred many fine horses for me; I shall therefore grant them a portion of land as vassals." He granted them the fief of Qin, allowing them to continue the ancestral rites of the Ying clan and called them Qinying. The daughter of Shen Hou who had been married as Daluo's legitimate wife was also not abandoned, in order to maintain harmony with the western Rong.

## 5

Qinying gave birth to Qin Hou. Ten years after Qin Hou ascended the throne, he died. He had a son named Gongbo. Three years after Gongbo ascended the throne, he died.  He had a son named Qin Zhong.

## 6

Three years after Qin Zhong ascended the throne, King Li of Zhou was tyrannical and unjust; some feudal lords rebelled against him. The western Rong rebelled against the royal court and destroyed the Daluo clan of Quanqiu. When King Xuanwang of Zhou ascended the throne, he appointed Qin Zhong as a grand officer to punish the western Rong. The western Rong killed Qin Zhong. Twenty-three years after Qin Zhong's enthronement, he died at the hands of the Rong people. He had five sons; the eldest was named Zhuang Gong. King Xuanwang of Zhou summoned Zhuanggong and his four brothers, gave them 7,000 troops, and ordered them to attack the western Rong; they defeated them. Thus, Quanqiu, which had belonged to Daluo and the descendants of Qin Zhong, was once again granted to them, and they were appointed as dukes in charge of the western frontier.

## 7

Zhuanggong lived at his former residence in Xiquanqiu and had three sons, the eldest of whom was Shifu. Shifu said: "The Rong people killed my grandfather Zhong; I will not dare to enter the city until I have killed their king." He therefore led an attack on the Rong and passed his position as ruler to his younger brother Xianggong. Xianggong became the crown prince. Forty-four years after Zhuanggong's enthronement, he passed away; Crown Prince Xianggong succeeded him as ruler. In the first year of Xianggong's reign, he married his younger sister Miu Ying to King Fengwang. In the second year of Xianggong's reign, the Rong surrounded Quanqiu (Shifu). Shifu attacked them but was captured by the Rong people. After more than a year, he returned with Shifu. In the spring of the seventh year, King Youwang of Zhou deposed the crown prince because of Baosi and instead made Baosi's son his legitimate heir. He repeatedly deceived the feudal lords, who then rebelled against him. The western Rong and Quanrong allied with Shen Hou to attack Zhou; they killed King Youwang at the foot of Lishan Mountain. Meanwhile, Qin Xianggong led troops to rescue Zhou and fought with great determination, achieving notable merit. The Zhou dynasty fled from the Quanrong threat and moved eastward to Luoyi; Xianggong sent troops to escort King Pingwang of Zhou. King Pingwang enfeoffed Xianggong as a feudal lord and granted him the land west of Qi. He said: "The Rong are unjust and have invaded and seized our lands of Qi and Feng; if Qin can attack and drive out the Rong, then they may possess that land." They made an oath, and enfeoffed him with a noble title. Xianggong thus began to establish his state, engaging in diplomatic exchanges and ceremonial visits with the feudal lords. He sacrificed three luoju horses, three yellow oxen, and three male sheep at the western altar (Zhi) to worship the Supreme Deity. In his twelfth year of reign, Xianggong attacked the Rong and reached Qi, where he died. He had a son named Wengong.

## 8

In the first year of Wengong's reign, he resided in Xichui Palace. In the third year, Wengong led 700 soldiers on an eastern hunting expedition. In the fourth year, he reached the confluence of the Qian and Wei Rivers. He said: "Long ago, King Xiaowang granted my ancestor Qinying this land as a fief; later, he was finally enfeoffed as a feudal lord." He then divined to determine whether it would be suitable for settlement. The omen was auspicious, so they established a city there. In the tenth year, he first built the Fu Zhi altar and performed sacrifices with three sets of livestock (three oxen). łó In the thirteenth year, they first appointed historians to record events; many people were thus influenced and transformed. In the sixteenth year, Wengong led troops against the Rong, who were defeated and fled. Thus, Wengong gathered the remaining people of Zhou under his control; his territory extended to Qi, which he then offered eastward back to Zhou. In the nineteenth year, they obtained the Chenbao treasure. In the twentieth year, legal codes were first established to punish crimes involving three clans. In the twenty-seventh year, they attacked the large zhi trees in Nanshan and captured a particularly large deer from Feng. In the forty-eighth year, Wengong's crown prince died; he was posthumously honored with the title Jinggong. Jinggong's eldest son became the crown prince, who was a grandson of Wengong. In the fiftieth year, Wengong died and was buried in Xishan Mountain. Jinggong's son ascended the throne; this was Ninggong.

## 9

In the second year of Ninggong's reign, the ruler moved his residence to Pingyang. He sent troops to attack Dangshe. In the third year, they fought against Bo; King of Bo fled to Rong and thus Dangshe was destroyed. In the fourth year, Gongzi Hui of Lu assassinated his ruler Duke Yin. In the twelfth year, they attacked Dangshi and captured it. Ninggong was ten years old when he ascended the throne; he reigned for twelve years before dying, and was buried in Xishan. He had three sons, with his eldest son Wugong being named crown prince. Wugong's younger brother Dugong was the son of Lu Jizi, their same mother. He had a son named Chuzi. After Ninggong died, Dashu chang Fuji, Weilei, and Sanfu deposed the crown prince and enthroned Chuzi as ruler. In the sixth year of Chuzi's reign, Sanfu and others again conspired to have assassins kill Chuzi. Chuzi was five years old when he became ruler; he reigned for six years before dying. Sanfu and others then restored the former crown prince Wugong to the throne.

## 10

In the first year of Wugong's reign, he attacked Pengxi Shi and advanced as far as beneath Mount Hua, where he resided in the palace at Pingyang Feng. In the third year, he executed Sanfu and others, exterminating three clans for their role in killing Chuzi. Gao Qumai of Zheng killed his ruler Duke Zhao. In the tenth year, they attacked Gui and Ji Rong tribes and established them as counties for the first time. In the eleventh year, Du and Zheng were first established as counties. They destroyed Xiao Guo.

## 11

In the thirteenth year, Guan Zhifu and Liancheng of Qi killed their ruler Duke Xiang and enthroned Gongsun Wuzhi. Jin destroyed Huo, Wei, and Geng. Yonglin of Qi killed Wuzhi, Guan Zhifu, and others, then enthroned Duke Huan of Qi. Qi and Jin became powerful states.

## 12

In the nineteenth year, Quwo of Jin first became ruler of Jin. Duke Huan of Qi held hegemony at Juan.

## 13

In the twentieth year, Wugong died and was buried at Yong Pingyang. Initially, people were sacrificed to accompany him in death; sixty-six individuals were sacrificed along with him. He had one son named Bai, but Bai was not enthroned and instead granted the fief of Pingyang. His younger brother Dugong ascended the throne.

## 14

In the first year of Dugong's reign, he initially resided in Dayizheng Gong palace in Yongcheng. He sacrificed three hundred sacrificial animals at Fu Zhi to perform ancestral rites. They divined the suitability of residing in Yong. Later, his descendants would drink horse water from the Yellow River. Liang Bo and Rui Bo came to pay homage. In the second year, they first observed the summer solstice; dogs were used to ward off malevolent spirits. Dugong was thirty-three years old when he ascended throne and reigned for two years before dying. He had three sons: the eldest son Xuangong, the second son Chenggong, and the youngest son Mugu. The eldest son Xuangong ascended the throne.

## 15

In the first year of Xuangong's reign, Wei and Yan attacked Zhou, expelled King Hui, and enthroned Prince Tui. In the third year, Duke Zheng of Bo and Guo Shu killed Prince Tui and restored King Hui to power. In the fourth year, they built Mi Zhi. They fought against Jin at Heyang and won. In the twelfth year, Xuangong died. He had nine sons, none of whom were enthroned; instead, his younger brother Chenggong became ruler.

## 16

In the first year of Chenggong's reign, Liang Bo and Rui Bo came to court for homage. ões Duke Huan of Qi attacked Shan Rong and stationed his troops at Gudong.

## 17

Chenggong reigned for four years before dying. He had seven sons, none of whom succeeded him; instead, his younger brother Mugu became ruler.

## 18

In the first year of Duke Muhao's reign, he personally led an attack on Maojin and won a victory. In the fourth year, he married a woman from Jin; she was the elder sister of Crown Prince Shen Sheng of Jin. That same year, Duke Huan of Qi attacked Chu and advanced as far as Shaoling.

## 19

In the fifth year, Duke Xian of Jin destroyed Yu and Guo, capturing Duke Yu and his minister Bai Li Xi; this was because they had previously offered jade and horses as bribes to Yu. After capturing Bai Li Xi, he was sent as a concubine attendant for Duke Muhao of Qin's wife to Qin. Bai Li Xi fled from Qin and went to Wan, where he was captured by a border official in Chu. Duke Muhao heard that Bai Li Xi was virtuous and wished to redeem him with a generous ransom, but feared the people of Chu would not agree. So he sent someone to tell Chu: "My 媵 (ying) servant Bai Li Xi is among you; please allow me to redeem him for five black-sheep hides." The people of Chu then agreed and allowed his release. At that time, Bai Li Xi was already over seventy years old. Duke Muhao released him from imprisonment and discussed state affairs with him. Bai Li Xi replied, "I am a minister of a fallen state; how could I be worthy of being asked?" Duke Muhao said, "Your former ruler Duke Yu did not employ you properly, so he fell; this is not your fault." He insisted on asking for advice and held discussions with him for three days. Duke Muhao was greatly pleased, entrusted him with state affairs, and gave him the title "Wugu Da Fu." Bai Li Xi declined, saying: "I am not as capable as my friend Jian Shu; Jian Shu is virtuous but the world does not know of him. When I was once in distress while traveling in Qi and begging for food at Zhi, it was Jian Shu who took me in." I then wanted to serve Duke Wuzhi of Qi, but Jian Shu stopped me. Because of his warning, I escaped the troubles in Qi and went on to Zhou. Prince Tui of Zhou loved cattle; I approached him by offering my skills in raising cattle. When Prince Tui wanted to employ me, Jian Shu again stopped me; I left and thus avoided being executed. When I served Duke Yu of Yu, it was also Jian Shu who advised me." I knew that Duke Yu would not employ me. If I had truly desired personal gain and official rank, I would have stayed. I heeded his advice twice and thus escaped danger; once when I ignored it, I encountered the downfall of Duke Yu: this is why I know him to be virtuous." Hearing this, Duke Muhao sent generous gifts to invite Jian Shu and appointed him as an upper-ranking official.

## 20

In autumn, Duke Muhao personally led a campaign against Jin, fighting them at Hequ. Li Ji of Jin instigated a rebellion; Crown Prince Shen Sheng was killed in Xincheng, and Chonger and Yiwu fled into exile.

## 21

In the ninth year, Duke Huan of Qi convened a meeting with feudal lords at Kuichiu.

## 22

Duke Xian of Jin died. Li Ji's son Xi Qi was enthroned, but his minister Li Ke killed Xi Qi. Xun Xi enthroned Zhuozi, but Li Ke then killed Zhuozi and Xun Xi as well. Yiwu sent envoys to request assistance from Qin in order to return to Jin. Thus, Duke Muhao agreed and dispatched Bai Li Xi with troops to escort Yiwu back. Yiwu said: "If I truly become ruler, I will cede eight cities in the western part of Jin to Qin." After arriving and being enthroned, he sent Pi Zheng to thank Qin but broke the agreement by not giving the western cities of Jin, while also killing Li Ke. Pi Zheng heard about this and became fearful; thus he conspired with Duke Muhao, saying: "The people of Jin do not want Yiwu as ruler; they truly desire Chonger. Now that we have broken our agreement with Qin and killed Li Ke, it was all the plan of Lü Sheng and Xi Rui. I wish Your Majesty would urgently summon Lü and Xi with some advantage; once they arrive, we can then easily replace Yiwu with Chonger." Duke Muhao agreed and sent someone along with Pi Zheng to return, summoning Lü Sheng and Xi Rui back. Lü Sheng, Xi Rui, and others suspected that Pi Zheng had ulterior motives, so they claimed Yiwu ordered the killing of Pi Zheng. Pi Zheng's son Pi Bao fled to Qin and advised Duke Muhao: "The ruler of Jin is unjust, the people do not support him; he can be attacked." Duke Muhao replied: "If the common people are indeed dissatisfied, why were they able to execute their ministers?" "The fact that they could execute their ministers shows that it is well-organized." He did not listen openly, but secretly employed Pi Bao.

## 23

In the twelfth year, Guan Zhong and Xi Peng of Qi died.

## 24

Jin suffered from a drought and came to request grain aid. Pi Bao advised Duke Muhao not to give it, suggesting instead that they should take advantage of Jin's famine and attack them. Duke Muhao asked Gongsun Zhi, who said: "Famine or abundance are just natural occurrences; we cannot refuse to give aid." He asked Bai Li Xi, and Bai Li Xi said: "Yiwu has offended Your Majesty; what crime have his people committed?" Thus, following the advice of Bai Li Xi and Gongsun Zhi, they finally gave grain to Jin. Using boats for water transport and carts for land transport, grain was conveyed from Yong, with caravans stretching continuously all the way to Jiang.

## 25

In the fourteenth year, Qin suffered a famine and requested grain aid from Jin. The ruler of Jin consulted his ministers about it. Guoye said: "Take advantage of their famine to attack them; we can achieve great success." The ruler of Jin followed his advice. In the fifteenth year, they raised an army and prepared to attack Qin. Duke Muhao mobilized his troops, appointed Pi Bao as their commander, and personally went to fight them. On the Renxu day of the ninth month (October 10, 645 BC), Duke Muhao and Jin Hui Gong Yiwu engaged in battle at the Han region. The ruler of Jin abandoned his army to compete for advantage with Qin, and when he returned, his horses were exhausted. Duke Muhao rode with his subordinates in pursuit of the Jin ruler, but failed to catch him and was instead surrounded by the Jin army. The Jin forces attacked Duke Muhao, wounding him. At that time, the three hundred men from Qi Xia who had been fed and trained with good horses rushed into the Jin army. The Jin forces lifted their siege, allowing Duke Muhao to escape and capturing the ruler of Jin alive in return. Originally, Duke Muhao had lost a fine horse, which was found and eaten by more than three hundred local people from Qi Xia. Officials caught them and intended to punish them according to the law. Duke Muhao said: "A gentleman does not harm others because of livestock." "I have heard that eating the meat of a fine horse without drinking wine harms people." He then gave them all wine and pardoned them. The three hundred men, upon hearing that Qin was attacking Jin, all requested to join the campaign. When they saw Duke Muhao in a difficult situation, they rushed forward and fought fiercely for their lives, seeking to repay his kindness of sparing them after they had eaten his horse meat. Thus, Duke Muhao captured the ruler of Jin and brought him back, issuing an order in the state: "Prepare accommodations for me; I will sacrifice the ruler of Jin to Heaven." The Zhou emperor heard about it and said, "Jin is my fellow surname," and he pleaded for the ruler of Jin. Yiwu's sister was also Duke Muhao's wife, and upon hearing this, the lady put on mourning clothes, removed her shoes, and said: "My brothers are unable to rescue each other; I am disgracing Your Majesty's command." Duke Muhao said: "I captured the ruler of Jin as a feat, but now the Son of Heaven is pleading for him, and my wife is worried." He then made an oath with the ruler of Jin, agreed to return him, lodged him in a superior residence, and provided him with seven sets of sacrificial offerings. In the eleventh month, Duke Muhao returned Yiwu, the ruler of Jin; in return, Yiwu offered the western region of the Yellow River and sent his crown prince Yu as a hostage to Qin. Qin gave Yu's wife a noblewoman from their clan. At that time, Qin territory extended eastward to the Yellow River.

## 26

In the eighteenth year, Duke Huan of Qi died. In the twentieth year, Qin annexed Liang and Rui.

## 27

In the twenty-second year, Jin's Prince Yu heard that his father was ill and said: "Liang is my mother's hometown, yet it has been destroyed by Qin." "I have many brothers; if the ruler lives a hundred years, after his death, Qin will surely keep me as hostage, and Jin will be weak. They may also install another son instead." Prince Yu then fled back to Jin. In the twenty-third year, King Hui of Jin died, and his son Yu was enthroned as ruler. Qin resented Prince Yu for fleeing, so they welcomed Jin's prince Chonger from Chu and married him to the former wife of their son Yu. Chonger initially refused but later accepted. Duke Muhao treated him with increasing courtesy and generous hospitality. In the spring of the twenty-fourth year, Qin sent envoys to inform Jin's ministers that they wished to bring Chonger into power. Jin agreed, and thus sent people to escort Chonger. In the second month, Chonger was enthroned as the ruler of Jin; this is known as Duke Wen. Duke Wen sent people to kill Prince Yu. Prince Yu was later known as King Huai.

## 28

That autumn, Duke Xiang of Zhou's younger brother Dai, with the help of Di people, attacked the king; the king fled and took refuge in Zheng. In the twenty-fifth year, the Zhou king sent envoys to seek help from Jin and Qin regarding his crisis. Duke Muhao of Qin led troops to assist Duke Wen of Jin in restoring King Xiang, killing the king's younger brother Dai. In the twenty-eighth year, Duke Wen of Jin defeated Chu at Chengpu. In the thirtieth year, Duke Muhao assisted Duke Wen of Jin in besieging Zheng. Zheng sent envoys to tell Duke Muhao: "Destroying Zheng would strengthen Jin; this benefit goes to Jin, but brings no advantage to Qin." "A strong Jin is a concern for Qin." Duke Muhao then withdrew his troops and returned. Jin also withdrew its forces. In the winter of the thirty-second year, Duke Wen of Jin died.

## 29

A Zheng man offered to sell Zheng to Qin, saying: "I control its city gates; Zheng can be attacked by surprise." Duke Muhao consulted Jian Shu and Bai Li Xi, who replied: "Marching through several states for a thousand li to launch a surprise attack is rarely successful." "Moreover, if someone sells Zheng, how can we be sure that our own people have not informed Zheng of our plans?" "Therefore, it is not advisable." Duke Muhao said: "You do not understand; I have already made up my mind." He then mobilized the troops, appointing Meng Ming Shi, son of Bai Li Xi; Xi Qishu, son of Jian Shu; and Bai Yi Bing as commanders. On the day they set out, Bai Li Xi and Jian Shu both wept for them. Duke Muhao heard about this and became angry, saying: "I have sent out my troops, yet you two are discouraging them with your weeping. Why?" The two elders said: "We dare not discourage Your Majesty's army." "The troops are marching, and our sons have gone with them; we are old men, and we fear that by the time we return, it may be too late to see each other again. That is why we cried." After the two elders left, they told their sons: "If your army is defeated, it will surely be trapped in the Xiao Pass." In the spring of the thirty-third year, Qin's troops marched eastward, crossing Jin territory and passing through the northern gate of Zhou. King Zhou's grandson Man said: "The Qin army is disrespectful; what else awaits but defeat!" When the troops reached Hua, Zhang Gao, a merchant from Zheng who was selling cattle in Zhou, saw the Qin army and feared being killed or captured. He therefore presented his twelve oxen and said: "I have heard that your great state is going to punish Zheng; our ruler has carefully prepared defenses, but he sent me with these twelve oxen as a gift for your soldiers." The three Qin generals said to each other: "We were going to attack Zheng, but it seems that Zheng has already learned of our plan. If we go now, it will be too late." They then destroyed Hua. Hua was a border town of Jin.

## 30

At that time, the funeral rites for Duke Wen of Jin had not yet been completed. Crown Prince Xiang Gong was furious and said: "Qin has insulted our young ruler, taking advantage of the mourning period to destroy our Hua." He then donned black mourning clothes and mobilized troops, blocking the Qin forces at Xiao Pass. He attacked them and utterly defeated the Qin army, leaving not a single soldier to escape. They captured the three Qin generals and returned with them. Duke Wen's wife, a daughter of Qin, pleaded for the three captured Qin generals, saying: "The resentment Duke Muhao holds against these three men is deep in his bones. I wish to have them returned so that my lord may personally execute them and find satisfaction." Duke Wen of Jin agreed, returning the three Qin generals to Qin. When the three generals arrived, Duke Muhao wore plain clothes and met them in the suburbs. Facing the three men, he wept and said: "I was wrong not to follow the advice of Bai Li Xi and Jian Shu, thus disgracing you three. What crime have you committed?" "You must all work diligently to avenge this disgrace; do not be lazy." He then restored their official ranks as before and treated them even more generously.

## 31

In the thirty-fourth year, Crown Prince Shangchen of Chu assassinated his father King Chengwang and took the throne in his place.

## 32

Duke Muhao therefore once again sent Meng Ming Shi and others to lead troops against Jin, engaging them in battle at Pengya. Qin suffered a disadvantage and withdrew its forces back.

## 33

The Rong king sent You Yu to Qin. You Yu was originally a native of Jin, who had fled into the Rong and could speak the Jin language. Having heard that Duke Muhao was virtuous, he therefore sent You Yu to observe Qin. Duke Muhao showed You Yu the palaces and accumulated wealth of Qin. You Yu said: "If ghosts built these, they would be exhausted from their efforts. If humans did it, the people must have suffered greatly." Duke Muhao found this strange and asked: "In the Central States, governance is based on poetry, books, rites, music, laws, and regulations, yet disorder still occurs from time to time. Now, the Rong and Yi peoples have none of these; how can they govern? Is it not difficult?" You Yu smiled and said: "This is precisely why the Central States are in disorder. The most exalted Yellow Emperor himself established rites, music, laws and regulations as a model for the people to follow, yet could only achieve minor governance. In later generations, however, rulers became increasingly arrogant and licentious. They relied on the authority of laws and regulations to impose demands upon their subjects, and when the people were exhausted from this oppression, they resented their rulers for not showing benevolence or righteousness. Thus, resentment arose between ruler and subject, leading to usurpation and regicide, until entire clans were destroyed—this was all due to such causes. The Rong and Yi peoples are not like this. Their rulers embody pure virtue in dealing with their subjects, while the people hold loyalty and trust to serve their leaders. The governance of a single state is like the regulation of one body; they do not know how it is governed—this is truly the rule of a sage." At this, Duke Muhao withdrew and asked Neishi Liao: "I have heard that a neighboring state has a sage; this is a concern for an enemy state. Now You Yu is virtuous, and thus a threat to me—what should I do about it?" Neishi Liao said: "The Rong king resides in seclusion, and has not heard of the customs of the Central States. Your Majesty should try sending him female musicians to distract his mind; make a request for You Yu, in order to create an opening between them; keep him here without sending him back so that he misses his return date. The Rong king will find this strange and must surely suspect You Yu. Once there is a rift between ruler and minister, he can then be captured." Moreover, the Rong king loves music and will surely neglect his governance." Duke Muhao said: "Good." He then sat on the same mat with You Yu, shared food from the same set of vessels, and inquired thoroughly about its terrain and military situation. After this, he ordered Neishi Liao to send two groups of eight female musicians as a gift to the Rong king. The Rong king accepted them and was delighted; he did not return for an entire year. At this, Qin sent You Yu back to his homeland. You Yu repeatedly advised but was not heeded; Duke Muhao then sent people several times to secretly intercept You Yu, and thus You Yu left and surrendered to Qin. Duke Muhao received him with the courtesy of a guest and inquired about the conditions for attacking the Rong.

## 34

In the thirty-sixth year, Duke Muhao once again greatly increased rewards for Meng Ming and others, sending them to lead troops against Jin. They crossed the Yellow River and burned their boats, decisively defeating the Jin forces, capturing Wangguan and Hao in retaliation for the Battle of Xiao. The people of Jin all remained within their walled cities and dared not come out. At this, Duke Muhao personally crossed the Yellow River at Maojin, buried the corpses in Xiao, held a mourning ceremony for them, and wept for three days. He then made an oath before his troops, saying: "Alas, soldiers! Be quiet and do not make noise; I shall swear to you. Ancient people, with their gray-haired wisdom and careful deliberation, did nothing excessive." He made this oath to express regret for not following the strategies of Jian Shu and Bai Li Xi, so that future generations would remember his mistake. When virtuous men heard this, they all wept, saying: "Alas! The kindness of Duke Muhao toward others ultimately brought about the good fortune of Meng Ming."

## 35

In the thirty-seventh year, Qin used You Yu's strategy to attack the Rong king, annexing twelve states and expanding its territory by a thousand li, thus establishing dominance over the western Rong. The Son of Heaven sent Zhaogong Guo to congratulate Duke Muhao with gongs and drums made of gold. In the thirty-ninth year, Duke Muhao died and was buried in Yong. One hundred seventy-seven people were sacrificed with him; among them, three virtuous ministers of the Yu clan—named Yan Xi, Zhonghang, and Zhen Hu—were also included in those who died with him. The people of Qin mourned for them and composed the poem "Huangniao" to commemorate their sacrifice. Virtuous men said: "Duke Muhao of Qin expanded his territory and strengthened the state, subduing the powerful Jin to the east and dominating the Rong and Yi to the west. Yet he did not become a leader among the feudal lords—this is indeed appropriate. After his death, he abandoned his people and took his virtuous ministers to die with him. When a former king passed away, he still left behind virtue and laws for posterity; how much more so should one refrain from taking away the virtuous men, good ministers, and people who grieve over their loss? Therefore, it is known that Qin would never again be able to launch eastern campaigns." Duke Muhao had forty sons; his crown prince Dao succeeded him, becoming Duke Kanggong.

## 36

The first year of Duke Kanggong's reign. Last year, when Duke Muhao passed away, Duke Xianggong of Jin also died; Xianggong's younger brother named Yong was born to a Qin woman and remained in Qin. In Jin, Zhao Dun wanted to install him as ruler and sent Sui Hui to come and welcome Yong back; Qin sent troops to escort him as far as Linghu. Jin instead installed Xianggong's son and attacked the Qin forces, which were defeated; Sui Hui then fled to Jin. In the second year, Qin attacked Jin and captured Wucheng as retaliation for the Battle of Linghu. In the fourth year, Jin attacked Qin and captured Shaoliang. In the sixth year, Qin attacked Jin and took Jima. A battle was fought at Hequ, where the Jin army suffered a major defeat. The people of Jin were concerned that Sui Hui, who was in Qin, would cause trouble. They therefore sent Wei Chouyu to pretend to defect and conspired with him, deceiving him into returning; thus, Sui Hui returned to Jin. Duke Kanggong reigned for twelve years before dying, succeeded by his son Duke Gonggong.

## 37

In the second year of Duke Gonggong's reign, Zhao Chuan of Jin assassinated his ruler King Linggong. In the third year, King Zhuangwang of Chu grew powerful and led troops north to Luo, where he inquired about the Zhou dynasty's tripod cauldrons. Duke Gonggong reigned for five years before dying; his son Duke Huangong succeeded him.

## 38

In the third year of Duke Huangong's reign, Jin defeated one of our generals. In the tenth year, King Zhuangwang conquered Zheng and defeated Jin forces north of the Yellow River. At that time, Chu was dominant and convened alliances among the feudal lords. In the twenty-fourth year, Duke Li of Jin had just ascended the throne and concluded an alliance with Duke Huangong of Qin across the Yellow River. After returning, however, Qin violated the treaty and conspired with Di to attack Jin. In the twenty-sixth year, Jin led feudal lords in an attack on Qin; the Qin army was defeated and fled, pursued as far as Jing before retreating. Duke Huangong reigned for twenty-seven years before dying; his son, Duke Jinggong, succeeded him.

## 39

In the fourth year of Duke Jinggong's reign, Luan Shu of Jin assassinated his king, Duke Li. In the fifteenth year, Qin rescued Zheng and defeated the Jin forces at Lu. At that time, Duke Dao of Jin was the leader among feudal lords. In the eighteenth year, Duke Dao of Jin grew powerful and frequently convened feudal lords to lead them in attacks on Qin, defeating its army. The Qin forces retreated, and the Jin troops pursued them across the Jing River to Yulin before returning. In the twenty-seventh year, Duke Jinggong went to Jin and formed an alliance with King Ping of Jin; however, he later violated it. In the thirty-sixth year, Gongzi Wei of Chu assassinated his king and declared himself ruler, becoming King Lingwang. Duke Jinggong's maternal younger brother, Houzi Zhen, was favored; however, because he was wealthy, someone slandered him. Fearing punishment, he fled to Jin with a thousand chariots and retinue. King Ping of Jin said: "If Houzi is so wealthy, why did he flee on his own?" He replied: "The Duke of Qin is unjust. I feared punishment and wanted to return only in the reign of a later generation." In the thirty-ninth year, King Lingwang of Chu was powerful and convened feudal lords at Shen to become their leader, killing Qing Feng of Qi. Duke Jinggong reigned for forty years before dying; his son became Duke Aigong. Houzi returned once again to Qin.

## 40

In the eighth year of Duke Aigong's reign, Gongzi Qiji of Chu assassinated King Lingwang and declared himself ruler, becoming King Pingwang. In the eleventh year, King Pingwang of Chu came to seek a Qin princess as a wife for his crown prince Jian. Upon arriving in the state, he found her beautiful and took her as his own wife. In the fifteenth year, King Pingwang of Chu wanted to execute Jian, who then fled; Wu Zixu fled to Wu. The Jin royal house had become weak while the six powerful ministers grew strong, and they desired to attack each other internally; thus, for a long time, Qin and Jin did not engage in war against one another. In the thirty-first year, King Helu of Wu and Wu Zixu attacked Chu; the king of Chu fled to Sui, and Wu forces entered Ying. Chu's official Shen Baoxu came to plead for help, fasting for seven days while crying day and night. At this, Qin dispatched five hundred chariots to rescue Chu, defeating the Wu forces. After the Wu army withdrew, King Zhao of Chu was able to return to Ying. Duke Aigong reigned for thirty-six years before dying. The crown prince Yigong died early and did not ascend the throne; his son was instead installed as ruler, becoming Duke Huigong.

## 41

In the first year of Duke Huigong's reign, Confucius served as an official in Lu. In the fifth year, Jin's ministers Zhongxing and Fan clans rebelled against Jin; Jin sent the Zhi clan and Zhao Jianzi to attack them. The Fan and Zhongxing clans fled to Qi. Duke Huigong reigned for ten years before dying; his son was installed as Duke Daogong.

## 42

In the second year of Duke Daogong's reign, Tian Qi, a minister of Qi, assassinated his ruler Ruzi and installed his elder brother Yangsheng, who became known as Duke Daogong. In the sixth year, Wu defeated the army of Qi. The people of Qi assassinated Duke Daogong and installed his son as Duke Jian Gong. In the ninth year, Duke Dinggong of Jin formed an alliance with King Fuchai of Wu; they vied for supremacy at Huangchi, and in the end, Jin prevailed over Wu. Wu grew strong and became arrogant toward the Central States. In the twelfth year, Tian Chang of Qi assassinated Duke Jian Gong and installed his younger brother as Duke Pinggong, with Chang serving as prime minister. In the thirteenth year, Chu annexed Chen. Duke Daogong of Qin reigned for fourteen years before dying; his son became Duke Ligong. Confucius died in the twelfth year of Duke Daogong's reign.

## 43

In the second year of Duke Ligong's reign, envoys from Shu came with tribute. In the sixteenth year, ditches were dug along the riverbanks. With an army of twenty thousand, they attacked Dali and captured its royal city. In the twenty-first year, Pingyang was first established as a xian (district). Jin captured Wucheng. In the twenty-fourth year, chaos erupted in Jin; Zhibo was killed and his territory was divided among Zhao, Han, and Wei. In the twenty-fifth year, Zhi Kai fled with townspeople to seek refuge. In the thirty-third year, they attacked Yiqu and captured its king. In the thirty-fourth year, a solar eclipse occurred. Duke Ligong died; his son was installed as ruler and became Duke Zao Gong.

## 44

In the second year of Duke Zao's reign, Nanzheng rebelled. In the thirteenth year, Yiqu launched an attack and reached Weinan. In the fourteenth year, Duke Zao died; his younger brother was installed as ruler and became Huai Gong.

## 45

In the fourth year of Huai Gong's reign, Shuzhang Chao and high-ranking ministers surrounded him; Huai Gong committed suicide. Huai Gong's crown prince was Zhaozi, who died early; the ministers then installed Zhaozi's son as ruler, who became Duke Linggong. Duke Linggong was a grandson of Huai Gong.

## 46

In the sixth year of Duke Linggong's reign, Jin built a city at Shaoliang; Qin attacked it. In the thirteenth year (of Duke Linggong's reign), they built a fortress at Jigu. Duke Linggong died; his son Xianggong did not ascend the throne, and instead Duke Linggong's younger uncle Daozi was installed as ruler, becoming known as Jian Gong. Jian Gong was the younger brother of Zhaozi and thus a son of Huai Gong.

## 47

In the sixth year (of Jiangong's reign), officials were first ordered to wear swords. Ditches were dug along the Luo River. A city was built at Chongquan. In the sixteenth year he died; his son became ruler and was known as Huigong.

## 48

In the twelfth year (of Huigong's reign), a son, Chuzi, was born. In the thirteenth year they attacked Shu and captured Nanzheng. Huigong died, and Chuzi was installed as ruler.

## 49

In the second year (of Chuzi's reign), Shuzhang Gai welcomed Xianggong, the son of Duke Linggong from the western bank of the Yellow River, and installed him as ruler. Chuzi and his mother were killed and drowned in a nearby ravine. In the past, Qin had frequently changed rulers; as a result, its officials and subjects became disordered, so Jin regained strength and seized western bank territories from Qin.

## 50

In the first year (of Xianggong's reign), he abolished the practice of human sacrifice upon death. In the second year, they built a city at Linyang. On the Gengyin day of the first month (January 21, 381 BC), Duke Xiao was born. In the eleventh year, Zhou's chief historian Dan met Xianggong and said: "Originally united with Qin but later separated, after five hundred years they will reunite again; seventeen (seven) years after their reunion, a hegemon or overlord shall emerge." In the sixteenth year, peach trees bloomed in winter. In the eighteenth year, it rained metal at Linyang. In the twenty-first year (of Xianggong's reign), Qin fought Jin at Shimen, beheading six thousand enemy soldiers; the Son of Heaven congratulated them with embroidered robes. In the twenty-third year (of Xianggong' reign), Qin fought Wei and Jin at Shaoliang, capturing their general Gongsun Cuo. In the twenty-fourth year (of Xianggong' reign), Xianggong died; his son Xiao Gong was installed as ruler, already twenty-one years old.

## 51

In the first year of Xiao Gong's reign, there were six powerful states east of the Yellow River and Taihang Mountains: Qi under Duke Wei, Chu under King Xuan, Wei under Duke Hui, Yan under Duke Dao, Han under Duke Ai, and Zhao under Marquis Cheng. There were more than ten small states between Huai and Si rivers. Chu, Wei, and Qin shared borders with each other. Wei built a long wall from Zheng along the Luoyang River to the north, controlling Shangjun Commandery. Chu controlled from Hanzhong in the north down to Ba and Qianzhong in the south. The Zhou royal house had declined, and feudal lords vied for power, competing to conquer one another. Qin was isolated in Yongzhou, did not participate in alliances among the Central States' feudal lords, and was treated as a barbarian. Duke Xiao thus extended kindness, supported orphans and widows, recruited warriors, and clarified rewards for meritorious service. He issued an edict throughout the state: "In former times, my predecessor Duke Mu, from between Qi and Yong, cultivated virtue and wielded military might. He pacified the chaos in Jin to the east, setting the Yellow River as a boundary; he dominated the Rong and Di tribes to the west, expanding territory by thousands of li. The Son of Heaven conferred hegemony upon him, and all feudal lords offered congratulations, laying an illustrious foundation for future generations." "However, in the past, during the reigns of Duke Li, Duke Zao, Duke Jian, and Chuzi, there was internal unrest; the state faced domestic troubles and had no time for external affairs. The Three Jins attacked and seized the western bank territories from our ancestors, and the feudal lords looked down upon Qin—nothing could be more humiliating." "When Duke Xiang ascended the throne, he stabilized and pacified the borders, moved the capital to Linyang, and wished to launch eastern campaigns to reclaim the territories once held by Duke Mu and restore his policies." "I often think of my ancestors' aspirations, which deeply pain me in my heart." "Any guest or official among you who can devise an extraordinary strategy to strengthen Qin, I shall bestow with high office and share territory with him." At this time, he dispatched troops eastward to encircle Shancheng and westward to slay the chieftain of Rong.

## 52

Wei Yang heard about this decree, traveled west into Qin, and through Jing Jian requested an audience with Duke Xiao.

## 53

In the second year (of Xiao Gong's reign), the Son of Heaven presented sacrificial meat as a token of recognition.

## 54

In the third year, Wei Yang advised Duke Xiao to reform laws and strengthen punishments; internally, he promoted agriculture, and externally, he encouraged rewards and penalties for battlefield valor. Duke Xiao approved of this plan. Gan Long, Du Zhi, and others disagreed, and they debated with him over the matter. In the end, Wei Yang's reforms were implemented; the common people suffered from them; After three years, the people found it convenient. He was then appointed as Zuo Shuzhang (Left Commander). The details of these events are recorded in the Discourses on Lord Shang.

## 55

In the seventh year, he met Duke Hui of Wei at Duping. In the eighth year, Qin fought against Wei at Yuanli and achieved a victory. In the tenth year, Wei Yang was appointed Daliangzao (a high-ranking official) and led troops to besiege Anyi of Wei, which surrendered. In the twelfth year, construction began on Xianyang; the Jique was built, and Qin moved its capital there. Small towns and villages were consolidated into large xians (districts), each governed by a county magistrate, totaling forty-one counties. The boundaries of farmland were standardized with roads and ditches. Territories to the east crossed the Luo River. In the fourteenth year, land taxes were first levied. In the nineteenth year, the Son of Heaven conferred the title of hegemon upon Qin. In the twentieth year, all feudal lords offered their congratulations. Qin sent Gongzi Shaoguan to lead an army and join other feudal lords at Fengze to pay homage to the Son of Heaven.

## 56

In the twenty-first year, Qi defeated Wei at Maling.

## 57

In the twenty-second year, Wei Yang attacked Wei and captured Gongzi Ang of Wei. Wei Yang was enfeoffed as a marquis with fiefdoms, given the title Lord Shang Jun (Lord Shang).

## 58

In the twenty-fourth year [of Duke Xiao's reign], Qin fought against Jin at Yanmen and captured their general Wei Cuo.

## 59

Duke Xiao died; his son Huwen Jun was installed as ruler. In this year, Wei Yang (Lord Shang) was executed. When Lord Shang first implemented laws in Qin, the laws were not enforced because the crown prince violated them. Lord Shang said: "The failure to enforce the law stems from the nobles and relatives of high rank." "If Your Majesty truly wishes to implement the law, it must begin with the crown prince." "The crown prince cannot be tattooed; let us tattoo his tutors and teachers instead." Thus, the law was strictly enforced, and the people of Qin were governed effectively. After Duke Xiao died and the crown prince ascended the throne, many members of the royal family resented Lord Shang. Fleeing for his life, he was accused of rebellion and ultimately torn apart by chariots as a warning to the state of Qin.

## 60

In the first year of Huwen Jun's reign, envoys from Chu, Han, Zhao, and Shu came to pay homage. In the second year, the Son of Heaven sent congratulations. In the third year, the king was crowned. In the fourth year, the Son of Heaven presented sacrificial meat from the rites for Wen and Wu. Qi and Wei also declared themselves kings.

## 61

In the fifth year, Yinjin native Xi Shou was appointed Da Liangzao (a high-ranking official title). In the sixth year, Wei ceded Yinjin; Yinjin was renamed Ningqin. In the seventh year, Gongzi Ang fought against Wei, captured their general Long Jia, and beheaded 80,000 enemy soldiers. In the eighth year, Wei ceded the Hexi region to Qin. In the ninth year, crossing the Yellow River, they captured Fenyin and Pishi. They met with King Wei at Ying. Jiao was besieged and surrendered. In the tenth year, Zhang Yi became prime minister of Qin. Wei ceded fifteen counties in Shangjun to Qin. In the eleventh year, Yiqiu was established as a county. Jiao and Quwo were returned to Wei. The ruler of Yiqiu became a vassal subject. Shaoliang was renamed Xiayang. In the twelfth year, the practice of holding a winter solstice ceremony began for the first time. On the Wuwu day of the fourth month in the thirteenth year, King Wei was declared king; Han also declared its ruler a king. Qin sent Zhang Yi to attack and capture Shan, expelling its people and transferring them to Wei.

## 62

In the fourteenth year, it was changed to the first year [of a new era]. In the second year, Zhang Yi met with high-ranking officials from Qi and Chu at Niesang. In the third year, the crown princes of Han and Wei came to pay homage. Zhang Yi became prime minister of Wei. In the fifth year, the king toured as far north as Beihe River. In the seventh year, Yuechi became prime minister of Qin. Han, Zhao, Wei, Yan, Qi, and the Xiongnu led a joint attack on Qin. Qin sent Shuzhang Ji to fight against them at Xiuyu, captured their general Shen Cha, defeated Zhao's Gongzi Ke and Han's Crown Prince Huan, and beheaded 82,000 enemy troops. In the eighth year, Zhang Yi once again became prime minister of Qin. In the ninth year, Sima Cuo attacked Shu and destroyed it. Ten years later, Qin captured Zhongdu and Xiyang from Zhao. In that year, Crown Prince Cang of Han came as a hostage to Qin. Qin attacked and captured Shizhang from Han. Qin defeated Zhao's general Ni in battle. Qin attacked and captured twenty-five cities from Yiqiu. In the eleventh year, Cui Li Ji attacked Jiao in Wei and forced it to surrender. Qin defeated Han at Anmen, beheaded 10,000 soldiers, and their general Xi Shou fled. Gongzi Tong was enfeoffed in Shu. The ruler of Yan ceded the throne to his minister Zi Zhi. In the twelfth year, King of Qin met with Liang Wang at Linjin. Shuzhang Ji attacked Zhao and captured their general Zhuang. Zhang Yi became prime minister of Chu. In the thirteenth year, Shuzhang Zhang attacked Chu at Danyang, captured their general Qu Gai, and beheaded 800,000 enemies; Qin then attacked the Hanzhong region of Chu again, captured six hundred li of territory, and established the Hanzhong Commandery. Chu besieged Yongshi; Qin sent Shuzhang Ji to assist Han and launched an eastern attack on Qi, reaching Man to help Wei in its campaign against Yan. In the fourteenth year [of King Huiwen's reign], Qin attacked Chu and captured Zhaoling. Dan and Li submitted to Qin; Shu prime minister Zhuang killed the ruler of Shu, who then surrendered to Qin.

## 63

King Huai died; his son King Wu was enthroned. Han, Wei, Qi, Chu, and Yue all submitted to Qin as vassals.

## 64

In the first year of King Wu's reign, he met with King Hui of Wei at Linjin. The prime minister of Shu, Zhuang, was executed. Zhang Yi and Wei Zhang both left for the east to go to Wei. Qin launched a campaign against Yiqiu, Dan, and Li. In the second year [of King Wu's reign], the office of Chancellor was first established; Cui Li Ji and Gan Mao were appointed as Left and Right Chancellors, respectively. Zhang Yi died in Wei. In the third year [of King Wu's reign], he met with King Xiang of Han outside Linjin. Nan Gong Jie died; Cui Li Ji became prime minister of Han. King Wu said to Gan Mao, "I wish for my chariot to pass through the Three Rivers region and glimpse the Zhou royal court; if I die with this accomplished, I shall have no regrets." That autumn, King Wu sent Gan Mao and Shuzhang Feng to attack Yiyang. In the fourth year [of King Wu's reign}}, Yiyang was captured, and 60,000 enemy heads were beheaded. Crossing the Yellow River, they built a city at Wusui. The crown prince of Wei came to pay homage. King Wu was strong and enjoyed physical contests; the wrestlers Ren Bi, Wu Huo, and Meng Yue all attained high-ranking positions. The king and Meng Yue lifted a bronze tripod together, causing the latter to break his knee. In August, King Wu died. The Meng Yue family was exterminated. King Wu had taken a woman from Wei as his queen, but they had no children. His half-brother was enthroned; this ruler became known as King Zhaoxiang. King Zhaoxiang's mother, a native of Chu, surnamed Mi, was called the Queen Dowager Xuan. When King Wu died, King Zhaoxiang had been a hostage in Yan; the people of Yan sent him back, and he was enthroned.

## 65

In the first year [of King Zhaoxiang's reign], Yan Jun Ji became prime minister. Gan Mao left for Wei. In the second year, a comet appeared in the sky. Shuzhang Zhuang and high-ranking officials, feudal lords, and gongzi (princes) who conspired to rebel were all executed. The queen dowager of King Huwen also did not die a natural death. The widow of Duke Dao Wu was sent back to Wei. In the third year, [King Zhaoxiang] was crowned. He met with King of Chu at Huangji and ceded Shangyong to Chu. In the fourth year, Qin captured Puban. A comet appeared in the sky. In the fifth year, King Wei came to pay homage at Yingting; Qin returned Puban to Wei once again. In the sixth year, Lord Hui of Shu rebelled; Sima Cuo pacified Shu and restored order. Shuzhang Huan attacked Chu and beheaded 20,000 of its soldiers. Jingyang Jun was sent as a hostage to Qi. A solar eclipse occurred, and daylight turned dark. In the seventh year [of King Zhaoxiang's reign], Xincheng was captured. Chu Li zi died. In the eighth year, General Mi Rong was sent to attack Chu and captured Xinshi. Qi sent Zhang Zi, Wei sent Gongsun Xi, and Han sent Bao Yuan to jointly attack Fancheng in Chu; they captured Tangmei. Zhao defeated Zhongshan; its ruler fled and eventually died in Qi. Prince Jing of Wei and Prince Zhang of Han were enfeoffed as feudal lords. In the ninth year, Mengchang Jun Xue Wen came to serve as chancellor of Qin. Huan attacked Chu, captured eight cities, and killed its general Jing Kuai. In the tenth year, King Huai of Chu came to pay homage to Qin, but Qin detained him. Xue Wen was dismissed from office for accepting bribes in gold. Lou Huan became chancellor. In the eleventh year, Qi, Han, Wei, Zhao, Song, and Zhongshan—the five states—jointly attacked Qin; they advanced as far as Yanshi before retreating. Qin ceded the area north of the Yellow River and Fengling to Han and Wei in order to make peace. A comet appeared. King Huai of Chu fled to Zhao, but Zhao refused him entry; he was returned to Qin, where he died and was sent back for burial. In the twelfth year, Lou Huan was dismissed from office, and Ranghou Wei Ran became chancellor. Five hundred thousand dan of grain were given to Chu.

## 66

In the thirteenth year, Xiang Shou attacked Han and captured Wushi. Zuogeng Bai Qi attacked Xincheng. Wudaidfu Li fled and defected to Wei. Ren Bi was appointed as the governor of Hanzhong. In the fourteenth year, Zuogeng Bai Qi attacked Han and Wei at Yique, beheaded 240,000 soldiers, captured Gongsun Xi, and seized five cities. In the fifteenth year, Daliangzao Bai Qi attacked Wei, captured Yuan, but returned it to them again. He attacked Chu and captured Wan. In the sixteenth year, Zuogeng Cuo captured Zhi and Deng. Wei Ran was dismissed; Gongzi Shi Wan, Gongzi Kui Deng, and Wei Ran Tao were enfeoffed as vassal lords. In the seventeenth year, Chengyang Jun came to pay homage, along with King of Dongzhou. Qin renamed Yuan as Puban and Pishi. The king went to Yiyang. In the eighteenth year, Cuo attacked Yuan and Heyong, breached the bridge, and captured them. In the nineteenth year, the king declared himself Western Emperor, while Qi declared itself Eastern Emperor; both later abandoned these titles. Lü Li returned from exile and came back to Qin. Qi defeated Song; King of Song, who was in Wei, died in Wen. Ren Bi died. In the twentieth year, the king went to Hanzhong, then to Shangjun and Behe. In the twenty-first year, Cuo attacked Heinei in Wei. Wei offered Anyi to Qin; Qin expelled its inhabitants and recruited people from Hedong, granting them noble ranks and pardoning criminals for relocation. Jingyang Jun was enfeoffed in Wan. In the twenty-second year, Meng Wu attacked Qi. Hedong was divided into nine counties. Met with King of Chu in Wan. Met with the King of Zhao at Zhongyang. In the twenty-third year, Wei Sili joined forces with the three Jin states and Yan to attack Qi; they defeated them at the western bank of the Ji River. The king met with King Wei in Yiyang, and with King Han in Xincheng. In the twenty-fourth year, he met with King of Chu at Yan, then again at Rang. Qin captured Ancheng from Wei and advanced as far as Daliang; however, the armies of Yan and Zhao came to its rescue, forcing Qin's troops to withdraw. Wei Ran was dismissed from his position as chancellor. In the twenty-fifth year, two cities of Zhao were captured. Met with King Han in Xincheng and with King Wei in Xinmingyi. In the twenty-sixth year, convicts were pardoned and relocated to Rang. Hou Ran was reinstated as chancellor. In the twenty-seventh year, Cuo attacked Chu. Convicts were pardoned and resettled in Nanyang. Bai Qi attacked Zhao and captured the city of Guanglang. Qin also sent Sima Cuo from Longxi, through Shu to attack Qianzhong in Chu, and captured it. In the twenty-eighth year, Daliangzao 白起 attacked Chu, capturing Yan and Deng, and pardoned convicts to relocate them there. In the twenty-ninth year, Daliangzao Bái Qǐ attacked Chu, captured Ying and established it as Nanjun; King of Chu fled. The ruler of Zhou came. The king met with the King of Chu at Xiangling. Bai Qi was conferred the title Wuan Jun. In the thirtieth year, Shou Ruo of Shu attacked Chu, captured Wujun, and took control of the southern bank of the Yangtze River, establishing it as Qianzhong Jun. In the thirty-first year, Bai Qi attacked Wei and captured two cities. The people of Chu rebelled against our forces in the southern bank region. In the thirty-second year, Chancellor Ranghou attacked Wei and advanced to Daliang. He defeated Bao Yuan, beheaded 40,000 troops; Yuan fled, and Wei offered three counties in exchange for peace. In the thirty-third year, Keqing Hu Yang attacked Juan, Caiyang, and Changshe in Wei and captured them. He defeated Mang Mao at Huayang, beheading 150,000 enemy soldiers. Wei ceded Nanyang to Qin in order to make peace. In the thirty-fourth year, Qin combined Shangyong territory of Wei and Han into one commandery; Nanyang exiles were relocated there. In the thirty-fifth year, Qin assisted Han, Wei, and Chu in attacking Yan. Nanyang Jun was first established as a commandery. In the thirty-sixth year, Keqing Zao attacked Qi and captured Gang and Shou, which were granted to Ranghou. In the thirty-eighth year, Zhonggeng Hu Yang attacked Eyu in Zhao but failed to capture it. In the fortieth year, Crown Prince Dao died in Wei and was returned for burial in Zhiyang. In summer of the forty-first year, Qin attacked Wei and captured Xingqiu and Huai. In the forty-second year, Anguo Jun was appointed crown prince. In the tenth month, Empress Dowager Xuan passed away and was buried at Lishan in Zhiyang. In September, Ranghou left for Tao. In the forty-third year, Wuan Jun Bai Qi attacked Han, captured nine cities, and beheaded 50,000 enemies. In the forty-fourth year, they attacked Nanyang in Han and captured it. In the forty-fifth year, Wudaidfu Ben attacked Han and captured ten cities. Ye Yang Jun Kui left his state but died before reaching his destination. In the forty-seventh year, Qin attacked Shangdang in Han; Shangdang surrendered to Zhao. Taking advantage of this, Qin then attacked Zhao, and Zhao mobilized troops to resist, leading to a standoff between the two sides. Qin sent Wuan Jun Bai Qi to attack and decisively defeated Zhao at Changping, killing over 400,000 Sui soldiers. In the tenth month of the forty-eighth year, Han offered Yuanyong to Qin as a tribute. The Qin army was divided into three divisions. Wuan Jun returned. Wang He led the campaign against Zhao (Wuan) Pilao and captured it. Sima Geng pacified Taiyuan to the north, completely securing Shangdang of Han. In the first month, the military campaign ended and forces were redeployed to defend Shangdang. In the tenth month of that year, Wudaidfu Ling attacked Handan in Zhao. In the first month of the forty-ninth year, more troops were dispatched to assist Ling. Ling performed poorly in battle and was relieved of his post; Wang He took over command instead. In the tenth month, General Zhang Tang attacked Wei, but Cai 尉 Juan failed to defend and retreated; he was executed upon his return. In the tenth month of the fiftieth year, Wuan Jun Bai Qi committed a crime and was demoted to commoner status, being exiled to Yinmi. Zhang Tang attacked Zheng and captured it. In the twelfth month, additional troops were deployed to reinforce the army near Fencheng. Wuan Jun Bai Qi was found guilty of a crime and died. Wang He attacked Handan but failed to capture it, withdrew, and returned to the army at Fen. After more than two months of attacking Jin's forces, they beheaded 6,000 enemy soldiers. The defeated Jin and Chu troops drowned in the river, with 20,000 perishing. They attacked Fencheng, then followed Zhang Tang to capture Ningxinzhong, which was renamed Anyang. The construction of a river bridge began for the first time.

## 67

In the fifty-first year, General Jiu attacked Han, capturing Yangcheng and Fusu, and beheaded 40,000 enemy troops. They attacked Zhao, seizing more than twenty counties and taking 90,000 prisoners. The ruler of Xi Zhou betrayed Qin and formed an alliance with the feudal lords, mobilizing the sharpest troops from all under heaven to attack Qin through Yiqu, aiming to prevent Qin forces from passing through Yangcheng. As a result, Qin dispatched General Jiu to attack Xi Zhou. The ruler of Xi Zhou fled and surrendered himself, kowtowing in submission and offering all thirty-six walled cities and 30,000 people under his control. King Qin accepted the surrender and returned the ruler to Zhou. In the fifty-second year, the people of Zhou fled eastward, and its Nine Tripod Cauldrons were brought into Qin. The state of Zhou was first to fall.

## 68

In the fifty-third year, all under heaven came as guests in submission. After Wei submitted later on, Qin sent Jiu to attack Wei and captured Wucheng. The King of Han paid homage, and the ruler of Wei surrendered his state and obeyed Qin's commands. In the fifty-fourth year, the king performed a sacrificial rite to Heaven at Yong. In autumn of the fifty-sixth year, King Zhaoxiang died and his son Xiaowenwang ascended the throne. Tang Bashizi was honored as Empress Dowager Tang, and her burial was combined with that of the former king. The King of Han entered in mourning attire to pay respects and perform funeral rites, while feudal lords all sent their generals or ministers to mourn and conduct the ceremonies.

## 69

In the first year of Xiaowenwang's reign, convicts were pardoned; meritorious subjects from previous kings were honored and rewarded; relatives were given generous honors; and hunting reserves were opened up. After completing the mourning period, Xiaowenwang took the throne on the Jihai day of the tenth month. He died three days later, on the Xichou day, and his son Zhuangxiangwang ascended to the throne.

## 70

In the first year of King Zhuangxiang's reign, convicts were granted a general pardon; meritorious subjects of previous kings were honored and restored to their positions; kindness was shown to close relatives, and benevolence was extended to the people. The ruler of Dong Zhou conspired against Qin with feudal lords, and Qin dispatched Chancellor Lu Buwei to execute him, completely annexing his state. Qin did not extinguish the Zhou ancestral rites but granted territory in Yangren to the Zhou ruler, allowing him to continue their sacrificial ceremonies. Qin dispatched Meng Ao to attack Han, and Han surrendered Chenggao and Gong. The Qin border reached Daliang, marking the initial establishment of the Sanchuang Commandery. In the second year, Meng Ao was sent to attack Zhao and pacify Taiyuan. In the third year, Meng Ao attacked Gaodu and Ji in Wei and captured them. They attacked Yuci, Xincheng, and Langmeng in Zhao and seized thirty-seven walled cities. On an unspecified day in the fourth month, there was a solar eclipse. (Wang He attacked Shangdang.) The Taiyuan Commandery was first established. Wei general Wu Ji led an alliance of five states to attack Qin, and the Qin forces were driven back beyond the Yellow River. Meng Ao was defeated and withdrew in retreat. On the Bingwu day of the fifth month, King Zhuangxiang died, and his son Zheng ascended the throne, later known as Qin Shi Huangdi.

## 71

In the twenty-sixth year of King Zheng's reign, he first unified all under heaven into thirty-six commanderies and declared himself the First Emperor. The First Emperor passed away in his fifty-first year, and his son Huhai ascended the throne as Second Emperor. In the third year, feudal lords rose up in rebellion against Qin simultaneously; Zhao Gao killed the Second Emperor and installed Ziying as ruler. Ziying reigned for more than a month, but feudal lords executed him and thus Qin was destroyed. The account is recorded in the Annals of Emperor Qin Shi Huang.

## 72

Grand Historian said: The ancestors of Qin were of the Ying surname. Later, they were divided into fiefs and took their states as surnames, including the Xu clan, Tan clan, Ju clan, Zhongli clan, Yunyan clan, Tuqiu clan, Jiangliang clan, Huang clan, Jiang clan, Xiuyu clan, Baiming clan, Feilian clan, and Qin clan. However, the state of Qin took its surname from Zhao Cheng, where their ancestor Zaofu was enfeoffed, making them the Zhao clan.
