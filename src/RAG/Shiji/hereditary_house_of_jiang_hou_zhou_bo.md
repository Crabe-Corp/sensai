# Hereditary House of Jiang Hou Zhou Bo

## 1

Jiang Hou Zhou Bo was a native of Pei. His ancestors were originally from Juan, but later moved to Pei. Bo made a living by weaving bamboo mats and often played the xiao flute at funerals for others; he was also physically strong as a military officer.

## 2

When Gaozu first rose to power as the Lord of Pei, Bo followed him as an attendant and participated in the attack on Huling and the southern area of Xiangyu. When Xiangyu rebelled, they fought against them and repelled their forces. They attacked Feng. They defeated the Qin army east of Dang. The troops returned to Liu and Xiao. They attacked Dang again and captured it. They took Xiayi, with Bo leading the first assault. He was granted the title of Wudaifu (a rank of nobility). They attacked Meng and Yu, capturing both. He fought against Zhang Han's chariot and cavalry forces, serving as the rearguard. They pacified the territory of Wei. They attacked Yuanqi and Dongmin, proceeding as far as Li, which they captured. They attacked Niesang, with Bo leading the first ascent. He defeated the Qin army at A, breaking their forces. They pursued them to Puyang and captured Jiancheng. They attacked Du Guan and Dingtao, launched a surprise attack on Wanqu, and captured the magistrate of Shanfu. At night they launched a surprise attack to capture Linji, attacked Zhang, advanced as far as Juan, and defeated them. He fought against Li You's army at Yongqiu. They attacked Kaifeng, with Bo arriving first under the city walls and earning much credit for it. Later, when Zhang Han defeated and killed Xiang Liang, Lord Pei Gong and Xiang Yu led their troops east to Dang. From the time they first rose in Pei until returning to Dang, it had been one year and two months. King Huai of Chu bestowed upon Lord Pei Gong the title Anwu Hou (Peaceful Martial Marquis) and appointed him as the magisterial official of Dang Jun. Lord Pei Gong appointed Bo as Hu Bin Ling (Commander of Tiger 贲), and he followed Lord Pei Gong to pacify the territory of Wei. They attacked the commandant of Dong Jun at Chengwu, defeating him. He fought against Wang Li's army and defeated them. They attacked Zhangshe, with Bo leading the initial assault. They attacked Yingyang and Guoshi, cutting off the river crossings. He fought against Zhao Bin's army north of Shi. They attacked Nanjun Shou Yi to the south, breaking through Wuguan and Yaoguan passes. They defeated the Qin army at Lantian, advancing as far as Xianyang and bringing about the fall of the Qin dynasty.

## 3

When Xiang Yu arrived, he appointed Lord Pei Gong as King of Han. King of Han bestowed upon Bo the title Weiwu Hou (Valiant Martial Marquis). He followed King of Han into Hanzhong and was appointed as a general. After returning to pacify the Three Qins, upon reaching Qin, he was granted a fief in Huide for provisions and land revenue. They attacked Huili and Haozhi, achieving the best results among their peers. He fought against Zhao Bin and Neishi Bao at Xianyang, achieving the most notable success. They attacked north to Qi. He fought against Zhang Ping and Yao Ang's army. They moved west to pacify Qian. The troops returned and captured Mei and Pinyin Yang. They besieged Zhang Han at Feiqiu. They defeated the western commandant. He fought against Dào Ba's army and defeated it. They attacked Shanggui. They moved east to defend Yaoguan Pass. He turned his forces against Xiang Ji (Xiang Yu). They attacked Quni, achieving the best results. The troops returned to defend Ao Granary and pursued Xiang Ji. After Ji's death, he proceeded eastward to pacify the regions of Si Shui and Donghai Jun in former Chu territory, capturing a total of twenty-two counties. The troops returned to garrison Luoyang and Linyang, and he was granted land revenue from Zhongli together with Yingyin Hou. As a general, he followed Emperor Gao to fight against Yan Wang Zang Chu, the rebel king of Yan, and defeated him at Yi. The soldiers under his command were most numerous along the imperial road. He was granted a noble title as a marquis, with a fief charter ensuring that it would be passed down through generations without interruption. His fief consisted of eight thousand one hundred and eighty households in Jiang, and he was given the title "Jiang Hou."

## 4

As a general, he accompanied Emperor Gao to attack Fan Hanwang Xin in Dai and successfully brought about the surrender of Huo Ren. Advancing as far as Wuquan, he fought against the Xiongnu cavalry and defeated them north of Wuquan. He turned to attack Han Xin's army at Tongdi, defeating it. Returning, he brought about the surrender of six cities in Taiyuan. He fought against Han Xin's Xiongnu cavalry forces at Jinyang, defeating them and capturing the city of Jinyang. Later, he fought against Han Xin's army at Keshi, defeated it, and pursued the retreating forces for eighty li. Returning, he attacked three cities in Loufan, then fought against the Xiongnu cavalry at Pingcheng, where his troops were most numerous along the imperial thoroughfare. Bo was promoted to Taiwei (Minister of War).

## 5

He attacked Chen Xi and razed Maiyi. The soldiers under his command beheaded Chen Xi's general Cheng Ma Chi. He fought against Han Xin, Chen Xi, and Zhao Li's army at Loufan, defeating them. He captured Chen Xi's general Song Zui and the commander of Yanmen Fu Hun. He then turned to attack, capturing Yunzhong Shou Su, Chancellor Ji Si, and General Xun. He pacified seventeen counties in Yanmen Jun and twelve counties in Yunzhong Jun. He then attacked Chen Xi at Lingqiu again, defeating him, killing Chen Xi, and capturing his chancellor Cheng Zong, general Chen Wu, and commandant Gao Si. He pacified nine counties in Dai Jun.

## 6

When Yan Wang Lu Wan rebelled, Bo took over as Chancellor from Fan Kuai and led the campaign. He captured Ji, capturing Wan's chief general Di, chancellor Yan, commandant Xing, Taiwei Ruo, and Grand Censor Shi, and razed Hundi. He defeated Wan's army at Shanglan, then attacked again and broke Wan's forces at Juyang. They pursued as far as the Great Wall, pacifying twelve counties in Shanggu, sixteen counties in Youbei Ping, twenty-nine counties in Liaoxi and Liao dong, and twenty-two counties in Yuyang. Bo, following Emperor Gao, captured one Chancellor, two chancellors, and three generals as well as three officials of the rank of Erqianshi. Separately, he defeated two armies, captured three cities, pacified five commanderies, seventy-nine counties, and secured one chancellor and one chief general.

## 7

Bo was a man of simple strength and sincerity; Emperor Gao considered him trustworthy for important matters. Bo had no interest in literature, and whenever he summoned scholars or eloquent men, he would sit facing east and scold them: "Hurry up and speak to me." He was as blunt and lacking in refinement as this.

## 8

After Bo pacified Yan and returned, Emperor Gaozu had already died; he then served as a marquis under Emperor Xiaohui. In the sixth year of Emperor Xiaohui's reign, the office of Taiwei was reestablished, and Bo was appointed as Taiwei. Ten years later, Empress Dowager Gao passed away. Lü Lu served as the supreme general of Han under his title as King of Zhao, while Lü Chan held the position of Chancellor of Han under his title as Prince of Lü. They controlled the power of Han and sought to endanger the Liu clan. As Taiwei, Bo was not allowed to enter the military headquarters. Chen Ping served as Chancellor but could not assume actual responsibilities. Thus, Bo and Ping plotted together, ultimately exterminated the Lü clan, and installed Emperor Xiaowen. The details of this are recorded in the annals concerning Empress Dowager Lü and Emperor Xiaowen's affairs.

## 9

After Emperor Xiaowen was enthroned, he appointed Bo as Right Chancellor and bestowed upon him five thousand catties of gold and a fief with ten thousand households. After more than a month, someone advised Bo: "Since you have already exterminated the Lü clan and enthroned King Dai, your authority has shaken the entire realm. Now that you are receiving generous rewards and holding such an exalted position, prolonged enjoyment of this favor will eventually bring disaster upon yourself." Bo was frightened and felt himself in danger. He therefore submitted a request to resign from his post as chancellor. The emperor approved it. After more than a year, Chancellor Ping died, and the emperor reappointed Bo as chancellor. After more than ten months, the emperor said: "The other day I issued an edict ordering marquises to return to their fiefs, but some have yet been unable to do so. The chancellor is someone I hold in high esteem; he should lead by example." Thus, Bo resigned from his position and returned to his fief.

## 10

After more than a year, whenever the governor and magistrates of Hedong passed through Jiang to conduct county inspections, Jiang Hou Bo, fearing punishment, would often wear armor and have his family members hold weapons when they met them. Later, someone submitted a petition accusing Bo of plotting rebellion, and the case was referred to the Tingwei (Office of Imperial Justice). The Tingwei sent the matter to Chang'an for investigation, whereupon Bo was arrested and interrogated. Bo was terrified and did not know how to defend himself. The officials gradually began to humiliate him. Bo gave a thousand gold pieces to the jailer, who then wrote on the back of a wooden tablet and showed it to him, saying, "Use the princess as evidence." The princess was the daughter of Emperor Xiaowen. Bo's eldest son, Shengzhi, had married her; therefore, the jailer instructed him to cite her as evidence. All of the additional fiefs and gifts that Bo received were given entirely to Bao Zhao. When he was in dire straits, Bao Zhao pleaded with the Dowager Empress Gao, who also believed that Bo had not plotted rebellion. During the reign of Emperor Wen, the dowager empress took a quilt and struck Emperor Wen with it, saying: "Jiang Hou held the imperial seal and commanded troops at the Northern Army. If he did not rebel then, why would he want to rebel now while merely residing in a small county!" After Emperor Wen saw the charges against Jiang Hou, he apologized and said: "The officials are just verifying the case before releasing him." Thus, he sent an envoy with a token of authority to pardon Jiang Hou and restored his title and fief. After he was released, Jiang Hou said: "I once commanded a hundred thousand troops, yet how could I have known the power of jailers!"

## 11

Jiang Hou returned to his fief again. In the eleventh year of Emperor Xiaowen's reign, he died and was posthumously honored with the title Wu Hou. His son Shengzhi succeeded him as marquis. Six years later, he married a princess, but they did not get along well; he was convicted of murder and his fief was abolished. After one year, Emperor Wen selected Yafu, the most virtuous son of Jiang Hou Bo and then-governor of Hedong, to be enfeoffed as Tiɑo Hou, continuing the lineage of the Jiang Hou title.

## 12

Before being enfeoffed as marquis, when Tiɑo Hou Yafu was still the governor of Hedong, Xu Fu read his physiognomy and said: "You will be made a marquis in three years." After eight years as a marquis, he became general and chancellor, holding the reins of state power. His position was extremely prestigious; among ministers, there was none equal to him. Nine years later, however, you will die of starvation." Yafu laughed and said: "My elder brother has already succeeded our father as marquis. If he were to die, my son would succeed him. How could I possibly become a marquis?" However, since your prediction has already come true in that I have risen to such high rank as you said, how can it also be true that I will die of starvation?" Show me." Xu Fu pointed to his mouth and said: "There is a vertical line entering the mouth; this is the mark of dying from starvation." Three years later, his elder brother Jiang Hou Shengzhi committed a crime. Emperor Xiaowen selected the most virtuous among the descendants of Jiang Hou and all recommended Yafu; thus, he was enfeoffed as Tiāo Hou to continue the lineage of Jiang Hou.

## 13

In the sixth year after Emperor Wen's death, the Xiongnu launched a large-scale invasion along the frontier. The emperor therefore appointed Liu Li of Zongzheng as general and stationed him at Bashi; Zhu Zi Hou Xu Li was made general and stationed at Jimen; and the governor of Hedong, Yafu, was appointed as general and stationed at Xiliu: all to guard against the Hu. The emperor personally went to visit the troops. When he arrived at the armies of Bashi and Jimen, his procession rode straight in without hindrance, and officers and soldiers mounted horses to meet and escort him. Later, when he arrived at the Xiliu army, the soldiers were clad in armor, sharp weapons drawn, crossbows loaded and held ready. When the emperor's advance guard arrived, they were not allowed to enter. The advance guard said: "The Son of Heaven is about to arrive!" The commander at the gate replied: "General has given orders that 'within the army, we obey only the general's commands and not the emperor's edicts.'" After a short while, the emperor himself arrived but was also denied entry. The emperor then sent an envoy with a token to summon General Yafu: "I wish to enter and visit the troops." Yafu then ordered that the gate be opened. The officers at the gate told the emperor's retinue: "General has given orders: within the army, one must not ride or drive recklessly." Thus, the Son of Heaven reined in his horse and rode slowly. When he reached the camp, General Yafu saluted with weapon in hand and said: "A soldier clad in armor cannot bow; I shall greet you according to military rites." The emperor was deeply moved, changed his expression, and bowed from the carriage. He sent an envoy to convey his thanks: "His Majesty respectfully greets General." The ceremony was completed, and the emperor departed. After leaving the camp gate, all of the ministers were astonished. Emperor Wen said: "Alas! This is truly a general!" "The armies at Bashi and Jimen before were like children's games; their commanders could certainly be ambushed and captured." "As for Yafu, how could one even dare to attack him!" The emperor praised him for a long time. After more than a month, the three armies were all disbanded. The emperor then appointed Yafu as Zhongwei (commander of the imperial guards).

## 14

On his deathbed, Emperor Wen warned the crown prince: "If there is an emergency, Zhou Yafu can truly be entrusted with command of troops." After Emperor Wen's death, Yafu was appointed as Chaji General.

## 15

In the third year of Emperor Jing's reign, Wu and Chu rebelled. Yafu was promoted from Zhongwei to Taiwei (Grand General) and led the eastern campaign against Wu and Chu. He requested permission from the emperor, saying: "The forces of Chu are swift and light; it is difficult to engage them in direct combat. I request that Liang be sacrificed to the enemy, so we may cut off their supply lines and thus bring them under control." The emperor approved.

## 16

After the Taiwei had gathered his troops at Xingyang, Wu was attacking Liang; Liang was in dire straits and requested reinforcements. The Taiwei led his army northeast to Changyi and entrenched himself there, building strong defenses. Liang sent envoys daily requesting the Taiwei's aid, but he adhered strictly to his strategy and refused to go. Liang submitted a petition to Emperor Jing, who then sent an envoy with imperial orders for the Taiwei to rescue Liang. The Taiwei did not obey the edict, maintaining his strong defenses without going out, while sending light cavalry under Gao Hou and others to cut off the supply lines of Wu and Chu forces. The Wu army, short on supplies and starving, repeatedly attempted to provoke a battle, but the Taiwei never emerged from his defenses. At night, the camp was thrown into chaos as soldiers attacked one another in confusion, reaching even to the Taiwei's tent. The Taiwei remained lying down and did not rise. After a while, order was restored. Later, when the Wu forces rushed toward the southeast corner of the defenses, the Taiwei ordered his men to prepare at the northwest. Soon after, their elite troops indeed charged toward the northwest but could not break in. With their forces starving, the Wu army withdrew. The Taiwei then sent out his elite troops to pursue and attack them, achieving a decisive victory. Wu Wang Bi abandoned his army and fled with several thousand strong men to Dan Tu in the south of the Yangtze River for refuge. The Han forces, taking advantage of their victory, captured all of Wu's troops and offered a reward of one thousand gold pieces for the capture of Wu Wang. After more than a month, people from Yue beheaded Wu Wang and presented it to report his death. In total, the campaign of attack and defense lasted three months before Wu and Chu were crushed and pacified. At this point, all the generals acknowledged that the Taiwei's strategy had been correct. From then on, Liang Xiao Wang and the Taiwei became estranged.

## 17

After returning, the position of Taiwei was reinstated. Five years later, he was promoted to chancellor, and Emperor Jing held him in high esteem. Emperor Jing deposed the crown prince Liu Li; the chancellor strongly opposed this decision but failed to change it. Because of this, Emperor Jing gradually grew distant from him. Meanwhile, whenever Liang Xiao Wang paid court visits, he often spoke to the empress dowager about Tiɑo Hou's shortcomings.

## 18

Empress Dowager Dou said: "The emperor's sister-in-law's elder brother, Wang Xin, is worthy of being enfeoffed." Emperor Jing rebuked her: "Initially, Marquises Nanpi and Zhangwu were not enfeoffed by the late emperor; it was only after I ascended the throne that they received their titles." "Wang Xin has yet to be granted a title as well." Empress Dowager Dou replied: "Each ruler acts according to his own time and circumstances." "When my elder brother, Dou Changjun, was alive, he never received a title; yet after his death, his son Peng Zu was enfeoffed." I am deeply resentful of this. "Then the emperor should quickly enfeoff Wang Xin!" Emperor Jing said: "I will consult with the chancellor on this matter." The chancellor convened a discussion, and Yafu said: "Emperor Gaozu decreed that 'only those of the Liu clan may be kings, and only those with merit may receive marquisates.'" "If this decree is violated, all under heaven shall rise against it." "Now Wang Xin, although he is the empress's brother, has no merit. To enfeoff him would violate the decree." Emperor Jing fell silent and did not proceed further.

## 19

Later, five Xiongnu chieftains, Weixulu et al., surrendered to the Han dynasty. Emperor Jing wished to enfeoff them in order to encourage others to follow suit. Chancellor Yafu said: "Those men abandoned their lord and surrendered to Your Majesty. If Your Majesty enfeoffs them, how can one then hold accountable those subjects who fail to uphold loyalty?" Emperor Jing said: "The chancellor's opinion cannot be followed." He then enfeoffed Weixulu and the others as marquises. Yafu therefore resigned on grounds of illness. In the third year of Zhong, during Emperor Jing's reign, he was relieved of his post as chancellor due to illness.

## 20

Soon after, while Emperor Jing resided in the palace, he summoned Marquis Tiao and granted him a meal. Only a large piece of raw meat was placed before him, without any sliced meat, nor were chopsticks provided. łożył Tiao Hou felt indignant and turned to the attendant in charge of the banquet, saying, "Go get me some chopsticks." Emperor Jing looked on and laughed, saying: "Is this not sufficient for you?" Tiao Hou removed his hat in apology. The emperor rose, and Tiao Hou hurriedly left the hall. Emperor Jing watched him leave with his eyes and said: "This man, full of resentment, is not a subject for a young emperor!"

## 21

Not long after, Tiao Hou's son bought from the official workshops of the Shangfang Bureau five hundred sets of armor and shields that could be used for burial. He had laborers toil on them, without paying them any money. The laborers learned that he had stolen and purchased official equipment illegally, became angry, and reported the son to authorities with accusations of treason. The case implicated Tiao Hou as well. After the report reached the emperor, officials were dispatched to investigate. The officials interrogated Tiao Hou with written charges, but he did not respond. Emperor Jing scolded him and said: "I will no longer use you." He was summoned to appear before the Minister of Justice. The Minister of Justice interrogated him, saying: "Lord Marquis, are you plotting rebellion?" Yafu said: "What I bought were burial goods; how can that be considered treason?" The official said: "Even if you did not plot rebellion on the surface, you certainly intended to rebel underground." The officials pressed him with increasing severity. Initially, when the officials arrested Tiao Hou, he wanted to commit suicide, but his wife stopped him. As a result, he did not die and was taken into custody by the Minister of Justice. He then refused food for five days and died from vomiting blood. His fief was abolished.

## 22

After a year of the line being extinguished, Emperor Jing posthumously enfeoffed another son of Marquis Jiang Hou Bo, Jian, as Pingqu Hou, continuing the lineage of the Jiang Hou title. He died in the nineteenth year and was posthumously honored with the title Gong Hou. His son Jiande succeeded him as marquis, and thirteen years later, he served as Taifu of the Crown Prince. He was found guilty for presenting substandard zhoujin (tribute gold), and in the fifth year of Yuanding, he committed a crime; his fief was abolished.

## 23

Tiao Hou indeed died from starvation. After his death, Emperor Jing enfeoffed Wang Xin as Gai Hou.

## 24

Grand Historian said: "Jiang Hou Zhou Bo, when he was still a commoner, was an unremarkable and plain man; his abilities were no greater than those of ordinary people." But when he followed Emperor Gaozu in pacifying the empire, and later held positions as general or chancellor, during the time when the Lu clan sought to instigate rebellion, Zhou Bo rescued the state from crisis and restored it to order. Even Yi Yin and Duke of Zhou could not surpass him! Yafu's use of military strategy was marked by firm authority, steadfast resolve, and unyielding discipline—how could Jiang Ziya surpass this! Yet he trusted in his own abilities without seeking further learning, upheld rigid principles without yielding, and ultimately met a difficult end. How tragic!
