# Lie Zhuan of Fan Li Teng Guan

## 1

Wuyang Hou Fan Kuai was a native of Pei. He made his living as a butcher and dog seller, and he hid in seclusion with Emperor Gaozu.

## 2

Initially following Emperor Gaozu to rise up from Feng, he captured Pei. When Emperor Gaozu was the Lord of Pei, he appointed Kuai as a retainer. He followed in attacking Huling and Fangyu, then returned to defend Feng, where he attacked the commander of Si Shui at the lower reaches of Feng and defeated him. He again moved eastward to pacify Pei, defeating the governor of Si Shui in Xixi. Fighting Sima Yi east of Dang, he repelled the enemy and beheaded fifteen foes; for this, he was granted the title of Guodaifu. Accompanying Lord Pei in attacking Zhang Han's forces at Puyang, he led the charge in capturing the city and beheaded twenty-three enemies; for this, he was awarded the title of Liedaifu. Again accompanying regularly, he followed in attacking Chengyang and led the charge to capture it. łoż He captured Huyou, defeated Li You's army, beheaded sixteen enemies, and was awarded a high-ranking noble title. Following in attacking and besieging the governor and military commander of Dongjun at Chengwu, he repelled the enemy, beheaded fourteen foes, captured eleven prisoners, and was granted the title Wudaifu. He followed in attacking the Qin forces, emerging south of Bo. The governor's troops of Hejian at Gangli were defeated by him. He attacked and defeated Zhao Bin's forces north of Kaifeng, repelling the enemy by leading the charge. He beheaded one officer and sixty-eight enemies, captured twenty-seven prisoners, and was awarded the title Qing. He followed in attacking and defeating Yang Xiong's forces at Quyu. In the attack on Wanling, he led the charge to capture the city, beheaded eight enemies, captured forty-four prisoners, and was granted a noble title with fiefdom as Xiancheng Jun. He followed in attacking Changshe and Huanyuan, cutting off the river crossing, then attacked the Qin forces eastward at Shi and southward at Chou. He defeated the governor of Nanyang, Yi, at Yangcheng. Moving eastward to attack Wancheng, he led the charge and captured it first. Advancing westward to Li, he repelled the enemy forces, beheaded twenty-four foes, captured forty prisoners, and was awarded an additional fief. In the attack on Wuguan, reaching Bashi, he beheaded one Duwei officer and ten enemies, captured 146 prisoners, and received the surrender of 2,900 soldiers.

## 3

Xiang Yu was at Xixia and wanted to attack Lord Pei. Lord Pei, accompanied by more than a hundred mounted attendants, met Xiang Yu in person through Xiang Bo and apologized for the matter of not closing the pass. After Xiang Yu had entertained his soldiers, the wine was flowing when Yafu plotted to kill Lord Pei. He ordered Xiang Zhuang to draw a sword and dance in the hall, intending to attack Lord Pei; however, Xiang Bo constantly shielded him. At that time, only Lord Pei and Zhang Liang were allowed to sit inside. Fan Kuai was outside the camp; upon hearing of the emergency, he took up an iron shield and rushed into the camp. The guards tried to stop Kuai, but Kuai pushed forward directly and stood under the tent. Xiang Yu looked at him and asked who he was. Zhang Liang said, "This is Fan Kuai, the charioteer of Lord Pei." Xiang Yu said, "A valiant man." He was given a cup of wine and a pig's shoulder. After drinking the wine, Kuai drew his sword to cut up the meat and ate it all. Xiang Yu asked, "Are you able to drink more?" Kuai replied, "I would not even refuse death; how could it be just a cup of wine!" Moreover, Lord Pei entered and pacified Xianyang first, stationed his troops at Bashi, and waited for Your Majesty. If Your Majesty arrives today and listens to the words of petty people, creating a rift with Lord Pei, I fear that the whole world will lose confidence and doubt Your Majesty." Xiang Yu fell silent. Lord Pei went to the latrine and signaled Fan Kuai to leave. After leaving, Lord Pei left behind his chariot and horsemen, rode alone on a single horse, and was accompanied by Fan Kuai and three others on foot. They took a secluded path down the mountain to return secretly to the Bashi army, while sending Zhang Liang to apologize to Xiang Yu. Xiang Yu also thus let it go and no longer had any intention of killing Lord Pei. On that day, if not for Fan Kuai rushing into the camp to remonstrate with Xiang Yu, Lord Pei's situation would have been in great danger.

## 4

The next day, Xiang Yu entered Xianyang and slaughtered its people, then appointed Lord Pei as King of Han. King of Han bestowed a noble title on Kuai as Liehou, with the designation Linwu Hou. He was promoted to Langzhong and followed into Hanzhong.

## 5

Returning, he pacified the Three Qins, separately attacking Xicheng north of Baishui and defeating You Qingcheqi at the southern part of Yong. He followed in attacking Yong and Licheng, leading the charge to capture them first. In attacking Zhang Ping's forces at Haozhi, he led the assault on the city walls, broke through enemy lines first, beheaded a county magistrate and his deputy, killed eleven enemies, captured twenty prisoners, and was promoted to Langzhong Qijiang. Following in attacking the Qin cavalry at Rangdong, he repelled the enemy; for this, he was promoted to general. He attacked Zhao Bin and captured Mei, Huaili, Liu Zhong, and Xianyang; He besieged Feiqiu and was the most outstanding. Upon reaching Linyang, he was granted a fief in Fan Xiang of Du. He followed in attacking Xiang Ji and slaughtered the defenders at Zhu Zao. He defeated Wang Wu's and Cheng Chu's forces at Waihuang. He attacked Zou, Lu, Xiaqiu, and Xue. Xiang Yu defeated King of Han at Pengcheng and completely recaptured the territories of Lu and Liang. Kuai returned to Yingyang, was granted an additional fief of 2000 households in Pingyin, and as a general, he defended Guangwu. After one year, Xiang Yu withdrew eastward. He followed Emperor Gaozu in attacking Xiang Ji, captured Yang Xia, and took prisoner 4000 soldiers under the command of Chu Zhou General. They besieged Xiang Ji at Chen and utterly defeated him. They slaughtered the people of Huling.

## 6

After Xiang Ji's death, King of Han became emperor and, in recognition of Kuai's steadfast defense and contributions to the war effort, granted him an additional fief of eight hundred households. He followed Emperor Gao to attack Yan Wang Zang Tu, who had rebelled; he captured Tu and pacified the territory of Yan. When King of Chu Han Xin rebelled, Kuai followed to Chen, captured Xin, and pacified Chu. He was again granted the noble title of Liehou, and a covenant was made with the feudal lords through engraved jade tokens, ensuring that his fief would last for generations. He received Wuyang as his territory, bearing the title "Wuyang Hou," replacing all previous fiefs. As a general, he followed Emperor Gaozu to attack King of Han Wang Xin, who had rebelled in Dai. From Huo Ren onward to Yunzhong, he joined with Jiao Hou and others to pacify the region and was granted an additional fief of one thousand five hundred households. He then attacked Chen Xi and Man Qiuchen's forces, fought at Xiangguo, captured Bai Ren by leading the charge, induced surrender in Qinghe and Changshan covering a total of twenty-seven counties, devastated Dongyuan, and was promoted to Left Chancellor. He defeated Qi Wuju's and Yin Pan's forces at Wuzhong and Guangchang. He defeated the separate command of Hu people Wang Huang, a subordinate general of Chen Xi, south of Dai, and then attacked Han Xin's forces at Canhe. The soldiers under his command beheaded Han Xin, defeated Chen Xi's Hu cavalry in Henggu Valley, killed General Zhao Ji, and captured Dai Chancellor Feng Liang, Governor Sun Fen, Grand General Wang Huang, General (Taibu) Taifu Jiefu, and nine others. Together with other generals, he pacified seventy-three towns and villages in Dai. Later, when Yan Wang Lu Wan rebelled, Kuai, as Chancellor, attacked Lu Wan, defeating his chancellor and advancing to the south of Ji, pacifying the territory of Yan, which included eighteen counties and fifty-one towns and villages. He was granted an additional fiefdom of 1300 households, making his total fief in Wuyang amount to 5400 households. He participated in a campaign, beheading 176 enemies and capturing 288 prisoners. Separately, he defeated seven armies, captured five cities, pacified six commanderies and fifty-two counties, and secured the surrender of one chancellor, twelve generals, and eleven officials ranked from two thousand dan to three hundred dan.

## 7

Kuai married Lu Xu, the younger sister of Empress Dowager Lü, and had a son named Kang; therefore, he was closer to the imperial family than any other general.

## 8

𝑙 When Qinq Bu rebelled earlier, Emperor Gaozu once fell seriously ill and disliked seeing people. He lay in the palace, issuing an order to the gatekeepers not to allow any ministers inside. The ministers, including Jiao and Guan, dared not enter. After more than ten days, Kuai pushed through the gates and entered directly, followed by the ministers. The emperor was lying alone with only one eunuch at his side. Kuai and others wept before the emperor, saying, "At first, Your Majesty rose with us from Feng and Pei to pacify the world—how mighty that was! Now that the empire is at peace, why are you so exhausted!" Moreover, Your Majesty's illness is severe and has caused great alarm among the ministers. If you do not see us to discuss affairs of state, are you going to remain secluded with just one eunuch?" "Besides, does Your Majesty have no memory of the matter involving Zhao Gao?" Emperor Gaozu smiled and got up.

## 9

Later, when Lu Wan rebelled, Emperor Gaozu sent Kuai as Chancellor to attack Yan. At that time, Emperor Gaozu was very ill. Someone accused Kuai of being a partisan of the Lu family, and if one day the emperor passed away suddenly, Kuai would use his troops to completely eliminate Qi Shi, Zhao Wang Yurui, and others like them. Emperor Gaozu heard of this and was furious; he then ordered Chen Ping to carry Jiao Hou as his replacement commander, and ordered Kuai's execution in the military camp. Chen Ping feared Empress Dowager Lu and arrested Kuai to bring him to Chang'an. By the time they arrived, Emperor Gaozu had already died; Empress Dowager Lu released Kuai and allowed him to resume his noble title and fief.

## 10

In the sixth year of Emperor Xiaohui's reign, Fan Kuai died; he was posthumously honored with the title "Wu Hou." His son Kang succeeded him as marquis. Kang's mother, Lu Xu, was also made the Lady of Lingguang Hou; during Empress Dowager Gao's reign, she wielded power and authority, and all ministers feared her. Kang held his title for nine years when Empress Dowager Gao died. The ministers purged the Lu clan, including Lu Xu's relatives and associates, and thus executed Kang as well. The title of Wuyang Hou was interrupted for several months. After Emperor Xiaowen ascended the throne, he regranted the title of Wuyang Hou to another son of Kuai named Shi Ren and restored his former noble rank and fief. Shi Ren held the title for twenty-nine years before dying, posthumously honored as "Huang Hou." His son Ta Guang succeeded him as marquis. After six years, a retainer of the marquis's household offended Ta Guang and bore resentment. He submitted a memorial stating: "Huang Hou Shi Ren was ill and unable to have children; he ordered his wife to commit adultery with her brother, giving birth to Ta Guang. In fact, Ta Guang is not the son of Huang Hou, and therefore should not succeed as heir." The emperor issued an order for the case to be investigated by officials. In the sixth year of the middle reign period of Emperor Xiaojing, Ta Guang was stripped of his marquis title and reduced to a commoner; the fiefdom was abolished.

## 11

Quzhou Hou Li Shang was a native of Gaoyang. When Chen Sheng rose in rebellion, Shang gathered young men and recruited people from the east and west, amassing several thousand followers. When Lord Pei was campaigning through the region and reached Chenliu, after more than six months, Shang brought with him 4,000 soldiers and officers to join Lord Pei at Qi. He followed in the attack on Changshe, was among the first to scale the walls, and was granted a noble title as Jin Cheng Jun. He followed Lord Pei in attacking Goushi, cut off the river crossing, and defeated the Qin forces east of Luoyang. He followed to attack and capture Wan and Rang, pacifying seventeen counties. As a separate commander, he attacked Xungan and secured Hanzhong.

## 12

After Xiang Yu destroyed the Qin dynasty, he appointed Lord Pei as King of Han. King of Han granted Shang the noble title Jin Cheng Jun and appointed him as General and Commandant of Longxi. As a separate commander, he pacified Beidi and Shangjun commanderies. He defeated the general Yan Shi of Yong at Xunyi, Zhou Lei's forces, and Su Bi's troops at Niyang. He was granted a fief in Wucheng with 6,000 households as his stipend. As Commandant of Longxi, he followed to attack Xiang Ji's forces for five months. He advanced from Juye and fought against Zhongli Mie, fighting fiercely. For his valor, he was awarded the seal of Liang's Chancellor and received an additional 4,000 households added to his stipend. As Chancellor of Liang, he led troops in the campaign against Xiang Yu for two years and three months, attacking Huling.

## 13

After Xiang Yu had died, Lord Pei became emperor. That autumn, Yan Wang Zang Tu rebelled. Shang, as a general, followed to attack Zang Tu. In the battle at Longtuo, he was among the first to charge into the enemy formation and broke through Zang Tu's forces below Yi, repelling the enemy. He was promoted to Right Chancellor, granted the title of Marquis, and received a jade seal with an oath ensuring his descendants would inherit it forever. He was given 5,000 households in Zhuo as his stipend, and his title was named Zhuo Hou. As Right Chancellor, he separately pacified Shanggu and then attacked Dai, receiving the seal of Chancellor of Zhao. As Right Chancellor and Chancellor of Zhao, he separately joined with Marquis Jiao and others to pacify Dai and Yanmen, capturing Dai's Chancellor Cheng Zong, the acting chancellor Guo Tong, and nineteen generals down to those holding posts worth 600 dan of grain. Upon his return, he served as a general in charge of the Emperor Emeritus's guards for one year and seven months. As Right Chancellor, he attacked Chen Xi and devastated Dongyuan. He again served as Right Chancellor and followed Emperor Gao to attack Peng Ju Bu, attacking the vanguard forces of Peng Ju Bu and breaking through two enemy formations, which enabled them to defeat Peng Ju Bu's army. He was then granted a stipend from Quzhou with 5,100 households, replacing his previous stipend. In total, he personally broke three armies, pacified six commanderies, seventy-three counties, captured one Chancellor, one acting chancellor, and one great general, two lesser generals, and nineteen officials down to those holding posts worth six hundred dan of grain.

## 14

During the reigns of Emperor Xiaohui and Empress Dowager Gao, Shang fell ill and did not recover. His son Ji, courtesy name Kuang, was on good terms with Lu Lu. After Empress Dowager Gao passed away, the ministers wanted to purge the Lu clan. Lu Lu was a general and commanded troops at Beijun. Grand Marshal Bo could not enter Beijun, so he sent someone to coerce Li Shang, ordering his son Kuang to deceive Lu Lu. Lu Lu believed him and went out for an outing; thus, Grand Marshal Bo was able to enter and take control of Beijun, leading to the extermination of the Lu clan. In that year, Shang died, posthumously honored with the title "Jing Hou." His son Ji succeeded him as marquis. The people throughout the land criticized Li Kuang for betraying his friendship.

## 15

In the third year of Emperor Xiaojing's reign before the middle period, Wu, Chu, Qi, and Zhao rebelled. The emperor appointed Ji as general to besiege the city of Zhao; after ten months, he could not capture it. When Marquis Yu Luan Bu arrived from the pacification of Qi, Ji then captured the city of Zhao, destroyed the state of Zhao, and King Zhao committed suicide; thus, the state was abolished. In the second year of Emperor Xiaojing's middle reign period, Ji wanted to take Pingyuan Jun as his wife. Emperor Xiaojing was furious and ordered officials to investigate Ji, who was found guilty and stripped of his marquis title. Emperor Xiaojing then granted another son of Shang named Jian the title Miu Hou, continuing the Li clan's succession. Miu Jing Hou died, and his son Kang Hou Suicheng succeeded him. Suicheng died, and his son Huai Hou Shizong succeeded him. Shizong died, and his son Marquis Zhonggen succeeded him; he served as Tai Chang but was convicted of a legal offense, leading to the abolition of the fiefdom.

## 16

Ruyin Hou Xiahou Ying was a native of Pei County. He served as an official in charge of stables and chariot driving for the county of Pei. Whenever he sent envoys or guests back, passing through Sishangting in Pei, he would always talk with Gaozu for a long time. Later, Ying was appointed as a county official and formed a close bond with Gaozu. Gaozu once playfully injured Ying, and someone reported this to the authorities. ල At that time, Gaozu was a village head (tingzhang), and if found guilty of injuring someone, he would face serious punishment. He claimed he had not injured Ying on purpose, and Ying testified to this. Later, when the case was reviewed, Ying was implicated in Gaozu's matter and imprisoned for over a year. He endured hundreds of lashes but ultimately helped clear Gaozu of blame.

## 17

When Gaozu first conspired with his followers to attack Pei, Ying served as a county official acting on Gaozu's behalf. On the first day that Lord Pei surrendered to Pei, Gaozu became Lord of Pei and granted Ying the title of Qi Da Fu (Seventh Rank Noble) and appointed him as Tai Pu. He followed to attack Huling, where Ying and Xiao He surrendered the commander of Sishui, Ping. Ping then surrendered Huling; for this, Ying was granted the title Wu Da Fu (Fifth Rank Noble). He followed to attack the Qin forces east of Dang, captured Ji Yang and Hu You, defeated Li You's army below Yongqiu, and due to his swift and vigorous use of chariots in battle, was granted the noble title Zhi Bo. He often served as Tai Pu, driving the imperial chariot and accompanying in attacks against Zhang Han's forces at Donga and below Puyang. His swift and vigorous use of war chariots led to victory; for this he was granted the noble title Zhi Gui. He again regularly served as Tai Pu, accompanying in attacks against Zhao Bin's forces at Kaifeng and Yang Xiong's army at Quyu. Ying captured 68 enemies, took 850 enemy soldiers as prisoners, and seized one set of seals. He then again regularly served as Tai Fu, accompanying in the attack against Qin forces east of Luoyang; due to his swift and aggressive use of war chariots, he was granted a noble title and enfeoffed as Gong of Teng. He again served in his role, accompanying the attack on Nanyang and fighting at Lantian and Zhiyang. With swift and vigorous chariot warfare, he advanced as far as Bashi. When Xiang Yu arrived, he destroyed the Qin dynasty and enfeoffed Lord Pei as King of Han . King of Han granted Ying the noble title of Marquis with the designation Zhao Ping Hou, and again appointed him as Tai Pu; he accompanied the king into Shu and Hanzhong.

## 18

After returning to pacify the Three Qins, he followed in attacks against Xiang Ji. When they reached Pengcheng, Xiang Yu heavily defeated the Han army. King of Han was defeated and suffered a setback; he fled in haste. He encountered Emperor Xiaohui, Lu Yuan, and carried them away with him. King of Han was in a desperate situation, his horse exhausted and the enemy troops close behind. He often stumbled with the two children (his son Emperor Xiaohui and daughter Lu Yuan) and wanted to abandon them, but Ying always pulled them back up and continued carrying them, proceeding slowly until they reached the shade of a tree before speeding away again. King of Han became furious and several times, more than ten in total, wanted to execute Ying. Eventually, he managed to escape, successfully delivering Emperor Xiaohui and Lu Yuan to Feng.

## 19

After King of Han arrived at Yingyang, he gathered scattered troops and regained strength; he granted Ying a stipend in Qiyang. He again regularly served as the charioteer, accompanying attacks against Xiang Ji and pursuing him to Chen. He ultimately pacified Chu and reached Lu, where he was granted an additional stipend in Zishi.

## 20

King of Han ascended the throne as emperor. That autumn, Yan Wang Zangtu rebelled; Ying, serving as Tai Pu, followed to attack him. The following year, he accompanied the emperor to Chen and captured King of Chu Xin. He was then granted a stipend in Ruyin, with an oath inscribed on a jade seal ensuring that his title would be inherited by his descendants forever. As Tai Pu, he accompanied in the campaign against Dai, reaching Wuquan and Yunzhong, for which he was granted an additional 1,000-household stipend. He then followed to attack Han Xin's forces of Hu cavalry near Jinyang and decisively defeated them. They pursued the retreating enemy as far as Pingcheng, where they were surrounded by the Hu forces and remained cut off for seven days. Emperor Gao sent envoys bearing rich gifts to Yuezhi, and Modu opened a gap in the encirclement at one corner. Emperor Gao wanted to ride out quickly, but Ying insisted on proceeding slowly, with all crossbowmen holding their weapons ready and facing outward; thus they managed to escape. An additional stipend of 1,000 households from Xi Yang was granted to Ying. He again served as Tai Pu and accompanied in attacks against the Hu cavalry north of Gouzhu, decisively defeating them. As Tai Pu, he attacked the Hu cavalry south of Pingcheng and three times broke through enemy formations; for his numerous contributions, he was granted 500 households from captured territories as a stipend. As Tai Pu, he fought against the armies of Chen Xi and Peng Ju Bu, breaking through enemy lines and repelling them. For this, he was granted an additional 1000 households to his stipend; his total stipend from Ruyin was fixed at 6900 households, replacing all previous grants.

## 21

Ying had served as Tai Pu since the beginning of Emperor Gaozu's uprising in Pei and continued to hold this position until Emperor Gaozu passed away. He continued serving as Tai Fu under Emperor Xiaohui. Emperor Xiaohui and Empress Dowanger Gao were grateful to Ying for rescuing Emperor Xiaohui and Princess Lu Yuan during their escape in Xi Yi. As a result, they granted Ying the first residence in the northern part of the county, naming it "Jin Wo" ("Near Me"), as a mark of respect and distinction. After Emperor Xiaohui passed away, Ying continued to serve as Tai Fu under Empress Dowager Gao. After Empress Dowager Gao's death, when Dai Wang arrived, Ying, as Tai Fu, joined with Dongmu Hou to clear the palace and deposed the young emperor. He then escorted Dai Wang from his residence in Dai using imperial ceremonial processions, and together with the ministers, they enthroned him as Emperor Xiaowen; Ying was again appointed as Tai Fu. Eight years later he died, posthumously honored as "Wen Hou." His son Yihou Zao succeeded him and died seven years later. His son Gonghou Ci succeeded him and died thirty-one years later. His heir, Marquis Po, married Princess Pingyang. After holding the title for nineteen years, in the second year of Yuanding, he committed suicide over a crime involving adultery with his father's concubine; the fief was abolished.

## 22

Yingyin Hou Guan Ying was originally a silk merchant from Suyang. When Gaozu was Lord Pei, he campaigned through the region and arrived below Yongqiu. Zhang Han defeated and killed Xiang Liang, prompting Lord Pei to retreat his forces to Dang. Ying initially served as Zhong Juan (an attendant) and followed in battles where they defeated the commandant of Dongjun at Chengwu and Qin forces at Kangli, fighting fiercely; for this he was awarded the noble title Qi Da Fu. He followed in attacks against Qin forces south of Bo, Kaifeng, and Quyu, fighting with great vigor; for this he was granted the title Zhi Bo and honored as Xuanling Jun. He followed in the attacks from Yangwu westward to Luoyang, defeating Qin forces north of Shi. He cut off river crossings to the north and defeated Nanyang's commander at Yiyang Chengdong, thus pacifying the entire Nanyang commandery. He advanced westward into Wuguan, fought fiercely at Lantian, and reached Bashang; for this he was granted Zhi Gui title and honored as Changwen Jun.

## 23

When Lord Pei was enfeoffed as King of Han, Ying was appointed Langzhong and followed into Hanzhong. In the tenth month, he was promoted to Zhong Yezhe. He followed in retaking the Three Qins, captured Liyang, and induced Sai Wang's surrender. They then returned to besiege Zhang Han at Feiqiu, but failed to capture it. He followed the eastern campaign through Linjin Guan, defeated and induced Yin Wang's surrender, pacifying his territory. He attacked Xiang Yu's general Long Ju and Wei Xiang Xiang Ta's forces south of Dingtao, fighting fiercely and defeating them. Ying was granted the noble title Lie Hou, honored as Changwen Hou, with fiefdom at Duping Xiang.

## 24

He again served as Zhong Yezhe and followed in subduing Dang until reaching Pengcheng. Xiang Yu attacked and heavily defeated King Han. King Han fled westward, and Ying followed him back to take up position at Yongqiu. When Wang Wu and Wei Gong Shentu rebelled, he joined in their defeat. He captured Huang, gathered troops to the west, and stationed at Yingyang. When the Chu cavalry came in large numbers, King Han selected from his troops a suitable commander for chariot and horse forces. All recommended Li Bi and Luo Jia, former Qin cavalrymen from Zhongquan who were now Lie Wei (officers), as capable commanders of cavalry. King Han wanted to appoint them, but Bi and Jia said: "We were once commoners of Qin; we fear the troops may not trust us. We would prefer a capable horseman from Your Majesty's entourage to assist us." Although Guan Ying was young, he had repeatedly fought bravely; thus, King Han appointed Guan Ying as Zhong Da Fu and ordered Li Bi and Luo Jia to serve as Left and Right Lie Wei. They led the Langzhong cavalry in a battle against Chu forces east of Yingyang, achieving a decisive victory. Ordered separately to attack the rear of the Chu army and cut off their supply lines from Yangwu to Xiangyi, he carried out this mission. He defeated Xiang Guan, a general of Xiang Yu, at Lu Xia, killing one Right Sima and one cavalry commander in the process. He defeated Zhe Gong Wang Wu, stationed at Yanshi to the west, and his troops killed five Loufan generals and one Lian Yin. He attacked Wang Wu's deputy general Huan Ying at the foot of Ba Ma, defeating him and having his troops kill one Du Wei. With cavalry he crossed south of the Yellow River to escort King Han to Luoyang and was sent northward to meet Chancellor Han Xin's forces at Handan. Returning to Ao Cang, Ying was promoted to Yu Shi Da Fu (Minister of Justice).

## 25

In the third year, as a Lie Hou noble, he received his fief at Duping Xiang. As Yu Shi Da Fu, he received orders to command Langzhong cavalry and report eastward to Chancellor Han Xin. He defeated the Qi forces at Lixia, capturing General Hua Wushang and forty-six other officers and soldiers in his unit. He subdued Linzi and captured Tian Guang, the governor of Qi. He pursued Qi's chancellor Tian Heng to Ying and Bo, defeating his cavalry. His troops killed one cavalry general and captured four others alive. He captured Ying and Bo and defeated General Tian Xi of Qi at Qiansheng, with his troops killing Xi. Moving east with Han Xin, he attacked Long Ju and Liu Gong Xuan at Gaomi. His troops beheaded Long Ju, captured one Right Sima, one Lian Yin, ten Loufan generals alive, and personally captured Zhou Lan, the deputy general.

## 26

With Qi territory pacified, Han Xin declared himself King of Qi and ordered Ying to lead a separate force against Chu's general Gong Gao at the north of Lu, defeating him. Turning southward, he defeated the commander of Xue Jun and personally captured one cavalry general. He attacked Fu Yang, advancing to Xiaxiang and further southeast to Tong, Qulv, and Xu. Crossing the Huai River, he brought all cities and towns under submission, reaching Guangling. Xiang Yu sent Xiang Sheng, Xue Gong, and Tan Gong to retake the region north of the Huai River. Ying crossed the northern Huai River and defeated Xiang Sheng and Tan Gong at Xiaopi, killing Xue Gong. He captured Xiaopi, defeated Chu cavalry at Pingyang, pacified Pengcheng, took prisoner Zhu Guo Xiang Tuo, and subdued Liu, Xue, Pei, Zan, Xiao, and Xiang. He attacked Ku and Qiao, recapturing the deputy general Zhou Lan. He met with King Han at Yixiang. He followed in attacking Xiang Ji's forces below Chen, defeating them; his troops killed two Loufan generals and captured eight cavalry commanders. He was granted an additional 2,500 households to his fief.

## 27

After Xiang Ji was defeated and fled from Gaixia, Ying, as Yu Shi Da Fu, received orders to lead chariots and cavalry separately in pursuit of Xiang Ji until Dongcheng, where he defeated him. Five soldiers under his command jointly beheaded Xiang Ji; all were granted the noble title of Lie Hou. He captured one Left Sima and one Right Sima, along with 12,000 soldiers, securing all the officers and men of their army. He subdued Dongcheng and Liyang. Crossing the river, he defeated Wu Jun Chang at Wuxia, captured the governor of Wu, and pacified the commanderies of Wu, Yuzhang, and Kuaiji. He returned to pacify the region north of Huai River, subduing a total of fifty-two counties.

## 28

When King Han was enthroned as emperor, he granted Ying an additional 3,000 households for his fief. That autumn, serving as Cheqi Jiangjun (General of Chariots and Cavalry), he followed in defeating Yan Wang Zang Tu. The following year, he accompanied the emperor to Chen and captured Chu Wang Xin. He returned, was granted a fief charter ensuring hereditary succession without interruption, with 2,500 household tax revenues from Yingyin, and was ennobled as Yingyin Hou.

## 29

As Cheqi Jiangjun, he followed in attacking the rebellious King Han Xin of Yan at Dai, reaching Maiyi. He received orders to separately subdue six counties north of Loufan, beheading Dai's Left Chancellor and defeating Hu cavalry north of Wuquan. He again followed in attacking Han Xin's Hu cavalry below Jinyang, where his troops beheaded one Hu Bai Ti general. Ordered to command the combined cavalry of Yan, Zhao, Qi, Liang, and Chu, he defeated Hu cavalry at Keshi. Reaching Pingcheng, his forces were surrounded by the Hu; he withdrew eastward to Dongyuan.

## 30

He followed in attacking Chen Xi, receiving orders to separately attack the forces of Xi's Chancellor Hou Chang at Quini, defeating them and having his troops behead Chang along with five other generals. He subdued Quini, Lunu, Shangquyang, Anguo, and Anping. He captured Dongyuan.

## 31

When Ling Bu rebelled, serving as Cheqi Jiang Jun, he led the advance forces and attacked Ling Bu's deputy general at Xiang, defeating him and beheading three Loufan generals. He then advanced to defeat Ling Bu's Shang Zhuguo forces and Da Sima forces. He further defeated another of Ling Bu's deputy generals, Feizhu. Ying personally captured one Left Sima alive; his troops beheaded ten of their junior generals, pursuing the defeated forces to the Huai River. He was granted an additional 2500 households for taxation. After Ling Bu's defeat, Emperor Gao returned and issued a decree granting Ying 5000 households in Yingyin for his fief, replacing the previous tax-revenue areas. In total, he captured two officials of the rank of Erqianshi, separately defeated sixteen armies, subdued forty-six cities, pacified one kingdom, two commanderies, and fifty-two counties, capturing two generals, one Zhu Guo (pillar of the state), one Xiang Guo (chancellor), and ten more Erqianshi officials.

## 32

After defeating Ling Bu, Ying returned but Emperor Gao died soon after; Ying then served as a noble under Xiao Hui Di and the Empress Dowager Lü. After the Empress Dowager's death, Lu Lu and others appointed themselves as generals under Zhao Wang, stationed in Chang'an, and plotted rebellion. Qi Ai Wang heard of this and raised an army to the west, intending to enter and punish those who were unworthy of their titles. Upon hearing this, Grand General Lu Lu dispatched Ying as the supreme commander to lead troops in repelling them. Ying advanced as far as Yingyang, where he conspired with Jiao Hou and others. Taking advantage of the situation, they stationed troops at Yingyang and sent a message to Qi Wang about the matter of 诛杀 Lu clan members; thus, Qi's forces halted their advance. After Jiao Hou and others had eliminated the Lu clan, Qi Wang withdrew his forces and returned. Ying also withdrew from Yingyang and returned, joining Jiao Hou and Chen Ping in jointly enthroning Dai Wang as Emperor Xiao Wen. Emperor Xiao Wen then increased Ying's fief by 3,000 additional households, bestowed upon him a thousand catties of gold, and appointed him as Tai Wei.

## 33

Three years later, Jiao Hou Bo resigned from his chancellor position to return to his fief; Ying became Chancellor and the Tai Wei office was abolished. In that year, the Xiongnu launched a large-scale invasion into Beidi and Shangjun commanderies. The emperor ordered Chancellor Ying to lead 85,000 cavalry troops to attack them. After the Xiongnu withdrew, Ji Bei Wang rebelled; thus, an imperial decree was issued recalling Ying's forces. More than a year later, Ying died while serving as Chancellor and was posthumously honored with the title Yi Hou. His son Pinghou A succeeded him as marquis. He died in the twenty-eighth year, and his son Qiang succeeded him as marquis. In the thirteenth year, Qiang committed a crime; the title was abolished for two years. In the third year of Yuanguang, the emperor enfeoffed Guan Ying's grandson Xian as Linru Hou to continue the Guan clan line. Eight years later, he was found guilty of bribery and corruption; his fief was abolished.

## 34

Grand Historian said: When I visited Feng and Pei, I inquired of the old men who had survived those times. Visiting the former homes of Xiao, Cao, Fan Kuai, and Teng Gong, as well as their past lives, what I heard was truly remarkable! At the time when they were butchers, dog-sellers, and silk merchants, could they have imagined that by hitching their fortunes to great men, they would leave a name in the Han court and pass down virtue through generations? I have spoken with others widely about it; this is how I describe the rise of Gaozu's meritorious ministers.
