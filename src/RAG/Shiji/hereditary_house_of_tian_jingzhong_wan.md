# Hereditary House of Tian Jingzhong Wan

## 1

Chen Wan was a son of Chen Liegong Ta. Wan was born when the Zhou historian passed through Chen. King Lie of Chen had divination performed for Wan, and the hexagram obtained was Guan turning into Pi: "This is 'Observing the Light of the State,' which is auspicious for serving as a guest at the king's court." Will this mean that he will succeed in ruling Chen? Or not here but in a foreign state? It will not be him himself, but his descendants. If it is in a foreign state, it must be of the Jiang surname. The Jiang surname descends from the four yue. No thing can have two great powers; as Chen declines, will this one flourish?"

## 2

King Lie was the youngest son of King Wen of Chen. His mother was a woman from Cai. After King Wen died, his elder brother Bao succeeded to the throne and became known as King Huan. King Huan was not of the same mother as Ta. When King Huan fell ill, the Cai people killed King Huan Bao and Crown Prince Mian on behalf of Ta and installed Ta as ruler; he became known as King Lie. After ascending to the throne, King Lie married a woman from Cai. The woman from Cai was unfaithful with men from Cai and often returned home; King Lie also frequently went to Cai. The youngest son of King Huan, Lin, resented King Lie for killing his father and elder brother. He therefore ordered the people of Cai to lure King Lie and kill him. Lin declared himself ruler and became known as King Zhuang. Therefore, Chen Wan could not ascend to the throne but served instead as a minister of Chen. King Lie's assassination occurred because he left the country for licentious reasons; therefore, the Chunqiu states, "The people of Cai killed Chen Ta," thus assigning blame.

## 3

After King Zhuang died, his younger brother Chujiu was installed as ruler and became known as Xuan Gong. In the eleventh year of King Xuan's reign (2), he killed his crown prince Yukou. Yukou and Wan were close friends, and fearing that disaster might befall him, Wan therefore fled to Qi. Duke Huan of Qi wanted to appoint him as an official, but Wan declined, saying: "A foreigner like me is fortunate merely to be spared from carrying burdens on my back; this is your grace, Your Majesty. I dare not accept a high position." Duke Huan appointed him as head of the artisans. Yizhong of Qi wished to marry his daughter to Wan, and divination was performed. The omen said: "This is called 'The phoenix in flight, harmonizing calls resoundingly.'" Descendants of the Gui will be nurtured by the Jiang. In five generations, it shall flourish and reach the rank of chief minister. After eight generations, none will surpass it." He finally married Wan. When Wan fled to Qi, Duke Huan of Qi had already reigned for fourteen years.

## 4

After Wan died, he was posthumously honored with the title Jingzhong. Jingzhong gave birth to Zhi Mengyi. When Jingzhong went to Qi, the family changed their surname from Chen to Tian.

## 5

Tian Zhi Mengyi gave birth to Min Mengzhuang; Tian Min Mengzhuang gave birth to Wenzizi Xuwu. Tian Wenzizi served Duke Zhuang of Qi.

## 6

A minister of Jin, Luan Cheng, rebelled in Jin and fled to Qi; Duke Zhuang of Qi received him as a valued guest. Yan Ying and Tian Wenzizi advised against it, but Duke Zhuang did not listen.

## 7

After Wenzizi died, his son Huanzi Wuyu was born. Tian Huanzi Wuyu had great strength and served Duke Zhuang of Qi, gaining much favor.

## 8

After Wuyu died, his sons Wuzi Kai and Lizi Qi were born. Tian Lizi Qi served Duke Jing of Qi as a minister. He collected taxes from the people using small measures but gave them grain with large measures, thus secretly performing benevolent acts for the people, and Duke Jing did not prohibit this. Because of this, the Tian family gained the hearts of the people of Qi; their clan grew stronger, and the populace came to think highly of the Tians. Yanzi repeatedly advised Duke Jing, but Duke Jing did not listen. Later, when he was sent as an envoy to Jin, Yanzi privately said to Shuxiang: "The administration of the state of Qi will ultimately belong to the Tian family."

## 9

After Yan Ying died, the Fan and Zhonghang families rebelled against Jin. Jin attacked them urgently, so the Fan and Zhonghang clans requested grain from Qi. Tian Qi wanted to rebel and build alliances among the feudal lords, so he persuaded Duke Jing: "The Fan and Zhonghang have often shown kindness to Qi; therefore, Qi cannot fail to come to their aid." Qi sent Tian Qi to rescue them and provided grain.

## 10

Duke Jing's crown prince died, and later he had a favored concubine named Rui Zi, who bore him a son named Tu. When Duke Jing fell ill, he ordered his prime minister Guiguo Huizi and Gao Zhaozi to name his son Tu as crown prince. After Duke Jing died, the two ministers Gao and Guo installed Tu on the throne; he became known as Yan Ruzi. However, Tian Qi was displeased and wished to install another son of Duke Jing, Yangsheng. Yangsheng had long been on good terms with Tian Qi. When Yan Ruzi was installed, Yangsheng fled to Lu. Tian Qi pretended to serve Gao Zhaozi and Guo Huizi, attending each court session as a charioteer. He said: "Initially, the ministers did not wish to install Ruzi." "Now that Ruzi has been installed as ruler, you are his prime minister and he is the sovereign; all the ministers feel threatened and are plotting rebellion." He also deceived the other ministers, saying: "Gao Zhaozi is formidable. We should act before he moves first." The various ministers followed his lead. Tian Qi, Bao Mu, and the other ministers led troops into the palace to attack Gao Zhaozi. Zhaosi heard of this and, together with Guo Huizi, went to rescue the ruler. The sovereign's forces were defeated. Tian Qi's troops pursued Guo Huizi, who fled to Ju and was then captured and killed by Tian Qi's forces. Yan (Ruzi) [Yu] fled to Lu.

## 11

Tian Qi sent men to Lu to bring back Yangsheng. When Yangsheng arrived in Qi, he was hidden at Tian Qi's home. He invited the ministers and said: "The mother of Chang has a sacrifice with fish and beans; it would be fortunate if you could come to join in the banquet." They gathered for a drinking feast at the Tian residence. Tian Qi placed Yangsheng in a large sack and put him in the center of the seating area. They opened the sack, brought out Yangsheng, and said: "This is the ruler of Qi." The ministers all prostrated themselves in homage. They were about to swear an oath and install him, when Tian Qi falsely claimed: "I conspired with Bao Mu to jointly install Yangsheng." Bao Mu was furious and said: "Have the ministers forgotten Duke Jing's command?" The ministers wanted to retract their decision, but Yangsheng then kowtowed and said: "If it is acceptable, install me; if not, I will stop." Bao Mu feared that disaster would befall himself and so said again: "They are all sons of Duke Jing; why should this not be acceptable!" Thus, Yangsheng was installed as ruler at Tian Qi's house and became known as Dao Gong. They then sent someone to move Yan Ruzi to Tai, where they killed Tu. After Dao Gong was installed, Tian Qi became prime minister and monopolized the administration of Qi.

## 12

In the fourth year, Tian Qi died, and his son Chang succeeded him; he became known as Tian Chengzi.

## 13

Bao Mu had a grudge against King Dao of Qi and assassinated him. The people of Qi jointly installed his son Ren, who became known as Jian Gong. Tian Chang Chengzi and Jian Zhi served together as the left and right prime ministers, assisting King Jian. Tian Chang was jealous of Jian Zhi in his heart; Jian Zhi enjoyed the king's favor and could not be stripped of power. Therefore, Tian Chang again implemented Lizi's policies, lending out grain with large measures and collecting it with small ones. The people of Qi sang: "O grandmother, gather the qǐ; return to Tian Chengzi!" When ministers from Qi came to court, Yu Yang advised King Jian: "The Tians and Jians cannot coexist; you must choose between them." The king did not listen.

## 14

Zi Wo was a relative of Jian Zhi and often had conflicts with the Tian family. Tian Bao, a distant relative of the Tians, served Zi Wo and gained his favor. Zi Wo said: "I want to completely exterminate the legitimate line of the Tians and replace them with Tian Bao as their new head." Tian Bao replied: "I am already a distant relative within the Tian family." Zi Wo did not listen. Later, Tian Bao told the Tians: "Zi Wo is going to exterminate our family. If we do not act first, disaster will befall us." Zi Wo had taken refuge in the palace. The four brothers of Tian Chang rode to the palace and intended to kill Zi Wo. Zi Wo closed the gates. King Jian and his concubines were drinking at Tantai, intending to attack Tian Chang. The historian Ziyu said: "Tian Chang dares not rebel; he is merely removing a threat." King Jian then stopped. Tian Chang went out, heard that King Jian was angry, and feared punishment; he planned to flee the country. Tianzi Xing said: "Delay is a thief of affairs." Therefore, Tian Chang attacked Zi Wo. Zi Wo led his followers to attack the Tians but failed and fled. Tian's followers pursued and killed Zi Wo and Jianzhi.

## 15

King Jian fled, but Tian's followers chased him down and captured him in Xuzhou. King Jian said: "If I had followed Yu Yang's advice earlier, I would not have encountered this disaster." Tian's followers feared that if King Jian were restored to the throne, they would be punished by him, so they killed King Jian. King Jian reigned for four years before he was killed. Tian Chang then installed King Jian's younger brother Kuang as ruler, who became known as King Ping. After King Ping ascended the throne, Tian Chang served as prime minister.

## 16

After Tian Chang killed King Jian, fearing that the feudal lords would unite to punish him, he returned all the territories of Lu and Wei that had been annexed. He made a western alliance with Jin, Han, Wei, and Zhao families, and established diplomatic relations with Wu and Yue in the south. By rewarding military achievements and distributing rewards, he won the favor of the people; thus, Qi was stabilized once again.

## 17

Tian Chang said to King Ping of Qi: "Benevolence and virtue are what people desire; you should practice them, Your Majesty. Punishment is something people detest; I shall carry it out on your behalf." After five years, all the administration of Qi State fell into Tian Chang's hands. Tian Chang then completely exterminated the powerful families of Bao, Yan, Jianzhi, and other influential branches of the royal family. He carved out territory from Anping in the west to Langya in the east as his own fiefdom. The size of this fief was greater than what King Ping himself received as stipend.

## 18

Tian Chang then selected women from the state of Qi who were over seven chi tall to serve in his harem, numbering hundreds. He allowed his guests and attendants free access to the harem without restriction. When Tian Chang died, he had over seventy sons.

## 19

After Tian Chang's death, his son Xiangzi Pan succeeded him and served as prime minister of Qi. Tian Chang was posthumously honored with the title Chengzi.

## 20

After Tian Xiangzi became prime minister under King Xuan of Qi, the three Jin states killed Zhi Bo and divided his territory. Xiangzi appointed his brothers and relatives as magistrates of the capital cities, maintained diplomatic relations with the three Jin states, and thus effectively controlled the state of Qi.

## 21

After Xiangzi's death, his son Zhuangzi Bai succeeded him. Tian Zhuangzi served as prime minister to King Xuan of Qi. In the 43rd year of King Xuan's reign, Qi attacked Jin, destroyed Huang Cheng, and besieged Yang Hu. The following year, Qi launched attacks on Lu, Ge, and Anling. The next year, they captured one city from Lu.

## 22

After Zhuangzi's death, his son Taigong He succeeded him. Tian Taigong served as prime minister to King Xun of Qi. In the 48th year of King Xuan's reign (, 325 BC), they captured a city from Lu. The following year, King Xuan met with the people of Zheng at Xicheng. They attacked Wei and captured Wuqiu. In the 51st year of King Xuan's reign he died, and Tian Hui rebelled from Linqiu.

## 23

After King Xuan's death, his son King Kang Dài ascended the throne. In the fourteenth year of King Dài's reign, he indulged in wine and concubines and did not attend to state affairs. Taigong then exiled King Kang to the seacoast, granting him only one city for sustenance and to maintain ancestral sacrifices. The following year, Lu defeated Qi at Pinglu.

## 24

In the third year, Taigong met with Duke Wen of Wei at Zuzhe and requested to be recognized as a feudal lord. Duke Wen of Wei then sent envoys to the Zhou Son of Heaven and the feudal lords, requesting that Tian He, the prime minister of Qi, be granted the title of a feudal lord. The Zhou Son of Heaven approved the request. In the nineteenth year of King Kang's reign, Tian He was enfeoffed as Marquis of Qi and officially recognized by the Zhou court, marking the beginning of his first year.

## 25

Two years after Marquis Taigong He became ruler of Qi, he died and was succeeded by his son Duke Huan Wu. In the fifth year of Duke Huan Wu's reign, Qin and Wei attacked Han, which sought military aid from Qi. Duke Huan of Qi summoned his ministers and asked: "Is it better to rescue them early or later?" Zou Ji said, "It is better not to rescue at all." Duangan Peng said: "If we do not come to their aid, Han will fall and be annexed by Wei; it is better to rescue them." Tian Chensi said: "Your Majesty's plan is mistaken! Qin and Wei are attacking Han and Chu, so Zhao must come to their rescue. This is an opportunity for Qi to take Yan from the heavens." Duke Huan said: "Well spoken." He secretly informed the Han envoy and sent him away. Han, believing they had secured Qi's aid, engaged in battle with Qin and Wei. Chu and Zhao heard of this and indeed raised armies to come to their rescue. Qi then launched a surprise attack on the state of Yan, capturing Sangqiu.

## 26

In the sixth year, Qi came to Wei's rescue. After Duke Huan's death, his son Wang Yingqi succeeded him as ruler. In this year, the former King Kang of Qi died without an heir; all his fiefs were incorporated into the Tian family's holdings.

## 27

In the first year of Duke Weiwang's reign, the three Jin states took advantage of Qi's mourning period to attack Lingqiu. In the third year, the three Jin states extinguished the Jin royal house and divided its territory among themselves. In the sixth year, Lu attacked Qi and captured Yangguan. Jin launched an attack on Qi, advancing as far as Boling. In the seventh year, Wei attacked Qi and captured Xueling. In the ninth year, Zhao attacked Qi and captured Zhen.

## 28

Since Duke Weiwang first ascended the throne, he did not govern effectively but delegated authority to high-ranking officials and ministers. During these nine years, various feudal lords launched joint attacks, and the people of Qi fell into disorder. At this point, Duke Weiwang summoned the Magistrate of Jimo and said to him: "Since you took charge of Jimo, I have received daily reports criticizing your governance. Yet when I sent people to inspect Jimo, the fields were cultivated, the people well-provided for, official matters handled promptly, and peace prevailed in the east. This means you have not flattered my attendants to gain praise." He was granted a fief of ten thousand households. Duke Weiwang summoned the Magistrate from A and said: "Since you have been in charge of A, I hear daily praises about your governance. Yet when I sent envoys to inspect A, the fields were uncultivated and the people poor and suffering. When Zhao previously attacked Zhen, you could not come to its rescue. Wei captured Xueling, yet you were unaware of it. This means that you bribed my attendants with gifts to gain praise." On this day, the Magistrate of A was boiled alive, and all those on his side who had praised him were also executed in the same manner. He then raised an army to attack Zhao and Wei in the west, defeating Wei at Zuzhe and besieging King Huai. King Huai requested to offer Guan as a gesture of reconciliation; in return, the people of Zhao returned Changcheng to Qi. Thus, the state of Qi was shaken and fearful; no one dared to conceal faults or falsehoods, and everyone strived to be sincere in their duties. The state of Qi then achieved great order and prosperity. When the feudal lords heard about this, none dared to send troops against Qi for over twenty years.

## 29

Zou Ji performed before Duke Weiwang by playing the guqin, and Duke Weiwang was pleased and housed him in a chamber to his right. After a short while, the king played the guqin; Zou Ji pushed open the door and said: "Your playing of the guqin is excellent!" The king was startled and displeased, put down his guqin, gripped his sword, and said: "You have not yet observed my playing closely. How can you know it is good?" Zou Ji replied: "The deep and warm tone of the main string represents Your Majesty; the sharp and clear tones of the smaller strings represent the prime minister; the deep pressing and smooth release represents government decrees; the harmonious resonance, where the large and small strings complement each other without clashing, like the four seasons in balance—this is why I know it to be excellent." The king said: "Your words are well-spoken." Zou Ji replied: "Why speak only of music? Governing a state and pacifying the people all lie within this principle." The king again became displeased and said: "If it is about the principles of the five tones, indeed no one compares to you, Master. But as for governing a state and pacifying its people, what business does that have with the guqin?" Zou Ji said: "The deep and warm tone of a large string represents the ruler; the sharp, clear tones of small strings represent the prime minister. The deep pressing and smooth release symbolizes government decrees; the harmonious resonance where the large and small tones complement each other, avoiding discord while maintaining balance—this represents the four seasons. The repetition without disorder is what brings about prosperity in governance; the continuity and directness preserve life or death: thus it is said that when the guqin's tones are harmonious, the world is well governed. Therefore, for governing a state and pacifying the people, nothing compares to the five tones." The king said: "Well spoken."

## 30

Zou Ji visited the court for three months and was appointed prime minister. Chunyu Kun saw him and said: "Well spoken indeed! I have a foolish thought, but I wish to present it before you." Zou Ji said: "Please instruct me carefully." Chunyu Kun said: "To preserve all is to have prosperity; to lose all is to face ruin." Zou Ji said: "I shall heed your words carefully. Please, do not move away from my presence." Chunyu Kun said: "The grease of a pig and the thorny axle are used to make things smooth, yet they cannot turn a square hole." Zou Ji said: "I shall heed your words. Please allow me to serve you diligently on all sides." Chunyu Kun said: "Bow glue and old dry wood are used for binding, yet they cannot mend a loose crack." Zou Ji said: "I shall heed your words. I will carefully align myself with the people." Chunyu Kun said: "Even a worn fox fur coat cannot be mended with the hide of a yellow dog." Zou Ji said: "I shall heed your words. Let me carefully choose virtuous men and not allow petty people among them." Chunyu Kun said: "A large cart without proper adjustment cannot bear its usual load; a zither or se lute not properly tuned cannot produce the five tones." Zou Ji said: "I shall heed your words. Allow me to carefully revise laws and supervise corrupt officials." After finishing his words, Chunyu Kun hurriedly left. Upon reaching the gate, he turned to his servant and said: "This man—when I spoke five subtle remarks to him, his responses were as immediate as an echo to a sound. This man will not be long before being enfeoffed." After one year, he was enfeoffed at Xiapi and given the title of Marquis Cheng.

## 31

In the twenty-third year of King Wei's reign, he met with the King of Zhao in Pinglu. In the twenty-fourth year, he held a hunting gathering with the King of Wei at the outskirts. The King of Wei asked: "Do you also have treasures?" King Wei replied: "No." The King of Liang said: "Even though my state is small, I still have ten pearls each an inch in diameter that can illuminate the front and back of twelve carriages. How then can a great state with ten thousand chariots lack treasures?" King Wei replied: "What I regard as treasure differs from what Your Majesty considers." My minister Tanshi, when stationed to defend the southern city, caused the people of Chu to dare not raid eastward; twelve feudal lords from the Si River region all came to pay homage. My minister Zi, when stationed at Gaotang, caused the people of Zhao to dare not fish eastward in the Yellow River. My official Qianfu, when stationed at Xuzhou, caused the people of Yan to hold sacrifices before their northern gate and those of Zhao before their western gate; over seven thousand families moved there to follow them. My minister Zhongshou, when tasked with guarding against thieves, ensured that nothing lost on the road was ever picked up. These treasures can illuminate a thousand li—how could they be limited to just twelve carriages!" The King of Liang, feeling ashamed, left without further words.

## 32

In the twenty-sixth year, King Hui of Wei besieged Handan, and the State of Zhao sought aid from Qi. King Wei of Qi summoned his ministers to deliberate: "Should we rescue Zhao or not?" Zou Ji said: "It is better not to rescue them." Duangan Peng said: "Not rescuing would be unjust, and it would also be disadvantageous." King Wei asked: "Why is that?" Duangan Peng replied: "If the State of Wei annexes Handan, what benefit would it bring to Qi? Moreover, if we rescue Zhao and station our troops at its outskirts, then Zhao will not be attacked while Wei remains intact. Therefore, it would be better to attack Xiangling in the south and weaken Wei; once Handan falls, we can take advantage of Wei's weakened state." King Wei followed his plan.

## 33

Later, Chenghou Zou Ji and Tian Ji did not get along well. Gongsun Yue said to Chenghou Ji: "Why don't you plan an attack on Wei? Tian Ji will certainly lead it." If the battle is won and a victory achieved, then your strategy has succeeded; if we are defeated in battle, either Tian Ji will die before or be captured after the retreat, and his fate will rest in your hands." Thus Chenghou advised King Wei to send Tian Ji on a southern campaign against Xiangling. In the tenth month, Handan fell, and Qi then mobilized its forces to attack Wei, decisively defeating them at Guiling. At this time, the State of Qi became the strongest among the feudal states; it declared itself a king and issued commands to all under heaven.

## 34

In the thirty-third year, he executed his official Mou Xin.

## 35

In the thirty-fifth year, Gongsun Yue again said to Chenghou Ji: "Why don't you have someone carry ten gold pieces and go to the market to consult a fortune-teller, saying, 'I am a man of Tian Ji. I have won three battles in succession, my reputation and prestige spread throughout the world." Do you think it is auspicious or not for me to undertake a great endeavor?'" After the fortune-teller left, they had someone arrest him and verify his words at the king's court. Tian Ji heard of this and led his followers to attack Linzi, seeking Chenghou, but failed and fled.

## 36

In the thirty-sixth year, King Wei died; his son Xuanwang Pijiang succeeded to the throne.

## 37

In the first year of King Xuan's reign, the State of Qin employed Shang Yang. The Zhou court conferred the title of marquis on Duke Xiao of Qin.

## 38

In the second year, Wei attacked Zhao. Zhao and Han formed an alliance to jointly attack Wei. Zhao suffered a setback, and the battle took place at Nanliang. King Xuan summoned Tian Ji to restore his former position. The State of Han requested aid from Qi. King Xuan summoned his ministers and deliberated: "Should we send help early or late?" Zou Ji said: "It is better not to save them." Tian Ji said: "If we do not rescue them, Han will be forced into the hands of Wei. It is better to send help early." Sunzi said: "If we rescue Han and Wei before their forces are exhausted, it means that our state will take the brunt of Wei's army on behalf of Han. In the end, we would be at Han's mercy." Moreover, Wei has the ambition to destroy a state; when Han sees its own impending destruction, it will certainly turn eastward and appeal for aid from Qi. If we then strengthen our alliance with Han and wait until Wei is weakened, we can gain great advantage while earning a respected reputation." King Xuan said: "Well spoken." He then secretly informed Han's envoy and sent him away. Han, relying on Qi, fought five battles without success and eventually surrendered its state to Qi in the east. Qi then mobilized its troops, appointing Tian Ji and Tian Ying as generals, with Sunzi serving as commander, to rescue Han and Zhao in their attack on Wei. They decisively defeated the Wei forces at Malin, killing their general Pang Juan and capturing Crown Prince Shen of Wei. After this, the kings of the three Jin states all came through Tian Ying to pay homage to King Qi at Bofang, where they formed an alliance and departed.

## 39

In the seventh year, he met with the king of Wei in the southern part of Ping'a. The following year, they again met at Zhen. King Hui of Wei died. The following year, he met with King Xiang of Wei in Xuzhou; the feudal lords conferred royal titles upon each other. In the tenth year, the State of Chu besieged our city of Xuzhou. In the eleventh year, Qi and Wei jointly attacked Zhao; in response, Zhao breached the Yellow River to flood Qi and Wei's forces, forcing them to withdraw.

## 40

In the eighteenth year, King Xuan favored scholars and traveling orators. Among them were figures such as Zou Yan, Chunyu Kun, Tian Pian, Jiezi, Shen Dao, and Huanyuan, totaling seventy-six individuals. All of them were granted noble titles and appointed as senior officials, yet they did not hold administrative posts but instead engaged in discussions and debates. Therefore, the scholars at Jixia in Qi flourished once more, numbering in the hundreds or even thousands.

## 41

In the nineteenth year, King Xuan died; his son King Min Di succeeded to the throne.

## 42

In the first year of King Min's reign, Qin sent Zhang Yi to meet with the chief ministers of the feudal lords at Niesang. In the third year, Tian Ying was enfeoffed in Xue. In the fourth year, a bride was fetched from Qin. In the seventh year, Qi and Song jointly attacked Wei and defeated them at Guanzhe.

## 43

In the twelfth year, they launched an attack on Wei. Chu besieged Yongshi, and Qin defeated Qu Gai. Su Dai said to Tian Zhen, "I wish to make a request of you. The matter I have in mind is very well-considered; if it benefits you through the state of Chu, whether it succeeds or not, it will be a blessing for you." Now, I was standing at the gate when a guest said: "King Wei told Han Feng and Zhang Yi, 'Zhu Zao is about to fall, and Qi's troops are advancing. If you come to rescue me, that would be good.'" "If you do not come to my aid, I will not be able to hold out." This is merely a turn of phrase. "If the armies of Qin and Han do not move eastward, within ten more days, the House of Wei will shift its allegiance from Han to Qin. Qin will then expel Zhang Yi and join hands with Qi and Chu. This would mean your matter is accomplished." Tian Zhen said, "How can we prevent them from moving east?" He replied, "Han Feng's words to the King of Han about rescuing Wei will certainly not be phrased as 'Feng believes in Wei,' but rather he must say, 'Feng intends to use the armies of Qin and Han to drive back Qi and Song from the east. Then, Feng can unite the forces of three states, take advantage of Qu Gai's exhaustion, and carve territory out of Chu to the south; thus, all the land will certainly be regained.'" Zhang Yi's words to the King of Qin about rescuing Wei will certainly not say, "Yi believes in Wei," but rather he must say: "Yi intends to use the armies of Qin and Hans to resist Qi and Song from the east; Yi will unite the forces of three states and take advantage of Qu Gai's exhaustion. Then we can carve territory out of southern Chu, save a dying state by name, and actually conquer Sanchuang and return it—this is your Majesty's great achievement." You should order the King of Chu to cede land to Han, and let Qin mediate. Tell the King of Qin: "Please grant Han this land, and Your Majesty may bestow it upon Sanchuan; thus, Han will gain territory from Chu without deploying its troops." What would Han Feng's argument for sending troops eastward say to Qin? He would say, "The Qin army will gain Sanchuan without fighting; by attacking Chu and Han to pressure Wei, the House of Wei dares not move eastward. This isolates Qi." What would Zhang Yi's argument for sending troops east say? He would say, "Qin and Han desire land but have troops on standby; by issuing a show of strength toward Wei, the House of Wei now has no choice but to maintain its alliance with Qi and Chu, as it is backed into a corner." The House of Wei, pressured by Qin and Han, will have no choice but to shift its allegiance and serve Qi and Chu. The King of Chu desires this outcome but has nothing to offer in return for land. If you can arrange for the armies of Qin and Han not to fight yet still gain territory, that would be a great favor to Chu. The kings of Qin and Han will be pressured by Han Feng and Zhang Yi to send troops eastward in order to subdue Wei. You, holding the upper hand, can then demand from Qin and Han with certainty. This situation would benefit you greatly while harming Zhang Zi's position significantly."

## 44

In the thirteenth year, King Hui of Qin died. In the twenty-third year, Qi and Qin jointly defeated Chu at Chongqiu. In the twenty-fourth year, Qin sent Jingyang Jun as a hostage to Qi. In the twenty-fifth year, Jingyang Jun was returned to Qin. Mengchang Jun Xue Wen entered Qin and immediately became its prime minister. Wen fled away. In the twenty-sixth year, Qi joined with Han and Wei to attack Qin, advancing as far as the Hangu Pass where they stationed their troops. In the twenty-eighth year, Qin made peace with Han by ceding territory outside the Yellow River; thus, hostilities ceased. In the twenty-ninth year, Zhao killed its Lord Fu. Qi assisted Zhao in annihilating Zhongshan.

## 45

In the thirty-sixth year, King Min was proclaimed East Emperor, and King Zhao of Qin became West Emperor. Su Dai came from Yan to Qi and met at the eastern gate of Zhanghua Palace. King Min said, "Ah! That's good. Come in, you!" "Qin sent Wei Ran to bestow the title of Emperor upon me. What do you think about it?" He replied, "Your Majesty's question to me was abrupt, and the source of this trouble is subtle. I hope Your Majesty will accept it but refrain from formally assuming the title." "If Qin claims the title, and all under heaven accepts this without objection, then Your Majesty may claim it as well. But if not, there will be no one to follow." Moreover, allowing Qin to take the title first and letting us yield in this matter of imperial designation would cause no harm. "If Qin claims it, but all under heaven resents it, then Your Majesty should refrain from claiming the title and use this to gain support from all under heaven. This would be a great advantage." Moreover, if two emperors are established in the world, will Your Majesty consider it more honorable to have Qi as one of them? Or will they respect Qin more?" The king said, "They would respect Qin." He said, "If we abandon the title of emperor, will all under heaven love Qi?" Or will they love Qin more?" The king said, "They would love Qi and hate Qin." He said, "If the two emperors form an alliance to attack Zhao, which is more advantageous: attacking Zhao or attacking the tyrant of Song?" The king said, "It would be more beneficial to attack the tyrant of Song." He replied, "The alliance is equal in status. However, if we accept the title of emperor with Qin, all under heaven will respect Qin and look down on Qi; but if we abandon the imperial title, all under heaven will love Qi and resent Qin. Attacking Zhao would be less advantageous than attacking the tyrant of Song. Therefore, I hope Your Majesty clearly abandons the title to gain support from all under heaven, breaks the alliance with Qin as a guest, avoids competing for supremacy, and in the meantime seizes control of Song." Possessing Song would place Wei's southern territories at risk; Controlling the western side of the Ji River would endanger Zhao's eastern territory near A; Possessing the north of the Huai River would threaten Chu's eastern territories; Controlling Tao and Pinglu would mean that the gates of Liang remain closed. If we abandon the imperial title and instead use it as a pretext to attack the tyrant of Song, our state will gain weight in influence while earning respect. Yan and Chu would thus be compelled into submission, and no one under heaven would dare disobey—this is an action worthy of Tang and Wu. Respecting Qin for the sake of appearances, yet causing all under heaven to resent it—this is what is called achieving respect through humility. I hope Your Majesty carefully considers this." Thus, Qi abandoned the imperial title and reverted to being a king; Qin also relinquished its imperial status.

## 46

In the thirty-eighth year, they attacked Song. King Zhao of Qin was furious and said, "My affection for Song is equal to my affection for Xincheng and Yangjin. Han Nie is a friend of mine, yet he attacks what I love—why?" Su Dai on behalf of Qi said to the King of Qin: "Han Nie's attack on Song is actually for Your Majesty's benefit. Qi is strong, and if it is supported by Song, Chu and Wei will surely be frightened. If they are frightened, they will turn westward to serve Qin. Thus, Your Majesty would not need to mobilize a single soldier or harm one man, yet you could gain Anyi without effort—this is exactly what Han Nie has been praying for on your behalf." The King of Qin said, "I am troubled by how difficult it is to understand Qi. Sometimes they align with the alliance and sometimes with the balance—what does this mean?" He replied, "Can any state in the world truly know what Qi intends? Qi attacks Song because it knows that by serving Qin, a powerful state can support itself. If Qi does not serve Qin to the west, then Song will remain unstable and uncontrollable. Among the white-haired, wandering scholars in the central regions who have accumulated wisdom, not one has spoken well of Qi while urging to sever ties between Qi and Qin. Those who ride westward with their chariots linked together do not praise Qi; those who ride eastward likewise do not commend Qin. Why is that? Because none of them wish to see Qi and Qin united. How wise are Jin and Chu, yet how foolish are Qi and Qin! If Jin and Chu unite, they will surely plot against Qi and Qin; if Qi and Qin unite, they will certainly target Jin and Chu. Please use this logic to decide the matter." The King of Qin said, "Agreed." Thus, Qi proceeded to attack Song. The king of Song fled and later died in Wen. Qi carved territory from the north of the Huai River in the south against Chu, and invaded westward into the three Jin states. It sought to annex the Zhou dynasty and become emperor. The lords of various states along the Si River, including Zou and Lu, all declared themselves subjects; the feudal lords were terrified.

## 47

In the thirty-ninth year, Qin launched an attack and captured nine of our fortified cities.

## 48

In the fortieth year, Yan, Qin, Chu, and the three Jin states conspired together. Each sent elite troops to attack us, defeating our forces at the western side of the Ji River. The king retreated in disarray. Yan's general Yue Yi then entered Linzi and seized all of Qi's treasures and valuables. King Min fled from the capital and went to Wei. The ruler of Wei made way in his palace for King Min, treating him as a subject and providing accommodations. King Min was arrogant; the people of Wei then harassed him. King Min left and fled to Zou and Lu, still displaying arrogance; the lords of Zou and Lu refused to admit him. He then went on to Ju. Chu sent Nao Chi with troops to rescue Qi, and he was appointed prime minister of King Min of Qi. Nao Chi then killed King Min and divided the captured lands and spoils of war with Yan.

## 49

After King Min was murdered, his son Fa Zhang changed his name and surname to become a servant in the household of Tai Shi Jiao in Ju. Tai Shi Jiao's daughter was impressed by Fa Zhang's appearance and demeanor, thinking he was no ordinary man. She pitied him and secretly provided him with clothes and food, eventually engaging in a secret relationship with him. After Nao Chi had left Ju, the people of Ju and exiled officials from Qi gathered together to search for King Min's son, intending to install him as ruler. Fa Zhang feared they might kill him, so after a long time, he finally dared to reveal, "I am the son of King Min." Thus, the people of Ju jointly installed Fa Zhang as ruler; this was King Xiang. He secured the city of Ju and issued a proclamation throughout Qi: "The king has already been established in Ju."

## 50

After King Xiang was enthroned, he made the daughter of the Tai Shi family his queen; she became known as Queen Junwang Hou. She bore a son named Jian. Tai Shi Jiao said, "My daughter did not go through the proper matchmaker and married on her own; she is no longer of my lineage. This disgraces our family." He refused to see Queen Junwang Hou for the rest of his life. Queen Junwang Hou was virtuous and did not let her father's refusal to see her affect the filial respect she showed as a daughter.

## 51

King Xiang remained in Ju for five years, when Tian Dan led the forces of Ji Mo to defeat Yan's army and welcomed King Xiang back from Ju into Linzi. All of Qi's former territories were restored to Qi. Qi granted Tian Dan the title of Anping Jun.

## 52

In the fourteenth year, Qin attacked our cities Gang and Shou. In the nineteenth year, King Xiang died; his son Jian ascended the throne.

## 53

lords In the sixth year of King Jian's reign, Qin attacked Zhao, and Qi and Chu came to its aid. Qin strategized: "If Qi and Chu are united in rescuing Zhao, we will withdraw our troops; if they are not united, we shall attack them." Zhao had no food and requested grain from Qi, but Qi refused. Zhou Zi said: "It would be better to grant their request in order to withdraw the Qin forces. If we refuse, then the Qin army will not retreat, which means that Qin's strategy has succeeded while Qi and Chu's plan has failed." Moreover, Zhao is a shield for Qi and Chu; it is like teeth having lips. If the lips are lost, the teeth will feel cold. If we lose Zhao today, tomorrow the trouble will reach Qi and Chu. Furthermore, rescuing Zhao is urgent, like pouring water from a leaking jar into a boiling pot on the verge of burning out. Rescuing Zhao is an act of noble righteousness; repelling the Qin forces would bring great renown. "To act with righteousness by rescuing a fallen state, and to display strength by repelling the powerful Qin army—yet not strive for these goals but instead be overly concerned about saving grain is a grave miscalculation in national strategy." King Qi did not listen. Qin defeated Zhao at Changping, killing over 400,000 soldiers, and then proceeded to besiege Handan.

## 54

In the sixteenth year, Qin destroyed Zhou. Queen Junwang Hou died. In the twenty-third year, Qin established the Dong commandery. In the twenty-eighth year, King Jian went to pay homage to Qin; Emperor Qin Shi Huang held a banquet in Xianyang. In the thirty-fifth year, Qin destroyed Han. In the thirty-seventh year, Qin destroyed Zhao. In the thirty-eighth year, Yan sent Jing Ke to assassinate the King of Qin; however, the king became aware and killed Ke. The following year, Qin defeated Yan, and the King of Yan fled east to Liaodong. The following year, Qin destroyed Wei, and its troops stationed at Lixia. In the forty-second year, Qin destroyed Chu. The following year, they captured Wang Jia of Dai and eliminated King Xi of Yan.

## 55

In the forty-fourth year, Qin forces attacked Qi. King Jian of Qi followed the plan of his prime minister Hou Sheng and did not fight; he surrendered his army to Qin. Qin captured King Jian and exiled him to Gong. Thus, Qi was destroyed and became a commandery of Qin. The entire world was unified under Qin; King Zheng of Qin declared himself emperor. Initially, Queen Junwang Hou was virtuous; she treated Qin with caution and maintained trust with the feudal lords. As Qi lay on the eastern coast by the sea, while Qin waged war day and night against the Three Jin states, Yan, and Chu, these five states each struggled to defend themselves from Qin. Therefore, during King Jian's forty-plus years of reign, Qi did not face military attacks. After Queen Junwang Hou's death, Hou Sheng served as prime minister of Qi. He accepted much gold from Qin and frequently sent guests to Qin. In return, Qin gave them more money; these guests became spies for Qin, advising the king to abandon resistance and submit to Qin. The state did not prepare for war or assist the Five States in attacking Qin. Because of this, Qin was able to destroy all five states. After the Five States had fallen, Qin troops finally entered Linzi, and no one among the people dared to resist. King Jian thus surrendered and was exiled to Gong. Therefore, the people of Qi resented King Jian for not joining the other feudal lords in a coalition to attack Qin early on. Instead, he listened to corrupt ministers and their guests, which led to the loss of his country. They sang: "Is it pine or cypress?" "Who is this guest who caused us to be exiled together?" This was a lament over King Jian's misfortune in trusting the wrong guests.

## 56

Grand Historian said: It is said that Confucius, in his later years, took great delight in the *Yi Jing* (Book of Changes). The art of the *Yi Jing* is profound and far-reaching; who but a learned and perceptive person could possibly pay attention to it! The historian of the Zhou dynasty, using divination, cast an oracle for Tian Jingzhong Wan and predicted events as far as ten generations later; And when Wan fled to Qi, Yizhong also divined it and said the same. The reason why Tian Qi and Chang were able to repeatedly defy two kings and monopolize the governance of Qi was not necessarily due to gradual changes in circumstances, but rather as if they followed ominous signs and auspicious omens.
