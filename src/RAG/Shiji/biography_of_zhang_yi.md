# Biography of Zhang Yi

## 1

Zhang Yi was a native of Wei. Initially, he once studied with Su Qin under Master Guigu, and both learned the art of strategy; Su Qin considered himself inferior to Zhang Yi.

## 2

Zhang Yi had already studied and traveled to persuade the feudal lords. He once attended a banquet with the prime minister of the State of Chu. Later, the prime minister lost a jade ornament, and his attendants suspected Zhang Yi, saying, "Yi is poor and has no virtue; he must be the one who stole your lord's jade." They seized Zhang Yi together and flogged him hundreds of times, but when he did not confess, they released him. His wife said, "Ah! Had you not studied and traveled to persuade others, how could you have suffered such humiliation?" Zhang Yi said to his wife, "Look, is my tongue still here?" His wife smiled and replied, "Yes, your tongue is still there." Yi said, "That will suffice."

## 3

After Su Qin had persuaded King Zhao and secured the alliance of mutual support among the states, he was still afraid that the State of Qin might attack the feudal lords, thereby breaking the agreement. Thinking about who could be sent to counteract this threat in Qin, he sent someone secretly to encourage Zhang Yi, saying: "You were once close friends with Su Qin. Now that I hold a powerful position, why don't you go there and travel around, so as to achieve your ambitions?" Zhang Yi therefore went to the State of Zhao and presented himself at Su Qin's residence seeking an audience. Su Qin then instructed his attendants not to admit him, while also ensuring that Zhang Yi could not leave for several days. After some time, he finally received him, seating him in the hall and giving him food fit only for servants and concubines. He then rebuked him several times, saying: "With your talent and ability, you have allowed yourself to be reduced to such a state of hardship and humiliation. I am certainly capable of speaking on your behalf so that you may attain wealth and rank, but you are not worth my bothering with." Zhang Yi bowed in thanks and departed. When Zhang Yi came, he had regarded Su Qin as an old friend and expected to gain support; instead, he was humiliated. Filled with anger, and thinking that none of the feudal lords were worth serving except Qin, which could harass Zhao, he therefore went directly to Qin.

## 4

Afterward, Su Qin told his attendants: "Zhang Yi is a virtuous man of the world; I fear I am not as capable as he." Now that I have been fortunate enough to be employed first, if there is anyone who can control the affairs of Qin, it would only be Zhang Yi. But he is poor and lacks the means to gain an audience. I was afraid that he might be content with small gains and not pursue greater ambitions, so I summoned him and humiliated him in order to provoke his resolve. "You must secretly support him on my behalf." He then advised King Zhao of Zhao, who issued gold and money along with carriages and horses. Su Qin sent someone to secretly follow Zhang Yi, sharing the same lodging with him, gradually drawing closer to him, providing him with carriages, horses, and money as he needed them, without informing Zhang Yi of their source. Zhang Yi thus gained an audience with King Hui of Qin. King Hui appointed him as a guest minister and consulted with him on plans to attack the feudal lords.

## 5

Su Qin's attendant then bid farewell and departed. Zhang Yi asked, "Because of you I have gained prominence; I was just about to repay my debt of gratitude. Why are you leaving?" The attendant replied, "I do not know your true worth; it is Su Qin who knows you." "Su Qin was worried that Qin would attack Zhao and break the alliance. He believed no one but you could control the affairs of Qin, so he provoked your anger and had me secretly provide you with resources—all part of Su Qin's strategy." "Now that you have been employed, I will return to report this to him." Zhang Yi said, "Alas! This was within my own strategy yet I failed to realize it. It is now clear that I am indeed inferior to Su Qin!" I have just been newly appointed; how could I possibly plot against Zhao? On my behalf, please thank Su Qin. While Su Qin is still in power, how dare I speak out?" "And as long as Su Qin remains in power, could I possibly act on my own!" After Zhang Yi became prime minister of Qin, he issued a formal proclamation to the prime minister of Chu, saying: "Initially, when I dined with you, I did not steal your jade ornament; yet you flogged me. If you can properly defend your state, then perhaps I will even dare to steal your city!"

## 6

The states of Ju and Shu attacked each other, and both sent urgent appeals for help to Qin. King Hui of Qin wanted to send troops to attack Shu, but he considered the route dangerous and narrow, making it difficult to reach. Meanwhile, Han had also launched an invasion against Qin. King Hui wished first to attack Han before attacking Shu, yet feared that this might be disadvantageous; if he attacked Shu first, he worried that Han would take advantage of Qin's weakened state. He hesitated and could not make a decision. Sim Ma Cuo and Zhang Yi debated before King Hui; Sima Cuo wished to attack Shu, while Zhang Yi said: "It would be better to attack Han." The king said, "Please explain your reasoning."

## 7

Zhang Yi said: "If we form close relations with Wei and maintain good ties with Chu, then deploy troops to the Three Rivers region, block the entrance of Shigu, control the road at Tunliu, cut off Wei's access to Nanyang, and have Chu approach Nanzheng, Qin can attack Xincheng and Yiyang to threaten the outskirts of the two Zhou states. We could then punish King Zhou for his crimes and encroach upon the territories of Chu and Wei." The Zhou royal house, knowing it cannot be saved, will surely surrender its nine tripods and other treasures. With possession of the Nine Tripods, holding the maps and records, and controlling the Son of Heaven to issue commands to all under heaven, no one in the world would dare refuse—this is the path to becoming a universal king. "Now, Shu is a remote western state and comparable to the Rong and Di tribes. Waging war there would exhaust our troops and weary our people without earning us fame; even if we gained its territory, it would not bring significant profit." I have heard that those who contend for reputation do so in the court, and those who compete for profit do so in the marketplace. "Now, the Three Rivers and the Zhou royal house are the court and marketplace of all under heaven. Yet Your Majesty does not contend for them, instead choosing to compete with the Rong and Di—this takes you far from the path of kingship."

## 8

Sima Cuo said: "That is not correct. I have heard that to enrich a state, one must expand its territory; to strengthen the army, one must first enrich the people; and to become a universal ruler, one must cultivate virtue. When these three resources are in place, kingship will naturally follow. "Now Your Majesty's territory is small and the people poor; therefore I wish to first deal with what is easy." "Shu, a remote western state, is the leader of the Rong and Di tribes and suffers from chaos akin to that of Jie and Zhou." To attack it with Qin's forces would be like sending wolves to chase a flock of sheep. Gaining its territory will suffice to expand our state, and seizing its wealth will be enough to enrich the people and strengthen the military—without exhausting our troops or causing resistance. Capturing one state will not be seen by the world as an act of aggression; gaining all the benefits from the western seas will not be considered greed. This single action would bring both reputation and tangible gains, while also earning us the name of suppressing violence and halting chaos." "But if we attack Han, seize the Son of Heaven, and take his territory, that would be a bad reputation. It may not even bring us real benefit, and we would also gain the name of being unjust. To attack what all under heaven do not desire is dangerous." I will explain why: Zhou is the ancestral house of all under heaven; Qi is a vassal state allied with Han. Zhou, knowing it has lost the Nine Tripods, and Han, aware of its loss of the Three Rivers region, will unite their forces and conspire together. They may seek alliances with Qi and Zhao to resolve matters with Chu and Wei, giving the tripods to Chu and land to Wei—Your Majesty would be unable to stop them. This is what I mean by danger." Therefore, it would be better to attack Shu. That is my conclusion."

## 9

King Hui said: "Well spoken; I shall follow your advice." Qin finally mobilized troops to attack Shu. In the tenth month, they captured it and pacified the state. The former King of Shu was demoted and given a new title as marquis, while Chen Zhuang was appointed prime minister over Shu. After Shu became part of Qin, Qin grew even stronger and wealthier, thus gaining the upper hand over the feudal lords.

## 10

略 In the tenth year of King Hui's reign, he sent Gongzi Hua and Zhang Yi to besiege Puyang, which surrendered. Zhang Yi then advised the state of Qin to renew its alliance with Wei, and sent Gongzi You as a hostage to Wei. Zhang Yi then advised King Wei, saying: "The King of Qin has treated the State of Wei very kindly; therefore, Wei cannot afford to be disrespectful." As a result, Wei ceded Shangjun and Shaoliang as tribute to King Hui of Qin in acknowledgment. King Hui then appointed Zhang Yi as prime minister, and renamed Shaoliang to Xiayang.

## 11

Zhang Yi served as prime minister of Qin for four years, during which he helped establish King Hui as king. After one year, he became a general of Qin and captured Shan. He built the Shangjun Pass defenses.

## 12

Two years later, he was sent to meet with the prime ministers of Qi and Chu at Niesang. On his return journey east, he was relieved of his post as prime minister. He then became prime minister of Wei on behalf of Qin, hoping to have Wei submit to Qin first so that the other feudal lords would follow suit. King Wei refused to listen to Zhang Yi's advice. The King of Qin became angry and launched an attack, seizing Quwo and Pingzhou from Wei. He secretly favored Zhang Yi even more intensely. Zhang Yi felt ashamed and had no way to return with a satisfactory report. He remained in Wei for four years when King Xiang of Wei died, and King Ai was enthroned. Zhang Yi once again advised King Ai, but the king did not listen. At this point, Zhang Yi secretly ordered Qin to attack Wei. Wei fought against Qin and was defeated.

## 13

The following year, Qi again defeated Wei at Guanjing. Qin once again wanted to attack Wei and first crushed the army of Han Shencha, beheading 80,000 soldiers. The feudal lords were shocked and terrified. Zhang Yi once again persuaded King Wei of Wei, saying: "The territory of Wei does not exceed a thousand li in area, and its population does not surpass 300,000. The land is flat on all sides, with the feudal lords converging from every direction like spokes of a wheel; there are no famous mountains or great rivers to serve as natural barriers. From Zheng to Liang, the distance is over 200 li; carriages can race and people can run there without needing much effort. To the south, Liang borders Chu; to the west, Han; to the north, Zhao; and to the east, Qi. Troops are stationed at all four directions, with no fewer than 100,000 men guarding watchtowers and outposts. The geographical position of Liang is inherently a battlefield. If Wei allies with Chu to the south but not with Qi, then Qi will attack from the east; if it allies with Qi to the east but not Zhao, then Zhao will attack from the north; 略微 If it does not ally with Han, then Han will attack from the west; and if it is not close to Chu, then Chu will attack from the south. This is what is known as a path of division and fragmentation."

## 14

"Moreover, when feudal lords form an alliance, it is to ensure the safety of their state, honor their ruler, strengthen their military, and gain prestige. "Now, those who form an alliance unite the world under one banner, swearing brotherhood and sacrificing white horses by the Huan River to solidify their commitment. "Even among close brothers who share the same parents, there are still disputes over money and wealth. How then can one rely on deceitful and fickle strategies left over from Su Qin to achieve success? It is clearly impossible."

## 15

"If Your Majesty does not serve Qin, then Qin will send troops to attack the area outside the Yellow River, occupy Juan, Yan, [Yan], and Suanzao, threaten Wei, seize Yangjin, and thus Zhao will be unable to move south. If Zhao cannot move south, then Liang cannot advance northward; if Liang does not go north, the alliance route is cut off. Once the alliance route is severed, Your Majesty's state will inevitably face danger—it will be impossible for your country to remain safe." "If Qin defeats Han and attacks Liang, and if Han is intimidated by Qin, then Qin and Han will unite. The fall of Liang can be expected to happen in an instant." "This is what I fear most for Your Majesty."

## 16

"For the sake of Your Majesty, there is no better plan than to serve Qin. If you serve Qin, then Chu and Han will certainly dare not act. Without the threat of Chu and Han, Your Majesty can rest easy with your head high on a pillow; there will surely be no worries for the state.

## 17

"Moreover, among all states that Qin wishes to weaken, none is more so than Chu; and among those who can weaken Chu, none are better suited than Liang. "Although Chu has the reputation of being rich and powerful, in reality it is empty and weak; its soldiers may be numerous, but they are light-footed and easily routed to the north, unable to fight with determination. If Liang mobilizes all its troops and attacks Chu from the south, it will surely achieve victory. "Seizing territory from Chu to benefit Liang, weakening Chu while pleasing Qin, and transferring disaster to another state for the safety of your own—this is a wise course of action." "If Your Majesty does not heed my advice, Qin will deploy its armored troops to launch an eastern campaign. At that point, even if you wish to serve Qin, it will no longer be possible."

## 18

"Moreover, many alliance advocates speak with fervent words but few are trustworthy. They persuade one feudal lord and become marquises; thus, all the traveling orators in the world cannot help but day and night clench their fists, glare fiercely, and gnash their teeth to extol the benefits of an alliance, trying to sway rulers." Rulers find their eloquence admirable and are swayed by their arguments—how could they avoid being deceived?

## 19

"I have heard it said that accumulated feathers can sink a boat, and many light objects can break an axle; the voices of the crowd can melt gold, and repeated defamation can destroy bones. Therefore, I hope Your Majesty will carefully deliberate on this plan, and kindly allow me to retire so as to step aside from Wei."

## 20

King Ai then abandoned the alliance agreement and followed Zhang Yi's advice to seek peace with Qin. Zhang Yi returned and was once again appointed as chancellor of Qin. After three years, Wei broke away from Qin once more to join the alliance. Qin attacked Wei and captured Quwo. The following year, Wei once again submitted to Qin.

## 21

Qin wished to attack Qi, but Qi and Chu had formed a close alliance; thus Zhang Yi went to serve as chancellor in Chu. King Huai of Chu heard that Zhang Yi was coming, and prepared the upper quarters for him, personally hosting his residence. He said, "This is a remote and humble state; what advice do you have to offer it?" Zhang Yi advised King Chu, "If Your Majesty sincerely listens to me and closes the borders with Qi, breaking all agreements with it, I will personally request for you six hundred li of land in Shangyu from Qin. Moreover, I shall arrange for a daughter of Qin to become one of your Majesty's attendants. Qin and Chu would then exchange brides and grooms, becoming eternal brotherly states." "This strategy weakens Qi in the north while benefiting Qin to the west—there is no more advantageous plan than this." King Chu was greatly pleased and agreed. All the ministers congratulated him, but only Chen Zhen mourned for him. King Chu became angry and said, "I have gained six hundred li of land without raising an army or sending troops. All my ministers are congratulating me, yet you alone mourn—why is that?" Chen Zhen replied, "Not so. In my view, the land of Shangyu cannot be obtained, and Qi and Qin will unite instead. If Qi and Qin form an alliance, disaster is sure to come." King Chu asked, "Do you have a reason for this?" Chen Zhen replied: "The reason why Qin values Chu is because of its alliance with Qi. Now, if we close the borders and sever ties with Qi, then Chu will be isolated. Why would Qin covet an isolated state like ours and give it six hundred li of Shangyu land? "When Zhang Yi arrives in Qin, he will surely betray Your Majesty. This would sever relations with Qi to the north and create a new threat from Qin to the west, and armies from both states will inevitably come." The best plan for Your Majesty is not to secretly ally with Qi while publicly severing relations, and send someone along with Zhang Yi. "If Qin gives us the land, it will not be too late to cut ties with Qi; if they do not give us the land, we can still secretly cooperate and devise a plan." King Chu said, "I wish you, Master Chen, would keep silent and say no more, while I await the acquisition of this land." He then gave Zhang Yi the seal of chancellor and lavishly bribed him. Thus, they immediately closed their borders with Qi and severed all agreements, sending one general to accompany Zhang Yi.

## 22

Zhang Yi arrived in Qin and pretended to fall from his carriage by losing the reins, not attending court for three months. King Chu heard of this and said, "Is it because I have not yet completely severed ties with Qi that Zhang Yi is acting this way?" He then sent a brave warrior to Song, borrowed the seal of Song, and went north to insult King Qi. King Qi was furious and humbled himself before Qin. The alliance between Qin and Qi was thus formed, so Zhang Yi then appeared at court and told the envoy of Chu, "I have a fief of six li in size. I wish to present it to Your Majesty's attendants." The envoy of Chu said, "I received orders from the king for six hundred li of land at Shangyu; I never heard anything about six li." The envoy returned to report this to King Chu, who became furious and mobilized troops to attack Qin. Chen Zhen said, "May I speak now?" "It is better to stop the attack and instead cede territory to bribe Qin, uniting forces with them to strike Qi. This way, we give land to Qin but take compensation from Qi; our kingdom may yet survive." King Chu did not listen and proceeded to mobilize troops, sending General Qu Gai to attack Qin. Qin and Qi jointly attacked Chu, beheading eight thousand soldiers, killing General Qu Gai, and seizing the territories of Danyang and Hanzhong. Chu then sent more troops to launch a surprise attack on Qin, reaching Lan Tian. A great battle ensued in which Chu suffered a crushing defeat. As a result, Chu ceded two cities to Qin and made peace with them.

## 23

Qin demanded from Chu the region of Qianzhong, offering in exchange the area beyond Wu Guan Pass. King Chu said, "I do not wish to exchange land; I would rather obtain Zhang Yi and then present the Qianzhong region." The King of Qin wanted to send him but could not bring himself to say it aloud. Zhang Yi then volunteered to go. King Hui said, "The King of Chu is angry with you for deceiving him about the Shangyu land. He will surely take pleasure in your death." Zhang Yi said, "Qin is strong and Chu is weak. I am on good terms with Jin Shang, who serves the Lady Zheng of Chu's wife, Zheng Xiu. Whatever Zheng Xiu says is always followed." Moreover, I am carrying Your Majesty's official credentials as an envoy to Chu; how dare they execute me?" Even if they kill me and thereby gain the Qianzhong land for Qin, it would be my greatest wish." With that, he was sent to Chu. When King Huai of Chu arrived, he had Zhang Yi imprisoned and intended to kill him. Jin Shang said to Zheng Xiu, "Do you also know that your position is low in the king's eyes?" Zheng Xiu asked, "Why do you say so?" Jin Shang said, "The King of Qin greatly values Zhang Yi and does not wish to release him. Now he is about to offer six counties in Shangyong as a bribe to Chu, send beautiful women to marry into the royal family, and provide talented female singers from the palace as attendants." "If King Chu values land and respects Qin, then these Qin women will surely be favored while you, Lady Zheng, will be discarded." "You should therefore speak on his behalf to have him released." Thus, Zheng Xiu day and night advised King Huai, saying, "Ministers serve their respective lords. Now that the land has not yet been given to Qin, they have sent Zhang Yi here, and he is of great importance to King Qin. If Your Majesty treats him without courtesy and kills Zhang Yi, then the King of Qin will surely become furious and attack Chu. I beg you to send both me and our children south to Jiangnan; otherwise we shall be at the mercy of Qin." King Huai regretted his decision, released Zhang Yi, and treated him with great courtesy as before.

## 24

After Zhang Yi was released, he had not yet left when he heard of Su Qin's death. He then advised King Chu, "The land of Qin covers half the world; its military is a match for four states. Surrounded by perilous terrain and rivers, it has four natural barriers to ensure security. It has more than a million brave warriors, a thousand chariots, ten thousand cavalry horses, and grain stores piled as high as hills. Its laws and decrees are clear, its soldiers face hardship with composure and embrace death joyfully. The ruler is wise and strict; the generals are intelligent and martial. Even without deploying troops, they could roll up the perilous terrain of Changshan Mountain and surely break the backbone of all under heaven. Any state that resists last will perish first. "Moreover, forming an alliance is no different from driving a flock of sheep to attack a fierce tiger; it is obvious that the tiger and the sheep cannot match in strength. Now, Your Majesty chooses to side with the flock of sheep rather than the fierce tiger; I secretly believe this is a grave miscalculation on your part."

## 25

"All the powerful states in the world are either Qin or Chu; if not Qin, then it is Chu. These two powers compete with each other, and their positions cannot coexist." "If Your Majesty does not side with Qin, then Qin will deploy troops to occupy Yiyang, cutting off the northern territories of Han. They will descend into Donghe, seize Chenggao, and Han must then submit as a vassal; Liang will follow suit like the wind. "If Qin attacks Chu from the west, and Han and Liang attack it from the north, how can your state possibly avoid danger?"

## 26

"Moreover, forming an alliance means gathering weak states to attack the extremely strong. It is unwise to underestimate one's enemy and engage in reckless warfare; when a state is poor yet frequently raises armies, it follows a path of peril and destruction." I have heard that if one's military forces are inferior, do not challenge for battle; if one's grain reserves are insufficient, do not engage in prolonged warfare. Those who advocate for alliances embellish their words with empty rhetoric, exaggerating the ruler's virtue. They speak only of benefits and not of dangers; when disaster from Qin finally strikes, it will be too late to take action. Therefore, I hope Your Majesty carefully considers this matter.

## 27

"Qin has Ba and Shu to the west, where large ships loaded with grain depart from Wenshan, float down the Yangtze River for over three thousand li, reaching Chu. "Each barge carries fifty soldiers and provisions for three months; drifting downstream, they can travel over three hundred li in a single day. Though the distance is great, it does not require the strength of oxen or horses. Within ten days at most, they will reach Guan Pass." "If the defenders of Guan Pass are alarmed, then from the border eastward all cities will be fortified. The regions of Qianzhong and Wu Jun will no longer belong to Your Majesty." "If Qin mobilizes its troops from Wu Guan Pass, attacks in the south, then your northern territories will be cut off. "When Qin's army attacks Chu, the danger will come within three months; but if Chu waits for aid from other feudal lords, it would take at least half a year. The timing simply does not match." "To rely on the aid of weak states and forget the calamity from powerful Qin—this is precisely what I fear for Your Majesty."

## 28

"Your Majesty once fought against Wu, winning three out of five battles, but your troops were exhausted; lodash you had to defend Xincheng alone, and the people suffered greatly. I have heard that great achievements are easily endangered, and when the people are exhausted, they resent their rulers. "To cling to an achievement prone to danger while opposing powerful Qin's intentions—this I secretly fear for Your Majesty."

## 29

"And the reason why Qin has not sent troops through Hangu Pass to attack Qi and Zhao for fifteen years is because they have been secretly plotting a strategy to unify all under heaven. "Chu once had difficulties with Qin, fighting a battle in Hanzhong. Chu was defeated, and more than seventy marquises holding jade seals were killed; as a result, Hanzhong fell to Qin." The King of Chu was greatly enraged and raised an army to attack Qin. A battle took place at Lantian. This is what is called a clash between two tigers. "When Qin and Chu exhaust each other, while Han and Wei remain intact to strike from behind—there is no more dangerous strategy than this. I hope Your Majesty will carefully consider this matter."

## 30

"If Qin sends its troops to attack Weiyang and Jin, it will surely provoke the anger of all under heaven. "If Your Majesty mobilizes all your troops to attack Song, within a few months Song can be captured. Once Song is taken and the army turns eastward, then the twelve feudal lords of Si Shang will all belong to Your Majesty."

## 31

"Su Qin, who once united all the states under a treaty of mutual trust and alliance, was enfeoffed as Lord Wu'an Jun and served as prime minister to Yan. Yet secretly he conspired with King Yan to attack and destroy Qi in order to divide its territory; he then pretended to be guilty of a crime, fled from Yan into Qi, where King Qi received him and appointed him as prime minister. After two years this was discovered, and King Qi became furious, ordering Su Qin to be torn apart by chariots in the marketplace. How could a deceitful and false man like Su Qin possibly manage the affairs of all under heaven, unify the feudal lords? It is clearly impossible.

## 32

"Now Qin and Chu share a border; they are naturally neighboring states. "If Your Majesty sincerely listens to my advice, I will request that the Crown Prince of Qin be sent as a hostage to Chu, and in return, the Crown Prince of Chu may go to Qin. I shall also offer a daughter of Qin for Your Majesty's household service, and present a city with ten thousand households as a fief for her personal use. Thus, our two states will remain like brothers forever, never waging war against each other." I believe there is no better plan than this."

## 33

Thus, after King Chu had captured Zhang Yi and was reluctant to cede the Qianzhong region to Qin, he wanted to agree. Qu Yuan said: "Previously, Your Majesty was deceived by Zhang Yi. When Zhang Yi arrived, I thought Your Majesty would have him boiled; now that you cannot bear to kill him, yet still listen to his fallacies, it is not acceptable." King Huai said: "If we agree with Zhang Yi and obtain Qianzhong, that would be a great advantage. To break the agreement afterwards would not be acceptable." Therefore, King Huai finally agreed to Zhang Yi's proposal and became friendly with Qin.

## 34

Zhang Yi left Chu and proceeded to Han, where he advised King of Han: "The land of Han is rugged and mountainous. Though it produces five kinds of grains, they are mostly beans and wheat. The people's diet mainly consists of bean porridge and vegetable soup. If there is one year without a harvest, the people cannot even afford to eat bran or chaff. The territory does not exceed nine hundred li in size and lacks food reserves for two years. Estimating Your Majesty's troops, even if they were all gathered together, they would not exceed three hundred thousand, and this number includes servants and those who carry burdens. "Subtracting the garrisons at border outposts, watchtowers, and fortifications, the actual number of available troops is no more than two hundred thousand. "Qin has over a million armed soldiers, a thousand chariots, ten thousand cavalrymen. The brave warriors with tigers' strength who leap forward bareheaded and thrust their halberds are too numerous to count. "The fine horses of Qin, the large number of armed soldiers—those who can leap forward with their front hooves and stretch back three xun between their hind legs—are beyond count. "The soldiers of the eastern mountains wear armor and helmets to engage in battle, but the people of Qin discard their armor and fight bare-chested against the enemy. They carry severed heads in one hand and capture live prisoners with the other. "The soldiers of Qin compared to those from the eastern mountains are like Meng Ben compared to a timid man; when they clash in strength, it is as if Wuhuo fights against an infant. "To send warriors as fierce as Meng Ben and Wuhuo to attack a weak state that refuses submission is no different from hanging a thousand-jun weight on an egg. There can be no hope of success."

## 35

"The ministers and feudal lords do not consider the scarcity of our land, but instead listen to flattering words from those who advocate alliance. They form cliques and flatter each other, all claiming 'If you follow my plan, we can dominate the world.'" "To disregard the long-term interests of the state and instead listen to momentary flattery is nothing but misleading the ruler, and there can be no greater mistake than this.

## 36

"If Your Majesty does not serve Qin, then Qin will send its troops to occupy Yiyang, cut off the northern territories of Han, and take Chenggao and Xingyang in the east. Then the Hongtai Palace and Sanglin Park will no longer belong to You Majesty." "To block Chenggao, cut off your northern territories—this would mean the division of Your Majesty's state. "To serve Qin first brings safety; to refuse service to Qin brings danger. "To invite disaster and yet hope for blessings, to have shallow plans but deep resentment—by opposing Qin and favoring Chu, even if you wish not to perish, it is impossible.

## 37

"Therefore, for Your Majesty's best interest, there is no better choice than serving Qin." "What Qin desires most is to weaken Chu, and among the states, Han has the greatest potential to weaken Chu. "It is not because Han is stronger than Chu, but due to its geographical position." "Now if Your Majesty serves Qin from the west and attacks Chu, King Qin will surely be pleased. "To attack Chu for its territory, turning disaster into favor with Qin—there can be no better plan than this."

## 38

King of Han accepted Zhang Yi's strategy. Zhang Yi returned to report, and King Hui of Qin enfeoffed him with five fiefs, bestowing on him the title Lord Wuxin Jun. He sent Zhang Yi eastward to advise King Mǐn of Qi: "Among all the powerful states in the world, none surpasses Qi. Its ministers and noble families are numerous, prosperous, and content. "Yet those who advise Your Majesty do so with short-sighted arguments, without considering the long-term benefits for a hundred generations. "Those who advocate alliance and advise Your Majesty always say: 'To the west of Qi is powerful Zhao, to the south are Han and Liang. "Qi, a state facing the sea, has vast territory, numerous people, strong armies and brave soldiers. Even if there were a hundred Qins, they would be powerless against Qi.'" Your Majesty finds their words wise but fails to consider the reality. "Those who advocate alliance form cliques and support each other, none of them considering it unwise. I have heard that Qi and Lu fought three times, with Lu winning all three. Yet the state of Lu soon faced danger and eventual destruction. Though it had the reputation of victory, in reality it was a doomed nation. Why is that? Because Qi is large and Lu is small. "Now, the relationship between Qin and Qi is just like that of Qi and Lu. "When Qin and Zhao fought on the banks of Hezhang River, Zhao won twice in two battles; when they fought again at Fanwu, Zhao once more defeated Qin. "After four battles, Zhao lost hundreds of thousands of soldiers and Handan was nearly destroyed. Though it gained the reputation of victory, its state had already been ruined." Why is that? Because Qin was strong and Zhao weak.

## 39

"Now, Qin and Chu have intermarried their daughters and sons-in-law, becoming like brothers. Han has offered Yiyang; Liang has ceded the area outside the Yellow River; Zhao paid homage at Mianchi, ceding Hejian to serve Qin. "If Your Majesty refuses to serve Qin, then Qin will drive Han and Liang to attack the southern territories of Qi. They will mobilize Zhao's entire army across Qinghe River, pointing toward Bohuan Pass. Linzi and Jimo will no longer belong to you." Once the state is attacked in a single day, even if you wish to serve Qin afterward, it will be too late. Therefore, I hope Your Majesty will carefully deliberate on this matter."

## 40

King of Qi said: "Qi is remote and obscure, secluded by the East Sea. I have never heard of long-term benefits for the state." And so he accepted Zhang Yi's proposal.

## 41

Zhang Yi departed and went west to advise King Zhao: "My lord, the King of Qin has sent me to offer my humble advice to Your Majesty. "Your Majesty united all under heaven against Qin, and for fifteen years, the Qin army dared not cross Hangu Pass. "Your Majesty's authority extended to the eastern mountains, and my humble state was filled with fear and submission. We repaired armor, sharpened weapons, maintained chariots and cavalry, practiced riding and archery, worked hard on farming, and stockpiled grain. Within our four borders, we lived in anxious vigilance, never daring to move or stir, all the while hoping only for Your Majesty's clemency."

## 42

"Now with Your Majesty's strength, you have captured Ba and Shu, annexed Hanzhong, encircled the two Zhou states, moved the Nine Tripods, and controlled the ferry at Baima. "Though Qin is remote, its resentment has been building for a long time." "Now, even with worn armor and weakened troops, Qin has stationed its army at Mianchi. We wish to cross the Yellow River and pass over Zhanghe River to occupy Fanwu, meeting under Handan. We hope for a decisive battle on the day of Jiazi, to settle accounts as in the case of Yin Zhout, and I respectfully sent me ahead to inform Your Majesty."

## 43

"All those who advise Your Majesty to form an alliance rely on Su Qin. "Su Qin deceived the feudal lords, turning right into wrong and wrong into right. He sought to overthrow Qi but was himself torn apart by chariots at the marketplace." Thus it is clear that unifying all under heaven is impossible. "Now, Chu and Qin have become like brothers; Han and Liang are called eastern vassal ministers; Qi has offered its fish and salt lands. This severs Zhao's right arm." "To have one's right arm cut off and yet fight with others, to lose allies and be left alone—how can one hope for safety?"

## 44

"Now, Qin has dispatched three generals: one army will block the Wudao Pass; we shall inform Qi to raise its troops and cross the Qinghe River, stationing them east of Handan; one army will be stationed at Chenggao, driving Han and Liang's forces beyond the Yellow River; one army will station itself at Mianchi. "Four states are united to attack Zhao; once Zhao is defeated, its territory will certainly be divided into four parts. Therefore, I dare not conceal my thoughts or hide the truth—I have come first to inform Your Majesty. "I secretly advise Your Majesty that there is no better course of action than to meet with King Qin at Mianchi, face-to-face and in person, to make a verbal agreement, requesting both sides to lay down their arms and refrain from attacking. I hope Your Majesty will decide on this plan."

## 45

King Zhao said: "In the time of my late father, Lord Fengyang held absolute power and authority. He deceived our late king, monopolized control over state affairs, while I was merely a child under the care of my teachers, never involved in national planning or decision-making. "After my late father passed away and left me behind, I was still young. My days of assuming the throne are recent, and in my heart I have always secretly doubted—believing that adhering to an alliance against Qin is not in our country's long-term interest." "Therefore, I wish to change my mind and reconsider, ceding territory as an apology for past mistakes in order to serve Qin. "I was just preparing the carriage to set out when I received your messenger's clear decree." King Zhao agreed with Zhang Yi, and so Zhang Yi departed.

## 46

He went north to Yan and advised King Zhao of Yan: "Your Majesty's closest ally is none other than Zhao. "In the past, Zhao Xiangzi once married his sister to King Dai, but he wished to annex Dai and arranged a meeting with King Dai at Guozhu Pass. "He then ordered craftsmen to make a golden ladle, lengthening its tail so that it could be used to strike people. "During the banquet with King Dai, he secretly told the cook: 'When the wine flows and the music plays, serve a hot soup, then turn the ladle to strike him.'" "At that moment, when the wine flowed and music played, they served a hot soup. The cook approached to pour it, then turned the ladle and struck King Dai, killing him; his brains were smeared on the ground. "His sister heard of this, took up a hairpin and stabbed herself in grief; thus to this day there is a mountain called Moji." "The death of King Dai was known by all under heaven.

## 47

"King Zhao is cruel and unfeeling, lacking in humanity—this Your Majesty clearly sees. Can you truly consider King Zhao to be trustworthy?" "Zhao raised an army to attack Yan, twice surrounding the capital and seizing Your Majesty as a hostage; you ceded ten cities in apology. "Now King Zhao has already paid homage at Mianchi, offering the region of Hejian to serve Qin. "If now Your Majesty refuses to serve Qin, then Qin will send troops down into Yunzhong and Jiuyuan, drive Zhao to attack Yan, and the Yishui River and the Great Wall will no longer be under your control.

## 48

"At present, Zhao's relationship with Qin is like that of a commandery or county; it dares not recklessly raise an army for war. "If now Your Majesty serves Qin, King Qin will be pleased and Zhao will not dare to act recklessly. This means having a powerful ally in the west from Qin while being free of threats from Qi and Zhao in the south; therefore, I hope Your Majesty carefully considers this."

## 49

King Yan said: "I am a ruler of a remote and uncivilized land; even the strongest man is but like an infant, my words are not sufficient to determine proper strategy. "Now that you, a distinguished guest, have kindly instructed me, I shall face west and serve Qin, offering five cities from the western end of Hengshan Mountain." King Yan heeded Zhang Yi's advice. Zhang Yi returned to report, but before reaching Xianyang, King Hui of Qin died and King Wu took the throne. King Wu had, when he was a crown prince, disliked Zhang Yi. Upon ascending the throne, many ministers slandered him, saying: "He is untrustworthy and sells his country to gain favor with those around the king." "Qin will surely use him again; this may bring ridicule from all under heaven." The feudal lords, hearing that Zhang Yi had fallen out of favor with King Wu, all abandoned the horizontal alliance and once again united in a vertical coalition.

## 50

In the first year of King Wu's reign, the ministers continued to criticize Zhang Yi day and night; meanwhile, envoys from Qi also arrived with accusations. Fearing punishment, Zhang Yi therefore said to King Wu of Qin: "I have a foolish plan I would like to propose." The king asked, "What is it?" Zhang Yi replied: "For the sake of Qin's state and people, there will be a great upheaval in the east; only then can Your Majesty gain more territory through division. "Now I have heard that King Qi intensely hates me, and wherever I am, he will surely raise an army to attack." "Therefore, I humbly request that Your Majesty allow this unworthy servant of mine to go to Liang; Qi will then surely raise an army and attack Liang. "While the armies of Liang and Qi are locked in battle beneath the walls and cannot withdraw, Your Majesty may take advantage of this opportunity to attack Han, enter the Sanchuan region, deploy troops at Hangu Pass without engaging in war, and march toward Zhou. The ritual vessels will surely be surrendered." "Controlling the Son of Heaven and holding the maps and records—this is the path to kingship." King Qin considered this plan reasonable, so he prepared thirty ox-drawn carts and sent Zhang Yi to Liang. As expected, Qi raised an army to attack Liang. King Ai of Liang was frightened. Zhang Yi said, "Your Majesty need not worry; I will have the Qi army withdraw." He then sent his retainer Feng Xi to Chu, who would serve as an envoy to Qi and said to King Qi: "Your Majesty intensely hates Zhang Yi; "Nevertheless, how generous it was of you to entrust him to Qin!" King Qi said: "I hate Zhang Yi; wherever he is, I will surely raise an army to attack. Why would I entrust him?" Feng Xi replied: "This very situation is proof of your trust in Zhang Yi. "When Zhang Yi left, he had already made an agreement with King Qin: 'For Your Majesty's benefit, there will be a major upheaval in the East; only then can you gain more territory through division.' "Now King Qi intensely hates Zhang Yi and, wherever he is, will surely raise an army to attack him. "Therefore, Zhang Yi requested permission from King Qin to go to Liang, knowing that Qi would then raise an army and attack him. "While the armies of Qi and Liang are locked in battle under the city walls and cannot disengage, Your Majesty can take advantage of this interval to attack Han, enter Sanchuan, deploy troops at Hangu Gate without engaging in combat, and approach Zhou. The ritual vessels will certainly be surrendered." "Controlling the Son of Heaven, holding maps and records—this would establish a kingly legacy.'" "King Qin believed this to be true, which is why he prepared thirty ox-drawn chariots and sent him to Liang." "Now that Zhang Yi has entered Liang, and Your Majesty indeed attacks him, this means your state is exhausted internally while waging war against an allied nation externally. You are expanding your enemies' strength to threaten yourself from within, yet you have still proven Zhang Yi's words true before King Qin." "This is what I mean by 'entrusting Zhang Yi.'" King Qi said, "Well spoken." He then ordered the army to disband.

## 51

Zhang Yi served as prime minister of Wei for one year, and died in Wei.

## 52

Chen Zhen was a traveling orator. He and Zhang Yi both served King Hui of Qin, and were equally esteemed, vying for the king's favor. Zhang Yi slandered Chen Zhen to King Qin, saying: "Chen Zhen uses generous gifts and light envoys between Qin and Chu; he is about to broker a state alliance. "Now, if Chu does not favor Qin but favors Chen Zhen instead, it is because Chen Zhen values himself highly while regarding Your Majesty lightly. "Moreover, Chen Zhen intends to leave Qin and go to Chu; why does not Your Majesty permit it?" The king said to Chen Zhen: "I have heard that you wish to leave Qin for Chu. Is this true?" Chen Zhen replied, "Yes." The king said: "Zhang Yi's words have indeed proven true." Chen Zhen said, "It is not only Zhang Yi who knows this; all traveling scholars are fully aware of it. "In the past, Wu Zixu was loyal to his lord and all under heaven vied to serve him as a minister; Cang Zhong was filial to his parents and all under heaven wished to have him as their son. "Therefore, servants or concubines who are sold without leaving the neighborhood are good ones; "Women who remarry within their hometowns are virtuous women. "Now, if I am disloyal to my lord, how can Chu regard me as loyal? "Even with loyalty, I would still be discarded. If not for Chu, where else could I go?" The king found his words reasonable and thus treated him kindly.

## 53

After a year in Qin, King Hui of Qin finally appointed Zhang Yi as prime minister, so Chen Zhen fled to Chu. Chu did not hold him in high regard and sent Chen Zhen on a mission to Qin. Passing through Liang, he wished to meet Xi Shou. Xi Shou declined and did not see him. Chen Zhen said: "I have come for a matter of state; if you do not see me, I must leave immediately and cannot wait until another day." Xi Shou then received him. Chen Zhen asked, "Why do you enjoy drinking so much?" Xi Shou replied: "Because there is nothing to do." Chen Zhen said: "May I help you find something to occupy yourself?" Xi Shou asked, "How so?" Chen Zhen said: "Tian Xu has arranged for the feudal lords to form an alliance. King Chu suspects him and does not yet trust him." "You should tell the king: 'I have old ties with the kings of Yan and Zhao, who frequently send envoys saying, "There is no war; why not meet?" I wish to request permission from Your Majesty to visit them.'" "Even if the king grants your request, you should ask for no more than a few chariots—say thirty in total. You may display them in the courtyard and clearly announce to Yan and Zhao that you are coming." The envoys of Yan and Zhao heard this, hurriedly sent chariots to inform their kings, and dispatched men to welcome Xi Shou. King Chu heard of this and was greatly angered, saying: "Tian Xu made an agreement with me, yet Xi Shou went to Yan and Zhao—this is deceiving me." He became furious and refused to listen to any further matters. Qi, hearing that Xi Shou had gone north, sent envoys with affairs to be entrusted to him. Xi Shou thus departed, and the matters of the three states were all decided by him. Chen Zhen then arrived in Qin.

## 54

Han and Wei engaged in war, which lasted for a whole year without resolution. King Hui of Qin wished to intervene but asked his ministers for advice. Some among the court advised that it would be beneficial to rescue them, while others said it was better not to intervene; King Hui could not decide. Chen Zhen had just arrived in Qin, and King Hui said: "Since you left me for Chu, have you missed me?" Chen Zhen replied: "Have Your Majesty heard of the Yue man Zhuang Xi?" The king said, "No." He said: "The Yue man Zhuang Xi served in Chu as an official holding the Gui seal. After a while he fell ill." King Chu said: "Xi was originally a humble and obscure man from the border of Yue. Now he serves in Chu holding the Gui seal, becoming noble and wealthy—does he still miss Yue?" Zhong Xie replied: "Generally speaking, people think of their homeland when they are ill." "If he misses Yue, his voice will reflect that of Yue; if he does not miss Yue, it will sound like that of Chu." They sent someone to listen, and indeed, his speech still bore the accent of Yue. "Now I, though exiled in Chu, how could I possibly not retain my Qin accent!" King Hui said: "Well spoken." "Now Han and Wei are waging war against each other, and the conflict has lasted for a full year without resolution. Some say it is advantageous to rescue them; others say it is better not to intervene. I cannot decide. I wish that while you advise in your own interest, you would also advise me." Chen Zhen replied: "Have Your Majesty ever heard of Bian Zhuangzi killing tigers, a story known to kings?" "Bian Zhuangzi wanted to kill a tiger, but the inn's attendant stopped him and said: 'Two tigers are now eating an ox. When they savor their meal, they will inevitably quarrel over it; when they quarrel, they must fight; in the fight, the larger one will be injured and the smaller one killed. If you then strike at the wounded tiger, with a single action you can gain the reputation of killing two tigers.'" Bian Zhuangzi considered this advice sound and waited for it to happen. After a while, the two tigers indeed fought; the larger one was injured, and the smaller one died. Bian Zhuangzi then attacked the wounded tiger, and with a single strike he achieved the feat of killing both tigers. "Now Han and Wei are attacking each other, with the conflict lasting for a full year without resolution; inevitably one of the major states will be wounded and the minor state destroyed. If we then attack the injured party, in one move we shall gain two advantages." "This is similar to Bian Zhuangzi's tiger-killing strategy." "My lord and Your Majesty are no different in this regard." King Hui said: "Well spoken." And so he finally did not intervene. The major state was indeed wounded, and the minor one perished; Qin then raised an army to attack and achieved a great victory. This was Chen Zhen's strategy.

## 55

Xi Shou was a native of Yinjin in Wei; his given name was Yan, and his family name was Gongsun. He did not get along well with Zhang Yi.

## 56

Zhang Yi went to Wei on behalf of Qin, and King Wei appointed Zhang Yi as his prime minister. Xi Shou found this disadvantageous, so he sent someone to tell Han Gongshu: "Zhang Yi has already formed an alliance between Qin and Wei. He said, 'Wei will attack Nanyang, while Qin attacks Sanchuan.'" The reason King Wei values Zhang Zi is that he wishes to obtain Han territory. Moreover, Han's Nanyang has already been captured; why not slightly assign it to Yan as a contribution, so that the alliance between Qin and Wei may be disrupted. "In this way, Wei will certainly plot against Qin and abandon Zhang Yi, while seeking an alliance with Han and appointing Yan as prime minister." Gongshu considered it advantageous and thus credited the achievement to Xi Shou. As a result, he was indeed appointed prime minister of Wei. Zhang Yi left.

## 57

The ruler of Yiqu chao visited Wei. Xi Shou heard that Zhang Yi had been reappointed as prime minister in Qin and felt threatened by this. Xi Shou then said to the ruler of Yiqu: "The road is far, so I cannot visit again; please allow me to present my request." He said: "If the Central States remain at peace, Qin will be free to burn and destroy your country; if there is a conflict, however, Qin will send envoys with generous gifts to serve your state." Later, five states launched an attack on Qin. At this time, Chen Zhen advised King Qin: "The ruler of Yiqu is a wise leader among the barbarians; it would be better to bribe him in order to pacify his ambitions." King Qin said, "Well spoken." He then sent a thousand bolts of embroidered silk and one hundred women to the ruler of Yiqu. The ruler of Yiqu convened his ministers and said: "Is this what Gongsun Yan suggested?" He then raised an army to launch a surprise attack on Qin, defeating the Qin forces under Li Bo.

## 58

After Zhang Yi's death, Xi Shou entered service in Qin and became prime minister. He once wore the seals of prime ministers from five states and served as the leader of their alliance.

## 59

Grand Historian said: The three Jin states produced many men skilled in strategy and diplomacy. Those who advocated for alliances or rivalries to strengthen Qin were mostly people from the three Jin states. Zhang Yi's actions were more prominent than those of Su Qin. However, the world resents Su Qin because he died first, and Zhang Yi exposed his shortcomings to support his own arguments and promote his policy of horizontal alliances. In short, these two men were indeed dangerous and untrustworthy individuals!
