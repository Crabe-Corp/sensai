# Liezhuan of Bai Qi and Wang Jian

## 1

Bai Qi was a native of Mei. He was good at using troops and served King Zhao of Qin. In the thirteenth year of King Zhao's reign, Bai Qi became Zuo Shuzhang and led troops to attack Xincheng in the state of Han. In that year, Changhou served as prime minister of Qin and recommended Ren Bi to serve as governor of Hanzhong. The following year, Bai Qi was promoted to Zuo Geng and attacked the states of Han and Wei at Yiqu. He beheaded 240,000 enemy soldiers, captured their general Gongsun Xi, and seized five cities. Bai Qi was promoted to State Marshal. He crossed the Yellow River and captured Anyi in Han and areas east of it, reaching Ganhe. The following year, Bai Qi became Daliangzao. He attacked the state of Wei, captured it, and seized 61 cities, large and small. The following year, Bai Qi and Keqing Cuo attacked Yuancheng and captured it. Five years later, Bai Qi attacked the state of Zhao and captured Guanglang Cheng. Seven years later, Bai Qi attacked Chu and captured five cities including Yan and Deng. The following year, he attacked Chu again, captured Ying, burned Yiling, and advanced eastward to Jingling. King of Chu fled from Ying and moved eastward to Chen. Qin established Nan Jun with Ying as its administrative center. Bai Qi was promoted to the title Wu'an Jun. Wu'an Jun then captured parts of Chu and pacified Wujun and Qianzhong Jun. In the thirty-fourth year of King Zhao's reign, Bai Qi attacked Wei, captured Huayang, drove Mang Mao into retreat, and captured a general from the Three Jin states, beheading 130,000 enemies. He fought against Zhao's general Jia Yan and drowned 20,000 of his soldiers in the river. In the forty-third year of King Zhao's reign, Bian Qi attacked Xingcheng in Han, captured five cities, and beheaded 50,000 soldiers. In the forty-fourth year, Bian Qi attacked the Taihang Dao in Nanyang and cut it off.

## 2

In the forty-fifth year, he launched a campaign against Ye Wang in Han. Ye Wang surrendered to Qin, and the Shangdang Dao was cut off. Its governor Feng Ting discussed with the people, saying: "The Zheng Dao has already been cut off; Han can no longer protect us as its subjects. "Qin troops are advancing daily, and Han cannot respond. It is better to surrender Shangdang to Zhao." "If Zhao accepts us, Qin will be angry and certainly attack Zhao." "Zhao under military pressure will necessarily ally with Han." "If Han and Zhao unite, they can resist Qin."" He therefore sent envoys to report to Zhao. Zhao Xiaocheng Wang deliberated with Pingyang Jun and Pingyuan Jun on the matter. Pingyang Jun said, "It is better not to accept it." "Accepting it will bring greater disaster than the benefits gained."" Pingyuan Jun said, "To gain a prefecture without cause, accepting it is advantageous."" Zhao accepted the offer and therefore enfeoffed Feng Ting as Huayang Jun.

## 3

In the forty-sixth year, Qin attacked Goushi and Lin in Han and captured them.

## 4

In the forty-seventh year, Qin sent Zuo Shuzhang Wang He to attack Han and capture Shangdang. The people of Shangdang fled to Zhao. Zhao stationed troops at Changping to station and control the Shangdang civilians. In April, Wang He therefore attacked Zhao. Zhao sent Lian Po to command the troops. Zhao army soldiers attacked Qin scouts, but the Qin scouts beheaded Zhao's deputy general Jia Qie (賈茄). In June, they breached Zhao's army and captured two outposts and four commanders. In July, the Zhao army built ramparts and entrenched themselves to defend them. Qin attacked their fortifications again, captured two commanders, routed their formation, and seized the western ramparts. Lian Po held fast to his defenses and waited for Qin; although Qin repeatedly provoked battles, Zhao's troops did not emerge. King Zhao frequently reprimanded him over this. Meanwhile, Qin's prime minister Ying Hou also sent agents to bribe 1000 gold pieces in Zhao for disinformation, saying: "What Qin fears most is only the son of Ma Fu, Zhao Kuo, as a general. Lian Po is easily dealt with and will surrender soon." King Zhao, already angry that Lian Po's army had suffered many losses and repeated defeats while stubbornly holding its defenses without fighting, and having also heard the Qin disinformation campaign, therefore sent Zhao Kuo to replace Lian Po as commander against Qin. Qin learned that General Ma Fu's son was in command, and secretly appointed Wu'an Jun Bai Qi as supreme general. Wang He was made deputy commander, and orders were given that anyone in the army who revealed Wu'an Jun's command would be executed. When Zhao Kuo arrived, he immediately deployed troops to attack Qin's forces. The Qin army pretended to retreat and feigned defeat, while deploying two hidden units to ambush them. Zhao's troops pursued the victory and advanced toward Qin's fortifications. The Qin defenses held firm and Zhao's forces could not penetrate them, while 25,000 hidden Qin troops cut off Zhao's rear. Another unit of 5,000 cavalry severed the gap between Zhao's fortifications, splitting Zhao's army into two parts and cutting off its supply lines. Qin then deployed light troops to attack them. Zhao's forces, suffering defeats, retreated and entrenched themselves in strong fortifications, waiting for reinforcements to arrive. King of Qin, hearing that Zhao's supply lines were cut off, personally went to Hedong and bestowed a rank increase on every commoner. He ordered all males aged 15 or older to be mobilized to Changping to block Zhao's reinforcements and food supplies.

## 5

By September, Zhao's soldiers had gone 46 days without food, and began secretly killing each other to eat. They launched an attack on Qin's defenses in a desperate attempt to break out. Divided into four units, they attacked repeatedly—four or five times—but could not break through. Zhao Kuo personally led his elite troops in a final desperate charge, but Qin forces shot and killed him. With Zhao Kuo dead, his army was defeated, and 400,000 troops surrendered to Wu'an Jun. Wu'an Jun calculated: "Previously, Qin captured Shangdang, and its people did not wish to serve Qin but instead submitted to Zhao. "The Zhao soldiers are fickle." "If we do not kill them all, they may cause chaos."" He therefore used deception to massacre them all, sparing only 240 of the youngest and sending them back to Zhao. Before and after this campaign, a total of 450,000 Zhao soldiers were beheaded or captured. The people of Zhao were greatly shaken.

## 6

In the tenth month of the forty-eighth year, Qin once again pacified Shangdang Jun. Qin divided its forces into two units: Wang He attacked Pilao and captured it; Simag Geng pacified Taiyuan. Han and Zhao, fearing Qin's advances, sent Su Dai with generous gifts to persuade Ying Hou, the prime minister of Qin: "Did Wu'an Jun capture Ma Fu's son?" He said, "Yes." Su Dai then asked, "Will you now besiege Handan?" He said, "Yes." "If Zhao falls, King of Qin will become emperor, and Wu'an Jun will be made a San Gong (one of the Three Dukes)." "Wu'an Jun has captured more than 70 cities for Qin through victories and conquests, pacified Yan, Ying, and Hanzhong in the south, and defeated Zhao Kuo's army in the north. Even the achievements of Zhou Gong, Zhao Gong, or Lü Wang would not surpass this." "Now that Zhao is about to fall and King of Qin becomes emperor, Wu'an Jun will certainly be made a San Gong. Can you accept being beneath him?" "Even if you have no desire to rank below him, it is unavoidable." "Qin once attacked Han and besieged Xingqiu, trapping Shangdang; the people of Shangdang all rebelled to Zhao. For a long time now, the world has not wished to be subjects of Qin." "Now that Zhao is about to perish, its northern territories will fall to Yan, the eastern lands to Qi, and the southern regions to Han and Wei. How many people will actually remain under your control?" "Therefore, it would be better to concede territory now rather than let Wu'an Jun claim the credit."" Ying Hou then advised King of Qin: "Our troops are exhausted. Please allow Han and Zhao to cede territory for peace, so our soldiers may rest." The king heeded this advice and accepted the cession of Yuanyong from Han and six cities from Zhao to secure peace. In January, all forces withdrew. Wu'an Jun heard of this and thus developed a rift with Ying Hou.

## 7

In the ninth month, Qin again mobilized troops and sent Dafu Wang Ling to attack Handan in Zhao. At this time, Wu'an Jun was ill and could not take command. In the first month of the forty-ninth year, Wang Ling attacked Handan but achieved little success; Qin sent additional reinforcements to support him. Wang Ling's forces suffered a loss of five xiao (military units). After Wu'an Jun recovered from his illness, King of Qin wanted to appoint him to replace Wang Ling as commander. Wu'an Jun said: "Handan is in fact not easy to attack." "Moreover, allied forces from the states are arriving daily. The other states have long harbored resentment toward Qin." "Now, although Qin has crushed the Changping army, more than half of its troops were killed, and the country is exhausted." "To cross distant rivers and mountains to contest a rival state's capital, while Zhao resists from within and the other states attack from without—Qin's forces will certainly be crushed." "It cannot succeed."" King of Qin insisted on his own command, and Wu'an Jun refused to comply; Ying Hou was then sent to persuade him, but Wu'an Jun ultimately declined and refused to go, claiming illness.

## 8

King of Qin appointed Wang He to replace Wang Ling as general; in the eighth and ninth months, they besieged Handan but could not capture it. Chu sent Chunshen Jun and Wei Gongzi to lead an army of hundreds of thousands to attack Qin forces, causing heavy losses for the Qin. Wu'an Jun remarked: "Qin did not heed my strategy; now what has become of it!" King of Qin heard this and became angry, forcing Wu'an Jun to resume duty. Wu'an Jun then claimed illness again. Ying Hou pleaded with him, but he refused to get up. Thus, Wu'an Jun was stripped of his noble status and demoted to the rank of common soldier, and exiled to Yinmi. Wu'an Jun was ill and unable to travel. After three months, the allied forces intensified their attacks on Qin's army; the Qin troops were repeatedly driven back, and envoys arrived daily. lineno King of Qin then sent someone to expel Bai Qi, forbidding him from remaining in Xianyang. After Wu'an Jun departed, he traveled ten li west of Xianyang's western gate and arrived at Duyou. King Zhao of Qin, along with Ying Hou and his ministers, discussed: "Bai Qi's exile has left him still resentful and defiant, making further complaints." King of Qin then sent an envoy to present Bai Qi with a sword, ordering him to commit suicide. Wu'an Jun took up the sword and was about to kill himself, saying: "What crime have I committed against heaven that I should come to this?" After a long pause, he said: "I indeed deserve death. "In the Battle of Changping, hundreds of thousands of Zhao soldiers surrendered to me, yet I deceived them and buried them all alive. This alone is sufficient for death."" He then committed suicide. Wu'an Jun's death occurred in the eleventh month of the fiftieth year of King Zhao of Qin. He died without having committed a crime, and the people of Qin pitied him; townspeople all made sacrifices in his honor.

## 9

Wang Jian was a native of Dongxiang, Pinyang. From an early age he had a passion for military affairs and served the First Emperor of Qin. In the eleventh year of the First Emperor's reign, Wang Jian led an attack on Eyu in Zhao and defeated them, capturing nine cities. In the eighteenth year, Wang Jian again led a campaign against Zhao. After more than a year, he finally captured Zhao; King of Zhao surrendered, and the entire territory of Zhao was pacified and established as commanderies. The following year, Yan sent Jing Ke to assassinate the King of Qin; in response, the King ordered Wang Jian to attack Yan. Yan's King Xi fled to Liaodong, and Wang Jian pacified Yan and Ji before returning. Qin sent Wang Jian's son, Wang Ben, to attack Jing, where the forces of Jing were defeated. Afterward, he attacked Wei; King of Wei surrendered, and thus the territory of Wei was pacified.

## 10

After the First Emperor of Qin had destroyed the three Jin states, driven King of Yan into exile, and repeatedly defeated Jing's forces, General Li Xin of Qin was young and vigorous. He once led several thousand troops to pursue the Crown Prince Dan of Yan as far as Yanzhong River, where he finally captured Dan. The First Emperor considered him wise and brave. Thus the First Emperor asked Li Xin: "I wish to attack Jing; in your estimation, how many troops would be sufficient?" Li Xin replied: "No more than twenty thousand men are needed." The First Emperor then asked Wang Jian, who said: "Without at least sixty thousand men, it cannot be done." The First Emperor remarked: "General Wang is old now; how timid he has become! "Li General's boldness and courage are indeed evident, and his words are correct."" Thus he dispatched Li Xin and Meng Tian with twenty thousand troops to launch a southern campaign against Jing. Wang Jian, whose advice was not accepted, then claimed illness and retired to Pinyang for his old age. Li Xin attacked Pingyu, while Meng Tian attacked Qin; they heavily defeated the forces of Jing. Li Xin then launched another attack on Yanying, captured it, and led his troops westward to rendezvous with Meng Tian at Chengfu. The forces of Jing pursued them, fighting for three days and nights without rest, and heavily defeated Li Xin's army. They captured two Qin camps, killed seven commandants, and the Qin troops fled.

## 11

The First Emperor heard of this and was furious; he personally rode to Pinyang, met with Wang Jian, and apologized: "I regret not following your strategy. Li Xin has indeed brought disgrace upon the Qin army." "Now I hear that Jing's forces are advancing westward daily. Although you are ill, how can you bear to abandon me!"" Wang Jian replied: "An old minister is weary and confused; please, Your Majesty should choose a more capable general." The First Emperor apologized and said: "Enough! General, do not mention it again!" Wang Jian replied: "If Your Majesty insists on using an old servant like me, no fewer than sixty thousand men are indispensable." The First Emperor said: "It is only to follow your strategy." Thus, Wang Jian led an army of sixty thousand men; the First Emperor personally saw him off at Ba Shang. As he departed, Wang Jian requested many fine fields, mansions, gardens, and ponds. The First Emperor said: "General, you are going now—why worry about poverty?" Wang Jian said: "As a general for Your Majesty, even if I achieve success, I will never be enfeoffed as a marquis. Therefore, while Your Majesty still favors me, I request these gardens and fields to secure my descendants' future." The First Emperor laughed heartily. After Wang Jian reached the pass, he sent envoys back five times to request more fine fields. Some said: "General's requests are excessive." Wang Jian replied: "Not so. "The King of Qin is suspicious and does not trust others. "Now he has emptied the entire country of its soldiers and entrusted them solely to me. If I do not request many fields and mansions for my descendants as a foundation, would it not cause King Qin to sit idly and suspect me?""

## 12

Wang Jian indeed replaced Li Xin in attacking Jing. Jing, hearing that Wang Jian had arrived with additional forces, mobilized all its national troops to resist Qin. When Wang Jian arrived, he entrenched his position and refused battle. Jing's forces repeatedly sallied out to provoke a fight, but Wang Jian remained unmoved and did not engage. Wang Jian daily allowed his soldiers to rest, bathe, and provided them with good food and care, personally sharing meals with the troops. After some time, Wang Jian sent someone to ask: "Are there games among the soldiers?" The reply was: "They are just throwing stones and practicing jumping." Wang Jian then said: "My soldiers are now ready for battle." Jing repeatedly provoked the Qin forces, but when they did not respond, Jing withdrew eastward. Wang Jian then launched a pursuit and ordered his elite troops to attack, heavily defeating the forces of Jing. They advanced as far as Qinan, where they killed General Xiang Yan; thus, the forces of Jing collapsed and fled. Qin then capitalized on its victory to conquer and pacify all the cities and towns in Jing's territory. After more than a year, they captured King Fu Chu of Jing, finally subduing the entire land of Jing and establishing it as commanderies and counties. They then proceeded south to campaign against the chieftains of Baiyue. Meanwhile, Wang Jian's son Wang Ben, along with Li Xin, subdued and pacified the territories of Yan and Qi.

## 13

In the twenty-sixth year of the First Emperor of Qin, all under heaven was unified; among those who contributed most were the Wang and Meng families, whose names were passed down to later generations.

## 14

During the reign of Qin Er Shi, both Wang Jian and his son Wang Ben had already died, and then the Meng family was also exterminated. When Chen Sheng rebelled against Qin, the dynasty sent Wang Jian's grandson, Wang Li, to attack Zhao; he besieged King of Zhao and Zhang Er in Julu City. Some said: "Wang Li is a renowned general of Qin." "Now he commands the strong forces of Qin to attack newly established Zhao; victory must be certain."" A guest replied: "Not necessarily. "A general from a family of three generations will inevitably suffer defeat." "Why is that so?" "Because they have committed excessive slaughter; their descendants will inevitably suffer the ill omen." "Now Wang Li is already a general of the third generation."" Not long after, Xiang Yu came to Zhao's rescue and attacked Qin forces; indeed, he captured Wang Li, and Wang Li's troops then surrendered to the allied states.

## 15

Grand Historian said: A vulgar saying goes "A foot has its shortness; an inch has its length." Bai Qi could assess the enemy and adapt to changing circumstances, employing endless surprises that reverberated across the land. Yet he failed to avert disaster at Yinghou's hands. Wang Jian, as a general of Qin, subdued the six states. At that time, Wang was an experienced commander; the First Emperor regarded him as a teacher. Yet he failed to assist Qin in establishing virtue or securing its foundation, instead merely seeking temporary favor and accommodation, which ultimately led to his downfall. When his grandson Wang Li was captured by Xiang Yu—was this not inevitable! Each had their own shortcomings.
