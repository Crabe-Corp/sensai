# Biography of Chunshen Jun

## 1

Chunshen Jun was a native of Chu, surnamed Huang, given name Xie. He traveled and studied widely, accumulating extensive knowledge, and served King Qingxiang of Chu. King Qingxiang regarded Xie as eloquent and sent him on a mission to the state of Qin. King Zhao of Qin dispatched Bai Qi to attack the states of Han and Wei, defeating them at Huayang. He captured Wei's general Mangmao, and as a result, Han and Wei submitted to Qin. King Zhao of Qin was just about to order Bai Qi and the states of Han and Wei to jointly attack Chu, but before this could be carried out, Chu sent Huang Xie to Qin, where he arrived in time to learn of Qin's plan. At that time, Qin had already previously sent Bai Qi to attack Chu, capturing the commanderies of Wu and Qianzhong, seizing Yanyang and Yingting, and advancing eastward as far as Jingling. King Qingxiang of Chu then moved his capital eastward to Chen Xian. Huang Xie witnessed how King Huai of Chu had been lured by Qin to attend a court audience, only to be deceived and detained, eventually dying in Qin. King Qingxiang was his son; Qin looked down on him and feared that a single military campaign would lead to the destruction of Chu. Xie therefore submitted a memorial to King Zhao of Qin, saying:

## 2

No state in the world is stronger than Qin and Chu. Now I have heard that Your Majesty wishes to attack Chu; this would be like two tigers fighting each other. When two tigers fight, it is the weak dogs who benefit from their exhaustion; therefore, it would be better to maintain good relations with Chu. I beg to explain my reasoning: I have heard that when things reach their extreme, they turn back; winter and summer are examples of this. lø When something reaches its utmost limit, it becomes dangerous; a pile of chess pieces is an example of this. Now the territory of your great state extends throughout the world, occupying two of its borders; since the beginning of mankind, there has never been a kingdom with ten thousand chariots that has possessed such an expanse. The late Emperor Wenwang and Zhuangwang, for three generations, did not recklessly extend their territory to Qi in order to cut off the key of a united alliance. Now Your Majesty has sent Shengqiao to oversee affairs in Han, and Shengqiao presented his territory to Qin; thus, without deploying troops or exerting military might, Your Majesty gains a hundred li of land. Your Majesty can truly be called capable. Your Majesty then mobilized troops to attack Wei, blocking the gates of Daliang, seizing Heinei, capturing Yan, Suanzao, Xu, Tao, and entering Xing. The armies of Wei scattered like clouds and dared not come to their rescue. Your Majesty's achievements have also been numerous. Your Majesty allowed the troops to rest and recover, waiting two years before resuming military action; You then annexed Pu, Yan, Shou, and Yuan to face Ren and Pingqiu, encircling Huang and Jiyang with walled cities until the House of Wei submitted; Your Majesty further carved out territory north of Puli, controlling the vital junction between Qi and Qin, severing the backbone of Chu and Zhao; even when the five states united in six alliances, they dared not come to their aid. Your Majesty's authority has indeed become overwhelming.

## 3

If Your Majesty can maintain your achievements and uphold your authority, restrain the desire for conquest, and cultivate a land of benevolence and righteousness to avoid future troubles, then three sage kings would not surpass you by one, nor would five hegemons exceed you by two. If Your Majesty relies on the multitude of your followers, wields a powerful military force, and exploits the momentum from Huiwei's defeat to attempt subjugating all other rulers by sheer strength, I fear that this will bring future troubles. The "Shijing" says: "All things have a beginning; few reach their end." The "Yijing" says: "A fox crossing water gets its tail wet." This means that the beginning is easy, but the end is difficult. Why do I know this to be so? In the past, the House of Zhi saw the benefit in attacking Zhao but failed to foresee the disaster at Yuci; Wu saw the advantage in attacking Qi but did not anticipate its defeat at Gansui. These two states were by no means lacking great achievements, yet they reaped benefits in the short term while inviting troubles for later. Wu trusted Yue, and followed its lead to attack Qi; after defeating the people of Qi at Ailing, Wu was on its return journey when it was captured by King Goujian of Yue at Sanzhu's shoal. The House of Zhi trusted Han and Wei, followed their lead to attack Zhao, laid siege to the city of Jinyang, and had already achieved victory for several days. However, Han and Wei betrayed them, killing Zhibo Yao beneath Zaotai. Now Your Majesty is envious of Chu's survival, yet forgets that destroying Chu would strengthen Han and Wei; I consider this for Your Majesty and find it unwise.

## 4

The "Shijing" says: "Great warriors dwell far away without crossing." Viewed from this, the state of Chu is a source of support; neighboring states are enemies. The "Shijing" says: "A nimble hare, caught by a lazy dog." "One may have intentions; I can fathom them." Now, Your Majesty halfway trusts the supposed goodwill of Han and Wei toward you—this is precisely like Wu's trust in Yue. I have heard it said that enemies must not be trusted, and opportunities must not be missed. I fear that Han and Wei are using humble words to eliminate threats while in reality they intend to deceive Your Majesty's great state. Why do I say this? Your Majesty has no accumulated virtue toward Han and Wei across generations, but rather harbors long-standing grievances. The fathers, sons, brothers, and uncles of Han and Wei have been successively killed by Qin for nearly ten generations. Their homeland has been ravaged, their state destroyed, and their ancestral temples ruined. Their bellies were cut open and their intestines severed, their necks broken and jaws crushed; heads separated from bodies, bones exposed in fields and marshes, skulls shattered and strewn across the borders. The old and weak of fathers and sons had their throats bound and hands tied, becoming captives by the thousands along the roads. Their ancestral spirits are left desolate and grieving, with no blood sacrifices to sustain them. The people can barely survive; their families have been scattered, and many have fled into servitude as slaves or concubines, filling the land. Therefore, the survival of Han and Wei is a concern for Qin's state; now Your Majesty supports them in attacking Chu—would this not be excessive!

## 5

Moreover, if Your Majesty attacks Chu, from where will the troops come? Will Your Majesty borrow a route through the hostile states of Han and Wei? On the day your troops march out, Your Majesty fears they may not return; this means you are providing military strength to your enemies, Han and Wei. If Your Majesty does not borrow a route through the enemy states of Han and Hui, then you must attack the territory to the right of the Suishui River. The land to the right of the Suishi River consists of vast rivers and streams, mountains, forests, and gullies—barren lands that cannot sustain agriculture. Even if Your Majesty possesses them, it will not be considered a true acquisition of territory. This would mean Your Majesty gains the reputation of destroying Chu without achieving any real territorial gain.

## 6

Moreover, on the day Your Majesty attacks Chu, four states will surely raise their armies to respond to you. While the armies of Qin and Chu are locked in conflict, the House of Wei will emerge to attack Liu, Fangyu, Zhi, Huling, Dang, Xiao, and Xiang; thus, the former state of Song will certainly be completely occupied. The people of Qi will attack Chu from the south, and the region along the Si River will surely fall. These are all vast plains with excellent access to roads and fertile land, yet you would leave them vulnerable to attack alone. Your Majesty destroys Chu to strengthen Han and Wei in the central regions while empowering Qi. The strength of Han and Wei will be sufficient to rival that of Qin. Qi, to the south with the Si River as its border, faces the sea in the east and relies on the Yellow River in the north, free from rear threats. Among all states of the world, none are stronger than Qi and Wei. If Qi and Wei gain territory and preserve their advantages while carefully managing affairs under their officials, within a year they will not yet be emperors, but they would have more than enough power to prevent Your Majesty from becoming emperor.

## 7

With Your Majesty's vast territory, numerous people, and powerful military forces, to take one action that incurs resentment from Chu, only to delay the return of Han and Wei's allegiance in favor of Qi, would be a miscalculation on Your Majesty's part. For Your Majesty's sake, I believe there is no better course than to maintain good relations with Chu.  If Qin and Chu unite as one to face Han, Han will surely surrender without resistance. Your Majesty can use the perilous terrain of Dongshan as a barrier and take advantage of the strategic benefits of Quhe River, ensuring that Han will become a vassal lord within Qin's borders. If this is done, and Your Majesty stations ten thousand troops at Zheng, the House of Liang will grow fearful. Xucheng and Yangling will be encircled in their walled cities, while Shangcai and Zhaoling will cease to communicate freely; thus, Wei too would become a vassal within Qin's borders. If Your Majesty maintains good relations with Chu, then the two great powers within Qin's borders, each commanding ten thousand chariots, will cede territory to Qi. The western territories of Qi can thus be taken effortlessly by Qin. Your Majesty's territory, stretching across the two seas and controlling key junctions of the world, would mean that Yan and Zhao could not exist without Qi and Chu, nor could Qi and Chu exist without Yan and Zhao. Only then can you threaten Yan and Zhao from a position of strength, directly shaking Qi and Chu; these four states would submit without needing to be struck.

## 8

King Zhao said: "Well spoken." Thus, he halted Bai Qi and apologized to Han and Wei. He sent envoys with gifts to Chu, forming an alliance as a friendly state.

## 9

Huang Xie accepted the agreement and returned to Chu; Chu then sent Xie along with Crown Prince Wan as hostages to Qin, where they were detained for several years. King Qingxiang of Chu fell ill, and the crown prince was not allowed to return. The Crown Prince of Chu had become friendly with Qin's Prime Minister Ying Hou, so Huang Xie then persuaded Ying Hou by saying: "Is it truly the case that you are on good terms with the Crown Prince of Chu?" Ying Hou said: "Indeed." Xie said: "Now, King Chu is afraid he will not recover from his illness. Qin would be better off sending the Crown Prince back to him. Once the crown prince is enthroned, he will surely value Qin highly and owe an eternal debt to you, the Prime Minister. This would mean a close allied state while securing the support of a kingdom with ten thousand chariots." If he is not sent back, then in Xianyang he will be nothing more than an ordinary commoner; Chu may appoint another crown prince, who would certainly not serve Qin. Losing an allied state and severing relations with a kingdom of ten thousand chariots is not a wise strategy. I hope you, the Prime Minister, will carefully consider this." Ying Hou reported this to King Qin. King Qin said: "Order the Crown Prince of Chu's tutor to go first and inquire about King Chu's illness; we will decide on further action after he returns." Huang Xie, considering the situation for the Crown Prince of Chu, said: "Qin's detention of the crown prince is intended to seek advantage. Now that the crown prince lacks the strength or means to offer Qin any benefit, I am greatly concerned about this. Moreover, there are two men of virtue and talent within the court—Yang Wen. If King Chu suddenly passes away while the crown prince is not present, these virtuous men will surely be installed as successors, and the Crown Prince will lose his right to serve in the ancestral temple. It would be better for you to escape from Qin and leave with the envoys; I will remain behind and take full responsibility, even at the cost of my life." The Crown Prince of Chu therefore changed his clothes, disguised as a Chu envoy's driver, and left the pass. Huang Xie remained in residence, frequently claiming illness to avoid suspicion. After estimating that the Crown Prince had already traveled far and could not be pursued by Qin, Xie then reported to King Zhao of Qin: "The Crown Prince of Chu has returned home and is now far away. I deserve death; I beg for the mercy of execution." King Zhao was furious and intended to allow him to commit suicide. Ying Hou said: "Xie, as a subject, risked his life to serve his lord. Once the Crown Prince is enthroned, he will certainly rely on Xie; therefore, it would be wiser to return him without punishment in order to foster closer relations with Chu." Qin thus sent Huang Xie back.

## 10

After returning to Chu, Xie arrived three months later when King Qingxiang of Chu died; the Crown Prince Wan ascended the throne and became King Kaolie. In the first year of King Kaolie's reign, Huang Xie was appointed as prime minister and enfeoffed as Chunshen Jun, receiving twelve commanderies in the northern region of Huai. Fifteen years later, Huang Xie said to King Chu: "The region north of the Huai River borders Qi and is strategically important; I request that it be administered as a commandery for greater convenience." He thus submitted the twelve counties in the northern part of the Huai region. Please grant me a fief in Jiangdong. King Kaolie agreed to the request. Chunshen Jun then built a city on the site of the former Wu capital, using it as his own administrative center.

## 11

After Chunshen Jun became prime minister of Chu, at that time Qi had Mengchang Jun, Zhao had Pingyuan Jun, and Wei had Xinling Jun. They all competed to attract talented individuals, recruiting guests in order to outmaneuver one another and consolidate power for their respective states.

## 12

In the fourth year of Chunshen Jun's tenure as prime minister of Chu, Qin defeated more than 400,000 troops from Zhao at the Battle of Changping. In the fifth year, they besieged Handan. Handan sent an urgent plea for help to Chu. Chu dispatched Chunshen Jun with troops to rescue it, and the Qin forces withdrew accordingly; Chunshen Jun then returned. After serving as prime minister of Chu for eight years, Chunshen Jun led a northern campaign and destroyed the state of Lu. He appointed Xun Qing as magistrat of Lanling. At that time, Chu regained its strength.

## 13

Zhao's Pingyuan Jun sent an envoy to Chunshen Jun, who lodged him in the upper guest quarters. The Zhao envoy wished to show off to Chu and made a hairpin of turtle shell, adorning the scabbard of his sword with jewels. He requested that Chunshen Jun receive him as a guest. Chunshen Jun had over 3,000 guests; his most honored ones all wore shoes inlaid with pearls to meet the Zhao envoy. The Zhao envoy was deeply ashamed.

## 14

In the fourteenth year of Chunshen Jun's tenure as prime minister, King Zhuangxiang of Qin ascended the throne and appointed Lu Buwei as prime minister, enfeoffing him as Wenxin Hou. He captured Dongzhou.

## 15

In the twenty-second year of Chunshen Jun serving as prime minister, the feudal lords were troubled by Qin's ceaseless attacks and thus formed an alliance to jointly attack Qin from the west. The King of Chu was appointed leader of this coalition, with Chunshen Jun wielding actual authority. They advanced to the Hangu Pass, but Qin sent out troops to attack and the allied forces of the feudal lords all fled in defeat. King Kaolie of Chu blamed Chunshen Jun for this failure, causing Chunshen Jun to become increasingly estranged from the king.

## 16

A guest named Zhu Ying, a native of Guanjin, said to Chunshen Jun: "Everyone considers Chu strong and yet you have used it to become weak. In my view, this is not the case." "For twenty years during your late lord's reign, Qin maintained good relations with Chu and did not attack it—why was that?" Because for Qin to cross the narrow pass of Mian Ai in order to attack Chu would be inconvenient; and it is also unfeasible for them to borrow a route through the two Zhou states and attack Chu from behind the positions of Han and Wei. Now, however, this is no longer the case. Wei is on the verge of collapse and cannot protect Xǔ or Yancheng; it will inevitably cede these to Qin. The Qin army is only 160 li away from Chen, and what I have observed indicates that the daily confrontations between Qin and Chu are already underway." Chu therefore abandoned Chen and moved its capital to Shouchun; while Qin relocated Wei Ye Wang, establishing the Dongjun commandery. From then on, Chunshen Jun took up residence in Wu under his fief and continued to exercise authority as prime minister.

## 17

King Kaolie of Chu had no sons, and Chunshen Jun was worried about this. He sought out many women who were believed to be likely to bear children and presented them to the king, but in the end there were still no offspring. A native of Zhao named Li Yuan brought his younger sister with him, intending to present her to King Chu. However, upon learning that she was not likely to bear children, he feared that if she remained in the palace for a long time, she would never gain favor. Li Yuan therefore sought to serve Chunshen Jun as a retainer. After some time he paid his respects and requested leave, intentionally missing the deadline for return. When he returned to pay his respects, Chunshen Jun asked about the reason for his absence. Li Yuan replied: "The King of Qi sent an envoy seeking my younger sister; I drank with the envoy, hence missing the deadline." Chunshen Jun asked: "Has she been betrothed?" He replied: "Not yet." Chunshen Jun said, "May I have the opportunity to see her?" He replied: "Yes." Thus, Li Yuan presented his younger sister to Chunshen Jun, and she soon became favored by him. When it became known that the woman was pregnant, Li Yuan conspired with her. Li Yuan's younger sister took the opportunity to advise Chunshen Jun: "King Chu holds you in such high esteem and favor that even his own brothers cannot compare. Now, you have served as prime minister of Chu for over twenty years, yet the king has no son. When he passes away, his brothers will be placed on the throne instead. Once a new ruler is installed, they will naturally favor those close to them from before; how can you ensure continued favor and power?" It is not only that. You have long held power and high rank, often treating the king's brothers disrespectfully. If they truly come to power, disaster will befall you personally; how can you then safeguard your prime ministerial seal or the fief of Jiangdong?" Now I know that I am pregnant myself, but no one else is aware. I have been favored by you for a short time; if I were truly to be presented to King Chu through your influence, the king would certainly favor me. If by Heaven's will I bear a son, then your child would become the king; you could thus control all of Chu. How does this compare to risking an unpredictable punishment?" Chunshen Jun found her argument very convincing, so he sent Li Yuan's younger sister to the palace and carefully presented her to King Chu. King Chu summoned her into the palace, where he favored her; she soon gave birth to a son who was made Crown Prince. Li Yuan's younger sister became Queen. King Chu held Li Yuan in high esteem, and Li Yuan began to wield power.

## 18

After Li Yuan had successfully placed his younger sister in the position of queen and their son as Crown Prince, he became fearful that Chunshen Jun might reveal this secret or grow increasingly arrogant. Consequently, Li Yuan secretly trained assassins to kill Chunshen Jun and eliminate any evidence, although many people within the state were already aware of these plans.

## 19

In the twenty-fifth year of Chunshen Jun's service as prime minister, King Kaolie of Chu fell ill. Zhu Ying said to Chunshen Jun: "In this world, there are blessings that come unexpectedly and also misfortunes that arise without warning. Now you find yourself in an era of unexpected events, serving a ruler who brings about unforeseen circumstances; how could you possibly be without someone prepared for the unexpected?" Chunshen Jun asked: "What do you mean by an unexpected blessing?" Zhu Ying replied: "You have served as prime minister of Chu now for over twenty years. Though your title is that of a chancellor, in reality you are the true ruler of Chu. Now that King Chu is ill and will die at any moment, you are about to serve as prime minister for a young ruler. If you then take over the administration of state affairs in his place, following the example of Yi Yin or Zhou Gong, when the new king grows up and returns power to him, would it not be possible for you instead to face south on the throne, declare yourself sovereign, and rule all of Chu?" This is what I mean by an unexpected blessing." Chunshen Jun asked: "What do you mean by unforeseen misfortune?" Zhu Ying replied: "Li Yuan does not govern the state and is in fact your enemy. Though he holds no military post, for a long time now he has secretly trained assassins. When King Chu passes away, Li Yuan will surely rush into power first and kill you to cover up his secrets." This is what I mean by unforeseen misfortune." Chunshen Jun asked: "What do you mean by a person prepared for the unexpected?" Zhu Ying replied: "If you appoint me as an imperial attendant, when King Chu passes away and Li Yuan inevitably moves in first, I will kill him for you. This is what I mean by a person prepared for unforeseen events." Chunshen Jun said: "Leave it be. Li Yuan is a weak man, and I have always treated him kindly; how could he possibly go so far?" Zhu Ying realized that his advice was not being accepted. Fearing that misfortune would soon befall him, he fled and left.

## 20

Seventeen days later, King Kaolie of Chu died, and as expected, Li Yuan rushed in first. He had already stationed his assassins inside the Jimo Gate. When Chunshen Jun entered through the Jimo Gate, Li Yuan's assassins ambushed him, stabbed him to death, cut off his head, and threw it outside the gate. He then ordered officials to completely exterminate Chunshen Jun's family. The son born from the union of Li Yuan's younger sister—who had first been favored by Chunshen Jun and later presented to the king—was then installed as ruler, becoming King You of Chu.

## 21

In this year, Emperor Qin Shi Huang had already reigned for nine years. Lao Ai also caused a rebellion in Qin; when discovered, his three clans were exterminated and Lu Buwei was deposed.

## 22

Grand Historian said: When I visited Chu, I observed the former city of Chunshen Jun; how grand and magnificent its palaces were! Initially, when Chunshen Jun persuaded King Zhao of Qin and risked his life to send the Crown Prince of Chu back home, how wise and discerning he was! Later, however, he fell under Li Yuan's control—how pitiful! The saying goes: "When one fails to make a decisive decision at the right moment, one ends up suffering from chaos." Was this not precisely what happened with Chunshen Jun and his failure to heed Zhu Ying's advice?
