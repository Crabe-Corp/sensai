# Lu Zhougong Genealogy

## 1

Zhou Gongdan was a younger brother of King Wu of Zhou. When King Wen was still alive, Dan was filial as a son and sincere in benevolence, standing out among his brothers. After King Wu ascended the throne, Dan often assisted and supported King Wu, handling many affairs. In the ninth year of King Wu's reign, when he launched an eastern campaign as far as Mengjin, Gongbo Zhou assisted on the expedition. In the eleventh year, when they attacked Zhòu and reached Muye, Gongbo Zhou assisted King Wu in composing the "Oath of Muyue." They defeated Yin and entered the palace of Shang. After killing Zhòu, Gongbo Zhou held the large yue axe while Zhao Gong held the small one; they stood on either side of King Wu to perform a blood sacrifice at the earth altar, informing Heaven and the people of Yin about Zhòu's crimes. They released Ji Zǐ from imprisonment. King Wu enfeoffed Zhòu's son Wugeng Lufu, appointing Guan Shu and Cai Shu as his tutors to continue the ancestral rites of Yin. He widely enfeoffed meritorious ministers and relatives with the same surname. Zhou Gongdan was enfeoffed in Qufu, the former site of Shao Hao, becoming known as the Marquis of Lu. Gongbo Zhou did not go to his fief but remained to assist King Wu.

## 2

้อย In the second year after King Wu conquered Yin, the world was not yet pacified. King Wu fell ill and could no longer attend to affairs; this alarmed his ministers. Therefore, Tai Gong and Zhao Gong conducted secret divinations. Gongbo Zhou said, "It is not yet time for our ancestors to be distressed." Gongbo Zhou then volunteered himself as a substitute. He set up three altars, stood facing north, placed a jade disc on his head and held a jade tablet in hand, and made an offering to Tai Wang, King Ji, and King Wen. The historian recorded the prayer: "Only your eldest grandson, King Fa, has been diligently burdened by illness." "If you, the three kings, have a responsibility to Heaven for your son, let me, Dan, take King Fa's place." Dan is clever and capable, possessing many talents and skills; he can serve spirits and deities. King Fa is not as talented or skilled as Dan, and cannot serve spirits and deities." "Thus, I request a decree from the imperial court to bestow protection upon all directions. By doing so, you may establish your descendants on earth, and the people of all regions will surely revere and fear them." "Do not let Heaven's bestowed mandate fall to ruin; our ancestors will also have a lasting place of reliance and return." "Now I shall seek the mandate through the great tortoise. If you approve my request, I will return this jade disc and tablet to await your decree." "If you do not approve, then I shall cast aside the jade disc and tablet." After Gongbo Zhou had ordered the historian to record a message for Tai Wang, King Ji and King Wen, expressing his desire to take King Wu's place, he then proceeded to divinate before the three kings. The diviners all declared it auspicious; when they opened the oracle records and examined them, indeed it was auspicious. Gongbo Zhou rejoiced, opened the lock, and saw that the written record confirmed good fortune. Gongbo Zhou entered to congratulate King Wu, saying, "The king will suffer no harm." "I have just received the mandate from the three kings and shall strive for a long-lasting peace." "May this path allow you, my king, to be remembered by Heaven alone." Gongbo Zhou stored the records in a golden-sealed chest and warned the guards not to speak of it. The next day, King Wu showed signs of recovery.

## 3

Afterward, when King Wu died and Crown Prince Cheng was still young, he remained in the palace under strict protection. Gongbo Zhou feared that upon hearing of King Wu's death, the people might rebel; thus, he ascended to the throne in place of Crown Prince Cheng and assumed administrative authority as regent. Guan Shu and his brothers spread rumors throughout the state, saying: "Gongbo Zhou intends to harm Crown Prince Cheng." Gongbo Zhou then informed Tai Gong Wang and Zhao Gong Shi, saying: "The reason I have not stepped aside but assumed administrative authority is my fear that the people of the world might rebel against Zhou, leaving no way to account for myself before our ancestors, Tai Wang, King Ji, or King Wen." "The three kings have long worried and labored for the world; only now can their efforts finally bear fruit." "King Wu died prematurely, and Crown Prince Cheng is still young. I must complete the Zhou dynasty's mission; this is why I have acted as I did." Thus, he finally assisted Crown Prince Cheng in his rule and sent his son Bo Qin to take up the fief at Lu. Gongbo Zhou warned Bo Qin, saying: "I am the son of King Wen, the brother of King Wu, and the uncle of Crown Prince Cheng. My status in the world is not lowly." "Yet, I have washed my hair three times and combed it each time, eaten a meal three times but stopped to spit out the food in order to attend to guests. I rise early to welcome scholars, yet still fear losing the virtuous men of the world." "When you go to Lu, be cautious not to let your position as a ruler make you arrogant toward others."

## 4

As expected, Guan Shu, Cai Shu, and Wu Geng led the Huai Yi people in rebellion. Gongbo Zhou then carried out Crown Prince Cheng's orders, raised an army to campaign eastward, and composed the "Great Oath." He thus executed Guan Shu, killed Wu Geng, and exiled Cai Shu. He gathered the remaining people of Yin and enfeoffed Kang Shu in Wei, as well as Wei Zi in Song, to continue the ancestral rites for Yin. After pacifying the Huai Yi and eastern territories, it took two years before peace was fully restored. All feudal lords submitted to the Zhou dynasty's authority.

## 5

Heaven bestowed blessings; Tang Shu found a grain plant with two different origins but the same ear. He presented it to Crown Prince Cheng, who then ordered Tang Shu to send this grain to Gongbo Zhou in the eastern territories, composing the "Gift of Grain." After receiving the grain plant, Gongbo Zhou expressed gratitude for the king's command and composed the "Ode to the Auspicious Grain." With peace restored in the eastern territories, Gongbo Zhou returned to report to Crown Prince Cheng. He then composed a poem for the king and named it "Chi Xiao." The crown prince also did not dare to reprimand Gongbo Zhou.

## 6

On the Yiwu day of the second month in the seventh year of Crown Prince Cheng's reign, the king traveled on foot from Zhou to Feng and sent Tai Bao Zhao Gong ahead to Luoyang to inspect the land. In the third month, Gongbo Zhou went to plan and build Chengzhou Luoyi, divined a suitable location for settlement, which was declared auspicious; thus he established the capital there.

## 7

As Crown Prince Cheng grew older, he became capable of handling state affairs. At this time, Gongbo Zhou returned the administration to Crown Prince Cheng, and King Cheng began to preside over court sessions. When Gongbo Zhou governed in place of Crown Prince Cheng, he sat facing south with his back toward the support beam to receive feudal lords at court. Seven years later, when returning power to Crown Prince Cheng, he faced north and resumed his position as a minister, showing humility and caution.

## 8

Initially, when Crown Prince Cheng was young and fell ill, Gongbo Zhou cut off his own hair and beard, threw them into the river, and prayed to the gods: "The king is still a child without awareness; if there be any who have offended divine will, it is I, Dan." He also stored his records in the government office. Crown Prince Cheng's illness improved. When Crown Prince Cheng began to rule, some people slandered Gongbo Zhou, who fled to the state of Chu. King Cheng opened the government records and found Gongbo Zhou's prayer; he wept and recalled him back from exile.

## 9

After returning, Gongbo Zhou feared that King Cheng might grow arrogant in his youth and indulge in excess; thus he composed "Many Scholars" and "No Indulgence." "No Indulgence states: 'As a parent, one establishes a legacy for the long term. If descendants become arrogant and extravagant and forget this, they will lose their family's fortune. How can children not be cautious!'" "In the past, King Zhongzong of Yin was strict and reverent toward Heaven's mandate. He carefully governed his people with awe and fear, never daring to indulge in idleness or peace; thus, King Zhongzong enjoyed a reign lasting seventy-five years." During King Gaozong's time, he endured prolonged toil outside the court and associated with commoners. Upon ascending the throne, he experienced a period of seclusion, remaining silent for three years; when he finally spoke, it was met with joy. He dared not indulge in idleness but diligently pacified Yin, ensuring that both great and small matters were free from resentment. Thus, King Gaozong enjoyed a reign of fifty-five years. "During King Zu Jia's time, he did not consider kingship as his right and had long lived among commoners outside the court. He understood their hardships, was able to protect and provide for the people, and did not look down upon the weak; thus, King Zu Jia enjoyed a reign of thirty-three years." "Many Scholars states: 'From Tang to Emperor Yi, none failed to lead by virtue and conduct proper ancestral rites; no emperor failed to be in harmony with Heaven.'" "But in the later successor, King Zhou, he indulged in debauchery and excess, disregarding both Heaven's will and the people's allegiance." "His people were all worthy of punishment." "(Zhou Many Scholars) 'King Wen was so busy that he had no time to eat during the day, and thus enjoyed a reign of fifty years.'" He composed this to admonish King Cheng.

## 10

While King Cheng was in Feng and the world had already been pacified, the Zhou government's administrative system remained disorganized. Thus, Gongbo Zhou composed "Zhou Guan," establishing distinct offices according to their appropriate functions, and wrote "Li Zheng" to facilitate governance for the people. The common people were pleased.

## 11

While in Feng, Gongbo Zhou fell ill and was nearing death; he said: "I must be buried in Chengzhou to show that I dared not leave Crown Prince Cheng." After Gongbo Zhou died, King Cheng also expressed regret and buried him in Bi, alongside King Wen, to show that he dared not regard Gongbo Zhou as a subject.

## 12

After Gongbo Zhou's death, before the autumn harvest, there was a sudden storm with thunder and rain; all the grain fell flat, and large trees were uprooted. The state of Zhou was greatly alarmed. King Cheng and the ministers donned formal court attire to open the golden-sealed chest, where King Cheng discovered Gongbo Zhou's written account of his self-sacrifice in place of King Wu. The two ministers and the king then questioned the historians and officials, who replied: "Yes, it is true. In the past, Gongbo Zhou ordered us not to speak of this." King Cheng wept while holding the document and said: "From now on, may there be no more deceptive divinations!" "In the past, Gongbo Zhou toiled for our royal house; as a young man, I did not understand." "Now Heaven has shown its power to highlight Gongbo Zhou's virtue; only I, this young man, should welcome it. It is also appropriate for our state and ancestral rites." The king went to the outskirts of the capital; Heaven then sent rain, reversed the wind, and all the grain stood upright again. The two ministers ordered the people to raise every fallen tree that had been uprooted and rebuild it. That year, there was a great harvest. Thus, King Cheng decreed that the state of Lu should be allowed to perform sacrificial rites in the suburbs for King Wen. The reason why Lu possessed the rituals and music of a Son of Heaven was to honor Gongbo Zhou's virtue.

## 13

After Gongbo Zhou passed away, his son Bo Qin had already received the fief earlier and became known as Marquis of Lu. When Marquis of Lu Bo Qin first received his fief in Lu, it was three years before he reported back on governance to Gongbo Zhou. Gongbo Zhou asked: "Why is this so late?" Bo Qin replied, "I changed their customs and reformed their rites. Mourning lasted three years before being lifted; that is why it was delayed." Tai Gong had also been enfeoffed in Qi and reported on governance to Gongbo Zhou after five months. Tai Gong replied, "I streamlined the rites between ruler and minister and followed their customs in doing so." He said: "I simplified the rites of ruler and minister, following local customs to do so." Later, upon hearing that Bo Qin's report on governance was delayed, he sighed and said: "Alas! In the future, Lu will bow to Qi in submission!" If governance is not simplified or made easy for people, they cannot be close to it; Only when policies are simple and accessible will the people surely return to them."

## 14

After Bo Qin assumed his position, there were rebellions by Guan Shu and Cai Shi; the Huai Yi, Xu Rong, and others also rose up in rebellion together. Thus, Bo Qin led his army to attack them at Xi and composed the "Oath of Xi," saying: "Display your armor and weapons; dare not be unprepared." Dare not harm the oxen. If horses and cattle run wild, or if servants flee, do not dare to chase them beyond your borders; respectfully return them. Dare not commit robbery or trespass walls and fences. The three suburbs and three tunnels of Lu shall prepare your fodder, dried food, provisions, and stakes; dare not fail to provide them. "On the Jia Xu day I will build fortifications and march against the Xu Rong; none shall be late, or they will face severe punishment." After composing this "Oath of Xi," he thus pacified the Xu Rong and stabilized Lu.

## 15

Duke Bo Qin of Lu died, and his son Kaogong Qiu succeeded him as ruler. Kaogong reigned for four years before dying; his younger brother Xi was enthroned, who is known as Yanggong. Yanggong built the Maoque Gate. After six years, he died and was succeeded by his son Yougong Zai. The fourteenth year of Yougong's reign. Yougong's younger brother Fei killed Yougong and enthroned himself, becoming Weigong. Weigong died after fifty years of reign; his son Ligong Zhuo succeeded him as ruler. Ligong reigned for thirty-seven years before dying, and the people of Lu enthroned his younger brother Ju, who became Xianggong. Xianggong died in the 32nd year of his reign; his son Zhen Gong Bi succeeded him as ruler.

## 16

In the fourteenth year of Zhen Gong's reign, King Li of Zhou was tyrannical and fled to Zhi; the Regency Administration began. In the 29th year, King Xuan of Zhou ascended the throne.

## 17

In the thirtieth year, Zhen Gong died and his younger brother Ao succeeded him as ruler, known as Wugong.

## 18

In the spring of the ninth year of Wugong's reign, Wugong traveled west with his eldest son Kuò and his youngest son Xi to pay homage to King Xuan of Zhou. King Xuan favored Xi and wished to appoint him as the Crown Prince of Lu. Fan Zhongshanfu of Zhou advised King Xuan, "To depose the eldest and appoint the younger is against propriety; it is disorderly and will certainly violate royal commands; to violate a royal command invites punishment: therefore, decrees must be issued in accordance with propriety. If commands are not carried out, governance cannot be established; if they are carried out but go against propriety, the people will abandon their superiors. Subjects serving rulers and the young respecting the elder—this is what constitutes order. Now, if His Majesty establishes feudal lords by appointing their younger sons, this teaches the people to be disobedient. If Lu follows this example and other feudal lords emulate it, royal commands will become obstructed; if we do not follow and instead punish them, that would be punishing the royal command itself. To punish is a mistake; to refrain from punishment is also a mistake. Your Majesty should consider this carefully." King Xuan did not listen and ultimately appointed Xi as the Crown Prince of Lu.  In summer, Wugong returned but died soon after; Xi was enthroned and became Yigong.

## 19

In the ninth year of Yigong's reign, Bonyu, the son of Kuò, Yigong's elder brother, and the people of Lu attacked and killed Yigong, enthroning Bonyu as ruler. Eleven years after Bonyu assumed the throne, King Xuan of Zhou attacked Lu and killed its ruler Bonyu. He then sought among the descendants of the House of Lu a prince who could properly manage relations with the feudal lords to succeed as the new ruler of Lu. Fan Muzhong said, "Duke Yigong's younger brother Cheng is solemn and respectful toward the gods, reverent in his service to the elderly; he administers affairs and enforces punishments only after consulting ancestral teachings and established facts; he does not act without inquiry, nor does he violate what has been advised." King Xuan said, "Indeed, he is capable of governing and educating his people." Thus, Cheng was enthroned at Yigong's palace and became Xiaogong. From then on, many feudal lords defied the king's commands.

## 20

In the 25th year of Xiaogong's reign, the feudal lords rebelled against Zhou, and the Quanrong killed King You. Qi began to be listed among the feudal lords.

## 21

In the 27th year, Xiaogong died and was succeeded by his son Fuhuang, who became Huigong.

## 22

In the thirtieth year of Huigong's reign, the Jin people assassinated their ruler Duke Zhao Hou. In the forty-fifth year, the Jin people again assassinated their ruler Duke Xiao Hou.

## 23

In the 46th year, Huigong died; his eldest non-royal son Xi assumed control of state affairs and acted as ruler, becoming Yingu. Initially, Huigong's principal wife had no children; his lowly concubine Shengzi gave birth to a son named Xi. When Xi grew up, he married a woman from Song. When the Song bride arrived and was beautiful, Huigong took her for himself as his wife. She bore a son named Yun. He elevated the Song woman to the rank of principal wife and appointed Yun as Crown Prince. After Huigong's death, because Yun was still young, the people of Lu jointly ordered Xi to assume regency and did not announce his enthronement.

## 24

In the fifth year of Yingu's reign, he observed fishing at Tang. In the eighth year, he exchanged the fief of Bening and Xu Tian in Tai Mountain belonging to the Son of Heaven with Zheng; this was criticized by men of virtue.

## 25

In winter, the eleventh year, Gongzi Hui flattered Yingu and said, "The people favor you; you should now formally assume the throne. I will kill Prince Yun for you, and you may appoint me as your prime minister." Yingu said, "There is an order from my late father. I assumed regency because Yun was young. Now that Yun is grown, I am preparing the land of Tuqiu to retire there and hand over governance to Prince Yun." Hui, fearing that Prince Yun would hear of the plot and retaliate by killing him, instead falsely accused Yingu to Prince Yun, saying: "Yingu wants to formally assume power and remove you; you must plan against him. I will kill Yingu for you." Prince Yun agreed. In the eleventh month, Yingu made an offering to Zhongwu and held a sacrifice at Shepu; he stayed as a guest in the house of Wei Shi. Hui sent men to kill Yingu at Wei Shi's residence and enthroned Prince Yun as ruler, who became Huangong.

## 26

In the first year of Huangong's reign, Zheng exchanged a jade disc for Xu Tian, which belonged to the Son of Heaven. In the second year, he placed the sacrificial tripod received as a bribe from Song in the Grand Temple; this was criticized by men of integrity.

## 27

In the third year, he sent Hui to welcome his wife from Qi and made her his principal consort. In the sixth year, the lady gave birth to a son on the same day as Huangong; thus he was named Tong. When Tong grew up, he became Crown Prince.

## 28

In the sixteenth year, they met at Cao to attack Zheng and captured Ligong.

## 29

In spring of the eighteenth year, as the ruler was about to travel, he went with his wife to Qi. Shen Xu advised against it, but the ruler did not listen and proceeded to Qi anyway. King Xiang of Qi had an affair with Huangong's wife. The ruler was angry at his wife, and she informed King Qi about it. On the Bingzi day of the fourth month in summer, King Xiang of Qi hosted a banquet for the ruler. When the ruler became drunk, he ordered Gongzi Pengsheng to hold onto Duke Huangong and then commanded Pengsheng to break his ribs; the ruler died while still in the carriage. The people of Lu informed Qi, saying: "Our lord feared your majesty's power and could not remain at peace; he came to offer friendly rites. But after the ceremony was completed, he did not return, and there is no one else to blame; we respectfully request that Pengsheng be handed over so that we may remove this disgrace among the feudal lords." The people of Qi killed Pengsheng in order to appease Lu. They enthroned Crown Prince Tong, who became Zhuanggong. Zhuanggong's mother, the principal consort, remained in Qi and dared not return to Lu.

## 30

In winter of the fifth year of Zhuanggong's reign, they attacked Wei and installed Weigong Huigong within its borders.

## 31

In the eighth year, Gongzi Jiu of Qi fled to Lu. In the ninth year, Lu intended to install Zi Jiu in Qi as a successor to Huangong, but when Huangong raised an army and attacked Lu, Lu, in desperation, killed Zi Jiu. Zhao Hu died. Qi informed Lu that they had captured Guan Zhong alive and requested his delivery to Qi. A man from Lu named Shibai said, "Qi wants Guan Zhong to be alive, not dead; they intend to use him. If he is used, it will become a threat to Lu." "It would be better to kill him and give his corpse to Qi instead." Zhuanggong did not listen, so Guan Zhong was imprisoned and sent to Qi. The people of Qi appointed Guan Zhong as their prime minister.

## 32

In the thirteenth year, Duke Zhuang of Lu and Cao Mei met Huangong of Qi at Ke. Cao Mei took Huangong hostage, demanding that Lu return the territories it had invaded; after an oath was sworn, he released Huangong. Huangong wanted to break the agreement, but Guan Zhong advised against it, and in the end, the territories that Lu had invaded were returned. In the fifteenth year, Huangong of Qi began to establish his hegemony. In the twenty-third year, Duke Zhuang went to Qi to observe the ancestral rites.

## 33

In the thirty-second year, initially, Duke Zhuang built a pavilion overlooking the Dang family and saw Mengnü. He was pleased with her and loved her, vowing to make her his wife and cutting his arm to swear an oath. Mengnü gave birth to a son named Ban. When Ban grew up, he liked the daughter of Liangshi and went to visit her. A groom named Luo played with Liangshi's daughter from outside the wall. Ban was angry and whipped Luo with a whip. Duke Zhuang heard about it and said, "Luo is strong; if we kill him now, he cannot be whipped and dismissed." Ban was not allowed to kill Luo. At this time, Duke Zhuang fell ill. Duke Zhuang had three younger brothers: the eldest was Qingfu, the second was Shuya, and the third was Jiyou. Duke Zhuang took a woman from Qi as his wife, named Aijiang. Aijiang had no children. Aijiang's younger sister was Shujiang, who gave birth to a son named Kai. Duke Zhuang had no legitimate heir and loved Mengnü; he wanted to make her son Ban the crown prince. When Duke Zhuang fell ill, he asked his younger brother Shuya about who should succeed him. Shuya said, "Succession by one's own son and then by a younger brother is the norm in Lu." "Qingfu is still alive; he can be the heir. Why should you worry, my lord?" Duke Zhuang was troubled by Shuya's desire to make Qingfu the heir and later asked Jiyou about it. Jiyou said, "Please allow me to ensure Ban becomes the heir even if I must die for it." Duke Zhuang asked, "Earlier, Shuya wanted to make Qingfu the successor. What should we do about that?" Jiyou, following Duke Zhuang's orders, instructed Shuya to wait at Zhenwushi and had Zhenji force Shuya to drink poison, saying, "Drink this, and you will have descendants to carry on the ancestral rites; otherwise, you will die without any descendants." Shuya drank the poison and died; Lu later established his son as the head of the Shusun clan. On the Guihai day of the eighth month, Duke Zhuang died. Jiyou ultimately installed his son Ban as ruler, in accordance with Duke Zhuang's command. While attending to the funeral arrangements, they stayed at the Dang family's residence.

## 34

Previously, Qingfu had an affair with Aijiang and wanted to make Kai, Shujiang's son, the heir. When Duke Zhuang died and Jiyou installed Ban as ruler, on the Jiuwei day of the tenth month, Qingfu sent Luo to kill Gongzi Ban at the Dang family. Jiyou fled to Chen. Qingfu eventually installed Gongzi Kai, the son of Duke Zhuang, as ruler; this was King Min.

## 35

In the second year of King Min's reign, Qingfu and Aijiang became even more deeply involved in their affair. Aijiang conspired with Qingfu to kill King Min and make Qingfu the ruler. Qingfu sent Buyi to attack and kill King Min at Wuwei. Jiyou heard about this, left Chen with Shen, the younger brother of King Min, and went to Zhu, requesting Lu to allow them to return. The people of Lu wanted to execute Qingfu. Fearing for his life, Qingfu fled to Ju. At this time, Jiyou escorted Shen into Lu and installed him as ruler; he became King Li. King Li was also the youngest son of Duke Zhuang. Aijiang, fearing for her life, fled to Zhu. Jiyou sent a bribe to Ju to request Qingfu's return. After Qingfu returned, they had him killed. Qingfu requested to be allowed to flee, but this was refused; so the court then sent Minister Xisi to go and weep at his execution site. Qingfu heard Xisi's lamentations and committed suicide. Huangong of Qi, having heard that Aijiang had engaged in an affair with Qingfu and endangered Lu, summoned her from Zhu and executed her. He returned her corpse to Lu for public display and execution there. King Li of Lu requested permission to bury her remains.

## 36

Jiyou's mother was a woman from Chen, so he had been in exile there; therefore, the state of Chen assisted in sending Jiyou and his son Shen back to Lu. Before Jiyou was born, his father, Huangong of Lu, had a diviner predict the child's future. The oracle said: "It will be a boy; his name should be 'You,' standing between two ancestral shrines, and he shall serve as an assistant to the royal house." "If Jiyou is lost, Lu will not prosper." When he was born, there was a mark on his palm that resembled the character "You," so they named him after it. He was known as Chengji. Later generations became the Ji clan, while Qingfu's descendants later became the Meng clan.

## 37

In the first year of King Li's reign, he granted Jiyou the fief of Wenyang Yi. Jiyou became prime minister.

## 38

In the ninth year, Li Ke of Jin killed his ruler Xiqi and Zhuozi. Huangong of Qi led King Li to punish the disorder in Jin, reaching Gaoliang before returning, and installed Huigong of Jin. In the seventeenth year, Huangong of Qi died. In the twenty-fourth year, Wen Gong of Jin ascended to the throne.

## 39

In the thirty-third year, King Li died and his son Xing succeeded him; this was King Wen.

## 40

In the first year of King Wen's reign, Crown Prince Shangchen of Chu assassinated his father Chengwang and took the throne in his place. In the third year, King Wen paid a court visit to Xianggong of Jin.

## 41

On the Jiawu day of the eleventh month in the eleventh year, Lu defeated the Di people at Xian and captured Qiao Ru, a tall Di warrior. Fufu Zhongsheng crushed his throat with a pestle, killed him with a halberd, and buried his head at the gate of Ziju to present it as an offering to Duke Xu Bo.

## 42

Initially, during the reign of King Wu of Song, the Suoman attacked Song. Siku Huangfu led an army to resist them and defeated the Di at Changqiu, capturing a tall Di warrior named Yuanshi. When Jin destroyed Lu, they captured Fengru, Qiao Ru's younger brother. In the second year of Huigong of Qi's reign, Suoman attacked Qi. Gongzi Chengfu captured his younger brother Rongru and buried his head at Beimen Gate. The people of Wei captured Jianru, his younger brother. From then on, the Suoman gradually perished.

## 43

In the fifteenth year, Ji Wenzhi was sent as an envoy to Jin.

## 44

In the second month of the eighteenth year, King Wen died. King Wen had two consorts: his senior consort was a woman from Qi named Aijiang, who bore him sons Wu and Shi; his junior consort Jingying, whom he favored greatly, gave birth to his son Tai. Tai privately served Xiang Zhong, who wanted to make him the heir; but Shu Zhong said it was not possible. Xiang Zhong requested support from Huigong of Qi, who had just ascended the throne and wished to be close to Lu; he agreed. In winter in the tenth month, Xiang Zhong killed Wu and Shi and installed Tai as ruler; this was Duke Xu Gong. Aijiang returned to Qi and wept while passing through the marketplace, saying, "Heaven! Xiang Zhong acted immorally by killing the legitimate heir and installing a younger son!" The people in the marketplace wept with her, and thus the people of Lu called her "Aijiang." From this time onward, the royal house of Lu became weak while the Three Huans grew powerful.

## 45

In the twelfth year of Duke Xu Gong Tai's reign, Zhuangwang of Chu was strong and besieged Zheng. Duke Bo of Zheng surrendered, and Zhuangwang restored his state.

## 46

In the eighteenth year, Duke Xu Gong died; his son Heigong succeeded him as King Chenggong. Ji Wenzhi said, "The one who caused me to kill the legitimate heir and install a younger son, thereby losing great support, was Xiang Zhong." Xiang Zhong installed Duke Xu Gong, and Gongsun Guifu gained favor. Duke Xu Gong wanted to eliminate the Three Huans and conspired with Jin to attack them. However, when Duke Xu Gong died, Ji Wenzhi resented this, so Guifu fled to Qi.

## 47

In spring of the second year of King Chenggong's reign, Qi attacked and captured Long from us. In summer, the king allied with Xi Ke of Jin to defeat King Qinggong of Qi at Ai. Qi then returned the territories it had invaded. In the fourth year, King Chenggong visited Jin, but Jinggong of Jin did not show respect to Lu. Lu wanted to abandon its alliance with Jin and join forces with Chu, but after some advised against it, they decided not to proceed. In the tenth year, King Chenggong went to Jin. When Jinggong of Jin died, they detained King Chenggong to accompany them in the funeral rites; Lu concealed this incident. In the fifteenth year, Lu first met with Wu Wang Shoumeng at Zhongli.

## 48

In the sixteenth year, Xuanbo reported to Jin and wanted to execute Ji Wenzhi. Ji Wenzhi was virtuous; the people of Jin did not agree.

## 49

In the eighteenth year, King Chenggong died and his son Wu succeeded him as king, who was known as King Xianggong. At that time, King Xianggong was only three years old.

## 50

lords In the first year of King Xianggong's reign, Jin established Duke Daogong. The previous winter, Luan Shu of Jin assassinated his ruler, King Liegong. In the fourth year, Duke Xianggong paid a court visit to Jin.

## 51

In the fifth year, Ji Wenzhi died. There were no concubines in his household who wore silk garments; there were no horses in the stable that ate grain; and there was no gold or jade in his treasury, as he served three rulers. The gentlemen said: "Ji Wenzhi is indeed upright and loyal."

## 52

In the ninth year, they joined Jin to attack Zheng. Duke Daogong of Jin held a capping ceremony for Duke Xianggong in Wei, and Ji Wusi followed along, assisting with the rituals.

## 53

In the eleventh year, the Three Huans divided into three armies.

## 54

In the twelfth year, they paid a court visit to Jin.  In the sixteenth year, Pinggong of Jin ascended the throne. In the twenty-first year, he paid a court visit to King Pinggong of Jin.

## 55

In the twenty-second year, Kong Qiu was born.

## 56

In the twenty-fifth year, Cui Zhu of Qi assassinated his ruler King Zhuanggong and installed his younger brother as King Jinggong.

## 57

In the twenty-ninth year, Ji Zi of Yanning from Wu was sent to Lu and inquired about Zhou music. He fully understood its meaning, and the people of Lu respected him for it.

## 58

In the thirty-first year, on the sixth month, Duke Xianggong died. In that September, the crown prince died. The people of Lu installed Chou, the son of Qi Gui, as ruler; he was known as Duke Zhao.

## 59

Duke Zhao was nineteen years old and still had childish tendencies. Mushu did not want to support this, saying: "When the crown prince dies, there is a younger full brother who can be installed as ruler. We should not bypass the eldest." "When their ages are equal, we choose the more virtuous one; when virtue is equal, then we resort to divination." "Now Chou is not the legitimate heir, and furthermore, during mourning he does not show sorrow but instead a joyful expression. If he were indeed installed as ruler, he would surely become a source of concern for the Ji family." Ji Wusi did not listen and ultimately had him installed. By the time of the burial, he changed his mourning clothes three times. The gentlemen said: "This one will not have a good ending."

## 60

In the third year of Duke Zhao's reign, when he went to pay homage to Jin and reached the Yellow River, King Pinggong of Jin sent him back in disgrace; Lu was shamed by this. In the fourth year, Lord Lingwang of Chu convened feudal lords at Shen, but Duke Zhao claimed illness and did not attend. In the seventh year, Ji Wusi died. In the eighth year, Lord Lingwang of Chuyou ZHANGHUATAI and summoned Duke Zhao. Duke Zhao went to offer congratulations and was granted precious vessels by him; Later, he regretted it and deceitfully took them back. In the twelfth year, when Duke Zhao went to pay homage to Jin as far as the Yellow River, King Ping of Jin sent him back without receiving him; this shamed Lu. In the thirteenth year, Gongzi Qiji of Chu assassinated his ruler Lord Lingwang and took over the throne in his place. In the fifteenth year, Duke Zhao paid a visit to Jin, but was detained by them to attend the funeral of King Zhao of Jin; this shamed Lu. In the twentieth year, King Jinggong of Qi and Yanzi went hunting at the border, and thus entered Lu to inquire about rites. In the twenty-first year, Duke Zhao went to Jin as far as the river, but was sent back without being received; this shamed Lu again.

## 61

In spring of the twenty-fifth year, a qiongzhi bird came to nest. Shi Ji said: "In the time of King Wengong, a children's rhyme went: 'When the qiongzhi comes to nest, the ruler will be at Qianhou.'" "When the qiongzhi enters and nests, the ruler is in the outer fields."

## 62

The Ji family and Hou Shi had a cockfight. The Ji family used chickens with feathers treated with mustard, while the Hou family used roosters fitted with metal spurs. Ji Pingzi became angry and invaded the Hou clan's territory; Hou Zhao Bo was also furious at Ji Pingzi. Zang Zhao Bo's younger brother Hui falsely slandered the Zang clan, hiding in the Ji family. In response, Zang Zhao Bo imprisoned someone from the Ji family. Ji Pingzi was furious and imprisoned an elder of the Zang family. The Zang and Hou families reported their grievances to Duke Zhao. On the Wuxu day of the ninth month, Duke Zhao attacked the Ji family and succeeded in entering their territory. Pingzi climbed onto a platform and pleaded, saying: "Your Majesty has not investigated my innocence due to slander. If you wish to punish me, please exile me to Yishang." It was not permitted. He then requested to be imprisoned in Bi, but this too was denied. He further asked for permission to flee with five chariots, but again it was refused. Zijia Ju said: "Your Majesty should grant his request." "For a long time, power has rested with the Ji family. They have many followers who will conspire together if they are not satisfied." He did not listen. The Hou family said: "We must kill him." Li, a retainer of the Shusun clan, told his followers: "Which is more beneficial for us, having or not having the Ji family?" They all replied: "Without the Ji family, there would be no Shusun family." Li said: "Then let us save the Ji family!" They then defeated the king's forces. Meng Yizi, upon hearing that the Shusun clan had won, also killed Hou Zhao Bo. Hou Zhao Bo was serving as an envoy for the king; thus, Meng's family captured him. The three families then jointly attacked the king, and he fled as a result. On the Jihai day, the king arrived in Qi. King Jinggong of Qi said: "Please allow me to grant you a thousand she of land for your residence." Zijia said: "Shall we abandon the legacy of Duke of Zhou and become subjects of Qi? Is that acceptable?" They then decided against it. Zijia said: "King Jinggong of Qi is untrustworthy; we had better go to Jin sooner." They did not follow this advice. Shusun met the king upon his return and saw Pingzi, who then kowtowed in submission. Initially they had wanted to welcome Duke Zhao back, but Mengsun and Jisun later regretted it and stopped the plan.

## 63

In spring of the twenty-sixth year, Qi attacked Lu, captured Yun, and settled Duke Zhao there. In summer, King Jinggong of Qi planned to bring the king back into Lu and ordered that no bribes from Lu be accepted. Shen Feng and Ru Jia, however, promised King Qi's ministers Gao Ge and Zi Jiang five thousand yu of grain. Zijiang said to the ruler of Qi: "The officials are unable to serve Duke Zhao of Lu, and there is something unusual about this. Duke Yuangong of Song went to Jin on behalf of Lu to request that the king be allowed back, but he died along the way. Shusun Zhaozi sought to have his ruler reinstated in Lu and died suddenly without illness. No one knew whether Heaven had abandoned Lu or not? Or perhaps the ruler of Lu had committed a sin against spirits and deities? "I hope Your Majesty will wait for now." King Jinggong of Qi followed this advice.

## 64

In the twenty-eighth year, Duke Zhao went to Jin and requested to return to Lu. Ji Pingzi secretly bribed the six high ministers of Jin, who accepted gifts from the Ji family and advised King Jin against allowing Duke Zhao to return. The king of Jin then refused, and Duke Zhao remained in Qianhou. In the twenty-ninth year, Duke Zhao went to Yun. King Jinggong of Qi sent someone to present a letter to Duke Zhao and referred to himself as "the sovereign lord." Duke Zhao was shamed by this, became angry, and left for Qianhou. In the thirty-first year, Jin wanted to allow Duke Zhao back into Lu and summoned Ji Pingzi. Pingzi went barefoot in plain clothes and through the six high ministers apologized for his sins. The six high ministers said on his behalf, "Jin wants to allow Duke Zhao back in, but the people do not agree." The people of Jin stopped this plan. In the thirty-second year, Duke Zhao died in Qianhou. The people of Lu jointly installed Duke Zhao's younger brother Song as ruler; this was King Dinggong.

## 65

After King Dinggong ascended the throne, Zhao Jianzi asked Shi Mo, "Will the Ji family be destroyed?" Shi Mo replied, "They will not be destroyed. Ji You had great merit for Lu and was granted the fief of Yi as a top minister. This continued through Wenzǐ and Wǔzǐ, with each generation increasing their achievements. When King Wen of Lu died, Dongmen Sui killed the legitimate heir and installed a younger son instead; from then on, the rulers of Lu lost control of state affairs. Power has been in the hands of the Ji family for four generations now. The people do not recognize their ruler; how can they retain control of the state! Therefore, a ruler must be cautious about power and titles, which cannot be entrusted to others."

## 66

In the fifth year of King Dinggong's reign, Ji Pingzi died. Yang Hu was privately angry and imprisoned Ji Huanzi; after making an oath with him, he released him. In the seventh year, Qi attacked us, captured Yun, and established it as a fief for Yang Hu of Lu to govern. In the eighth year, Yang Hu wanted to kill all three legitimate heirs of the Huan clan and replace them with younger sons he favored. He planned to have Ji Huanzi killed, but Huanzi deceived him and escaped. The three Huan families jointly attacked Yang Hu, who then took refuge at Yangguan. In the ninth year, Lu attacked Yang Hu, and Yang Hu fled to Qi; later he went on to Sishu of Jin.

## 67

In the tenth year, King Dinggong met with King Jinggong of Qi at Jiagu, and Confucius served as chief minister. Qi wanted to attack King Lu, but Confucius ascended the steps in accordance with ritual and punished Qi's lewd music; King of Qi was frightened and stopped his plan. He returned the occupied land to Lu and apologized for the offense. In the twelfth year, they sent Zhong You to destroy the walls of the three Huan fiefs and confiscate their weapons and armor. The Meng family refused to dismantle its city wall, so they attacked them but failed and stopped. Ji Huanzi accepted female musicians from Qi, and Confucius left Lu.

## 68

In the fifteenth year, King Dinggong died; his son was installed as ruler, this being King Aigong.

## 69

In the fifth year of King Aigong's reign, King Jinggong of Qi died. In the sixth year, Tian Qǐ killed his ruler Ruzi.

## 70

In the seventh year, King Fuchai of Wu was powerful and attacked Qi, reaching Zeng, and demanded a hundred sacrificial offerings from Lu. Ji Kangzi sent Zi Gong to persuade King Wu and the Grand Minister Tai Zai Pi, using ritual to dissuade them. King Wu said, "I am tattooed; I cannot be held to standards of ritual." They then stopped.

## 71

Wu, on behalf of Zou, attacked Lu and reached the city walls; after making an alliance, they withdrew. Qi attacked us and captured three fiefs. In the tenth year, they attacked the southern border of Qi.

## 72

Qi attacked Lu. Ji Shi employed Ran You, who achieved success and thought of Confucius; thus, Confucius returned from Wei to Lu. Tian Chang killed his ruler King Jian of Qi in Xuzhou. Confucius requested to attack them, but King Aigong did not listen.

## 73

In the fifteenth year, they sent Zifu Jingbo and Zi Gong as envoys to Qi, and Qi returned the occupied land. Tian Chang was newly appointed prime minister and wished to cultivate good relations with the feudal lords.

## 74

In the sixteenth year, Confucius died.

## 75

In the twenty-second year, King Goujian of Yue destroyed King Fuchai of Wu.

## 76

In spring of the twenty-seventh year, Ji Kangzi died. In summer, King Aigong was troubled by the three Huan families and planned to use feudal lords to intimidate them. The three Huan were also worried about the king causing trouble, so relations between ruler and ministers became strained. The king was touring Lingban when he met Meng Wubei on the street, who said, "May I ask if you will live to see my death?" He replied, "I do not know." The king wanted to use Yue to attack the three Huan. In August, King Aigong went to Xingshi. The three Huan attacked the king; he fled to Wei, then left for Zou and eventually arrived in Yue. The people of Lu welcomed King Aigong back and he returned, dying at Youshan Shi's residence. Zining was installed as ruler; this was Duke Daogong.

## 77

During the reign of Duke Daogong, the three Huan families prevailed, and Lu became like a minor lord, subordinate to the Ji family.

## 78

In the thirteenth year, the Three Jin states destroyed Zhi Bo and divided his territory among themselves.

## 79

In the thirty-seventh year, Duke Daogong died; his son Jia was installed as ruler, this becoming King Yuangong. King Yuangong died in the twenty-first year of his reign; his son Xian was installed as ruler, known as Duke Mugu. Duke Mugu died in the thirty-third year of his reign; his son Fen was installed as ruler, becoming King Gonggong. King Gonggong died in the twenty-second year of his reign; his son Tun was installed as ruler, called Duke Kanggong. Duke Kanggong died in the ninth year of his reign; his son Yan was installed as ruler, named King Jinggong. King Jinggong died in the twenty-ninth year of his reign; his son Shu was installed as ruler, who became King Pinggong. At this time, all six states had declared themselves kings.

## 80

In the twelfth year of King Pinggong's reign, King Huaiwang of Qin died. In the twentieth year, King Pinggong died; his son Jia became ruler, known as King Wengong. In the first year of King Wengong's reign (the first year), King Huaiwang of Chu died in Qin. In the twenty-third year, King Wengong died; his son Chou was installed as ruler, this known as King Qinggong.

## 81

In the second year of King Qinggong's reign, Qin captured Ying from Chu; King Qingwang of Chu moved east to Chen. In the nineteenth year, Chu attacked us and took Xuzhou. In the twenty-fourth year, King Kaolie of Chu launched a campaign that destroyed Lu. King Qinggong fled and was relocated to Xiayi, where he became an ordinary family member; the ancestral rites of Lu were extinguished. King Qinggong died in Ke.

## 82

From Duke of Zhou to King Qinggong, Lu had a total of thirty-four generations.

## 83

Grand Historian said: I have heard Confucius say, "How greatly has the way of Lu declined! The debates and discussions by the rivers Zhu and Si are still vigorous." Observing the time of Qingfu, Shuya, and King Min, how chaotic it was! The events involving Duke Yin and Duke Huan; Xiang Zhong killed the legitimate heir to install a younger son; The three families, though seated in northern ministerial positions, personally attacked Duke Zhao, who was forced into exile. Although they followed the formal courtesies of yielding and courtesy, how greatly did their actions deviate from righteousness!
