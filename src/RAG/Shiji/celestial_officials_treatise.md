# Celestial Officials Treatise

## 1

The central palace Tianjixing, among which one is bright, is Taiyi Changju. Three stars beside it represent the Three Dukes; some say they belong to the constellation Zi. Behind them are four stars, with the last large star representing the principal queen consort; the remaining three stars represent the concubines of the imperial harem. The twelve stars surrounding and guarding them represent vassal ministers. They are all called Zigong (the Purple Palace).

## 2

Three stars in front, aligned straight with the mouth of Dou, follow the northern end toward Dui; they are sometimes visible and sometimes not. They are called Yinde, or alternatively Tianyi. Three stars to the left of Zigong are called Tianqiang; five stars to the right are called Tianbai. Six stars behind them cross over the Milky Way and reach Yingshi, known as Geda (the Gallery Path).

## 3

The Seven Stars of Beidou are what is referred to as "Xuan, Ji, and Yuheng used to regulate the seven official duties." The Shao star carries Longjiao; Heng aligns with Nandou in the south; Kui rests at the head of Shen. At dusk, the position is established by the Shao. The Shao points from Hua to the southwest. At midnight, the position is established by Heng; Heng points between the central states of He and Ji rivers. At dawn, the position is established by Kui; Kui points toward the northeast of Hai and Dai. Dou is the emperor's chariot, moving in the center, overseeing all four directions. Dividing yin and yang, establishing the four seasons, balancing the five elements, adjusting seasonal rhythms, fixing all calendars—these are all related to Dou.

## 4

Six stars surrounding the Kui of Dou form a constellation called Wenchanggong: the first is Shangjiang, the second Cijiang, the third Guixiang, the fourth Siming, the fifth Sizhong, and the sixth Silu. Within the Kui of Dou is a prison for nobles. Six stars beneath Kui, paired two by two, are called Saneng. When the colors of Saneng are equal, ruler and minister are in harmony; If they are unequal, it signifies discord. When the Fu star is bright and close, the assistant minister is intimate and powerful; If it is distant and small, the relationship is distant and weak.

## 5

At the end of Shao are two stars: one inside is Mao, Zhaoyao; one outside is Dun, Tianfeng. Fifteen stars in a curved formation belong to Shao; they are called the prison of lowly people. When the stars within this prison are bright, prisoners are numerous; when dim or absent, it indicates release.

## 6

If Tianyi, Qiang, Bai, Mao, and Dun stars move or tremble, with their angles expanding, it signifies the outbreak of war.

## 7

The eastern palace Canglong includes Fang and Xin. Xin represents the Mingtang (Hall of Light), with the large star as Tianwang, and stars before and after representing his sons. They should not be straight; if they are straight, it means the heavenly king has lost control. Fang represents the government office and is called Tiansi. Its shadow, Youcan. Two stars beside it are called Jin; one star to the north is Xie. Twelve stars curving northeast are called Qi. Four stars within Qi represent Tianshi (Heavenly Market); six stars in the center are called Shilou. When many stars appear in Tianshi, it indicates abundance; if they are sparse or absent, it signifies depletion. Many stars to the south of Fang are called Qiguan (Equestrian Officials).

## 8

The left horn is Li; the right horn is Jiang. The large horn represents the imperial court of the heavenly king. On each side are three stars, forming a tripod-like pattern; they are called Sheti. Sheti points directly to the direction indicated by Shao of Dou, establishing the seasons and times; hence it is called "Sheti Ge." Kang represents a distant temple and governs illness. The two large stars to its north and south are called Nanmen (Southern Gate). Di is the root of heaven, governing epidemics.

## 9

Wei represents nine sons, symbolizing ruler and minister; If they are distant or disconnected, it signifies disharmony. Ji represents outlaws and guests, associated with disputes and words.

## 10

If fire-like stars invade or guard the horns, war will occur. Fang and Xin are considered ominous by the king.

## 11

The southern palace Zhuniao includes Quan and Heng. Heng is Taiwei, the court of the three lights (sun, moon, stars). Twelve surrounding stars represent vassal ministers: to the west are generals; to the east, are prime ministers; four stars to the south represent Zhifa (executive law); in the center is Duanmen (the Gate of Ceremony); on both sides of the gate are Yimen (side gates). Six stars inside the gate represent feudal lords. Within them are five stars representing the seats of the Five Emperors. Behind them, fifteen clustered stars appear dense and bright; they are called Langwei (positions for officials). Beside it is a large star representing the position of a general. When the moon and five planets enter in an orderly manner along their orbital paths, they oversee departures, what is guarded, and are signs of punishment by the Son of Heaven. If they enter in a disorderly manner or deviate from their orbital paths, names are assigned according to what is violated; When they appear centered and take clear form, it signifies that the group below has conspired together. Metal and fire are especially significant in this context. Five stars to the west of the court's outer wall are called Shaowei, representing scholars and officials. Quan is Xuanyuan. Xuanyuan represents the body of Huanglong (the Yellow Dragon). The large star in front symbolizes a female ruler; the small stars beside it represent attendants and the imperial concubines. When the moon or five planets guard or invade this area, their omens are interpreted as with Heng's observations.

## 12

Dongjing governs water affairs. The curved stars to its west are called Yue. To the north of Yue is Beihe; to the south, Nanhe; The space between the two rivers Beihe and Nanhe and Tianque is called Guanliang (a pass or bridge). Yugu represents ancestral temples and rituals; The white star in the center is called Zhi. If fire-like stars guard or occupy Beihe and Nanhe, war will arise and crops will fail. Therefore, virtue is fulfilled in Heng; observation in Huang; injury in Yue; disaster in Jing; punishment in Zhi.

## 13

Liu represents birds' nests and governs trees and grasses. Seven stars form the neck, representing Yuanguan (circular officials). They govern urgent affairs. Zhang, Su, represents the kitchen and hosts banquets for guests. Yi is wings, governing distant guests.

## 14

Zhen represents a cart and governs wind. A small star beside it is called Changsha; stars should not be too bright; if its brightness equals that of four stars, or if the five planets enter Zhen, great war will erupt. Many stars to the south of Zhen are called Tiankulu (Celestial Granary Tower); the granary has five carts. If the stars of the cart appear sharp or increase in number, or are incomplete, it indicates a lack of carriages and horses.

## 15

The western palace Xiantchi is called Tianwuhuang.  If fire enters, drought will occur; if metal appears, war follows; water signifies water. There are three pillars in it; if the pillars are incomplete, war will arise.

## 16

Kui is called Fengshi and represents ditches and canals. Lou signifies the gathering of crowds. Wei is the Celestial Granary. The many stars to its south are called Kuangji (Granaries).

## 17

Mao is called Maotou, a Hu star, representing an assembly of commoners. Bi is called Hanchē, representing border troops and overseeing hunting with bows. The small stars beside its main star are called Fuer (attached ears). If the attached ears shake, there will be treacherous ministers nearby. Between Mao and Bi is Tiandu (the Celestial Street). Its southern side represents the Yin state; its northern side, the Yang state.

## 18

Shen is the White Tiger. The three stars aligned straight are called Hengshi (the Weights). Beneath them are three stars, Dui, called Fa, representing executions and punishments. The four stars outside represent the left and right shoulders and thighs. Three small stars placed at the corners are called Zuixi, representing the tiger's head and overseeing military affairs. To its south there are four stars called Tianguan (Celestial Toilets). One star beneath the toilets is called Tianzhi (Celestial Arrow). If the arrow appears yellow, it is auspicious; if green, white, or black, it is inauspicious. To its west are nine curved stars forming a hook-like shape, arranged in three clusters: the first is Tianqi (Celestial Banner), the second Tiayuan (Celestial Park), and the third Jiuyou (Nine Drifts). To its east there is a large star called Lang. If the color of Lang's horns changes, it indicates an increase in thieves and bandits. Beneath it are four stars called Hu, aligned straight with Lang. A large star near the ground compared to Lang is called Nanji Laoren (the Southern Pole Old Man). If the old man appears, it signifies peace and order; if he does not appear, war will break out. He should always be observed in the southern suburbs during the Autumnal Equinox.

## 19

If Fuer enters Bi, war will arise.

## 20

The northern palace Xuanwu includes Xu and Wei. Wei represents the roof of a house; Xu signifies matters related to weeping and mourning.

## 21

To its south are many stars called Yulin Tianjun (Celestial Army of the Feathered Forest). To the west of the army is a formation, or it may be called Yue. Beside it there is one large star called Beiluo. If Beiluo appears faint or disappears, and the stars of the army move with their horns becoming more prominent, or if the Five Planets invade Beiluo and enter the military formation, war will erupt. Fire, metal, and water are especially significant: fire signifies concern for the army; water indicates a water disaster; wood and earth signify good fortune for the army. Six stars to the east of Wei, paired two by two, are called Sikong.

## 22

Yingshi represents the Clear Temple, also known as Ligung and Gedaodao (Detached Palace and Gallery Path). Four stars in Hanzhong are called Tiangui. One star beside them is called Wangliang. Wangliang guiding the horse signifies carriages and cavalry filling the fields. Beside them are eight stars, separated by the Milky Way, called Tianhuang. Near Tianhuang is Jiangxing (River Star). If the river star moves, people will cross water.

## 23

Chu and Jiu (four stars) are located to the south of Wei. Paogua, if guarded by a blue-black star, signifies high prices for fish and salt.

## 24

Nandou represents the temple; to its north is Jianxing. Jianxing signifies a banner. Qianniu represents sacrificial animals. To its north is Hedou (the Drum of the River). The main star of Hedou represents a supreme general; the stars to its left and right represent the left and right generals. Wunv, to its north is Zhinv (Weaving Maid). Zhinv is the daughter of the Celestial Woman.

## 25

Observe the movements of the sun and moon to determine whether the movement of the age star (Jupiter) is in harmony or disorder. The east is wood, governing spring, corresponding to days Jia and Yi. If righteousness is lost, punishment will come from the age star. The advancement or retreat of the age star determines the fate of the state it resides in. The country where it is located should not be attacked, but its people may be punished. If it moves ahead of the expected position, it is called ying; if it lags behind, it is called suo. Ying indicates that a country's military forces will not return; suo signifies that the country will face troubles, its leaders may perish, and the state will collapse. If all five planets gather in one position where it resides, the country beneath it can bring order to the world through righteousness.

## 26

Using Shetige as the year: in this year, the dark star moves leftward into Yin, while the age star turns rightward and resides in Chou. In the first month, it rises with Dou and Qianniu in the east at dawn; this is called Jiande. Its color is bluish-gray with a glow. If it departs from its proper position, there will be an omen appearing in Liu (a constellation). An early year brings floods; a late year brings drought.

## 27

When the age star appears, it moves eastward twelve degrees and stops after a hundred days, then reverses its course; it moves westward eight degrees over another hundred days before resuming its eastward movement. Each year it travels thirty degrees and seven sixteenths of a degree, averaging one twelfth of a degree per day, completing a full orbit around the sky in twelve years. It usually appears in the east at dawn; it sets in the west during dusk.

## 28

The year Dan'e: the dark star is at Mao, and the age star resides at Zi. In February, it rises in the east with Wunv, Xu, and Wei; this is called Jiangru (Descending Entry). It has a very bright light. If it departs from its position, an omen will appear in Zhang (a constellation). [Called Jiangru], this year brings great floods.

## 29

The year Zhuxu: the dark star is at Chen, and the age star resides in Hai. In March [residing], it rises with Yingshi and Dongbi at dawn; this is called Qingzhang. It appears very clearly green. If it departs from its place; an omen will appear in Zhen (a constellation). [Called Qingzhang], the year is early, with drought; late, there will be floods.

## 30

The year Dahuangluo: the dark star is at Si, and the age star resides in Xu. In April, it rises with Kui, Lou [Wei Mao] at dawn; this is called Pianzhong. It glows in a fiery red color, with light. If it departs from its order, an omen will appear at Kang (a constellation).

## 31

The year Dunzang: the dark star is in Wu, and the age star resides in You. In May, it rises with Wei, Mao, and Bi at dawn; this is called Kaiming. It glows intensely with light. Lay down arms; only beneficial to the public and kings, not beneficial for military governance. If it departs from order, an omen will be seen in Fang (a constellation). An early year brings drought; A late year brings floods.

## 32

The year Xieqia: the dark star is at Wei, and the age star resides in Shen. In June, it rises with Zuixi and Shen at dawn; this is called Changlie. It shines clearly with light. Favorable for military campaigns. If it departs from its sequence, an omen will appear in Ji (a constellation).

## 33

The year Hutan: the dark star is at Shen, and the age star resides in Wei. In July, it rises with Dongjing and Yugui at dawn; this is called Dayin. It shines clearly white. If it departs from its proper order, an omen will appear near Qianniu (a constellation).

## 34

The year Zuoe: the dark star is at You, and the age star resides in Wu. In August, it rises with Liu, Qixing, and Zhang at dawn; this is called (as) Changwang. It appears active with rays or brilliance. The state will flourish, and grain will be abundant. If it departs from its regular position, an omen should appear in Wei (a constellation). [Called Dayin], there will be drought and prosperity, female deaths, and people's illnesses.

## 35

The year Yanmao: the dark star is at Xu, and the age star resides in Si. In September, it rises with Yi and Zhen at dawn; this is called Tiansui. It shines in a bright white color. If it departs from its normal position, an omen will be visible at Dongbi (a constellation). This year brings floods and female deaths.

## 36

The year Dayuankan: the dark star is at Hai, and the age star resides in Chen. In October, it rises with Jiao and Kang at dawn; this is called Dazhang. It appears dark blue, the stars as if leaping out in the morning darkness; this is called "Zhengping." When raising an army, its commander must be martial; if the state has virtue, it will rule over all under heaven. If it departs from its usual position, an omen appears in Lou (a constellation).

## 37

The year Kundun: the dark star is at Zi, and the age star resides in Mao. In November, it rises with Di, Fang, and Xin at dawn; this is called Tianquan. It shines very clearly in a dark color. The rivers and pools will flourish, which is unfavorable for raising an army. If it departs from its due position, there should be an omen (at) [visible in] Mao.

## 38

The year Chifanruo: the dark star is at Chou, and the age star resides in Yin. In December, it rises with Wei and Ji at dawn; this is called Tianhao. It appears very bright in a deep black color. If it departs from its course, an omen will appear visible in Shen (a constellation).

## 39

When it should be in a position but is not, or when it occupies the position and sways left and right; if it departs before its time or meets with other stars, the state will suffer misfortune. If it remains in a place for a long time, the country has deep virtue. If its horn moves, suddenly small then large, or if its color and brightness change frequently, the ruler will have worries.

## 40

If it departs from the lower positions of its dwelling, moving northeastward, after three months a tianbang star will appear, four zhang long, with a dui-shaped end; if it moves southeastward, after three months an xinghui (comet-like) star will emerge, two zhang long, resembling a comet. If it retreats northwestward, after three months the tianchan star will appear, four zhang in length, with a dui-shaped tip. If it retreats southwestward, after three months the tianguang star will appear, several zhang in length, with both ends shaped like dui. Carefully observe the state where it is seen; one must not undertake military actions or wars. If its emergence appears as if floating and sinking, there will be earthworks in that country; if it seems to sink and float, the countryside will perish. If its color is red and it has horns, the country where it resides will flourish. Those who fight by confronting its horn will not win. If the star appears red-yellow and sinks, the countryside where it resides will have a great harvest. If its color is blue-white with tinges of grayish-red, there will be worries in the countryside where it appears. When the Suixing enters the moon, there will be a struggle for power among officials in that region; if it confronts Taibai (Venus), there will be defeated armies in that area.

## 41

The Suixing is also called Sheti, Chonghua, Yingxing, and Jixing. Yingshi represents the Qingmiao (Hall of Clarity), which is the temple of Suixing.

## 42

Examine the rigid qi to determine the position of Yinghuo. It is called the Southern Fire, in charge of summer, corresponding to days Bing and Ding. When rites are neglected, punishment manifests as Yinghuo; this is when Yinghuo deviates from its course. If it appears, there will be warfare; if it disappears, the armies will disband. Its position determines the fate of a state. (Yinghuo) Yinghuo signifies disorder, destruction, disease, funerals, famine, and warfare. If it reverses its course by two positions or more and remains there, after three months calamity will occur; after five months the state will face warfare; after seven and a half months territory will be lost; after nine months most of the land will be lost. If it moves in and out together with another celestial body, the state's ancestral sacrifices will cease. If it resides there, calamity returns; even if great, it becomes small; if the calamity arrives after a long time, what was small will become large. Its southern position signifies men's funerals; its northern position signifies women's funerals. If it turns and circles around, or moves forward and backward suddenly to the left or right, the calamity becomes even greater. When it confronts other stars and their lights reach each other, harm will occur; if their lights do not meet, no harm will be done. When the five planets all follow and gather in one position, the state beneath it can bring the whole world under its rule through rites.

## 43

According to law, when emerging from the east, it travels sixteen positions before stopping; then moves backward two positions; after sixty days, it resumes its eastward movement. Counting ten positions from where it stopped, after ten months it enters the west; it remains hidden for five months before emerging in the east again. Its emergence from the west is called "Fanming," which rulers dislike. When moving eastward quickly, it travels one and a half degrees per day.

## 44

Its movement to the east, west, south, and north is swift. Armies gather beneath its position; When waging war, those who follow it will triumph, while those who oppose it will suffer defeat. If Yinghuo follows Taibai, the army will be in distress; if they separate, the army will retreat. When emerging from the dark side of Taibai, there is a divided army; when moving on its bright side, there will be a battle led by a deputy general. When it is in motion and Taibai catches up with it, the army will be destroyed and generals killed. If it enters and intrudes upon Taiwei, Xuanyuan, or Yingshi, the ruler will suffer misfortune from this. Xin is the Mingtang; it is the temple of Yinghuo. Be cautious in observing this.

## 45

By recording the conjunctions of the stars, one can determine the position of Tianxing. It is called Central Earth, governing mid-summer, corresponding to the days Wu and Ji; it represents Huangdi, who embodies virtue, and symbolizes a female ruler. Each year, Tianxing resides in one mansion; the state where it dwells will experience good fortune. If it occupies a position before its time, or returns after having departed and resides there again, the state will gain territory; otherwise, it may gain women. If it should occupy a position but does not, or having occupied it moves away to the west and east, then the state will lose territory; otherwise, it may lose women. In such cases, one must not undertake military actions. The longer it resides in a position, the greater the blessings for that state; if it changes positions easily, its blessings are shallow.

## 46

It is also known as Dihou, and governs the year. Each year it travels thirteen degrees and five one-hundred-and-twelfths of a degree, moving at a rate of one twenty-eighth of a degree per day, completing a full circuit around the heavens in twenty-eight years. Wherever it resides, all five planets gather and follow to one mansion; the state beneath will be able to bring the whole world under its control through great authority. When rites, virtue, righteousness, punishment, and criminal justice are all lost, Tianxing will then be disturbed and agitated.

## 47

If it lags behind, the king will not find peace; if it retreats, an army will not return. Tianxing has a yellow color, nine rays, and its tone is Huangzhong Gong. If it falls behind by two or three star positions, it is called "ying," indicating that the ruler's plans will not succeed, or there may be a great flood. If it falls short by two or three star positions and is below its proper place, it is called "suo," indicating that there will be troubles involving the queen's relatives; in such a year, things will not return to normal, or alternatively, there may be an earthquake or a splitting of the heavens.

## 48

Dou is Wen Taishi; it is the temple for Tianxing and represents the star of the Son of Heaven.

## 49

When Jupiter combines with Earth, internal disorder will occur. Famine will follow; the ruler should not engage in war, for it will lead to defeat; there will be floods, and plans must change with new circumstances; Fire brings drought; Metal signifies a gathering of commoners in white clothes or floods. When Metal is located to the south, it indicates harmony between male and female; the grain will be abundant. When Metal is in the north, the year's harvest will be uneven or nonexistent. When Fire combines with Water, it is called "Cui"; when combined with Metal, it is called "Shuo," both indicating mourning. In such cases, no important matters should be undertaken; waging war will result in a great defeat. Earth signifies worry and the rise of rebellious ministers; there will be great famine, battlefield defeat, a northern army in retreat, an encircled force, and any undertaking will end in major failure. When Earth combines with Water, the land becomes fertile but blocked; there may be a defeated army, and no important matters should be initiated in that state. If it emerges, territory will be lost; if it enters, territory will be gained. Metal signifies rapid events and internal warfare, resulting in the loss of territory. If three stars converge together, within or beyond the borders of the state corresponding to that mansion, there will be warfare and mourning, and a new ruler may be established. When four stars converge, war and mourning arise together; the virtuous are distressed while the unworthy scatter. When all five stars converge, it is called "Yi Xing," indicating virtue and the receipt of blessings. A great leader will be established, ruling over all directions; descendants will flourish abundantly; but if there is no virtue, calamity or downfall will follow. When all five stars are bright and large, the events they portend will also be significant; if they are small, the matters involved will also be minor.

## 50

Those that emerge early are called "ying," and those who are ying are considered guests. Those that appear late are called "suo," and those who are suo are regarded as the hosts. There must be a celestial response visible in the Biu star. When they are in the same mansion, it is called "he." If one star dominates another, it is a sign of conflict; if within seven cun (units), this will certainly occur.

## 51

When the five stars appear white and round, they portend mourning and drought; if red and round, their centers are uneven, indicating war; if blue-green and round, they signify sorrow and floods; if black and round, they indicate disease and many deaths; if yellow and round, it is an omen of good fortune. A red horn that invades our city signifies conflict; a yellow horn indicates disputes over land; a white horn brings the sound of weeping; a blue-green horn portends military worries; and a black horn foretells floods. Yi marks the end of where armies have exhausted their strength. When all five stars share the same color, weapons will be laid down across the land and the people will live in peace and prosperity. Spring winds and autumn rains, winter cold and summer heat—these movements are always governed by this pattern.

## 52

Tianxing travels east for 120 days before moving westward in retrograde motion; after traveling west for another 120 days, it turns back to the east. It remains visible for 330 days before disappearing, and reappears in the east after 30 days. When Taisu is at Jia Yin, Zhenxing is located at Dongbi, hence it appears in Yingshi.

## 53

Observe the movement of the sun to determine the position of Taibai. It is associated with the west, autumn, (the month governing military affairs and the movement to Tian Shi), corresponding to the days Geng and Xin; it presides over killing. When killing is misused or lost, punishment will emerge from Taibai. If Taibai deviates from its proper course, the state corresponding to its position will suffer a loss of life or calamity. When it travels outward, it moves through eighteen star positions over 240 days before returning. Upon entering the east, it remains hidden and moves through eleven star positions for 130 days; when entering the west, it stays concealed and passes through three star positions over sixteen days before emerging again. If it should emerge but does not, or if it should enter but does not, this is called "sheshed," indicating a disruption in its course. In such cases, there will either be the destruction of an army or a coup by a state ruler.

## 54

Its cycle begins at Shangyuan, in the year Shetige; it rises in the east with Ying Shi at dawn and sets after reaching Jiao. It also appears in the west at dusk with Ying Shi, setting after reaching Jiao; it rises in the east together with Jiao at dawn and sets in Bi; it appears in the west together with Jiao at dusk, also setting in Bi; it rises with Bi at dawn and sets in Ji; it appears with Bi in the west at dusk, setting in Ji as well; it rises together with Ji at dawn and sets in Liu; it appears with Ji in the west at dusk and also sets in Liu. It rises together with Liu at dawn and sets in Ying Shi; it appears with Liu in the west at dusk, also setting in Ying Shi. In total, it rises and sets from east to west five times each, completing a cycle of eight years and 220 days, after which it again rises in the east at dawn with Ying Shi. Its general pattern is to complete one full circuit around the sky each year. When it first emerges in the east, its movement is slow, averaging half a degree per day; after 120 days, Taibai must retrograde by one or two star positions; After reaching its highest point in the sky, it reverses course and moves eastward again at a rate of 1.5 degrees per day; after another 120 days it will set. When it is low in the sky and near the sun, it is called Mingxing, indicating a gentle nature; when high and far from the sun, it is known as Da Xiao, signifying rigidity. When it first appears in the west, its movement is swift, averaging one and a half degrees per day for 120 days; After reaching the highest point in its path, it slows down to half a degree per day for another 120 days. At dawn, when it sets, it must retrograde by one or two positions before disappearing. When low and near the sun, it is referred to as Dabai, indicating a soft nature; when high and distant from the sun, it is named Daxiang, signifying firmness. It rises in the hours of Chen and Xu, and sets in the hours of Chou and Wei.

## 55

If it should rise but does not rise, or if it rises before its time; if it should set but has already set, or sets later than expected—this signifies that the world will lay down arms. If troops are stationed abroad, they will return. If it rises before its due time or sets when it should not set—this indicates that the world will mobilize armies, and there will be a country destroyed. When it appears at the appointed time, the state will flourish. If it rises in the east, it is associated with the east; if it sets in the east, it signifies the north. If it rises in the west, it corresponds to the west; if it sets in the west, it indicates the south. Wherever it remains for a long time, that region will be prosperous. If it moves quickly or changes positions easily, that direction will bring misfortune.

## 56

When it rises in the west and retrogrades to the east, the country in the exact western direction will be auspicious. When it rises in the east and moves to the west, the country in the direct eastern direction will prosper. Its rising does not pass through the heavens; If it traverses the sky, a revolution in governance will occur throughout the world.

## 57

When small and its horn-like shape moves, war will arise. If it first appears large but later becomes small, the military power will be weak; if it initially appears small and then grows larger, the army will become strong. When it rises high in the sky, deep military campaigns bring good fortune, while shallow ones bring misfortune; when low, shallow military actions are auspicious, but deep ones are ominous. When the sun is in the south and Taibai is to its south, or when the sun is in the north and Taibai is to the north, it is called Ying. This indicates that marquises and kings will be restless; advancing with military action brings good fortune, while retreating brings misfortune. When the sun is in the south but Taibai resides to its north, or when the sun is to the north and Taibai to its south, it is called Suo. This signifies that marquises and kings have worries; retreating with military action brings good fortune while advancing brings misfortune. Military movements follow the pattern of Taibai: when Taibai moves swiftly, troops should advance quickly; when it moves slowly, they should proceed cautiously. When Taibai appears with a horn-like shape, the army dares to fight. If it wavers and moves restlessly, the troops will be in turmoil. If it appears round and still, the forces will remain calm. Following the direction indicated by its horn-like shape brings good fortune; going against it, all will be ominous. When it rises, troops are dispatched; when it sets, they return. If this lasts for the duration of a meal, there will be minor damage; if it lasts as long as it takes to cook five dou of rice, there will be major damage. Then the wind rises again, clouds appear, and the crops recover. At each respective time, use the color of clouds to determine what is suitable for planting. If there is rain, snow, or cold weather, it indicates a poor harvest year.

## 111

Grand Historian Tai Shigong said: Since the beginning of mankind, have rulers throughout history ever not observed the sun, moon, and stars? By the time of the Five Houses and Three Dynasties, they inherited and clarified these practices. Internally, they adorned themselves with official ranks; externally, they dealt with the Hu and Di (barbarian) peoples. They divided China into twelve states: looking upward, they observed celestial phenomena; looking downward, they followed patterns on earth. The heavens have the sun and moon; the earth has yin and yang. The heavens have Five Planets; the earth has Five Elements. The heavens have constellations arranged in order, and the earth is divided into states and regions. The Three Lights—the sun, moon, and stars—are the essence of yin and yang; their qi originates from the earth, yet sage rulers manage and regulate them.

## 112

Since the time of Youli and earlier, this has been long established. The celestial omens observed were all different according to states and regions, with each family interpreting portents of strange phenomena in their own way, attempting to align them with the times. Their written records, diagrams, and auspicious signs lacked any consistent method or law. Therefore, Confucius discussed the Six Classics, recording anomalies but not explaining them in writing. Regarding the principles of heavenly ways and fate, they were not transmitted; they were passed down to certain individuals without needing explanation; if told to the wrong person, even if spoken, it will not be understood.

## 113

In ancient times, those who transmitted knowledge of celestial phenomena: before Gaoxin, they were Zhong and Li; during the Tang and Yu dynasties, it was Fuxi and He; under the Xia dynasty, it was Kunwu; during the Yin-Shang dynasty, it was Wuxian; under the Zhou dynasty, it was Shi Yi and Chang Hong; in Song state, it was Zi Wei; in Zheng state, it was Bi Zhao; in Qi state, it was Gan Gong; in Chu state, it was Tang Mei; in Zhao state, it was Yin Gao; in Wei state, it was Shi Shen.

## 114

The movement of heaven undergoes a minor change every thirty years, a moderate change every hundred years, and a major change every five hundred years; three major changes make one ji (cycle), and three ji complete the grand cycle: this is its general pattern. A ruler of a state must value the numbers Three and Five. With one thousand years above and below, only then will the connection between heaven and humanity be fully prepared.

## 115

Grand Historian Tai Shigong examined ancient celestial changes but found none that could be verified in modern times. Generally speaking, within the 242 years of the Spring and Autumn period, there were thirty-six solar eclipses, three appearances of comets, and during Duke Xiang of Song's time, stars fell like rain. The Son of Heaven was weak, and feudal lords wielded political power; the Five Hegemons rose in succession, each taking turns to issue commands. Since then, the many have oppressed the few, and the strong have annexed the small. Qin, Chu, Wu, and Yue were originally Hu and Di peoples but became powerful hegemons. The Tian clan usurped Qi, the Three Houses divided Jin, and together they formed the Warring States. They competed in waging war and seizing territory, with military campaigns arising repeatedly. Cities were frequently razed, leading to famine, plague, and hardship. Both subjects and rulers shared in the suffering; therefore, they especially urgently observed omens, celestial phenomena, and atmospheric signs. In recent times, among the twelve feudal lords and seven warring states, those who claimed kingship spoke of vertical or horizontal alliances one after another. Therefore, Yin Gao, Tang Mei, Gan Gong, and Shi Shen composed their writings in accordance with contemporary affairs; thus, their predictions and verifications became a mixture of trivial details.

## 116

The Twenty-Eight Mansions govern the twelve states, with the Dou constellation overseeing them collectively; this system has been in place for a long time. When Qin was powerful, its omens were observed in Taibai (the planet Venus), and predictions were made based on Lang and Hu constellations. When Wu and Chu were powerful, their omens were observed in Yinghuo (the planet Mars), and predictions were made based on the Niao Heng constellation. When Yan and Qi were powerful, their omens lay in Chenxing (the planet Mercury), with predictions made based on the Xu and Wei constellations. When Song and Zheng were powerful, their omens resided in Suixing (the planet Jupiter), with predictions based on the Fang and Xin constellations. When Jin was powerful, its omens also lay in Chenxing, with predictions made according to the Shen Fa constellation.

## 117

And when Qin annexed the Three Jin states, Yan, and Dai, all lands south of the Yellow River and Taihang Mountains became part of China. Within the Four Seas, China lies in the southeast, which is yang; yang corresponds to the sun, Suixing (Jupiter), Yinghuo (Mars), and Tianxing (Saturn); predictions are made in the southern street, with Bi constellation overseeing it. To its northwest were the Hu, He, and Yuezhi peoples who wore felt and furs and used bows—these were considered yin; yin corresponds to the moon, Taibai (Venus), and Chenxing (Mercury); predictions are made in the northern street, with Mao constellation overseeing it. Therefore, China's rivers and mountains flow northeastward; its axis begins in Long and Shu, ending at Bo and Jie. This is why the states of Qin and Jin often used military force and frequently observed Taibai (Venus). Taibai governs China; while the Hu and He peoples frequently raided, they only observed Chenxing (Mercury). Chenxing moves in and out quickly, often governing the Hu and Di: this is its fundamental principle. This distinction determines who becomes the guest and who remains the host. When Yinghuo (Mars) appears as a comet, externally it signifies military affairs, internally it signifies governance. Hence it is said: "Even with an enlightened Son of Heaven, one must always observe where Yinghuo (Mars) is located." As feudal lords alternately grew powerful, various disasters and strange occurrences were recorded at different times, but none are worth recording.

## 118

During the reign of Qin Shi Huang, comets appeared four times over fifteen years; some lasted as long as eighty days, with their tails stretching across the entire sky. Afterward, the Qin dynasty used military force to eliminate the six kings and unify China. They repelled the four Hu and Di peoples outwardly; people died as if cut down like tangled hemp. Thus, Zhang Er and the Chu forces rose together, and for thirty years, armies trampled over each other in battle, too numerous to count. Since Chiyou's time, there has never been a war like this.

## 119

Xiang Yu rescued Ju Lu, and curved arrows flew westward; the eastern mountain regions then united feudal lords in a vertical alliance to attack westward, where they slaughtered Qin people and razed Xianyang.

## 120

When the Han dynasty rose to power, the Five Planets gathered at the constellation Dongjing. During the siege of Pingcheng, a lunar halo surrounded the constellations Shen and Bi seven times over. When the Lu clan plotted rebellion, there was a solar eclipse, and daylight turned dark. During the rebellion of Wu, Chu, and seven other states, comets appeared several zhang long, and a heavenly dog passed over the Liang region. And when warfare broke out, corpses lay piled up and blood flowed beneath it. During Yuanguang and Yuanshou reigns, the Chiyou's banner appeared twice; its length reached half the sky. Afterward, for several decades, imperial armies marched out four times to punish and subdue the Hu and Di peoples, with campaigns against the Hu being especially intense. When Yue fell, Mars remained in the constellation Dou. When Chaoxian was captured, a star appeared as an omen at He Shu along the river. When troops campaigned against Dayuan, a star omen appeared in Zhaoyao: these are among the most prominent examples. As for more subtle and minor changes, they are too numerous to recount. From this we can see that there has been no instance where an event did not first manifest in celestial omens before its earthly response followed.

## 121

Among those who studied the heavenly patterns for the Han dynasty, Tang Du was responsible for stars, Wang Shuo for atmospheric phenomena, and Wei Xian for divining the years. Therefore, in the Gan-Shi calendar's Five Planets method, only Mars exhibited retrograde motion; the positions it occupied during its retrogression, as well as the retrogressions of other planets and solar or lunar eclipses, were all used for divination.

## 122

I have examined the Records of the Grand Historian and verified past events, and within a hundred years, none of the Five Planets moved outward without retrograding; whenever they did retrograde, their brightness often increased and their colors changed. Solar and lunar eclipses occurred at times when the sun and moon moved north or south: this was their general pattern. Therefore, the Purple Palace, Fangxin, Quan Heng, Xuchi, Xu Wei, and other constellations that form the stellar divisions are the five official seats of Heaven. They serve as fixed reference points, unchanging in position; they differ only in size, with widths and distances remaining constant. Water, Fire, Metal, Wood, and the Filling Star—these Five Planets are Heaven's five assistants. They serve as the [meridional] weft of the celestial order; their appearances and disappearances occur at set times, and their movements along their paths involve expansions and contractions within fixed limits.

## 123

When omens appear in the sun, cultivate virtue; when they appear in the moon, examine punishments; when they appear among stars, seek harmony. In general, celestial changes are only taken as omens if they exceed normal limits. When a state's ruler is powerful and virtuous, the state prospers; when weak and deceitful, it perishes. The highest priority is to cultivate virtue; next comes improving governance; then comes remedial action after an event occurs; lastly, one resorts to rituals for warding off disaster. The lowest level has none of these. Changes in the fixed stars are rarely observed, while omens related to the three lights—the sun, moon, and planets—are frequently used for divination. Lunar halos, solar halos, clouds, and winds are transient atmospheric phenomena of Heaven; their occurrences also follow grand cycles. Yet their rise and fall in relation to political affairs is the closest manifestation of the symbolic connection between Heaven and humanity. These five phenomena are signs of Heaven's response or reaction. Those who study the patterns of Heaven must understand the principles of three and five. lords By examining changes through past and present, thoroughly observing temporal transformations, and discerning their subtleties and coarseness, the discipline of celestial phenomena is complete.

## 124

When the Green Emperor practices virtue, Heaven's gate opens for him. When the Red Emperor practices virtue, Heaven's prison becomes empty. When the Yellow Emperor practices virtue, the heavenly calamities rise in response. Winds coming from the northwest must occur on Geng or Xin days. lodash In one autumn, if this wind arrives five times, a general pardon will be issued; three arrivals mean a minor pardon. When the White Emperor practices virtue, on the twentieth or twenty-first day of the first lunar month, if a lunar halo forms around the moon and encircles it, this is often followed by a general pardon. This is said to signify the presence of the great sun. One record says: When the White Emperor practices virtue, the constellations Bi and Mao form a surrounding pattern around it. If this encircling lasts for three evenings, then virtue is fulfilled; if it does not last three evenings or the surrounding pattern is incomplete, virtue remains unfulfilled. Secondly: If a celestial formation encircles at the hour of Chen (7–9 PM), it will not extend beyond its ten-day cycle. When the Black Emperor practices virtue, Heaven's Gate stirs in response. When Heaven itself manifests virtue, the Son of Heaven will change the era name; if virtue is lacking, winds and rains will shatter stone. The Three Powers and the Three Balances are the heavenly court. When a guest star appears in the heavenly court, there will be an unusual decree.
