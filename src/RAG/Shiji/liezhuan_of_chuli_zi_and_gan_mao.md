# Liezhuan of Chuli Zi and Gan Mao

## 1

Chuli Zi was named Ji, a younger brother of King Hui of Qin, and they were not full siblings. His mother was a woman from the state of Han. Chuli Zi was witty and resourceful, and the people of Qin nicknamed him "The Brain."

## 2

In the eighth year of King Hui's reign, Ji was granted the title Yougeng and appointed to lead an expedition against Quwo. He expelled all its inhabitants, captured the city, and annexed the territory for Qin. In the twenty-fifth year of King Hui's reign (157 BC), Chuli Zi was appointed as general to attack Zhao, captured General Zhuang Bao of Zhao, and seized Lin. The following year, he assisted Wei Zhang in attacking the state of Chu, defeating General Qu Gai of Chu and seizing the Hanzhong region. Qin granted Chuli Zi a fiefdom and bestowed upon him the title Yan Jun.

## 3

After King Hui died, Crown Prince Wuwang ascended the throne and expelled Zhang Yi and Wei Zhang. He appointed Chuli Zi and Gan Mao as his left and right chancellors respectively. Qin sent Gan Mao to attack Han, and he captured Yiyang. They also dispatched Chuli Zi with a hundred chariots into the Zhou state. The Zhou forces met him with soldiers, showing great respect. King of Chu was angry and rebuked the Zhou state for giving too much importance to Qin's envoy. You Teng, on behalf of Zhou, advised King of Chu: "When Zhibo attacked Chouyou, he gave them large chariots as a gift and then followed up with troops. As a result, the state of Chouyou was destroyed." Why did that happen? It happened because they were unprepared. Duke Huan of Qi attacked Cai, claiming it was to punish Chu, but in reality he launched a surprise attack on Cai. Now, Qin is a country of tigers and wolves. They sent Chuli Zi with one hundred chariots into Zhou, so Zhou observed the fate of Chouyou and Cai and took precautions. Thus, they placed long halberds in front and strong crossbows behind, calling it an escort for Ji but actually detaining him. Moreover, could Zhou possibly be without concern for its state? They fear that the loss of their country would cause distress to Your Majesty." King of Chu was then pleased.

## 4

After King Wu of Qin died, King Zhao ascended the throne, and Chuli Zi gained even greater respect and authority.

## 5

In the first year of King Zhao's reign (306 BC), Chuli Zi led an expedition to attack Pu. The governor of Pu was afraid and invited Hu Yan for assistance. Hu Yan, on behalf of Pu, said to Chuli Zi: "Is your attack on Pu for the benefit of Qin?" Or is it for Wei? If it is for Wei, that would be good; but if it is for Qin, then you will not gain the trust of others. The reason why Wei exists as a state is because of Pu. If you now attack Pu and it falls to Wei, the state of Wei will certainly break away and follow them. The reason why Wei has lost the area beyond the Xihe River and cannot recover it is because its military strength is weak. If you now unite Wei with Hui, Wei will surely become powerful. On the day when Wei becomes strong, the area beyond the Xihe will certainly be in danger. Moreover, King of Qin will observe your actions. If you harm Qin and benefit Wei, he will surely hold you accountable." Chuli Zi said: "Then what should I do?" Hu Yan said, "You should abandon the attack on Pu. Let me try to go in and speak for you, so that you may gain favor with the ruler of Wei." Chuli Zi said: "That is good." Hu Yan entered Pu and told its governor, "Chuli Zi knows that Pu is in trouble. He said the city will certainly fall." "I can make him abandon the attack on your city." The governor of Pu, fearing this, bowed twice and said: "I would be grateful if you could arrange it for me." He then presented three hundred jin of gold and said, "If the Qin army withdraws, I will certainly recommend you to the ruler of Wei, so that you can become a lord ruling in his own fief." Thus, Hu Yan accepted gold from Pu to elevate himself at court in Wei. Therefore, Chuli Zi lifted the siege of Pu and withdrew. He then attacked Pi Shi, but before it surrendered, he left again.

## 6

In the seventh year of King Zhao's reign , Chuli Zi died and was buried east of Zhangtai in Weinan. He said: "After a hundred years, there will be an imperial palace on either side of my tomb." Chuli Zi Ji's residence was located west of King Zhao's temple in Yin Xiang, Weinan, at Chuli. Hence, the common people referred to it as "Chuli Zi." When the Han dynasty rose to power, Changle Palace was built to its east and Weiyang Palace to its west, with the Wu Ku (Munitions Warehouse) directly aligned over his tomb. A popular saying among the people of Qin was: "Strength belongs to Ren Bi, and wisdom to Chuli."

## 7

Gan Mao was a native of Xiacai. He studied under Master Shi Ju of Xiacai, learning the doctrines of all schools of thought. Through Zhang Yi and Chuli Zi, he sought an audience with King Hui of Qin. The king received him and was pleased, appointing him as a general to assist Wei Zhang in pacifying the region of Hanzhong.

## 8

After King Hui died, King Wu ascended the throne. Zhang Yi and Wei Zhang left for the east to Wei. Shu Hou Hui and his prime minister Zhuang rebelled, so Qin sent Gan Mao to pacify Shu. After returning, he appointed Gan Mao as the Left Chancellor and Chuli Zi as the Right Chancellor.

## 9

In the third year of King Wu's reign (308 BC), he said to Gan Mao: "I wish to have a chariot pass through the Three Rivers region, so that I may observe the Zhou royal house. If I can do this, my name will live on forever." Gan Mao said: "Please allow me to go to Wei and make an agreement to attack Han, while I have Xiang Shou accompany me as my assistant." Gan Mao arrived and said to Xiang Shou: "You should return and tell the king, 'Wei has agreed with my proposal, but I hope Your Majesty will not attack Han.'" "If the matter is successful, all of it will be credited to you." Xiang Shou returned and reported this to the king. The king then met Gan Mao at Xirang. When Gan Mao arrived, the king asked him why he had acted that way. Gan Mao replied: "Yiyang is a large county, and it has been long contested by Shangdang and Nanyang." "It is called a xian (county), but in reality it functions as a jun (commandery)." "Now, Your Majesty wishes to cross multiple perilous passes and march over a thousand li to attack it. This will be difficult." "In the past, when Zeng Shen was in Fei, a man from Lu who shared his name and surname killed someone. Someone told Zeng Shen's mother: 'Zeng Shen has killed a person,' but she continued weaving as if nothing had happened." "After some time, another person came and told her again: 'Zeng Shen has killed someone,' but his mother still continued weaving as before." "Shortly after, a third person came and said the same thing. His mother then threw down her shuttle, jumped off the loom, and ran over the wall." "Even with Zeng Shen's virtue and his mother's trust in him, when three people cast doubt upon it, she became fearful." "Now, my virtue is not as great as Zeng Shen's, and Your Majesty's trust in me does not equal the trust his mother had in him. Those who doubt me are not just three people; I fear that Your Majesty may also cast aside your trust in me." "When Zhang Yi first expanded westward to incorporate the lands of Ba and Shu, opened up territory beyond the Xihe River in the north, and captured Shangyong in the south, the world did not criticize Zhang Zi but praised our late king for his wisdom." Duke Wen of Wei ordered Yue Yang to lead an army in attacking Zhongshan, and after three years the city was captured. When Yue Yang returned to claim credit for his achievements, Duke Wen of Wei showed him a box full of defamatory letters about him. Yue Yang kowtowed twice and said: "This is not my achievement, but the result of Your Majesty's support." "Now I am merely a foreign retainer. "If the two men, Chuli Zi and Gongsun Shi, use Han to argue against me, Your Majesty will certainly listen. That would mean deceiving King of Wei while I suffer resentment from Gong Zhong Chi." The king said: "I will not listen to them. Please join me in an oath." He finally dispatched Chancellor Gan Mao with troops to attack Yiyang. After five months the city had still not fallen, and as expected, Chuli Zi and Gongsong Shi began to argue against it. King Wu summoned Gan Mao and wanted to withdraw the troops. Gan Mao said: "The oath was made at Xirang." The king replied, "Yes, it is true." Therefore, he mobilized all the troops and sent Gan Mao to launch an attack. They beheaded six thousand enemy soldiers and finally captured Yiyang. King Xiang of Han sent Gong Zhong Chi to apologize, seeking peace with Qin.

## 10

King Wu eventually reached Zhou and died there. His younger brother ascended the throne as King Zhao. King Zhao's mother, Xuan Taichou, was a woman from Chu. King Huai of Chu resented the previous Qin defeat at Dan Yang and Han's failure to come to his aid, so he led an army to besiege Yongshi in Han. Han sent Gong Zhong Chi as envoy to urgently request assistance from Qin. King Zhao of Qin had just ascended the throne, and since his mother, Xuan Taichou was a native of Chu, she refused to send aid. Gong Zhong Chi therefore approached Gan Mao, who spoke on behalf of Han to King Zhao of Qin and said: "Gong Zhong Chi dares to resist Chu because he is expecting rescue from Qin." "Now that Yongshi is under siege and the Qin army does not descend to Xiao, Gong Zhong Chi will look up in despair and refuse to pay homage, while Gongsu will ally Han with Chu." "If Chu and Han unite, Wei will not dare to disobey. In that case, a coalition against Qin will be formed." "Which is more advantageous: waiting passively for an attack or launching the attack yourself?" King Zhao of Qin said, "Well spoken." He then sent troops to Xiao to rescue Han. The Chu forces withdrew.

## 11

Qin sent Xiang Shou to pacify Yiyang, and dispatched Chuli Zi and Gan Mao to attack Pi Shi in Wei. Xiang Shou was a relative of Xuan Taichou by marriage, and had grown up with King Zhao since childhood; therefore, he was entrusted with important duties. Xiang Shou went to Chu, where the court heard of Qin's high regard for Xiang Shou and treated him with great courtesy. Xiang Shou was serving as the Qin garrison commander in Yiyang, preparing to launch an attack on Han. Gong Zhong Chi of Han sent Su Dai to tell Xiang Shou, "A cornered bird will crash into a cart." "If you conquer Han and humiliate Gong Zhong Chi, he will recover his state and resume service to Qin. He would believe that he is certain to be granted a fief." "Now you have given the land of Jiekou to Chu and bestowed Du Yang upon its minor Ling Yin." "With Qin and Chu united, they will attack Han again. Han is bound to fall." "If Han falls, Gong Zhong Chi would personally lead his private followers to resist Qin." "I hope you will carefully consider this matter," said he. Xiang Shou replied, "My alliance of Qin and Chu is not aimed at Han. You go to Gong Zhong Chi on my behalf and tell him that the relations between Qin and Han can be reconciled." Su Dai replied, "I would like to make a proposal to you. "People value what makes them valuable." "The King's affection and trust in you are not as great as those he has for Gongsun Shi; "his wisdom and ability to manage affairs, they are not comparable to Gan Mao." "Now these two men have both been excluded from involvement in Qin's affairs, yet you alone are the one who makes decisions for the state with the King. Why is that?" "They lost it because they had reasons to do so." "Gongsun Shi was partial to Han, and Gan Mao favored Wei; therefore, the King did not trust them." "Now that Qin and Chu are vying for dominance, yet you side with Chu, this makes you no different from Gongsun Shi and Gan Mao. In what way are you distinct?" "People all say that Chu is fickle, yet you insist on relying on it; this would be holding yourself responsible for failure." "You are better off discussing with the King about how to prepare for any changes, cultivating good relations with Han and guarding against Chu. In this way, there will be no danger." "The House of Han would certainly first submit its state to Gongsun Shi before entrusting it to Gan Mao." "Han is your enemy." "Now you suggest cultivating good relations with Han to guard against Chu; this means recommending an external ally without avoiding a former enemy," said he. Xiang Shou said, "That is true. I very much want Han to cooperate." He replied, "Gan Mao promised Gong Zhong Chi the territory of Wusui and returned the people from Yiyang. Now you want to simply take it back; that will be very difficult." Xiang Shou asked, "Then what should we do?" "Is Wusui ultimately unattainable then?" He replied, "Why don't you have Qin request Yingchuan for Han from Chu?" "This is a territory entrusted to Han." "If you request it and obtain it, this would demonstrate Qin's authority over Chu and allow us to bestow the land as a favor upon Han." "If you request it but fail to obtain it, then the enmity between Han and Chu will remain unresolved, and both will turn toward Qin." "With Qin and Chu vying for dominance, you can proceed slowly through Chu to gain Han's submission. This is advantageous for Qin." Xiang Shou asked, "How should I do this?" He replied, "This is a good plan. Gan Mao wanted to use Wei to attack Qi, while Gongsun Shi wanted to use Han for the same purpose. "Now you capture Yiyang to establish your achievements, secure the alliance with Chu and Han for stability, and then hold Qi and Wei accountable for their offenses. In doing so, Gongsun Shi and Gan Mao will have no further role."

## 12

Gan Mao eventually persuaded King Zhao of Qin to return Wusui back to Han. Xiang Shou and Gongsun Shi argued against it, but failed to change the decision. Because of this, Xiang Shou and Gongsun Ji harbored resentment and slandered Gan Mao. Fearing for his safety, Gan Mao halted the attack on Puzhan in Wei and fled away. Chuli Zi concluded a peace agreement with Wei, ending hostilities.

## 13

When Gan Mao fled from Qin to Qi, he encountered Su Dai along the way. Su Dai was serving as an envoy of Qi in Qin at that time. Gan Mao said, "I have offended the state of Qin and am now fleeing out of fear, with nowhere to hide my tracks. "I have heard of a poor woman and a rich woman meeting to spin thread. The poor woman said, 'I cannot afford to buy candles, but your candlelight is fortunate enough to be abundant. You could share some of the remaining light with me; it would not diminish your brightness while providing me convenience.'" "Now I am in a difficult situation, and you are just now serving as Qi's envoy to Qin and hold an influential position." "My wife and children are there. I hope you will use your influence to assist them," said he. Su Dai agreed. He then proceeded to serve as an envoy in Qin. Afterward, he advised King Zhao of Qin, saying, "Gan Mao is no ordinary man. "He has held a position in Qin for generations and holds great influence." He is fully aware of the terrain, from Xiaosai all the way to Guigu. The dangerous and easy parts are all known to him. "He has used Qi's alliance with Han and Wei to turn against Qin, which is not in Qin's interest," said he. King Zhao of Qin asked, "Then what should be done?" Su Dai said, "Your Majesty would do better to honor him with generous gifts and a substantial salary to invite him back. If he comes, place him in Guigu Valley and never let him out for the rest of his life." King Zhao of Qin said, "Well spoken." He immediately bestowed upon him the title of Shangqing and sent the chancellor's seal to welcome him in Qi. Gan Mao did not go. Su Dai told King Min of Qi, "Gan Mao is a man of virtue and talent. "Now Qin has bestowed upon him the rank of Shangqing and sent its chancellor's seal to invite him." "Gan Mao is grateful for Your Majesty's favor, and wishes to serve as your subject. Therefore, he declined the invitation and did not go." "Now, how should Your Majesty honor him?" King Min of Qi said, "Well spoken." He immediately appointed him to the rank of Shangqing. Qin then restored Gan Mao's family and allowed them to conduct business in Qi.

## 14

Qi sent Gan Mao as an envoy to Chu, but King Huai of Chu had just formed a marital alliance with Qin and was in good relations. When Qin learned that Gan Mao was in Chu, it sent someone to tell the King of Chu, "We wish to send Gan Mao back to Qin." King Huai asked Fan Juan, "I want to appoint a chancellor in Qin. Who would be suitable?" Fan Juan replied, "I am not capable of identifying the right person." King Huai said, "Would it be acceptable for me to appoint Gan Mao as chancellor?" Fan Juan replied, "No." "Shi Ju was a gatekeeper in Xiacao. He did not serve his lord with great loyalty, nor did he care for his family; instead, he became notorious for being cheap and corrupt. Gan Mao served him faithfully." "Therefore, despite King Hui's wisdom and King Wu's discernment, as well as Zhang Yi's eloquence, Gan Mao served them all and rose to ten official positions without committing any crime." "Gan Mao is indeed a virtuous man, but he cannot be appointed as chancellor in Qin." "If Qin has a capable chancellor, it would not be beneficial to the State of Chu." "Moreover, Your Majesty once employed Zhao Hua in Yue, but while he was there, the internal troubles of Zhang Yi arose. Yue became chaotic, so Chu had to block its southern border at Limen and establish a commandery over Jiangdong." The reason Your Majesty's achievements could reach such an extent is because Yue was in chaos while Chu remained orderly. "Now, if Your Majesty knows how to use the people of Yue but forgets about those from Qin, I consider this a grave mistake on your part." "If so, then if Your Majesty wishes to appoint someone as chancellor in Qin, no one is more suitable than Xiang Shou." "Xiang Shou has a close relationship with King Zhao of Qin. When they were young, they shared the same clothes; as adults, they rode in the same carriage and made decisions together." "If Your Majesty appoints Xiang Shou as chancellor in Qin, it will be beneficial for the State of Chu," said he. Thus, King Huai sent envoys to request from Qin that Xiang Shou be appointed as chancellor. Qin finally appointed Xiang Shou as its chancellor. Gan Mao, however, was never allowed to return to Qin and eventually died in Wei.

## 15

Gan Mao had a grandson named Gan Luo.

## 16

Gan Luo was the grandson of Gan Mao. After Gan Mao's death, when Gan Luo was twelve years old, he served Lord Wenxin Hou Lü Buwei, the chancellor of Qin.

## 17

The First Emperor of Qin sent Gangcheng Jun Cai Ze to Yan. Three years later, King Xi of Yan sent his crown prince Dan as a hostage to Qin. Qin dispatched Zhang Tang to serve as chancellor in Yan, intending to ally with Yan and jointly attack Zhao in order to expand the territory between the Yellow River. Zhang Tang told Lord Wenxin Hou, "I once served King Zhao of Qin in an attack on Zhao. Zhao resents me and has declared, 'Whoever captures Zhang Tang will be granted a territory of one hundred li.'" "Now to travel to Yan I must pass through Zhao; therefore, I cannot go." Lord Wenxin Hou was displeased but had no way to compel Zhang Tang. Gan Luo asked, "My lord, why are you so unhappy?" Lord Wenxin Hou said, "I had Lord Gangcheng Jun Cai Ze deal with Yan for three years, and Yan's Crown Prince Dan has already come as a hostage. I personally requested Chancellor Zhang to serve in Yan, but he refused to go." Gan Luo said, "I will go instead." Lord Wenxin Hou scolded him and said, "Go away!" "I personally requested it and he still refused. How could you possibly succeed?" Gan Luo replied, "The great Shang Tu was seven years old when he became a teacher of Confucius." "Now I am twelve years old. My lord, please test me; why do you rush to scold me?" Thus, Gan Luo met with Chancellor Zhang and said, "Whose achievements are greater, yours or those of General Wu'an Jun?" Zhang replied, "General Wu'an Jun defeated the powerful state of Chu in the south and imposed his will on Yan and Zhao to the north. He won battles, captured cities, and destroyed walled towns too numerous to count; my achievements cannot compare with his." Gan Luo said, "When Lord Yinghou served Qin, whose authority was greater—his or that of Lord Wenxin Hou?" Chancellor Zhang replied, "Lord Yinghou did not have as much authority as Lord Wenxin Hou." Gan Luo said, "Do you know for certain that he was less powerful than Lord Wenxin Hou?" Zhang replied, "I do." Gan Luo said, "Lord Yinghou once wished to attack Zhao, but General Wu'an Jun opposed him. Lord Yinghou had him executed just seven li outside Xianyang at Duyou." "Now, Lord Wenxin Hou has personally requested you to serve as chancellor in Yan but you refuse. I do not know where your place of death will be." Zhang Tang said, "Then please allow me to go with this young man." He ordered preparations for the journey.

## 18

After a date was set for departure, Gan Luo told Lord Wenxin Hou, "Please lend me five carriages. I will go ahead and report to Zhao on behalf of Zhang Tang." Lord Wenxin Hou then entered and reported to the First Emperor, "Long ago, Gan Mao's grandson, Gan Luo, is still young, but as a descendant of a famous family, he is well known among the feudal lords. Now Zhang Tang claimed illness and refused to go, but Gan Luo persuaded him until he agreed." "Now I wish for him to report first to Zhao. Please allow me to send him on his way." The First Emperor summoned Gan Luo and sent him to Zhao. King Xiang of Zhao personally met Gan Luo at the outskirts in welcome. Gan Luo addressed King Zhao and said, "Have you heard that Crown Prince Dan of Yan has been sent as a hostage to Qin?" The king replied, "Yes, I have." Gan Luo said, "Have you heard that Zhang Tang is to serve as chancellor of Yan?" The king replied, "Yes, I have." "The fact that Crown Prince Dan of Yan entered Qin means that Yan is not deceiving Qin. "Zhang Tang serving as chancellor in Yan indicates that Qin is not deceiving Yan. "If Yan and Qin do not deceive each other, then an attack on Zhao will be imminent, which would place you in grave danger." "There is no other reason for Yan and Qin not deceiving each other; they intend to attack Zhao and expand the territory between the rivers. "Your Majesty would be better off giving me five cities to expand the river region, and I will request that Crown Prince Dan of Yan return. Then we can ally with strong Zhao to attack weak Yan." King Zhao immediately ceded five cities himself in order to expand the river region. Qin returned Crown Prince Dan of Yan. Zhao attacked Yan and captured thirty cities in Shanggu, granting eleven of them to Qin.

## 19

Gan Luo returned to report to Qin, and was then enfeoffed as an Upper Chancellor (Shangqing). The fields and houses originally belonging to Gan Mao were also granted to him.

## 20

Grand Historian said: "Chuli Zi was favored due to his blood relation, which is natural. The people of Qin praised his wisdom; therefore, I have included some accounts about him." Gan Mao rose from a humble family in Xiapi to achieve fame among the feudal lords, gaining great influence over powerful states such as Qi and Chu. Gan Luo was young, yet he devised a brilliant strategy that earned him lasting fame in later generations. Although not an upright gentleman of solid virtue, he was also a strategist during the Warring States period. At the time when Qin was at its height of power, the world especially favored schemes and deceit.
