# Annals of Zhou

## 1

Zhou Houji, courtesy name Qi. His mother was a daughter of the You Tai clan, named Jiang Yuan. Jiang Yuan was the principal wife of Emperor Ku. Jiang Yuan went out into the fields and saw a giant's footprint; her heart was delighted, and she wished to step on it. When she stepped on it, her body moved as if pregnant. After a full term, she gave birth to a child; thinking it an ill omen, she abandoned him in a narrow alley. Horses and cattle passing by all turned aside and did not trample on him; she moved him to place him in the forest, but at that time there happened to be many people in the mountains and woods, so she relocated him again; she then abandoned him on ice in a ditch, but birds flew over with their wings to cover and protect him. Jiang Yuan considered this to be divine, so she took him back, raised him, and he grew up. Initially wanting to abandon him, they named him Qi.

## 2

When he was a child, Qi stood tall with the aspirations of a giant. His games involved planting flax and beans; the flax and beans were beautiful. When he became an adult, he took up farming, observing the suitability of the land. Where it was suitable for grain, he cultivated and harvested crops; the people all followed his example. Emperor Yao heard of this and appointed Qi as chief agricultural officer. The world benefited from his work, and he achieved great success. Emperor Shun said: "Qi, when the people first suffered hunger, you, Houji, sowed the hundred grains in their season." He was enfeoffed at Tai and given the title Houji; he was distinguished by the surname Ji. Houji's rise to prominence occurred during the time of Taotang, Yu, and Xia; in each era he displayed noble virtue.

## 3

After Houji died, his son Bu Zhu succeeded him. In the later years of Bu Zhu's life, the rule of the Xia Hou clan declined; they abandoned agriculture and neglected it. As a result, Bu Zhu lost his official position and fled between Rong and Di territories. After Bu Zhu died, his son Ju succeeded him. After Ju died, his son Gong Liu succeeded him. Although Gong Liu was living among the Rong and Di, he revived Houji's endeavors, focusing on farming and cultivating land suited to its nature. He crossed from Qi and Ju rivers to Wei River, obtaining materials for use; travelers had provisions, residents had stored goods, and the people relied on his blessings. The common people cherished him, so many migrated to take refuge under his protection. The rise of the Zhou way began from this point; therefore, poets sang and rejoiced in thinking about his virtue. After Gong Liu died, his son Qingjie succeeded him, establishing the state at Bin.

## 4

After Qingjie died, his son Huangpu succeeded him. After Huangpu died, his son Chafu succeeded him. After Chafu died, his son Huishu succeeded him. After Huishu died, his son Gongfei succeeded him. After Gongfei died, his son Gaoyu succeeded him. After Gaoyu died, his son Yayu succeeded him. After Yayu died, his son Gongsushu Leilei succeeded him. After Gongsushu Leilei died, his son Gu Gong Danfu succeeded him. Gu Gong Danfu revived the endeavors of Houji and Gong Liu, accumulating virtue and practicing righteousness; all the people of the state revered him. The Xunyu Rong and Di attacked them, seeking wealth and goods, which were given to them. When they attacked again, wanting land and people. The people were all angry and wished to fight. Gu Gong said: "A ruler is established for the people; it is to benefit them." "Now, what the Rong and Di do in attacking us is because of my land and people. Whether the people are with me or with them makes no difference." "The people wish to fight because of me, killing fathers and sons so they may be ruled by another; I cannot bear to do this." Thus he left Bin with his private followers, crossed the Qi and Ju rivers, passed over Liangshan Mountain, and settled at Qixia. The people of Bin carried their elderly on their backs and led the young with them, all returning to Gu Gong at Qixia as a whole nation. Other neighboring states hearing of Gu Gong's benevolence also returned in large numbers to him. Thus, Gu Gong abandoned the customs of the Rong and Di and began building city walls, houses, and established separate settlements. He appointed five officials with their respective duties. The people all sang and rejoiced, praising his virtue.

## 5

Gu Gong had an eldest son named Taibo, the second was Yuzhong. Taijiang bore a younger son Jili; Jili married Tairen, both of whom were virtuous women. They gave birth to Chang, who was born with signs of sainthood and auspicious omens. Gu Gong said: "There should be one in my lineage destined to rise, perhaps it is Chang?" The eldest son Taibo and Yuzhong knew that Gu Gong wanted to establish Jili as heir in order to pass on the succession to Chang, so the two of them fled to Jingman, tattooed their bodies, cut off their hair, and thus yielded the position to Jili.

## 6

After Gu Gong died, Jili succeeded him; he was known as Gong Ji. Gong Ji followed the legacy of Gu Gong, being earnest in practicing righteousness, and the feudal lords obeyed him.

## 7

After Gong Ji died, his son Chang succeeded him; he was called Xibo. Xibo, known as King Wen, followed the endeavors of Houji and Gong Lü, adhered to the laws of Gu Gong and Gong Ji, was earnest in benevolence, reverent toward elders, and kind to the young. He treated those of virtue with courtesy below him; he was so busy receiving scholars at midday that he had no time to eat, and many scholars thus returned to his side. Bo Yi and Shu Qi were in Gu Zhu; hearing that Xibo was good at caring for the elderly, they thought it best to go and submit to him. Tai Dian, Hong Yao, Sanyi Sheng, Yu Zi, and the official Xinjia were among those who went to submit to him.

## 8

Chong Hou Hu slandered Xibo to Yin Zhou, saying: "Xibo has accumulated virtue and good deeds; the feudal lords all look up to him. This will be detrimental to Your Majesty." King Zhou then imprisoned Xibo at Youli. Hong Yao and his followers were worried about this. 𝑙 They then sought a beautiful woman from the You Shen clan, fine horses from Li Rong, nine teams of horses from You Xiong, and other rare and strange treasures, which they presented to Zhou through his favored minister Fei Zhong. King Zhou was greatly pleased and said: "Even one of these items would be enough to release Xibo; how much more so with so many!" He then released Xibo and bestowed on him bows, arrows, axes, and halberds, allowing Xibo the right to conduct military campaigns. Saying: "The one who slandered Xibo was Chong Hou Hu." Xibo then offered the western territory of Luo in exchange for Zhou's removal of the punishment of cauldron roasting. King Zhou agreed to this.

## 9

Xibo secretly practiced virtue, and feudal lords all came for him to adjudicate disputes. At this time, people from Yu and Rui who had unresolved legal cases went to Zhou. Upon entering the territory, they saw farmers all yielding their field boundaries; the local customs emphasized yielding to elders. The people of Yu and Rui had not yet met Xibo, but they were already ashamed. They said to each other: "What we are disputing is considered a disgrace by the people of Zhou; why should we go further? We would only bring shame upon ourselves." They then returned, both yielding and leaving. The feudal lords hearing of this said: "Xibo must be the one who has received the Mandate of Heaven."

## 10

The following year, he attacked Quan Rong. The next year, he attacked Mixu. The following year, he defeated the state of Qi. Zu Yi of Yin heard about this and was frightened; he reported it to King Zhou. King Zhou said: "Is there not the Mandate of Heaven?" "What can they possibly do!" The following year, he attacked Yu. The next year, he attacked Chong Hou Hu. He then built the city of Feng Yi and moved his capital from Qixia to Feng. The following year, Xibo died; his crown prince Fa succeeded him as King Wu.

## 11

Xibo had reigned for approximately fifty years. During his imprisonment at Youli, he is said to have expanded the eight trigrams of Yi into sixty-four hexagrams. Poets recounting Xibo say that in the year he received the Mandate, he declared himself king and adjudicated the dispute between Yu and Rui. Ten years later he died; his posthumous title was King Wen. He reformed laws and regulations, and established the calendar system. He posthumously honored Gu Gong as Taowang and Gong Ji as Wangji: it was said that the kingship's auspicious signs began with Taowang.

## 12

After King Wu ascended the throne, Tai Gong Wang served as his military advisor, and Zhou Gong Dan assisted him. Zhao Gong, Bi Gong, and others stood by the king on both sides, guiding and continuing King Wen's legacy.

## 13

In the ninth year, King Wu made a sacrifice at Bi. He inspected troops to the east and advanced as far as Mengjin. They made a wooden effigy of King Wen, placed it in a cart, and positioned it at the center of the army. King Wu referred to himself as Crown Prince Fa, stating that he was carrying out King Wen's will in waging war and did not dare act on his own authority. He then addressed the generals of War, Works, and Agriculture, as well as all officials: "Be orderly and cautious; be faithful indeed! "I am not wise, but because our ancestors had virtuous subjects, I, a mere youth, have inherited their achievements. We must fully establish rewards and punishments to determine the merit of each." Thus he raised his army. The general Shangfu proclaimed: "Gather your people and prepare your boats; those who arrive late will be executed." As King Wu crossed the river, a white fish leaped into his boat; he bent down to catch it and used it for sacrifice. After crossing, a fire appeared from above and descended to the ground, reaching King Wu's palace. It transformed into a bird with red feathers, its cry echoing like "po." At this time, 800 feudal lords gathered at Mengjin without prior appointment. The feudal lords all said: "Zhou can be attacked now." King Wu replied, "You do not yet know the Mandate of Heaven; it is not yet time." He then withdrew his army and returned.

## 14

After two years, he heard that Zhou's corruption and cruelty had grown even worse; he had killed Prince Bi Gan and imprisoned Ji Zi. The Grand Master Ci and the Junior Master Qiang carried their musical instruments and fled to Zhou. At this time, King Wu informed all the feudal lords: "The Yin dynasty has committed grave crimes; we cannot refrain from completely attacking it." Thus following King Wen's example, he led 300 chariots, 3,000 elite warriors, and 45,000 armed soldiers to launch an eastern campaign against Zhou. On the Wumu day of the twelfth month (January 21, 1048 BC), the entire army crossed the Mengjin ford and all feudal lords assembled. He said: "Work diligently without idleness!" King Wu then composed the Tai Shi, addressing the common people: "Now King Zhou of Yin has followed his concubine's words and cut himself off from Heaven. He destroyed the three legitimate systems, alienated his father, mother, elder brothers, and younger brothers, abandoned the music of his ancestors, created licentious sounds to replace the proper harmonies, and delighted in pleasing women. Therefore, today I, Fa, march forward to carry out Heaven's punishment. Strive on, my men! This is a matter that cannot be repeated, not once, nor twice!"

## 15

On the Jiazi day of the second month (February 5, 1046 BC), at dawn, King Wu arrived in the Shang capital's outskirts at Musye and delivered a military oath. King Wu held a yellow halberd in his left hand and a white banner in his right to command the troops. He said: "How far have you people from the western lands come!" King Wu said, "Alas! I have my noble lords, the ministers of Works, War, and Agriculture, the officers of rank, the military commanders, the leaders of a thousand men and a hundred men, as well as the people from Yong, Shu, Qiang, Mao, Wei, Lu, Peng, and Pu. Raise your spears, align your shields, and hold up your halberds; I shall now give my oath." The king said: "There is an old saying that 'a hen does not crow in the morning. When a hen crows, it means the family will be ruined.'" "Now King Zhou of Yin follows only the words of his concubines, abandoning the ancestral sacrifices and forsaking his family and state. He neglects his father, mother, elder brother, and younger brothers and instead elevates and trusts fugitives from all directions who have committed many crimes, allowing them to oppress the people and commit corruption in the Shang state. Now I, Fa, lead this campaign to carry out Heaven's punishment together with you all. Today's battle will not last more than six or seven paces, and then we shall stop to align ourselves. Strive on, my men!" It will not go beyond four or five or six or seven attacks, and then we shall pause to align. Strive on, my soldiers!" Be valiant and fierce, like a tiger or a bear, like a jackal or a leopard. In the outskirts of Shang, do not resist but pursue the fleeing enemy to serve the western lands. Strive on, my warriors!" "If you do not strive, there will be punishment for your bodies." After the oath was given, 4,000 chariots from allied feudal lords gathered and deployed their troops at Musye.

## 16

King Zhou heard of King Wu's approach and also mobilized 700,000 men to resist him. King Wu sent the general Shangfu and a hundred officers to challenge the enemy, while leading his elite troops in a charge against King Zhou's forces. Although Zhou's army was numerous, it had no will to fight and wished for King Wu to enter quickly. Zhou's troops all turned their weapons against each other in battle, opening the way for King Wu. King Wu charged forward, and Zhou's soldiers all collapsed and deserted him. Zhou fled back into the Lutai pavilion, wrapped himself in his special jade ornaments, and set himself on fire to die. King Wu held up a large white banner to signal the feudal lords, and all of them bowed before him. King Wu then saluted the feudal lords, who in turn all followed his lead. King Wu arrived at the Shang capital, where the people of Shang were already waiting for him in the suburbs. At this time, King Wu ordered his ministers to announce to the people of Shang: "Heaven has bestowed peace upon you!" The people of Shang all bowed twice in deep respect, and King Wu also returned their bows. They then entered the city and arrived at the place where Zhou had died. King Wu shot arrows at the body, fired three times before dismounting. He then struck it with a light sword and beheaded Zhou using his yellow halberd, hanging the head on a large white flag. Afterward, they arrived at the two favorite concubines of Zhou; both women had already committed suicide by hanging. King Wu shot three arrows at them, struck with a sword, and beheaded them using a black halberd, hanging their heads on small white flags. After this, King Wu returned to his army.

## 17

The next day, they cleared the roads and repaired the ancestral shrine as well as Zhou's palace in Shang. On schedule, a hundred men carried the Han flag to lead the way. King Wu's younger brother, Shuzhen Duo, presented the ceremonial chariot with its standard; Duke of Zhou Dan held a large halberd, and Bi Gong held a small halberd to flank King Wu. Sanyi Sheng, Taodian, and Hong Yao all held swords to guard King Wu. After entering, they stood on the left side of the large formation in front of the ancestral shrine; all soldiers followed them to both sides. Mao Shu Zheng presented clear water, Wei Kangshu Feng arranged the sacrificial cloth, Zhao Gong Shi assisted in presenting the offerings, and General Shangfu led the sacrificial animal. Yin Yi recited the sacrificial prayer, saying: "The last descendant of Yin, Ji Zhou, destroyed and abandoned the virtuous deeds of his ancestors. He insulted and neglected the gods and spirits by not offering sacrifices, and tyrannized the people of Shang 邑, whose crimes are clearly known to Heaven and the Supreme Deity." At this, King Wu bowed twice in deep respect and said: "I accept the great mandate of Heaven. I will overthrow Yin and receive the clear Mandate from Heaven." King Wu again bowed twice in deep respect before leaving.

## 18

He granted the descendants of Zhou, Lu Fu, and the remaining people of Yin their fiefs. Since the newly conquered Yin territories were still unsettled, King Wu sent his younger brothers Guan Shu Xian and Cai Shu Du to assist Lu Fu in governing Yin. Later he ordered Zhao Gong to release Ji Zi from imprisonment. He also ordered Bi Gong to free the common people who had been imprisoned and marked the neighborhood of Shang Rong with honor. He ordered Nan Gong Kua to distribute the wealth from Lutai and release the grain stored at Juqiao in order to assist the poor, weak, and oppressed commoners. He ordered Nan Gong Kua and Shi Yi to inspect the Nine Tripods and preserve the jade treasures. He ordered Hong Yao to build a tomb for Bi Gan. He commanded the head priest to offer sacrifices in the army. Then he disbanded his troops and returned westward. He conducted a hunting expedition, recorded political affairs, and composed the Wu Cheng. He enfeoffed feudal lords, distributed ancestral ritual vessels as rewards, and created objects to divide Yin's possessions. King Wu, reflecting on the virtuous kings of old, honored and enfeoffed the descendants of Shen Nong in Jiao, those of Huang Di in Zhu, those of Emperor Yao in Ji, those of Emperor Shun in Chen, and those of Da Yu in Qi. At this time he enfeoffed meritorious ministers and strategists, with the general Shangfu being the first to receive a fief. He enfeoffed Shangfu at Yingqiu, naming it Qi. He enfeoffed his younger brother, the Duke of Zhou Dan, in Qufu, naming it Lu. He enfeoffed Zhao Gong Shi at Yan. He enfeoffed his brother Shu Xian in Guan and his brother Shu Du in Cai. The rest were granted fiefs in order of their rank.

## 19

King Wu summoned the lords from nine regions, climbed a hill in Bin, and looked out toward the Shang capital. When King Wu arrived at Zhou, he could not sleep through the night. Duke of Zhou Dan went to where King Wu was and asked: "Why are you unable to sleep?" The king said, "I will tell you: Heaven no longer supports Yin. Since the time when this calamity began until now, sixty years have passed; deer graze in pastures and flocks of wild geese fill the fields. "Heaven no longer favors Yin, and now we have succeeded." "Heaven established Yin, raising 360 virtuous men to prominence. They were neither honored nor eliminated, and this has continued until now." "I have not yet secured Heaven's protection; how can I find time to sleep!" The king said: "To secure Heaven's protection, we must rely on the Mandate of Heaven. We should thoroughly seek out those who committed evil and punish them for following King Zhou." Day and night, we have toiled to stabilize our western lands. I must now clearly demonstrate virtue and righteousness. From the Luo River to the Yi River, we should settle in a place that is convenient but not too vulnerable—this was where Xia once dwelled. I look south to Santu, north to Yuebi, glance at the Yellow River behind me, and also toward Luo and Yi rivers. We must not be far from Heaven's Mandate." We should establish a capital in Luoyi before departing. Release horses on the southern slopes of Mount Hua, and let cattle graze at Taolin's plains; Lay down weapons and disband troops: this shows to the world that we will no longer use force.

## 20

Two years after King Wu had conquered Yin, he asked Ji Zi why Yin had fallen. Ji Zi could not bear to speak of Yin's evils and instead explained the principles for preserving or losing a state. King Wu was also ashamed, so he asked about the Mandate of Heaven as well.

## 21

King Wu fell ill. Before the world was fully pacified, the feudal lords were alarmed. They performed solemn divinations, and Duke of Zhou purified himself in fasting, offering to sacrifice his own life as a substitute for King Wu; King Wu then recovered from his illness. Later he died, and Crown Prince Song succeeded him, becoming King Cheng.

## 22

King Cheng was young when the Zhou dynasty had just stabilized its rule over the world; fearing that feudal lords might rebel, Duke of Zhou assumed regency and took charge of state affairs. Duke Guan, Duke Cai, and their brothers doubted the Duke of Zhou; they conspired with Wu Geng to rebel against the Zhou. The Duke of Zhou, acting on King Cheng's orders, launched a campaign and executed Wu Geng and Duke Guan, while exiling Duke Cai. He appointed Wei Zi Kai as the successor to the Yin dynasty, establishing his state in Song. He gathered many of the remaining Yin people and enfeoffed King Wu's younger brother Feng as Duke Kangshu of Wei. The Jin ruler Tang Shu obtained a rare grain, presented it to King Cheng, and King Cheng sent the grain back to Duke of Zhou at his military headquarters. Duke of Zhou received the grain in the eastern territories, with an imperial decree from the Son of Heaven for Lu. Initially, Duke Guan and Duke Cai rebelled against the Zhou; the Duke of Zhou quelled them, achieving full stability after three years. Hence, he first composed the Da Gao, then Wei Zi Zhi Ming, followed by Gui He, Jia He, Kang Gao, Jiu Gao, and Zi Cai; these events are recorded in the chapter on the Duke of Zhou. After seven years as regent, when King Cheng came of age, the Duke of Zhou returned power to him and resumed his position among the ministers with his face toward the north.

## 23

King Cheng was in Feng; he ordered Zhao Gong to resume construction of Luoyi as King Wu had intended. The Duke of Zhou again performed divinations and personally oversaw the site, finally completing its construction and placing there the Nine Tripods. He said: "This is the center of the world; distances for tribute from all directions are equal." The Zhao Gao and Luo Gao were composed. After King Cheng had relocated the remnants of Yin people, the Duke of Zhou issued a royal decree to them, composing Dushi and Wuyi. Zhao Gong served as the guardian (Bao), while Duke of Zhou was the teacher (Shi); they led an eastern campaign against the Huai Yi, destroyed Yan, and exiled its ruler to Bofeng. King Cheng returned from Yan and stayed in Zongzhou, composing Duofang. After the Yin dynasty was extinguished and the Huai Yi subdued, King Cheng returned to Feng, where he composed Zhou Guan. He revived proper rites and music; regulations were reformed accordingly, and the people lived in harmony, giving rise to songs of praise. After King Cheng had campaigned against the eastern Yi, Xishen came to offer congratulations; the king bestowed a reward on Rong Bo and composed Ming Zhi Hui Xi Shen.

## 24

As King Cheng was about to die, fearing that Crown Prince Zhao would be unfit for rule, he ordered Zhao Gong and Bi Gong to lead the feudal lords in assisting and enthroning the crown prince. After King Cheng died, the two ministers led the feudal lords and presented Crown Prince Zhao at the temple of his ancestors. They solemnly declared to him how arduous it had been for King Wen and King Wu to establish their dynasty, emphasizing the importance of frugality, avoiding excessive desires, and governing with sincerity; this is recorded in Gu Ming. Crown Prince Zhao then ascended the throne, becoming King Kang. Upon ascending the throne, King Kang addressed all feudal lords, reiterating their ancestors' achievements of King Wen and King Wu; this is recorded in Kang Gao. Thus, during the reigns of Kings Cheng and Kang, the world enjoyed peace, and punishments were not used for over forty years. King Kang ordered Bi Gong to divide the population into residential districts in Chengzhou's suburbs; this is recorded in Bi Ming.

## 25

After King Kang died, his son Zhao Wang Xia ascended the throne. During King Zhao's reign, the royal way began to show slight decline. King Zhao went on a southern inspection tour and did not return, dying by the river. His death was not announced to the feudal lords; it was concealed. They enthroned King Zhao's son Man, who became King Mu. When King Mu ascended the throne, he was already fifty years old. The royal way had declined; King Mu, lamenting the neglect of King Wen and King Wu's principles, ordered Bo Li to reiterate warnings about governance for the Grand Master of Horses (Taifu); this is recorded in Li Ming. Peace was restored once more.

## 26

King Mu planned to campaign against the Quan Rong, and Jia Gong Mou Fu advised: "You must not." The former kings demonstrated virtue without displaying military might. Armed forces should be kept in readiness and used only at the right time; when used, they inspire awe. If merely displayed, they become trivialized, and people will no longer fear them. Therefore, the ode of Duke Zhao of Zhou says: "Store away weapons and bows in their sheaths; I seek noble virtue to spread across this land, so that the king may preserve it." The former kings treated the people by cultivating virtue and strengthening their moral character, enriching their resources and improving their tools for use. They clarified what was beneficial or harmful, refined these through culture, guided them to pursue benefits while avoiding harm, made them cherish virtue and respect authority—thus they could preserve their dynasty and grow stronger. In the past, our ancestors, as Houji, served the Yu and Xia dynasties in succession. When the Xia dynasty declined, they abandoned agriculture; our ancestor Bu Zhu lost his official position and fled into the lands of Rong and Di. They dared not neglect their duties, orderly cultivating virtue, following and restoring the legacy, preserving their teachings and traditions. Day and night they remained diligent, steadfast in loyalty and faithful in service. For generations, we have carried virtue forward, never disgracing our ancestors. By the time of King Wen and King Wu, they illuminated their predecessors' achievements with added benevolence and harmony; in serving deities and protecting the people, there was no one who did not rejoice. King Di Xin of Shang committed great evils against the people; the common folk could no longer endure it, and joyfully welcomed King Wu to bring war upon the Shang at Muyu. Therefore, our former kings did not seek warfare for its own sake; they encouraged compassion for the people's hardships and removed their afflictions. The former kings' system divided the realm: within the kingdom were the Dian Fu (immediate territories), beyond were the Hou Fu (marquisates); further still, the Bin Fu (attending vassals); then the Yao Fu of Yi and Man (frontier tribes); and finally the Huang Fu of Rong and Di (remote barbarians). Those in Dian Fu offered sacrifices; those in Hou Fu performed ancestral rites; those in Bin Fu presented offerings of tribute; those in Yao Fu paid tributes; and those in Huang Fu acknowledged the Son of Heaven's supremacy. Daily sacrifices, monthly ancestral rites, seasonal offerings, annual tribute, and perpetual submission to the king. When the former kings maintained proper rites, if a vassal failed to offer sacrifices, they would examine their own sincerity; if ancestral rites were neglected, they refined their words. If offerings ceased, they improved their culture; if tribute stopped, they corrected names and titles; if submission was refused, they cultivated virtue. Only after all these measures had been completed but still not achieved, then punishments were applied. Thus, there were penalties for those who did not offer sacrifices; they were attacked if ancestral rites were ignored, campaigned against if offerings ceased, admonished if tribute failed, and warned if submission was denied. Thus there were laws for punishment, armies for attack, forces for expedition, commands of authority and admonition, and written proclamations. If decrees and proclamations were issued but still not heeded, then efforts should be redoubled in cultivating virtue, without burdening the distant people with toil. Therefore, nearby vassals would all obey, and distant ones would submit without exception. Now, since the time of Da Bi and Bo Shi passed away, the Quan Rong people have come to submit with their duties. The Son of Heaven said: "I must campaign against them for not offering tribute, just to test our military strength." Is this not abandoning the teachings of former kings, risking a sudden collapse of royal authority? I have heard that the Quan Rong are loyal and steadfast, upholding old virtues with unwavering resolve; they will surely be prepared to resist us." The king proceeded to campaign against them anyway, capturing four white wolves and four white deer before returning. From then on, the vassals of Huang Fu no longer submitted.

## 27

When feudal lords showed discord, Fu Hou advised the king and composed Xiu Xing Bi (Revised Criminal Code). The king said: "Ah, come here! You who hold states and lands, I now instruct you in the proper application of punishments. Now that you govern your people, who do you choose if not the right men? What punishment do you revere if not just? And what settlement is inappropriate for its purpose? When both parties are present, the judge shall examine five types of statements. If these five statements are concise and truthful, then judgment is rendered according to the Five Punishments. If the Five Punishments do not apply, then judgment falls under the Five Penalties. If the Five Penalties are still unsatisfactory, then judgment is based on the Five Faults. For faults under the Five Faults, officials and prison authorities must examine the truth of the crime, ensuring equal judgment for equal offenses. Doubts regarding the Five Punishments should be pardoned; doubts regarding the Five Penalties should also be pardoned—this must be carefully examined. Evidence and testimony must be abundant, and interrogations must have records for verification. Without thorough examination, there should be no doubt; together uphold the majesty of Heaven's authority. If branding punishment is questionable for pardon, then impose a fine of one hundred units and examine the truth of their crime. If cutting off the nose (Yi Bi) is questionable for pardon, impose a double fine and verify the actual crime. If amputating the knee bones (Bin Bi) is questionable for pardon, apply a doubled penalty and examine their true offense. If castration punishment (Gong Bi) is questionable for clemency, impose a fine of five hundred units and verify the crime's truth. If death penalty (Da Bi) is questionable for pardon, exact a fine of one thousand units and investigate the actual offense. Cases under the branding penalty totaled one thousand; those under nose-cutting also numbered one thousand. Cases of knee amputation amounted to five hundred, castration cases three hundred, and death penalty cases two hundred: altogether, 3,000 cases fell under the Five Punishments." This was called Fu Xing.

## 28

King Mu reigned for fifty-five years, then died; his son Gong Wang Yi Hu succeeded him. King Gong once toured the banks of the Jing River, accompanied by Mi Kanggong, when three women suddenly ran to him. Their mother said: "You must present them to the king. Animals in threes form a group, people in threes make a crowd, and three women together are dazzling. The king's hunting grounds do not take entire groups; the duke's procession does not pass over crowds; the king's harem does not include an entire family. Such beauty is a precious thing. Many people would offer such beautiful things to you—what virtue do you possess that could deserve it? Even the king himself might find it difficult to bear; how much more so for someone as lowly as you! To possess such treasures while lacking virtue, one is destined to perish." Mi Kanggong did not present the women. One year later, King Gong destroyed Mi. King Gong died and was succeeded by his son Yi Wang Jian. During King Yi's reign, the royal house gradually declined, prompting poets to compose satirical verses.

## 29

After King Yi died, King Gong's younger brother Pifang ascended the throne and became King Xiao. After King Xiao died, the feudal lords again enthroned Crown Prince Xie of King Yi, who became King Yi.

## 30

King Yi died and was succeeded by his son Hu, known as King Li. In the thirtieth year of King Li's reign, he favored profit and drew close to Gong Rongyigong. Minister Ruiliangfu advised King Li, saying: "Is the royal house about to become humble? Gong Rongyigong likes monopolizing profit without being aware of great dangers. Profit is the source from which all things arise, and it is supported by heaven and earth; to monopolize it will bring many harms. All things in heaven and earth will take from it—how can one possibly monopolize them? He has provoked great anger, yet he is unprepared for great dangers. Teaching the king in this way, how can King Li endure long? The ruler of a kingdom should guide profit and distribute it among all levels. He ensures that gods, humans, and all things receive their due share, always remaining vigilant against resentment arising. Therefore the "Song" says: 'Thoughtful was Houji, who could match heaven; he established our people, none without your due share.' The Da Ya says: 'He bestowed blessings upon the Zhou dynasty.' This is because he did not monopolize profit and feared difficulties, thus enabling the Zhou to endure until now. Now that King Li learns to monopolize profit, how can this be acceptable? Even an ordinary person who monopolizes profit is called a thief; if the king does so, few will remain loyal to him. If Gong Rongyigong is employed, the Zhou dynasty will surely fall." King Li did not listen and eventually appointed Gong Rongyigong as a high official in charge of affairs.

## 31

The king acted with cruelty, extravagance, and arrogance, prompting the common people to criticize him. Zhao Gong advised, saying: "The people can no longer bear their burdens." The king became angry and obtained a diviner from the State of Wei to supervise those who criticized him, ordering that anyone reported would be executed. Criticism soon ceased, and feudal lords no longer paid homage. In the thirty-fourth year, King Li became even more severe; the people dared not speak openly, communicating only with glances on the roads. King Li was pleased and told Zhao Gong: "I have silenced criticism; they now dare not speak." Zhao Gong said, "This is merely blocking it. Blocking the people's mouths is worse than blocking water. When water is dammed up, it will burst forth and cause great harm; so too with the people. Therefore, those who manage water must channel it to prevent flooding; those who govern the people should encourage them to speak freely. Therefore, when the Son of Heaven administers government affairs, he invites ministers and officials down to commoners to present poems; blind musicians offer songs; historians submit records; teachers give advice; sightless men recite verses; young boys chant; artisans criticize; common people pass on words; close attendants offer their best counsel; relatives supplement and observe; blind historians teach and instruct, while elders revise them. Only after this does the king deliberate carefully, thus ensuring that affairs proceed without contradiction. The people have mouths, just as the land has mountains and rivers from which wealth arises; just as it has plains, lowlands, and fertile fields where clothing and food are produced. From spoken words come declarations, in which good or bad fortune is revealed. To act virtuously and prepare for failure—this is the way to produce wealth, clothing, and food. The people consider matters in their hearts and express them with their mouths; when they reach consensus, they act upon it. If you block their mouths, how long can your support last?" King Li did not listen. As a result, no one in the kingdom dared to speak; three years later, they united and rebelled, attacking King Li. King Li fled to Zhi.

## 32

Crown Prince Jing, the son of King Li, hid in Zhao Gong's house; when the people learned of this, they surrounded it. Zhao Gong said: "In the past I repeatedly advised the king, but he did not listen, and thus this disaster has come. If we now kill the king's crown prince, will the king regard me as an enemy and become enraged? To serve a ruler, one may face danger without becoming enemies or harboring resentment; how much less so when serving the king!" He therefore offered his own son in place of the crown prince. The crown prince thus managed to escape.

## 33

Zhao Gong and Zhou Gong, the two ministers, administered government affairs together; this period was known as "Gonghe." In the fourteenth year of the Gonghe era, King Li died in Zhi. Prince Jing had grown up in Zhao Gong's household; the two ministers then jointly enthroned him as king, who became King Xuan. After King Xuan ascended the throne, the two ministers assisted him in restoring government and following the legacy of Kings Wen, Wu, Cheng, and Kang; feudal lords once again revered the Zhou dynasty. In the twelfth year, Lord Wu Gong of Lu came to pay homage.

## 34

King Xuan did not conduct a land survey at Qianmu; Gao Wengong advised that it was unwise, but the king would not listen. In the thirty-ninth year, a battle took place at Qianmu, where King Xuan's army suffered a defeat by the Jiangshi Rong.

## 35

After losing the southern forces, King Xuan conducted a census of the people in Taiyuan. Zhong Shanfu advised: "The people cannot be counted." King Xuan did not listen and proceeded to conduct the census.

## 36

In the forty-sixth year, King Xuan died; his son Gongsheng was enthroned as King You. In the second year of King You's reign, three rivers in the western region of Zhou all experienced earthquakes. Boyangfu said: "The Zhou dynasty is about to fall. Heaven and earth's vital energies do not lose their order; If they exceed this order, it is due to the people causing disorder. When yang energy is suppressed and cannot emerge, and yin pressure cannot rise, earthquakes occur as a result. Now that the three rivers have indeed experienced an earthquake, it means yang has lost its proper place and is filling yin's domain. When yang is displaced into yin, the springs must become blocked; If the springs are blocked, the state will surely perish. Water and land provide for the people's use. If the land provides nothing, the people will lack resources; how long can it avoid collapse! In the past, when the Yi and Luo rivers dried up, the Xia dynasty fell; when the Yellow River dried up, the Shang dynasty perished. Now the virtue of Zhou is as in the final years of the Xia and Shang dynasties; its rivers and springs are also blocked, and when they are blocked, they will dry up. A state must rely on mountains and rivers; if the mountains collapse and the rivers dry up, it is an omen of a fallen kingdom. When rivers dry up, mountains will inevitably collapse. If a kingdom is to fall, it should not take more than ten years; this is the cycle of numbers. What heaven abandons will not exceed its destined cycle." In this year, the three rivers dried up and Mount Qi collapsed.

## 37

In the third year, King You favored Bao Si. Bao Si bore a son named Bo Fu; King You wanted to depose the crown prince. The crown prince's mother was the daughter of Shen Hou and had been queen. Later, King You acquired Bao Si and loved her dearly; he wanted to depose Queen Shen and remove Crown Prince Yi Jiu, making Bao Si the queen and Bo Fu the crown prince. Zhou's chief historian Boyangfu read historical records and said: "The Zhou dynasty is doomed." In the waning years of the Xia royal house, two divine dragons stopped at the court of a Xia emperor and said: "We are the two deities of Bao." The Xia emperor divined whether to kill them, drive them away, or leave them be; none of these options proved auspicious. He then divined that if he collected their saliva and preserved it, this would bring good fortune. Thus, the emperor offered sacrifices with offerings and made a vow to them; then the dragons vanished but left behind their saliva, which was placed in a chest and stored away. When the Xia fell, this vessel passed into the hands of the Shang dynasty. lando Thus it passed to the Zhou when the Shang fell. For three successive dynasties, no one dared open it; but in the later years of King Li's reign, it was opened and examined. The saliva flowed into the courtyard and could not be removed. King Li ordered women to strip naked and shout at it. The saliva transformed into a black tortoise, which entered the king's harem. A young concubine in the palace, after bathing and encountering it, became pregnant upon reaching marriageable age; she gave birth to a child without a husband and was afraid, so she abandoned it. During King Xuan's reign, young girls sang a nursery rhyme: "The bow and 箕 robe—these will bring the downfall of the Zhou kingdom." Hearing this, King Xuan ordered that a couple selling such an object be arrested and executed. They fled along the road and saw a child abandoned by the palace concubine on the roadside; hearing it cry at night, they felt pity and took it in. The couple then escaped to Bao. The people of Bao committed a crime and requested to present the abandoned girl to the king as compensation for their guilt. The abandoned girl came from Bao, and this was Baosi. In the third year of King You's reign, a concubine in his harem saw her and loved her. A son named Bofu was born to her, and eventually, Queen Shen and Crown Prince were deposed; Baosi became queen and Bofu became crown prince. The Grand Historian Boyang said: "Disaster is now inevitable, there's nothing we can do about it!"

## 38

Baosi did not like to laugh. King You tried every means to make her smile, but she never laughed. King You set up beacon towers and large drums; when enemies approached, he would light the beacons. The feudal lords all arrived, but found no enemy forces—Baosi then laughed heartily. King You was pleased and repeatedly lit the beacons. Later, when real danger came, the feudal lords no longer believed him and did not come to his aid.

## 39

King You appointed Guoshi Fu as an official in charge of affairs, which caused resentment among the people of the state. Shifu was a flatterer who was cunning and greedy for profit; King You trusted him. He also deposed Queen Shen and removed the crown prince. Shen Hou was furious and allied with Zeng, the western Yi, and Quanrong to attack King You. King You lit the beacon fires to summon troops, but none came. They then killed King You at Lishan and captured Baosi, taking all the treasures of Zhou as spoils before leaving. At this time, the feudal lords then approached Shen Hou and jointly enthroned Youwang's former crown prince Yijiu, who became King Ping, to continue the ancestral rites of Zhou.

## 40

King Ping was enthroned and moved eastward to Luoyi to avoid the Rong and Koui invaders. During King Ping's reign, the Zhou royal house declined and grew weak; feudal lords began to dominate the weak. Qi, Chu, Qin, and Jin started to grow powerful, with authority resting in regional hegemonies.

## 41

In the forty-ninth year [of King Ping's reign], Duke Yin of Lu ascended the throne.

## 42

In the fifty-first year, King Ping died; Crown Prince Xiefu had died early, so his son Lin was enthroned, who became King Huan. King Huan was a grandson of King Ping.

## 43

In the third year of King Huan's reign, Duke Zhuang of Zheng paid homage, but King Huan did not treat him with courtesy. In the fifth year, Zheng resented this and exchanged Xu Tian with Lu. Xu Tian was a field used by the Son of Heaven for sacrifices at Mount Tai. In the eighth year, Lu killed Duke Yin and enthroned Duke Huan. In the thirteenth year, they attacked Zheng; Zheng shot and wounded King Huan, who then left in retreat.

## 44

In the twenty-third year, King Huan died, and his son Tuota was enthroned as King Zhuangwang. In the fourth year of King Zhuang's reign, Zhou Gong Heijian wanted to kill King Zhuang and install Crown Prince Ke as ruler. Xinbo informed the king, who then killed Zhou Gong. Prince Ke fled to Yan.

## 45

In the fifteenth year, King Zhuang died and was succeeded by his son Huqi, who became King Liwang. In the third year of King Li's reign, Duke Huan of Qi began to establish hegemony.

## 46

In the fifth year, King Li died and was succeeded by his son Lang, who became King Huwang. The second year of King Hui's reign. Initially, King Zhuang had a favored concubine named Yao, who bore him a son named Tui; Tui was also favored. When King Hui ascended the throne, he seized land from his ministers to create a hunting ground. Thus, officials Bianbo and five others rebelled, planning to summon armies from Yan and Wei to attack King Hui. King Hui fled to Wen and later took refuge in Lu of Zheng. They enthroned King Li's younger brother Tui as king. The music and dancing continued, which angered the rulers of Zheng and Guo. In the fourth year, the rulers of Zheng and Guo attacked and killed King Tui, restoring King Hui to power. In the tenth year of King Hui's reign, he granted Duke Huan of Qi the title of hegemon.

## 47

In the twenty-fifth year, King Hui died and was succeeded by his son Zheng, who became King Xiangwang. King Xiang's mother had died early; his stepmother was known as Huiguo. Huiguo bore a son named Shudai, who was favored by King Hui, and thus King Xiang feared him. In the third year, Shudai conspired with Rong and Di tribes to attack King Xiang. In response, King Xiang wanted to execute Shudai, who fled to Qi. Duke Huan of Qi sent Guan Zhong to pacify the Rong tribes in Zhou and Xi Peng to pacify them in Jin. The king received Guan Zhong with the courtesy due to a high-ranking official. Guan Zhong declined, saying: "I am but a lowly official; the two ministers Guo and Gao are present to serve as guardians of the Son of Heaven." "If they come in accordance with the seasons, following Spring and Autumn, to receive the king's commands, what need is there for special rites?" "A subordinate official dares to decline." The king said: "Uncle, I commend your achievements; do not refuse my command." Guan Zhong finally accepted the rites befitting a lower-ranking official and returned. In the ninth year, Duke Huan of Qi died. In the twelfth year, Shudai returned to Zhou once more.

## 48

In the thirteenth year of King Xiang's reign, Zheng attacked Hua; the king sent You Sun and Bo Fu to plead for Hua, but the people of Zheng imprisoned them. Duke Wen of Zheng resented King Hui for not granting his cousin Duke Li the title upon his return, and also resented King Xiang for supporting Wei's claim over Hua; thus he imprisoned Bo Fu. The king was furious and planned to attack Zheng with the Di tribes. Fu Chen advised: "Whenever our Zhou dynasty moved east, we relied on Jin and Zheng." "The rebellion of Prince Tui was quelled thanks to Zheng's efforts. Now you would abandon them for a minor grievance!" The king did not listen. In the fifteenth year, the king allied with Di forces to attack Zheng. The king favored the Di people and planned to take their daughter as queen. Fu Chen advised: "Kings Ping, Huan, Zhuang, and Hui all benefited from Zheng's loyalty. The king is now abandoning close relatives to favor the Di; this cannot be allowed." The king did not listen. In the sixteenth year, the king dismissed the Di queen; in retaliation, the Di people came to punish him and killed Duke Tan. Fu Chen said: "I have repeatedly advised but was not heeded." "If I do not act now, will the king think me resentful?" So he led his followers to their deaths.

## 49

Initially, Huiguo wanted to install Prince Dai as king, so she conspired with the Di people; thus, they entered Zhou. King Xiang fled to Zheng and was housed in Si by Zheng. Prince Dai declared himself king, taking the Di queen whom King Xiang had dismissed and settling her in Wen. In the seventeenth year, King Xiang urgently appealed to Jin; Duke Wen of Jin welcomed him back and executed Shudai. King Xiang then bestowed on Duke Wen of Jin the jade gui, chang liquor, bow and arrows, granting him hegemony and ceding the area within the Yellow River to Jin. In the twentieth year, Duke Wen of Jin summoned King Xiang; King Xiang met him at Heyang and Jiantu. All feudal lords paid homage, and it was recorded euphemistically as "The Son of Heaven hunting in Heyang."

## 50

In the twenty-fourth year, Duke Wen of Jin died.

## 51

In the thirty-first year, Duke Mu of Qin died.

## 52

In the thirty-second year, King Xiang died and was succeeded by his son Renchen, who became King Qingwang. In the sixth year of King Qing's reign, he died; his son Ban was enthroned as Duke Kuangwang. In the sixth year of Duke Kuang's reign, he died and was succeeded by his younger brother Yu, who became King Dingwang.

## 53

In the first year of King Ding's reign, Duke Zhuang of Chu attacked the Rong tribes at Lukou and stationed his troops near Luoyi, sending envoys to inquire about the Nine Tripods. The king sent Wangsun Man to respond with diplomatic words, and the Chu forces withdrew. In the tenth year, Duke Zhuang of Chu besieged Zheng; Duke Bo of Zheng surrendered but was later restored to power. In the sixteenth year, Duke Zhuang of Chu died.

## 54

In the twenty-first year, King Ding died and was succeeded by his son Yi, who became King Jianwang. In the thirteenth year of Duke Jian's reign, Jin killed its ruler Li Gong and welcomed Prince Zhou from Zhou to be enthroned as Dou Gong.

## 55

In the fourteenth year, King Jian died and was succeeded by his son Xie Xin, who became King Lingwang. In the twenty-fourth year of King Ling's reign, Cui Zhu of Qi assassinated his ruler Duke Zhuang.

## 56

In the twenty-seventh year, King Ling died and was succeeded by his son Gui, who became King Jingwang. In the eighteenth year of King Jing's reign, the crown prince, Sheng, died young. In the twentieth year of King Jing's reign, King Jing favored his son Zhao and wished to make him heir. However, he died before doing so; factions supporting Prince Gai vied for power. The people enthroned the eldest son Meng as king, but Prince Zhao attacked and killed him. Meng became King Douwang. The Jin people attacked Prince Zhao and enthroned Gai, who became King Jing Wang.

## 57

In the first year of King Jing's reign (Jingwang), the Jin people brought in King Jing, but Prince Zhao declared himself ruler. King Jing could not enter and resided at Ze instead. In the fourth year, Jin led the feudal lords to bring King Jing into Zhou; Prince Zhao became a subject and the feudal lords built walls around Zhou. In the sixteenth year, followers of Prince Zhao again rebelled, forcing King Jing to flee to Jin. In the seventeenth year, Duke Ding of Jin escorted King Jing back into Zhou.

## 58

In the thirty-ninth year, Tian Chang of Qi killed his ruler Jian Gong.

## 59

In the forty-first year, Chu destroyed Chen. Confucius died.

## 60

In the forty-second year, King Jing died and was succeeded by his son Ren, who became King Yuanwang. In the eighth year of King Yuan's reign, he died and was succeeded by his son Jie, who became King Dingwang.

## 61

In the sixteenth year of King Ding's reign, the Three Jin states destroyed Zhi Bo and divided his territory among themselves.

## 62

In the twenty-eighth year, King Ding died; his eldest son Quji was enthroned as King Ai Wang. King Ai reigned for only three months, when his younger brother Shu attacked and killed him before enthroning himself as King Siwang. King Si reigned for five months, when his younger brother Wei attacked and killed him before taking the throne as King Kao Wang. These three kings were all sons of King Ding.

## 63

In the fifteenth year of King Kao's reign, he died; his son Wu was enthroned as Wang Wei Lie Wang.

## 64

King Kao granted a fief to his younger brother in the southern part of Henan, who became Duke Huan; this was done to continue the official duties previously held by the Zhou Gong. After Duke Huan died, his son Weigong succeeded him. Weigong died and was succeeded by Hugong, who then granted a fief to his youngest son in Gong to serve the king, titled Duke Hui of Dongzhou.

## 65

In the twenty-third year of Wang Wei Lie's reign, the Nine Tripods trembled. He conferred the title of feudal lords upon Han, Wei, and Zhao.

## 66

In the twenty-fourth year, he died; his son Jiao was enthroned as Anwang. That year, thieves killed King Sheng of Chu.

## 67

After reigning for twenty-six years, Anwang died and was succeeded by his son Xi, who became Liwang. In the second year of Liwang's reign, Zhou Taishi Dan met Duke Xian of Qin and said: "Initially, Zhou and the State of Qin were united but later separated. After five hundred years they will reunite again; seventeen years after their reunion, a hegemon or emperor shall emerge."

## 68

In the tenth year, Liwang died and was succeeded by his younger brother Bian, who became Xian Wang. In the fifth year of King Xian's reign, he congratulated Duke Xian of Qin, and Duke Xian declared himself Hegemon (Bo). In the ninth year, sacrificial offerings from Wenwang and Wuwang were presented to Duke Xiao of Qin. In the twenty-fifth year, Qin convened feudal lords in Zhou. In the twenty-sixth year, Zhou conferred the title of Hegemon (Bo) upon Duke Xiao of Qin. In the thirty-third year, he congratulated King Hui of Qin. In the thirty-fifth year, sacrificial offerings from King Wen and King Wu were presented to King Hui of Qin. In the forty-fourth year, King Hui of Qin declared himself king. After that, all feudal lords became kings.

## 69

In the forty-eighth year of King Xian's reign (Xianwang), he died and was succeeded by his grandson Ding, who became Shenjing Wang. Shenjing Wang reigned for six years before dying; he was succeeded by his son Yan, who became King Nanwang. During the reign of King Nan, Eastern and Western Zhou were separately governed. King Nan moved his capital to Western Zhou.

## 70

The Crown Prince Gong of Duke Wu of Western Zhou died, leaving five commoner sons with no clear heir designated. Sima Jian said to the King of Chu: "It would be better to offer land to Gongzi Jiu and request that he be named crown prince." Zuo Cheng said, "No. "If Zhou does not listen, it would mean your wisdom is in trouble and relations with Zhou become distant." "It would be better to ask the Zhou ruler who he wishes to appoint, and discreetly inform Jian. Jian can then request that Chu provide land support for him." As a result, Gongzi Jiu was indeed appointed as crown prince.

## 71

In the eighth year, Qin attacked Yiyang and Chu came to its rescue. But because Zhou had allied with Qin, Chu planned to attack it. Su Dai spoke on behalf of Zhou to the King of Chu: "Why should Zhou be considered a disaster for Qin?" "To say that Zhou is more harmful to Qin than Chu is an attempt to force Zhou into the hands of Qin, hence the term 'Zhou Qin'." "Knowing that it cannot be resolved otherwise, Zhou will inevitably fall into the hands of Qin. This is the most effective way for Qin to obtain control over Zhou." "For your Majesty's benefit, if Zhou is friendly with Qin, you should be friendly to them; if not, still appear friendly. This will create distance between Zhou and Qin." "If Zhou severs ties with Qin, it must inevitably fall under your control in Ying."

## 72

Qin requested a passage through the territory between Eastern and Western Zhou to attack Han. Zhou feared that granting it would provoke Han's resentment, while refusing would anger Qin. Shi Yan said to the Zhou ruler: "Why not have someone tell Gongshu of Han, 'Qin dares to cut off Zhou and attack Han because they trust Eastern Zhou?" "Why don't you give land to Zhou and send a hostage to Chu?'" "Qin will certainly doubt that Chu does not trust Zhou, so Han will not be attacked." "He should also tell Qin: 'Han is strong and has given land to Zhou, intending to sow doubts about Zhou in the eyes of Qin. Zhou dares not refuse.'" "Qin will have no grounds to prevent Zhou from accepting, which means Zhou would receive land from Han while still complying with Qin."

## 73

Qin summoned the ruler of Western Zhou. The Western Zhou ruler feared going, so he had someone tell King of Han: "Qin has summoned the Western Zhou ruler to attack your southern region of Nanyang. Why don't you send troops to defend Nanyang?" "The Zhou ruler will use this as an excuse against Qin." "If the Zhou ruler does not enter Qin, then Qin certainly dare not cross the Yellow River to attack Nanyang."

## 74

Eastern Zhou and Western Zhou went to war; Han came to the aid of Western Zhou. Someone on behalf of Eastern Zhou advised King of Han: "Western Zhou is an ancient state of the Son of Heaven, possessing many precious ritual objects and treasures. "If Your Majesty keeps your troops in readiness without moving out, it will demonstrate virtue toward Eastern Zhou, and all the treasures of Western Zhou can certainly fall into our hands."

## 75

King Nan addressed Cheng Jun. Chu besieged Yongshi, and Han requested weapons and grain from Eastern Zhou. The ruler of Eastern Zhou was alarmed and summoned Su Dai to inform him. Dai said: "What is there for you to worry about in this matter? "I can ensure that Han will not request weapons and grain from Zhou, and I can also secure Gaodu for you." The ruler of Eastern Zhou said: "If you are truly capable, please proceed with full authority over the state." Dai met with Han's prime minister and said: "Chu has been besieging Yongshi for three months as expected, but after five months they still cannot capture it. This indicates that Chu is in a weakened state." "Now, your prime minister requests weapons and grain from Zhou; this is equivalent to informing Chu of its weakness." The Han prime minister said: "Well said. "The envoys have already been dispatched." Dai asked: "Why not give Zhou the city of Gaodu?" The Han prime minister became very angry and said: "We have already sent too many weapons and grain to Zhou. Why should we give them Gaodu?" Dai said: "Granting Gaodu to Zhou means that Zhou will be weakened and fall under Han's control. When Qin hears of this, they will certainly become furious with Zhou and refuse to allow Zhou envoys passage. This way, Han can secure Gaodu while preserving Zhou." "Why not do it?" The prime minister said: "Good idea." As a result, Gaodu was indeed granted to Zhou.

## 76

In the thirty-fourth year, Su Li said to the Zhou ruler: "The Qin forces that defeated Han and Wei, crushed Shiwu's army, and captured northern Zhao cities such as Lin and Lishi were all led by Bai Qi. "He is a master of military strategy and also favored by the Mandate of Heaven." "Now he is leading troops beyond the frontier to attack Liang. If Liang falls, Zhou will be in danger." "Why doesn't Your Majesty send someone to persuade Bai Qi?" "He said, 'Chu has Yang Youji, an excellent archer. "From a hundred paces away from the willow leaf, he shoots and hits it every time out of a hundred attempts." "Hundreds of onlookers watched and all praised his archery skills." "A man stood by the side and said, 'Good. You can teach him to shoot.'" "Yang Youji became angry, dropped his bow, gripped his sword, and said, 'How dare you teach me to shoot?'" "The man replied, "It is not that I can teach you how to hold the bow with your left hand and draw it back with your right." "Shooting at a willow leaf from a hundred paces, hitting it every time out of a hundred shots, without pausing to catch your breath—after a while, when one's strength wanes and fatigue sets in, the bow becomes unsteady and the arrow bends. If you miss even once, all the previous efforts are wasted.'" "Now, having defeated Han and Wei, shattered Shiwu's forces, and captured the northern Zhao cities of Lin and Lishi, you have achieved many great feats." "Now you are leading troops beyond the frontier, crossing both Zhou states, bypassing Han, and attacking Liang. If this campaign fails even once, all previous achievements will be lost." "You would be better off claiming illness and not going out.'"

## 77

In the forty-second year, Qin defeated Huyang Yue. Ma Fan said to the ruler of Zhou: "Please have Liang build a city for Zhou." He then told King Liang, "If the king of Zhou falls ill or dies, I will certainly die as well. "I request to present the Nine Tripods myself to Your Majesty; if you accept the Nine Tripods, please also consider my fate." King Liang said, "Good." He then gave him troops and declared it was to garrison Zhou. But he told King Qin, "Liangan is not garrisoning Zhou; he is going to attack Zhou." "Your Majesty should send troops near the border to observe what happens.'" Qin indeed sent out troops. He then told King Liang again, "The king of Zhou is very ill; I request to delay and return later." "Now that Your Majesty has sent troops to Zhou, the feudal lords are all suspicious. If we take further action afterward, they will not believe us." "It would be better to have the soldiers build a city for Zhou in order to conceal any signs of trouble.'" King Liang said, "Good." He then ordered the construction of a city for Zhou.

## 78

In the forty-fifth year, a guest of the Zhou ruler told Zhou Ju: "Instead of what you are doing now, you should praise King Qin's filial piety and recommend Ying as the land for the 太后 to reside in. King Qin will surely be pleased; this way, you would gain good relations with Qin." "By cultivating good relations, the Zhou ruler will certainly regard it as your achievement." "If relations turn bad, those who advise the Zhou ruler to enter Qin will surely be punished.'" "When Qin attacked Zhou, Zhou Ju told King Qin: "For Your Majesty's benefit, it is not advisable to attack Zhou. "Attacking Zhou would bring little actual gain and would only cause fear among the states of the world." "If the world fears Qin in name, they will certainly unite with Qi to the east." "The army would be exhausted by fighting Zhou." "If all the states unite with Qi, then Qin will never become a hegemon." "The world wants to exhaust Qin and is urging Your Majesty to attack Zhou." "If Qin becomes exhausted by the whole world, then its commands will no longer be heeded.'"

## 79

In the fifty-eighth year, the Three Jin states resisted Qin. Zhou sent its prime minister to Qin, but because he considered Qin to be contemptible, he returned without proceeding further. A guest told the prime minister: "It is still uncertain whether Qin is strong or weak." "Qin wants to know the situation of the three states. "You should urgently meet with King Qin and say, 'I will inform Your Majesty about changes in the eastern states.' King Qin will certainly value you highly." "If he values you, then Qin will value Zhou. Through this, Zhou can gain influence over Qin; "if Qi is valued, we already have Zhou Ju to manage relations with Qi: this way, Zhou will always maintain good relations with a powerful state.'" Qin trusted Zhou and sent troops to attack the Three Jin states.

## 80

In the fifty-ninth year, Qin captured Yangcheng Fushu of Han. The Western Zhou was frightened and turned against Qin, forming an alliance with other feudal lords. They mobilized the sharpest forces from all states to attack Qin at Yiqu, preventing Qin from accessing Yangcheng. King Zhao of Qin was furious and sent General Jiu to attack Western Zhou. The ruler of Western Zhou fled to Qin, kowtowed in submission and accepted guilt, offering all thirty-six of his fiefs and 30,000 people. Qin accepted the offerings and returned its ruler to Zhou.

## 81

The Zhou ruler and King Nan died, and the people of Zhou gradually fled eastward. Qin seized the Nine Tripods and other treasures, and relocated the Duke of Western Zhou to Danhu. Seven years later, King Zhuangxiang of Qin destroyed Eastern (Western) Zhou. Both the Eastern and Western Zhou were incorporated into Qin; thus, the Zhou dynasty ceased to exist with no further sacrifices offered in its name.

## 82

Grand Historian said: Scholars all claim that Zhou attacked the Shang dynasty and settled at Luoyi, but in reality this is not entirely accurate. King Wu planned it, and King Cheng sent Zhaogong to divinate a suitable location for the capital, where the Nine Tripods were placed. However, Zhou once again made Feng and Hao its capitals. It was not until the Quanrong defeated King You that Zhou moved east to Luoyi. What is referred to as "the Duke of Zhou was buried (me) [at] Bi," the location of Bi lies in Du, southeast of Hao. Qin destroyed Zhou. More than ninety years after the rise of Han, when the emperor was about to hold a ceremony on Mount Tai and conducted an eastern inspection tour reaching Henan, he sought descendants of Zhou. He granted thirty li of land to his descendant Jia, bestowing him with the title "Zhou Zinan Jun," ranking equivalent to marquises, in order to continue offering sacrifices to their ancestors.
