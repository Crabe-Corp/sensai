# Biographies of Diligent Officials

## 1

The Grand Historian said: "Laws and decrees are used to guide the people; punishments are used to deter evildoers." When both civil and military affairs are not properly managed, upright citizens may become fearful and cautious in their conduct, yet the officials have never been corrupt. If one performs his duties with integrity and follows reason, it is also sufficient for good governance; why must one rely on authority and severity?

## 2

Sun Shuao was a recluse of the state of Chu. Yuqiu Xiang recommended him to King Zhuang of Chu in order to replace himself. After three months as prime minister of Chu, he educated and guided the people. The relations between superiors and inferiors were harmonious; society was flourishing and virtuous. Governance was lenient yet effective in preventing misconduct. Officials had no corrupt or wicked behavior, and bandits did not arise. In autumn and winter he encouraged the people to gather resources from the mountains; in spring and summer, they relied on water. Each activity suited what was convenient for them, and thus the people all enjoyed their lives.

## 3

King Zhuang considered the currency too light in value, so he changed it to make small coins into large ones. This was inconvenient for the people, and they all abandoned their occupations. The market official told the prime minister: "The market is in chaos; the people cannot find peace, and the order of transactions is unsettled." The prime minister said, "How long has this been going on?" The market official replied, "For about three months." The prime minister said, "Enough. I will now issue an order to restore things as they were." Five days later, in court, the prime minister told the king: "The other day you changed the currency because you thought it was too light. Now the market official has come to report that 'the market is in chaos, and the people cannot find peace; transactions are unsettled.'" I respectfully request permission to immediately issue an order restoring things as they were." The king agreed, and after issuing the order, within three days the market returned to its former state.

## 4

The common people of Chu had a custom of preferring low carts; King Zhuang thought that low carts were inconvenient for horses, and wanted to issue an order requiring them to be made higher. The prime minister said: "If orders are frequently issued, the people will not know what to follow; this is not advisable. If Your Majesty insists on having higher carts, I respectfully request permission to instruct the neighborhoods and villages to raise their cart frames themselves." Those who ride in carriages are all gentlemen; gentlemen cannot be seen frequently getting out of their carriages." The king agreed. After half a year, the people had all voluntarily raised their carts on their own initiative.

## 5

This is an example of governance without direct instruction, yet the people followed its influence; those nearby observed and emulated it, while those far away looked toward it from all directions and took it as a model. Therefore, he attained the position of prime minister three times without showing joy, knowing that his abilities naturally earned him this position; he left the post of prime minister three times without regret, knowing it was not due to any fault of his own.

## 6

Zi Chan was a high-ranking official in the state of Zheng. During the reign of Duke Zhao of Zheng, he appointed Xu Zhi, a man he favored, as prime minister. The state fell into disorder; relations between superiors and subordinates became estranged, and father-son relationships were disharmonious. Dagong Ziqi advised the ruler to appoint Zi Chan as prime minister. After one year in office, young boys no longer played roughly or behaved disrespectfully; the elderly were not carried about by their attendants; and servants did not plow along the field boundaries. After two years, merchants in the market no longer speculated or hoarded goods. After three years, gates were not closed at night and lost items were never picked up from roadsides. After four years, farming tools left in the fields were no longer taken back by their owners, as they remained undisturbed. After five years, soldiers did not need to be registered for military service, and mourning periods were observed without official orders—governance was already well-ordered. Zi Chan governed Zheng for twenty-six years before he died; the strong and vigorous wept aloud, while the elderly cried like children, saying: "With Zi Chan gone from us, we are as good as dead! To whom shall we turn for peace and stability?"

## 7

Gongyi Xi was a scholar-official of the state of Lu. He was appointed prime minister of Lu due to his outstanding academic achievements and high moral character. Upholding the law and following reason, he made no changes or alterations; all government officials naturally conducted themselves properly. He ensured that those who received salaries could not compete with common people for profit, and those in high positions could not take advantage of the small or weak.

## 8

A guest once presented the prime minister with fish, but he did not accept it. The guest said: "I heard that you enjoy eating fish; I have given you fish—why do you refuse to accept it?" The prime minister replied, "Precisely because I like fish, I cannot accept it. Now that I am the prime minister, I can afford to buy my own fish; If I were to accept this fish and later be dismissed from office for corruption, who would still provide me with fish?" Therefore, I do not accept it."

## 9

When he tasted a vegetable and found it delicious, he uprooted the entire patch of kui (a type of vegetable) in his garden and discarded it. When he saw that the cloth woven by his wife was of fine quality, he quickly sent her away from home and burned their loom, saying: "How can farmers and artisans sell their goods if my family's weaving is superior?"

## 10

Shi She was the prime minister of King Zhao of Chu. He was resolute, upright, incorruptible, and impartial, showing no favoritism or avoidance. While touring the counties on official business, he encountered a man who had committed murder; upon pursuit, it turned out to be his own father. He released his father and then returned to surrender himself in chains. He sent someone to inform the king: "The murderer is my father. To uphold justice by punishing one's own father would be an act of filial impiety; Yet to disregard the law and let a criminal go unpunished is not loyalty. Therefore, I deserve death." The king said: "You pursued him but could not catch him; you are not guilty of failing to punish a criminal. Resume your duties." Shi She replied, "To show no mercy toward my own father is not the act of a filial son; Yet to disobey the ruler's law is not the act of a loyal minister. If Your Majesty pardons my crime, it would be an expression of your grace; But to accept punishment and die is the duty of a subject." Thus, he refused the king's pardon and cut his own throat in death.

## 11

Li Li was a judge under Duke Wen of Jin. Due to an erroneous judgment, he had caused the death of an innocent person and thus confined himself, accepting that he should be put to death. Duke Wen said: "Offices have ranks of high or low; punishments vary in severity. The mistake was made by a subordinate official, not your fault." Li Li said: "I hold the position of chief judge and did not share authority with my subordinates; I received a generous salary, yet I did not share its benefits with those below me. Now that an error in judgment has led to the death of an innocent man, if I were to shift the blame onto my subordinates, it is a practice I have never heard justified." He refused to accept the order. Duke Wen said: "If you consider yourself guilty, am I also guilty?" Li Li replied: "As a judge, there are laws to follow. If one misapplies punishment, he himself should be punished; if one causes an innocent man's death, he himself deserves death. You appointed me as a judge because you believed I was capable of discerning subtle matters and resolving doubts. Now, having made an erroneous judgment that led to the killing of an innocent man, my crime deserves death." Thus, he refused to accept the pardon and committed suicide by drawing his sword.

## 12

lington The Grand Historian said: "A single word from Sun Shuao restored order in the markets of Ying. Zi Chan fell ill and died, and the people of Zheng wept in mourning. Gongyi Xi saw fine cloth and sent his wife away from home. Shi She released his father and died as a result, thus establishing the reputation of King Zhao of Chu. Li Li accepted punishment for an erroneous killing by committing suicide with a sword; Duke Wen of Jin thereby upheld the state's legal integrity.
