# Biography of Su Qin

## 1

Su Qin was a native of Luoyang, Eastern Zhou. He went east to study in Qi and learned from Master Guigu.

## 2

After traveling for several years, he returned home greatly impoverished. His brothers, sisters-in-law, and concubines secretly laughed at him, saying: "The custom of the people of Zhou is to manage property, work hard in commerce and industry, and pursue a ten or twenty percent profit as their main concern. Now you have abandoned the fundamentals and engaged in mere words and persuasion; thus your hardship is entirely appropriate!" Su Qin heard this and felt ashamed. He was distressed, so he shut himself in his room and did not go out, taking out all of his books to read through them thoroughly. He said: "A scholar who has already lowered his head to receive instruction but cannot thereby attain honor and prestige, even if he possesses much knowledge, what is it good for!" Thus he obtained the "Yin Fu" section of the Zhou Shu and read it diligently. After a year, having emerged to study and contemplate, he said: "This can be used to persuade the rulers of my time." He sought an audience with King Xian of Zhou to offer his advice. King Xian's attendants had long been familiar with Su Qin and all looked down on him. They did not believe in him.

## 3

He then traveled west to the state of Qin. King Xiao of Qin had died. Su Qin advised King Hui, saying: "Qin is a country enclosed on all sides by natural barriers. It is surrounded by mountains and the Wei River; to its east are the Guan and He rivers, to its west is Hanzhong, to its south are Ba and Shu, and to its north are Da Ma. This is a land of heavenly blessings." "With Qin's numerous people and soldiers, trained in the art of war, it can swallow all under heaven and proclaim itself emperor to rule." King Qin said: "Before a bird's feathers are fully grown, it cannot fly high; its principles and order have not yet been clarified, so it cannot unify or conquer others." lø At that time, Shang Yang was being punished, and eloquent men were disliked; Su Qin's advice was not accepted.

## 4

He then went east to Zhao. Zhao Su Hou appointed his younger brother Cheng as prime minister, who was given the title Fengyang Jun. Fengyang Jun did not like him.

## 5

He left and traveled to Yan, where after more than a year he was finally granted an audience. Su Qin advised King Wen of Yan, saying: "Yan has to the east Chaoxian and Liaodong, to the north Linhu and Loufan, to the west Yunzhong and Jiuyuan, and to the south the Pei River and Yishui. The territory spans more than two thousand li, with hundreds of thousands of armed soldiers, six hundred chariots, six thousand cavalry horses, and grain reserves sufficient for several years. To the south are the abundant resources of Jieshi and Yanmen, and to the north there is the advantage of jujubes and chestnuts; even if the people do not till or farm, they would still have enough from jujubes and chestnuts. This is what is called a land of heavenly blessings.

## 6

"Peace and ease without war, with no instances of defeated armies or slain generals, are not surpassed by any state other than Yan. Does Your Majesty know the reason why this is so? The reason Yan has not been invaded or subjected to armed conflict is because Zhao serves as a shield to its south. Between Qin and Zhao, there have been five battles; Qin won twice while Zhao won three times. When Qin and Zhao exhaust each other in mutual destruction, Your Majesty can control the situation from a secure Yan in the rear; this is why Yan has not suffered invasions. Moreover, if Qin were to attack Yan, it would have to cross Yunzhong and Jiuyuan and pass through Dai and Shanggu. The distance spans thousands of li; even if they captured Yan's cities, the Qin plan could not possibly hold them. It is clear that Qin cannot harm Yan. Now, if Zhao were to attack Yan and issue a call for troops, within no more than ten days an army of hundreds of thousands would be stationed at Dongyuan. Crossing the Hutuo River and wading through Yishui, within no more than four or five days they would reach the capital. Therefore it is said that if Qin attacks Yan, the battle will be fought thousands of li away; if Zhao attacks Yan, the battle takes place within a hundred li. To neglect a threat within a hundred li and instead worry about one thousands of li away is no greater miscalculation than this. Therefore, I wish Your Majesty to form a close alliance with Zhao and unite the whole world into one; then Yan will surely be free from all dangers."

## 7

King Wen said: "Your words are reasonable, but my state is small. To the west lies powerful Zhao, and to the south is Qi, both strong states. If you truly wish to form an alliance to ensure Yan's safety, I will certainly join with my state."

## 8

Thus he provided Su Qin with carriages, horses, gold, and silk so that he could proceed to Zhao. By this time, Fengyang Jun had already died. Su Qin then advised King Su of Zhao, saying: "All the high officials and ministers throughout the world, as well as common scholars, hold Your Majesty's virtue and righteousness in great esteem; for a long time they have wished to offer their instruction and loyalty before you. Nevertheless, because Fengyang Jun was in charge of affairs and Your Majesty did not personally assume responsibility, the guests and traveling scholars dared not fully devote themselves to you. Now that Fengyang Jun has passed away, Your Majesty is once again able to be close to the scholars and people. Therefore, I dare to present my humble thoughts.

## 9

"I secretly advise Your Majesty that there is nothing better than ensuring the people's peace and tranquility, and avoiding any need to impose burdens upon them. The foundation of ensuring the people's peace lies in choosing the right alliances; if an alliance is chosen wisely, the people will be at ease. If not, the people will remain unsettled for their entire lives. Please allow me to speak of external dangers: if Qi and Qin are both adversaries, the people cannot be at peace; if one leans on Qin to attack Qi, the people still cannot be at peace. If one leans on Qi to attack Qin, the people remain unsettled as well. Therefore, those who plot against a ruler and attack another state often find it difficult to issue justifications that sever the alliances of others. I hope Your Majesty will be cautious not to utter such words from your mouth. Please distinguish clearly between right and wrong, black and white; it is nothing more than the difference between yin and yang. If Your Majesty sincerely listens to me, Yan will certainly present its land of felt and furs as well as dogs and horses; Qi will offer the sea rich in fish and salt; Chu will contribute its groves of tangerines and oranges. Han, Wei, and Zhongshan can all be made to provide tribute for your bath and meals, and Your Majesty's noble relatives and uncles and elder brothers may all receive titles as marquises or dukes. The acquisition of territory for profit is what the Five Hegemons sought, even at the cost of defeated armies and captured generals; the granting of marquisates to noble relatives is what Tang and Wu contended for, even through rebellion or regicide. Now Your Majesty can sit back with both achievements secured; this is why I wish to serve you.

## 10

"If now Your Majesty allies with Qin, then Qin will certainly weaken Han and Wei; lords if you ally with Qi, then Qi will certainly weaken Chu and Wei. If Wei is weakened, it will cede the area outside the Yellow River; if Han is weakened, it will offer Yiyang. If Yiyang is offered, then Shangjun will be cut off. If the territory beyond the river is ceded, the route will become impassable. If Chu is weakened, there will be no support. These three strategies cannot fail to be carefully considered.

## 11

"If Qin sends troops down the Zhi Road, then Nanyang is in danger; if it attacks Han and encircles Zhou, then Zhao will naturally take up arms; if it occupies Wei and captures Juan, then Qi must submit to Qin. If Qin has already gained control of the area east of Mount Taihang (Shandong), it will certainly raise its army and turn against Zhao. If Qin's troops cross the Yellow River, pass through Zhang River, and occupy Fanwu, then a battle will surely take place beneath Handan. This is why I am concerned for Your Majesty.

## 12

"At present, among the states east of Mount Taihang, none are stronger than Zhao. Zhao's territory spans more than two thousand square li, with hundreds of thousands in armed forces, a thousand chariots, ten thousand cavalry horses, and enough grain to last for several years. To the west are the Changshan Mountains, to the south lie the He and Zhang rivers, to the east is the Qinghe River, and to the north lies Yan State. Yan is originally a weak state; it is not worth fearing. Of all the states in the world, Qin poses the greatest threat to none more than Zhao; yet why does Qin dare not raise its army to attack Zhao? It is because they fear that Han and Wei will act against them from behind. Therefore, Han and Wei are Zhao's southern shield. When Qin attacks Han and Wei, there is no barrier of great mountains or rivers to hinder it; gradually they will devour their territories until reaching the capital cities. Han and Wei cannot resist Qin and must submit as vassals to Qin. If Qin does not have Han and Wei to serve its interests, then the disaster will surely fall upon Zhao. This is why I am worried for Your Majesty.

## 13

"I have heard that Yao did not possess the share of three men, and Shun had no land even as small as a chi in size, yet they both ruled over all under heaven; Yu did not command an assembly of one hundred men but still became the ruler of the feudal lords; Tang and Wu had no more than three thousand soldiers, no more than three hundred chariots, and no more than thirty thousand footmen, yet they became Son of Heaven: this was because they truly grasped the right way. Therefore, a wise ruler assesses the strength and weakness of his enemies from outside, and measures within whether his soldiers are virtuous or not. He does not need to wait for two armies to face each other before he already knows in his heart the outcome—victory or defeat, survival or destruction. How could such a ruler be swayed by the words of ordinary people and make decisions blindly!

## 14

"I have secretly examined the maps of all the lands in the world. The combined territory of the feudal states is five times that of Qin, and their estimated number of soldiers is ten times greater than Qin's. If the six states unite into one and combine their strength to attack Qin from the west, then Qin will surely be defeated. Now if we bow to the west in submission and present ourselves as subjects of Qin, the difference between breaking others and being broken by others, or between ruling others and being ruled by others—how can these be compared on the same day!

## 15

"The advocates of horizontal alliances all wish to carve up the territories of the feudal states and give them to Qin. Once Qin succeeds, they will build high terraces and beautiful palaces, listen to the music of yu and se flutes, have pavilions and gates in front, and behind them be surrounded by tall beauties. The state suffers from Qin's aggression but does not share its burdens or worries. Therefore, these advocates of horizontal alliances work day and night to use Qin's power to intimidate the feudal states in order to obtain territorial concessions. Hence, I hope Your Majesty will carefully consider this matter.

## 16

"I have heard that a wise ruler cuts off doubts and dispels slander, removes the traces of malicious rumors, and blocks the doors to factionalism. Therefore, plans for honoring the sovereign, expanding territory, and strengthening the military can be presented by loyal ministers before him. Therefore, I secretly advise Your Majesty: there is nothing better than uniting Han, Wei, Qi, Chu, Yan, and Zhao into a close alliance to resist Qin. Let the generals and ministers of all states gather by the banks of the Huang Shui River, exchange hostages, slaughter a white horse, and swear an oath. The agreement shall be: 'If Qin attacks Chu, Qi and Wei will each send their elite troops to assist; Han will cut off its supply lines; Zhao will cross the He and Zhang Rivers; Yan will guard the northern part of Changshan. If Qin attacks Han and Wei, then Chu will cut off its rear; Qi will send elite troops to assist, Zhao will cross the He and Zhang rivers again, and Yan will guard Yunzhong. If Qin attacks Qi, then Chu will cut off the rear; Han will guard Chenggao; Wei will block its route; Zhao will cross He Zhang and Boguan Pass; Yan will send elite troops to support. If Qin attacks Yan, then Zhao will guard Changshan; Chu's army will station at Wuguan Pass; Qi will cross Bohai Sea; Han and Wei will both send elite troops to support. If Qin attacks Zhao, then Han's army will station at Yiyang; Chu's troops will be stationed at Wuguan Pass; Wei's forces will occupy the area beyond the Yellow River; Qi will cross Qinghe River; Yan will send its elite soldiers to support. If any feudal state fails to abide by this agreement, the armies of the five states shall jointly attack it.' With the six states united in a close alliance against Qin, then surely Qin's troops will not dare to cross Hangu Pass and threaten the lands east of Mount Taihang. If this is done, then the great cause of hegemony or kingship will be accomplished."

## 17

King Zhao said: "I am young and have ruled the state for only a short time, so I have never heard of long-term strategies for preserving our nation. Now that my honored guest has such lofty intentions to preserve the world and bring peace to the feudal lords, I respectfully offer my state in alliance." Thus he prepared a hundred carriages, one thousand yu of gold, a hundred pairs of white jade discs, and a thousand bolts of brocade to form an alliance with the feudal lords.

## 18

At that time, the Zhou emperor presented sacrifices from Wenwang and Wuwang to King Hui of Qin. King Hui sent Xi Shou to attack Wei, captured the general Long Jia, seized Diao Yin from Wei, and was about to launch troops eastward. Su Qin feared that Qin's army would march on Zhao, so he incited Zhang Yi and sent him to Qin.

## 19

Thus, he advised King Xuan of Han: "To the north, Han has the strongholds of Gong and Chenggao; to the west, it controls Yiyang and Shangban Passes; to the east are Wan, Rang, and Weishui River; to the south lies Xing Mountain. The territory spans more than nine hundred li, with tens of thousands of armed soldiers. All the powerful crossbows and strong bows in the world come from Han. The Xi Zi bow, the Shao Fu Shili bow, and the Ju Lai bow can all shoot beyond six hundred bu. Han soldiers could leap forward to shoot, firing a hundred arrows without pause; those at long range would pierce the chest through armor, and those nearby would strike straight into the heart. The swords and halberds of Han soldiers all come from Ming Shan, Tang Xi, Mo Yang, He Fu, Deng Shi, Wan Feng, Long Yuan, and Tai E. These weapons can sever oxen and horses on land and cut through cranes and geese in water; when facing the enemy, they can slash through tough armor and iron shields, cutting through leather armors and thick padding without fail. All such equipment is fully prepared. With the bravery of Han soldiers, clad in strong armor, armed with powerful crossbows and sharp swords, one man can match a hundred; this is not an exaggeration. Given Han's strength and Your Majesty's virtue, yet still bowing westward to serve Qin, clasping arms in submission, disgracing the state and becoming a laughingstock for all under heaven—there is no greater disgrace than this. Therefore, I hope Your Majesty will carefully consider this matter.

## 20

"If Your Majesty serves Qin, then Qin will certainly demand Yiyang and Chenggao. If we give them up now, next year they will again demand more territory to be ceded. To comply would leave us with no land left to give; not to comply would mean abandoning our previous efforts and inviting future disaster. Moreover, Your Majesty's territory is limited while Qin's demands are endless. To oppose these unceasing demands with a finite amount of land is to invite resentment and disaster; even without war, the land will already be lost. I have heard an old saying: "Better to be the mouth of a chicken than the behind of an ox." Now, bowing westward and serving Qin as a vassal—what is this different from being at the back of an ox? With Your Majesty's virtue and the powerful Han army at hand, yet to bear the name of being behind an ox—I secretly feel ashamed for Your Majesty."

## 21

At this, King Han became greatly indignant, raised his arm in anger, glared fiercely, gripped his sword, and looked up to the sky with a deep sigh; "I may be unworthy, but I certainly will not serve Qin. "Now that Your Lordship has conveyed King Zhao's instructions, I respectfully offer the state in alliance."

## 22

He then advised King Xiang of Wei: "Your Majesty's territory, to the south includes Honggou, Chen, Runan, Xu, Yan, Kunyang, Shaoling, Wuyang, Xindu, and Xinqi; to the east lies the Huai River, Ying River, Zhu Zao, and Wu Xu; to the west is the boundary of the Great Wall; to the north are the areas beyond the Yellow River, Juan, Yan, and Suanzao. The land spans a thousand li in area. Though the territory may seem small by name, yet the number of fields, houses, and granaries is so great that there are no pastures or fodder left unutilized. The population is numerous, and the carriages and horses are countless; day and night they travel without ceasing, with a constant hum of activity, as if an entire army were present. I secretly estimate that Your Majesty's state is no less than Chu in strength. Yet the balance-makers have frightened Your Majesty into allying with the fierce tiger and wolf of Qin to invade all under heaven, ultimately facing a threat from Qin without considering its consequences. To use the power of strong Qin as leverage to internally intimidate one's ruler is an offense with no greater guilt. Wei is a powerful state in all under heaven; Your Majesty is a virtuous ruler of all under heaven. Now, however, there is an intention to bow westward and serve Qin as a vassal state in the east, building imperial palaces, accepting royal attire, performing spring and autumn sacrifices—this secretly brings shame upon Your Majesty.

## 23

"I have heard that King Goujian of Yue led a weary army of three thousand men and captured Fu Chai at Gansui; King Wu led three thousand soldiers and three hundred chariots, and subdued Zhou at Muye: was it because of the large number of troops? No—it was truly due to his ability to inspire their might. Now I have secretly learned that Your Majesty's forces include twenty thousand elite warriors, twenty thousand armored soldiers, twenty thousand shock troops, ten thousand attendants and laborers, six hundred chariots, and five thousand cavalry horses. This far surpasses the forces of King Goujian and King Wu; yet now, Your Majesty listens to your ministers' arguments and wishes to serve Qin as a subject. To serve Qin necessarily requires ceding territory as proof, so the state is already weakened even before any war has been fought. All ministers who advise serving Qin are villains, not loyal subjects. To be a minister who severs his ruler's territory to seek foreign alliances, secretly gaining temporary success without considering the consequences, damaging public interests for private gain, and using the power of mighty Qin from outside to intimidate one's sovereign internally in order to demand territorial concessions—Your Majesty should carefully examine such matters.

## 24

"The Book of Zhou says: 'When the threads are endless, how can one deal with the spreading vines? 'If a single hair is not cut, it will eventually require an axe to sever the trunk.'" "If one does not decide on precautions beforehand, great disasters will follow—how can this be handled?" If Your Majesty truly listens to me, the six states will form a close alliance, uniting our strength and focus—then surely we shall be free from Qin's threat. Therefore, my lord King Zhao of Zhao has sent me to offer this humble strategy and present a clear agreement, awaiting Your Majesty's command."

## 25

King Wei said: "I am unworthy and have never had the opportunity to hear such wise instruction. Now that Your Lordship has delivered King Zhao's decree, I respectfully place my state in alliance."

## 26

He then went east to advise King Xuan of Qi: "To the south, Qi has Mount Tai; to the east is Langya; to the west lies Qinghe River; and to the north is Bohai Sea. This is what is known as a state surrounded by four natural barriers. Qi's territory spans over two thousand li, with armed soldiers in the tens of thousands and grain stores as abundant as hills and mountains. The elite forces of Qi's three armies, the five renowned military units, advance like sharp arrows, fight with thunderous force, and retreat as swiftly as wind and rain. Even in times of military service, they have never crossed Mount Tai, traversed the Qinghe River, or sailed across Bohai Sea. In Linzi alone, there are seventy thousand households. I secretly estimate that each household has at least three able-bodied men; seven times three is twenty-one. Thus, without needing to mobilize from distant counties, the soldiers of Linzi already number no less than twenty-one thousand. Linzi is very wealthy and prosperous, with its people all engaged in playing the yu flute, plucking zithers, striking qin, beating chimes, cockfighting, dog racing, playing six-boards games, and playing cuju. The streets of Linzi are so crowded that wheels bump into each other, shoulders press against one another; the folds of robes form curtains and sleeves create canopies, sweat falls like rain. Every household is prosperous and its people well-provided for, with high spirits and proud bearing. With Your Majesty's virtue combined with Qi's strength, no state under heaven can rival it. Yet now to bow westward and serve Qin—this secretly brings great shame upon Your Majesty.

## 27

"Moreover, the reason why Han and Wei fear Qin so greatly is because they share borders with it. When armies meet on the battlefield, within no more than ten days the outcome of victory or defeat—and thus survival or destruction—is decided. If Han and Wei win against Qin, their armies will be half destroyed, and they will no longer be able to defend their borders; if they lose the battle, then national peril and destruction will surely follow. This is why Han and Wei are reluctant to fight Qin but easily choose to become its vassals. But when it comes to Qin's attack on Qi, the situation is different. To attack Qi, Qin must cross the territories of Han and Wei, pass through Weiyang and Jin Dao, traverse the perilous terrain of Kangfu. The roads are so narrow that chariots cannot travel side by side, and cavalry cannot march in formation; a hundred men defending such a pass can prevent even a thousand from passing. Even if Qin wishes to launch a deep invasion, it must constantly look back in fear of Han and Wei attacking its rear. Therefore, Qin hesitates with suspicion and false threats, displaying arrogance yet daring not to advance; it is clear that Qin cannot harm Qi.

## 28

"To fail to thoroughly assess that Qin can do nothing against Qi, yet still wish to bow westward and serve it—this is a grave miscalculation by the ministers. Now, we can avoid the name of serving Qin while enjoying the reality of being a strong state; this is why I hope Your Majesty will give careful thought to this matter."

## 29

King Qi said: "I am not wise, a remote ruler guarding the sea in this land of narrow roads at the eastern frontier, who has never had the opportunity to receive such instruction. Now that you have delivered King Zhao's decree on behalf of Zhao, I respectfully place my state under your alliance."

## 30

He then traveled southwest to advise King Wei of Chu: "Chu is a powerful state in all of China; Your Majesty, you are the virtuous ruler of this world. To the west are Qianzhong and Wujun; to the east lie Xia Zhou and Haiyang; to the south are Dongting and Cangwu; to the north stand Xing Sai and Xunyang. The territory spans over five thousand li, with a million armed soldiers, one thousand chariots, ten thousand cavalry horses, and grain reserves sufficient for ten years. This is the foundation of hegemony or kingship. With Chu's strength and Your Majesty's wisdom, no state in the world can rival you. Yet now to bow westward in service to Qin would mean that all feudal lords will also face west and pay homage at the gates of Zhangtai.

## 31

"Of all states, Qin has no greater enemy than Chu; when Chu is strong, Qin becomes weak; when Qin grows powerful, Chu must weaken. Their positions cannot coexist. Therefore, for Your Majesty's benefit, there is no better plan than to form a close alliance and isolate Qin. If Your Majesty does not join the alliance, then Qin will surely launch two armies: one to emerge from Wuguan Pass and another to descend through Qianzhong; in that case, Yanying will be thrown into turmoil.

## 32

"I have heard that one should manage affairs before they become chaotic and act before problems arise. Once a disaster has arrived, it is too late to worry about it. Therefore, I hope Your Majesty considers this matter carefully and promptly.

## 33

"If Your Majesty truly heeds my advice, I shall ensure the states east of Mount Tai present their seasonal tributes to honor your wise decree, entrust their nations and ancestral temples, train soldiers and sharpen weapons—all at Your Majesty's command. 𝑙 If Your Majesty sincerely adopts my humble strategy, then the finest music and beautiful women from Han, Wei, Qi, Yan, Zhao, and Wei will surely fill your harem; fine camels and superior horses from Yan and Dai will certainly populate your stables. Therefore, if the vertical alliance is formed, Chu shall become king; if the horizontal alliance succeeds, Qin shall ascend as emperor. Now to abandon the great cause of kingship and hegemony while bearing the name of serving others—I secretly consider this unacceptable for Your Majesty.

## 34

"Qin is a state of tigers and wolves, harboring ambitions to swallow all under heaven. Qin is the sworn enemy of all China. Those who advocate for horizontal alliances all wish to cede territory from the feudal states to serve Qin; this is what is called nourishing an enemy and serving a foe. To be a minister who carves up one's ruler's territory to form an alliance with mighty, tiger-like Qin and invade all under heaven—only to eventually face the threat of Qin without considering its consequences—is most grievous. To use the might of mighty Qin externally to intimidate one's ruler internally and demand territorial concessions is an act of great treachery and disloyalty, with no greater crime than this. Therefore, if a vertical alliance is formed, the feudal lords will cede land to serve Chu; but if a horizontal alliance is made, then Chu must cede its own territory to serve Qin. These two strategies are vastly different—between these two options, which does Your Majesty choose? Therefore, my lord King Zhao has sent me to present this humble strategy and offer a clear agreement; it is now at Your Majesty's discretion."

## 35

King Chu said: "My state lies to the west in direct border with Qin, and Qin has ambitions to conquer Ba, Shu, and unify Hanzhong. Qin is a land of tigers and wolves; it cannot be trusted. And Han and Wei are pressured by the threat from Qin; we cannot engage in deep planning with them, for if we do, they might betray us to Qin. Thus, before our plans can be made public, our state is already in peril. I estimate that with my own strength, if I were to confront Qin alone, I would not see victory; and relying solely on internal counsel from ministers is insufficient. I lie awake in bed, unable to rest; I eat without savoring the taste of food. My heart is restless like a banner hanging in the wind with no direction or final destination. Now, if Your Majesty wishes to unify all under heaven, bring the feudal lords together, and preserve endangered states, I respectfully offer my state's ancestral temples and land in obedience."

## 36

Thus, the six states formed a vertical alliance and united their strength. Su Qin became the leader of the vertical alliance and served as chancellor to all six states.

## 37

While heading north to report to King Zhao, Su Qin passed through Luoyang. His retinue of chariots and horses, along with supplies, was so grand that many envoys from the feudal lords sent off to see him, their numbers resembling those of a king's procession. King Xian of Zhou heard about this and was frightened; he cleared the road and sent envoys to meet Su Qin in the suburbs with honors. Su Qin's brothers, sisters, wife, and sister-in-law looked at him sideways without daring to raise their eyes; they knelt in submission, waiting on him as he ate. Su Qin laughed and said to his sister-in-law, "Why were you so arrogant before but now so respectful?" His sister-in-law bowed low with a snake-like posture, covering her face with the ground and apologized, saying: "It is because I see that Younger Brother Ji has attained high rank and much wealth." Su Qin sighed deeply and said, "In this one person's life, when he is rich and noble, relatives fear him; but when poor and lowly, they treat him lightly. How much more so for the common people! If I had merely two qing of farmland within the walls of Luoyang, how could I possibly wear the seals of chancellor to six states!" Thus, he distributed a thousand gold pieces to bestow upon his clan and friends. Initially, when Su Qin went to Yan, he borrowed a hundred coins as capital; after attaining wealth and rank, he repaid the debt with a hundred gold pieces. He thoroughly repaid all those who had once shown him kindness or favor. Among his followers, one man alone remained unrewarded and thus stepped forward to speak for himself. Su Qin said, "I have not forgotten you. When you accompanied me to Yan, you repeatedly wanted to leave me on the banks of the Yi River. At that time, I was in dire straits and thus held a deep resentment toward you; this is why I delayed rewarding you. "You have now also received your reward."

## 38

After Su Qin had concluded the vertical alliance among the six states, he returned to Zhao. King Su of Zhao bestowed upon him the title Lord Wu'an, and then sent the treaty of the alliance to Qin. For fifteen years, Qin forces dared not look toward Hangu Pass.

## 39

Later on, Qin sent Xi Shou to deceive Qi and Wei, joining them in an attack against Zhao, intending to break the vertical alliance. Qi and Wei attacked Zhao, and King Zhao rebuked Su Qin. Su Qin was afraid and requested to be sent as an envoy to Yan, vowing to take revenge on Qi. After Su Qin left Zhao, the vertical alliance collapsed entirely.

## 40

King Hui of Qin gave his daughter in marriage to the crown prince of Yan. In this year, King Wen of Yan died and the crown prince ascended the throne; he is known as King Yi of Yan. When King Yi first came to power, King Xuan of Qi took advantage of Yan's mourning period and attacked it, seizing ten cities. King Yi said to Su Qin: "In the past, when you came to Yan, the late king provided resources for you to visit Zhao, and thus you concluded the alliance of the six states. Now Qi first attacked Zhao, then came to Yan; because of you, we have become the laughingstock of all under heaven. Can you recover the lost territories for Yan?" Su Qin was deeply ashamed and said: "Please allow me to retrieve them for Your Majesty."

## 41

Su Qin met with King Qi, bowed twice, knelt low in celebration, then looked up and mourned. King Qi said: "Why does celebration follow mourning so quickly?" Su Qin said, "I have heard that a starving person refuses to eat the root of the urushi plant because although it would fill his belly, it would still result in death by starvation. Now Yan is weak and small, yet it is the young son-in-law of King Qin. Your Majesty benefits from its ten cities but will thus become a long-term enemy of mighty Qin. Now, by making weak Yan the vanguard and having powerful Qin follow behind to draw in the elite troops of all under heaven, it is like eating the urushi root." King Qi's expression turned grave as he asked: "Then what should we do?" Su Qin said, "I have heard that those skilled in managing affairs in ancient times could turn misfortune into fortune and transform defeat into success. If Your Majesty sincerely heeds my strategy, you will return the ten cities to Yan. Yan would gain ten cities without cause and must be pleased; King Qin, knowing that Yan has returned the ten cities because of him, will also surely be pleased. This is what is called abandoning an enemy to gain a firm friend. If Yan and Qin both serve Qi, then Your Majesty's commands will be heeded by all under heaven without exception. This way, Your Majesty would gain the allegiance of Qin with mere words and secure dominion over all China through ten cities. This is the great cause of a king or hegemon." The king said, "Well spoken." Thus, he returned the ten cities to Yan.

## 42

There were people who slandered Su Qin, saying: "He is a fickle minister who sells out the state; he will soon start a rebellion." Fearing punishment upon his return, Su Qin found that King Yan no longer wished to grant him an official post. Su Qin presented himself before King Yan and said: "I am a humble man from the Eastern Zhou, with not even an inch of merit; yet Your Majesty personally honored me in the ancestral temple and bestowed courtesy upon me in court. Now I have repelled Qi's army for Your Majesty and [recovered] ten cities; this should make our bond even closer. Yet now that I have returned, if Your Majesty does not grant me an official position, it must be because someone has sown distrust and harmed my reputation with you. If I am untrustworthy, that is Your Majesty's good fortune. I have heard that loyalty and trustworthiness are for one's own sake; ambition and striving are for the benefit of others. Moreover, when I advised King Qi, I did not deceive him at all. I left my aged mother behind in the Eastern Zhou; this was to pursue personal sacrifice for the sake of ambition and advancement. Now, even if one is as filial as Zeng Shen, as upright as Boyi, or as trustworthy as Weisheng, if they were to serve Your Majesty, how would that be?" The king said: "That would be sufficient." Su Qin said, "One as filial as Zeng Shen would not stay away from his parents for even a single night; how could Your Majesty possibly send such a person on foot thousands of miles to serve a weak and endangered king of Yan? One as upright as Boyi would not accept the succession of the Guzhu lineage, refused to serve King Wu, declined a marquis title, and died of starvation beneath Shouyang Mountain. With such integrity, how could Your Majesty send him on foot thousands of miles to pursue advancement in Qi? One as trustworthy as Weisheng made an appointment with a woman beneath a bridge; when the woman did not arrive, even as water rose and threatened to drown him, he refused to leave, holding onto the pillar until death. With such trustworthiness, how could Your Majesty possibly send him on foot thousands of miles alone to repel Qi's mighty army? This is precisely what I mean by being punished for loyalty and trustworthiness." King Yan said, "If you are not loyal or trustworthy, how could one possibly be punished for being so?" Su Qin said: "Not at all. I have heard of a guest who was a distant official, and his wife committed adultery. When her husband returned, the lover worried about this; the wife said, "Do not worry—I have already prepared poisoned wine for him." Three days later, her husband indeed arrived. The wife sent a concubine to raise the poisoned wine and offer it to him. The concubine wanted to warn that there was poison in the wine, but feared her mistress would drive her away; yet if she did not speak up, she feared her master might be killed. Thus, she pretended to collapse and discarded the wine. The master became furious and beat her fifty times with a bamboo stick. Therefore, the concubine's collapse and spilling of wine preserved her master above and her mistress below; yet she could not escape punishment. Where then is the innocence in loyalty and trustworthiness? My offense, I fear, has unfortunately resembled this!" King Yan said: "Master Su, return to your former post." He treated him even more generously.

## 43

King Yi's mother, the wife of King Wen, had an affair with Su Qin. King Yan learned about it but treated him even more kindly. Fearing punishment, Su Qin advised King Yan: "While I am in Yan, I cannot make Yan influential; but if I go to Qi, Yan will surely gain influence." King Yan said: "Do as you wish, Master Su." Thus, Su Qin pretended to have offended the court of Yan and fled to Qi. King Xuan appointed him as a guest minister.

## 44

After King Xuan of Qi died, King Min ascended the throne. Su Qin advised him to lavish burials in order to demonstrate filial piety and build grand palaces and vast gardens to show his pride, intending thereby to weaken Qi for the benefit of Yan. King Yi of Yan died, and Yan Kuai succeeded him as king. Later on, many Qi officials vied with Su Qin for favor and sent assassins to kill him; he was wounded but not killed and fled. King of Qi sent men to search for the assassin, but they could not find him. On the verge of death, Su Qin said to King Qi: "When I die, have my body torn apart by chariots and displayed in the marketplace with a notice saying 'Su Qin stirred up chaos for Yan in Qi,' and if you do this, then the assassin who killed me will surely be caught." Thus they followed his words, and the man who had killed Su Qin indeed came forward; King Qi then executed him. Yan heard of this and said: "How deeply Qi has avenged Su Qin!"

## 45

After Su Qin's death, his affairs were fully exposed. Later Qi learned of this and became furious with Yan. Yan was greatly frightened. Su Qin's younger brother was named Dai; Dai had a younger brother, Su Li. Seeing their elder brother succeed, they too studied diligently. After Su Qin died, Dai requested an audience with King Yan and wished to follow in his elder brother's footsteps. He said: "I am a humble man from the eastern frontier of Zhou." "I have heard that Your Majesty's sense of righteousness is very high. Though I am not intelligent, I have abandoned my farming tools to seek an audience with you." "When I arrived at Handan, what I saw fell short of what I had heard about in the Eastern Zhou; secretly, I felt my aspirations were unfulfilled." "When I arrived at Yan's court and observed Your Majesty's ministers and officials, I realized that you are indeed a wise ruler of the world." King Yan asked: "What is it like to be the wise ruler you mentioned?" He replied: "I have heard that a wise ruler is eager to hear of his faults and not so eager to hear about his virtues. I would like to point out Your Majesty's faults." Qi and Zhao are Yan's sworn enemies; Chu and Wei are Yan's allied states. Now, Your Majesty supports the enemies to attack the allies; this is not a way to benefit Yan. "If the king himself considers it carefully, then this plan is mistaken. Those who do not speak out about such matters are not loyal ministers." The king said: "Qi has long been my enemy, the one I wish to attack; I am only concerned that our state is weak and lacks sufficient strength." "If you can lead Yan in attacking Qi, then I will entrust the entire country to you." He replied: "Among all the warring states of the world there are seven, and Yan is the weakest among them. If we fight alone, we cannot succeed; but if we have an ally, then nothing will be impossible." "Allying with the south to Chu, and Chu is strong; allying with the west to Qin, and Qin is strong; allying in the center with Han and Wei, and Han and Wei are strong." Moreover, if we ally ourselves with a powerful state, this will surely make Your Majesty influential. "Now Qi is ruled by an arrogant and self-willed sovereign. Its southern campaign against Chu lasted five years, exhausting its resources; its western struggle with Qin lasted three years, wearing out its soldiers and weakening the state; and in the north it fought against Yan, destroying three armies and capturing two generals. Yet with its remaining forces, Qi turned southward to conquer a great state of Song with 5000 chariots and encircled twelve feudal lords. Its ruler is greedy, its people are exhausted—how could it not be vulnerable to attack!" Moreover, I have heard that frequent warfare tires the people and prolonged military campaigns weaken the army." King Yan said: "I have heard that Qi has the clear Ji River and muddy He River to serve as strong defenses, and its Great Wall and massive fortifications are sufficient for defense. Is this true?" He replied: "If Heaven does not favor us, even with the clear Ji and muddy He Rivers, how could they be sufficient as defenses! The people's strength is exhausted; even if there are the Great Wall and massive fortifications, how could they serve as barriers!" Moreover, in the past, Qi did not station troops at the western bank of the Ji River to guard against Zhao; nor did it station troops along the northern He River to guard against Yan. Now, all the forces at the western Ji and northern He Rivers have been deployed, and within its borders, Qi is exhausted. A conceited ruler must be fond of profit, and ministers who bring about the downfall of a state are always greedy for wealth. "If Your Majesty can truly bear the shame of sending your son or younger brother as a hostage, and offer precious jewels and silk to please Qi's court officials, they will feel indebted to Yan and neglect Song. Then Qi can be destroyed." King Yan said: "I shall finally entrust my fate to Heaven through you." Yan then sent one of its princes as a hostage to Qi. Meanwhile, Su Li used the Yan prince in captivity to request an audience with King Qi. King Qi resented Su Qin and wanted to arrest Su Li. The Yan hostage prince pleaded on his behalf, and thus Su Li submitted himself as a subject of Qi.

## 46

Yan's prime minister Zi Zhi was married to Su Dai, and wished to gain power in Yan; thus he sent Su Dai to serve the Yan hostage prince in Qi. Qi sent Dai back to Yan as an envoy, and King Kuai of Yan asked: "Will King Qi become the hegemon?" He replied: "No." King Kuai asked: "Why not?" He said: "Because he does not trust his ministers." Thus King Yan entrusted full authority to Zi Zhi, and later abdicated the throne; this caused great chaos in Yan. Qi attacked Yan and killed King Kuai and Zi Zhi. Yan enthroned King Zhao, and Su Dai and Su Li dared not return to Yan; they both eventually went back to Qi, where they were treated kindly.

## 47

While passing through Wei, Su Dai was arrested by Wei on behalf of Yan. Qi sent an envoy to King Wei and said: "Qi is willing to offer the territory of Song as a fief for Lord Jingyang, but Qin will certainly refuse. "It's not that Qin is unwilling to benefit from an alliance with Qi and obtain the land of Song; it is because they do not trust King Qi or Su Zi. "Now that Qi and Wei are so deeply at odds, then Qi will not deceive Qin. "If Qin trusts Qi, and Qi and Qin unite, Lord Jingyang would obtain the land of Song, which is not in Wei's interest. "Therefore, Your Majesty should release Su Zi to the east; Qin will then doubt Qi and no longer trust Su Zi." "If Qi and Qin do not unite, there will be no major changes in the world, and a coalition to attack Qi will form." Thus Wei released Su Dai. Dai went to Song, and Song treated him well.

## 48

When Qi attacked Song, the situation in Song became dire; Su Dai then sent a letter to King Zhao of Yan saying:

## 49

"Being one of the great warring states yet sending hostages to Qi, our name is humiliated and our authority weakened; supporting Qi with a state of ten thousand chariots in its attack on Song exhausts the people and wastes resources; "Destroying Song, devastating northern Chu along the Huai River, and strengthening Qi—this will create a powerful enemy that harms our state. These three outcomes are all great disasters for the country." Yet Your Majesty still carries this out, in order to gain Qi's trust. "Qi will become even more distrustful of Your Majesty and resent Yan all the more; this is a miscalculation on your part." "To add Song and northern Huai to Qi, which is already a powerful state of ten thousand chariots, will make it even stronger—this only adds another Qi." "The northern Yi tribes cover seven hundred li, and when combined with Lu and Wei, they form another powerful state of ten thousand chariots. If Qi annexes them, this adds yet another Qi." "With the strength of one Qi, Yan is already forced to look back in fear and cannot resist; now with three Qis facing Yan, the disaster will surely be great."

## 50

"Nevertheless, a wise man undertakes actions by turning misfortune into fortune and transforming failure into success. "For example, purple cloth from Qi is dyed silk of inferior quality, yet it sells for ten times the price; King Goujian of Yue, after being driven to Kuaiji, eventually destroyed the mighty state of Wu and became hegemon over all under heaven—these are examples of turning misfortune into fortune, transforming failure into success."

## 51

"Now, if Your Majesty wishes to turn misfortune into fortune and transform failure into success, there is no better course than to provoke Qi's hegemony and honor it. Send envoys to make an alliance with the Zhou royal house, burn Qin's diplomatic tokens, and declare: 'The highest strategy is to destroy Qin; the next best option is certainly to extend lasting hospitality.'" "By holding out the promise of an alliance while waiting for Qin's defeat, King Qin will surely be concerned. "Qin has attacked the feudal lords for five generations, and now it is beneath Qi. If King Qin can achieve his ambition of defeating Qi, he will not hesitate to sacrifice his state for success. "Then why does Your Majesty not send a capable orator to King Qin with these words: 'Yan and Zhao have helped Qi by destroying Song, placing it above themselves. But Yan and Zhao do this out of self-interest, not because they truly benefit from it. "They are compelled to act in this way despite their own disadvantage, because they distrust King Qin." "If that is the case, why does Your Majesty not appoint a trustworthy person to negotiate with Yan and Zhao, sending Lord Jingyang and Lord Gaoling ahead to establish relations with them?" "If Qin makes changes, use this as a pretext to take hostages; then Yan and Zhao will trust Qin. "Qin becomes the Western Emperor, Yan the Northern Emperor, and Zhao the Central Emperor; establishing three emperors to issue commands over all under heaven. "If Han and Wei refuse, then Qin will attack them; if Qi refuses, Yan and Zhao will attack it. Who in the world would dare to defy?" "When all under heaven submit and obey, we can then lead Han and Wei in attacking Qi, declaring: 'You must return the land of Song to its rightful owner and restore northern Chu along the Huai.'" "Returning the land of Song and restoring northern Chu are in Yan and Zhao's interests; and establishing three emperors is what Yan and Zhao desire. "Since they actually gain their desired benefits and achieve their ambitions, Yan and Zhao would abandon Qi as easily as discarding a slipper." "Now, if we do not bring in Yan and Zhao, the hegemony of Qi will surely be established. "If other feudal lords support Qi while Your Majesty does not follow suit, it would amount to a declaration of war against your state; "if you do follow them, then your status will be lowered." "Now, by bringing in Yan and Zhao, your state will enjoy peace and your name will gain respect; "if you do not bring them in, your state will be endangered and your status lowered." "To abandon security and honor for danger and lowliness is something a wise man would never do.'" "When King Qin hears this argument, he will surely feel as if it pierces his heart. "So why does Your Majesty not dispatch a capable orator to present these words to King Qin?" "Then Qin will certainly accept, and Qi will inevitably be attacked."

## 52

"Gaining the support of Qin means a strong alliance; "attacking Qi is in your direct interest." "Honoring a strong ally and pursuing one's own just interests—this is the conduct of a sage ruler.

## 53

Yan Zhao Wang appreciated his letter and said, "My ancestors once had a favor from the Su family. During the chaos of Duke Wu's usurpation, the Su clan left Yan. "Now that Yan wishes to take revenge on Qi, no one is more suitable than the Su family.'" "He then summoned Su Dai and treated him well once again, consulting with him about attacking Qi." "Eventually they defeated Qi, and King Min fled the country.

## 54

"After a long time, Qin summoned King Yan. King Yan wanted to go, but Su Dai advised the king: "Chu gained Zhi yet lost its state; Qi gained Song yet also lost its state. Why is it that neither Qi nor Chu could retain their states despite having obtained Zhi and Song, and why did they still have to submit to Qin? What was the reason?" "It is because those who achieve great success become Qin's bitter enemies. "Qin's conquest of the world was not through righteousness, but through brutality. "When Qin acts brutally, it openly declares its intentions to all under heaven.

## 55

"'Announce to Chu: 'The troops from Shu, traveling by boat along the Wen River and descending via the summer floods of the Yangtze, can reach Ying in five days. "The troops from Hanzhong, traveling by boat out of Ba and descending the summer floods along the Han River, can reach Wuzhu in four days. "My troops are gathering at Wan and advancing eastward to Sui; even the wisest cannot devise a strategy, and even the bravest warriors cannot muster anger. I will strike them down like shooting a falcon.'" "'Your Majesty wishes to wait until all under heaven attacks Hangu Pass—does this not seem too distant!'" "For this reason, King Chu submitted to Qin for seventeen years.

## 56

"'Qin openly declared to Han: 'I will march from Shaoqu and in one day I can cut through Daxing Pass. "I will march out of Yiyang to strike Pingyang, and within two days the entire region will be under my control. "I will separate the Two Zhou states and attack Zheng; in five days, your state will fall.'" "The Han clan believed this to be true, so they submitted to Qin.

## 57

"'Qin openly declared to Wei: 'I will capture Anyi, block the Nuji Pass, and Han's Taiyuan region will be rolled up. "I will descend to Zhi, pass through Nanyang, reach Fengji, and encircle the Two Zhou states. "Traveling by summer floods in light boats, with crossbows at the front and halberds at the rear, I will breach Yingkou. Wei will lose Daliang; "breaching the Bai Ma sluice gate, Wei will lose Waihuang and Jiyang; "and breaching Su Xu's sluice, Wei will lose Xu and Dunqiu. "If I attack by land, I shall strike He Nei; if by water, I shall destroy Daliang.'" "The Wei clan believed this to be true and submitted to Qin."

## 58

"'If Qin wants to attack Anyi but fears Qi will come to its aid, it can offer Song to Qi as a bribe. "They said: 'King Song is unjust—he made wooden figures (to depict) me and shot at their faces. "My territory is distant, my troops far away; I cannot attack them." "If Your Majesty can conquer and possess Song, it will be as if I have gained it myself.'" "After capturing Anyi and blocking Nuji Pass, Qin would then use the destruction of Song as a pretext to accuse Qi.

## 59

"'If Qin wants to attack Han but fears that all under heaven will come to its rescue, it can offer Qi as a target for the world. "They would say: 'King Qi has made four agreements with me, but each time he has deceived me. He must have led all under heaven to attack me three times already. "If there is Qi and no Qin, or if there is Qin but not Qi, I must attack it and destroy it.'" "After capturing Yiyang and Shaoqu, reaching Lin and [Li] Shi, Qin would then use Qi's destruction as a crime against all under heaven."

## 60

"'If Qin wants to attack Wei but values Chu highly, it can offer Nanyang to Chu. "They would say: 'I have already decided to cut ties with Han. "Destroying Junling, blocking Meng'e—if it benefits Chu, I will treat it as my own.'" "Wei abandons its allied states and aligns with Qin; thus, blocking Meng'e becomes a crime against Chu."

## 61

"'When troops are trapped in the forest and Qin wants to appease Yan and Zhao, it can offer Jiaodong to Yan and the western side of Ji River to Zhao. "After securing peace with Wei up to Gongzi Yan, Qin would then send Su Shi's forces under command and attack Zhao."

## 62

"'When troops suffer losses at Qiaoshi and are defeated at Yangma, yet Qin still values Wei highly, it can offer Ye and Cai to Wei. "After securing peace with Zhao, Qin would then threaten Wei, but Wei will not agree to cede territory." "When in a difficult situation, they use the emperor's aunt and Duke Ranghou as mediators; when victorious, they deceive both uncle and mother.

## 63

"'Those who go to Yan say, 'We will give Jiaodong'; those going to Zhao say, 'Jixi'; those to Wei, 'Ye and Cai'; those to Chu, 'blocking Meng'e'; and those to Qi, 'Song.' These promises are made in a cycle, like a wheel. Military actions are as swift as striking flies—neither mother nor uncle can control or restrain them."

## 64

"The battles of Longjia, Anmen, Fengling, Gao Shang, and Zhaozhuang—Qin has killed hundreds of thousands of people from the Three Jin states. Now those who survived are all orphans left behind by Qin." "The lands beyond Xhe, the Shangluo region, and the calamities in Sanchuan—half of Jin's territory has been lost. Qin's devastation is this great." "And yet, envoys from Yan and Zhao to Qin all try to persuade their rulers by competing in flattery toward Qin. This is what I fear most.'"

## 65

King Zhao of Yan did not act on this advice. Su Dai regained influence in Yan once again.

## 66

Yan sent envoys to form an alliance among the feudal lords as Su Qin had done, with some joining and others not. Nevertheless, all under heaven came to revere Su's alliance agreements. Both Dai and Li died of old age, their names renowned among the feudal lords.

## 67

Grand Historian said: Su Qin and his two brothers all traveled to persuade the feudal lords, thereby gaining fame. Their strategy was particularly skillful in adapting to shifting circumstances. "Yet Su Qin was falsely accused and died as a result, and all under heaven mocked him, avoiding the study of his methods. "However, many accounts about Su Qin contain inconsistencies; events that occurred at different times and were similar in nature have all been attributed to him. "Su Qin rose from humble origins, uniting the alliance of six states—this demonstrated wisdom surpassing that of ordinary men. "Therefore, I have recorded his deeds in chronological order to ensure he is not left with a reputation solely for evil."
