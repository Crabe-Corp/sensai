# Biographies of Han Xin and Lu Wan

## 1

Han Wang Xin was a collateral descendant of King Xiang of Han, and he was eight chi five cun tall. When Xiang Liang established the post of King Huai of Chu, Yan, Qi, Zhao, and Wei had already previously been kings. Only Han lacked a successor; therefore, they appointed Gongzi Hengyang Jun Cheng as King of Han, hoping to pacify and stabilize Han's former territory. After Xiang Liang was defeated and died at Dingtao, Cheng fled to King Huai. Peigong led his troops to attack Yangcheng, and sent Zhang Liang as Han's Siku to subdue Han's former territory. There he captured Xin, appointed him a general of Han, and with his forces followed Peigong into Wuguan Pass.

## 2

After Peigong was established as King of Han, Han Xin followed him into Hanzhong. He then advised King Han, saying: "Xiang Wang has granted nearby territories to his generals, but you alone are stationed far away here; this is a demotion." "The soldiers and troops are all from Shandong, standing on tiptoe longing to return. If we take advantage of their momentum and turn eastward, we can contend for the empire." King Han returned to pacify the Three Qins and then agreed to make Xin King of Han. First, he appointed Xin as Taiwei of Han and ordered him to lead troops in capturing Han's territory.

## 3

Xiang Ji awarded all the kings their fiefs, and King Cheng of Han was not allowed to take his fief because he had not followed orders and thus achieved no merit; instead, he was made a marquis. When they heard that Han had sent Han Xin to capture Han's territory, they ordered Zheng Chang, who had previously been the governor of Wu during Xiang Ji's travels in Wu, to become King of Han to resist Han. In the second year of Han, Han Xin pacified more than ten cities in Han. King Han arrived at Henan, and Han Xin quickly attacked King Chang of Han at Yangcheng. After Chang surrendered, King Han appointed Han Xin as King of Han; he often led the Han troops in his campaigns. In the third year, King Han left Xingyang, and King Xin of Han, Zhou Ke, and others defended Xingyang. After Chu was defeated at Xingyang, Xin surrendered to Chu. Later he managed to escape and returned to Han; Han reappointed him as King of Han. Eventually, he followed the campaign that broke Xiang Ji, and the empire was pacified. In spring of the fifth year, he was thus granted a covenant seal as King of Han, ruling Yingchuan.

## 4

The following spring, the emperor considered Han Xin capable and martial. The territory he ruled was near Gong and Luo to the north, bordered by Wan and Ye to the south, and had Huaiyang to the east—all regions with strong military forces under heaven. Therefore, he issued an edict transferring King Xin of Han to rule Taiyuan and areas to its north, tasked with defending against the Hu peoples, with his capital at Jinyang. Xin submitted a memorial stating: "My fief is on the frontier, and the Xiongnu frequently raid. Jinyang is far from the border passes; I request to establish my capital at Mayi." The emperor approved it, so Xin moved his administration to Mayi. In autumn, the Xiongnu Modu launched a major siege against Xin, who frequently sent envoys to the Hu peoples seeking peace and reconciliation. Han dispatched troops to rescue him but suspected that Xin had repeatedly sent spies, showing divided loyalties, so he sent someone to reprimand Xin. Xin feared punishment and therefore made an agreement with the Xiongnu to jointly attack Han, rebelled, surrendered Mayi to the Hu peoples, and attacked Taiyuan.

## 5

In winter of the seventh year, the emperor personally went to attack, defeating Xin's forces at Tongdi and beheading his general Wang Xi. Xin fled to the Xiongnu. (With) the Bai Tu native Manqiuchen, Wang Huang and others, they established Zhao Lixiao, a descendant of the Zhao royal family, as king. They once again gathered Xin's scattered and defeated troops, and plotted with Xin and Modu to attack Han. The Xiongnu, with more than 10,000 cavalry led by the Left and Right Xianwang, along with Wang Huang and others, stationed themselves south of Guangwu to Jinyang, where they fought Han troops. Han decisively defeated them, pursuing them as far as Lishi, where they were once again crushed. The Xiongnu regrouped their forces northwest of Loufan, and Han ordered the Chaji troops to defeat them. The Xiongnu often suffered defeats and retreated, and Han took advantage of the victory to pursue them. Hearing that Modu resided in Daigu, Emperor Gaozu stayed at Jinyang and sent someone to scout Modu. The scout returned with a report saying, "He can be attacked." The emperor thus proceeded to Pingcheng. When the emperor left Baideng, Xiongnu cavalry surrounded him; he then sent generous gifts to Yizhi. Yizhi then advised Modu: "Now that we have obtained Han territory, it is still not possible for us to settle there; Moreover, two rulers cannot coexist." After staying seven days, the Hu cavalry gradually withdrew. At that time, there was a heavy fog in the sky, and Han sent people back and forth without being detected by the Hu. Hu Jun Zhongwei Chen Ping said to the emperor: "The Hu forces are fully armed. Please order strong crossbowmen to carry two arrows outward, and slowly proceed out of the encirclement." After entering Pingcheng, Han's relief forces also arrived, so the Hu cavalry withdrew. Han also withdrew his troops and returned. Han Xin served as a Xiongnu general, leading forces to attack the borders back and forth.

## 6

In the tenth year of Han, Xin ordered Wang Huang et al. to deceive and mislead Chen Xi. In spring of the eleventh year, former King Xin of Han once again joined Hu cavalry to settle at Cenhe, resisting Han. Han sent General Chai to attack them and sent a letter to Xin saying: "His Majesty is lenient and kind. Even if feudal lords have rebelled or fled, when they return, their former titles are restored without punishment." This is something you well know. "Now, Your Majesty fled to the Hu after defeat; there was no grave crime—hurry and return yourself!" King Xin of Han replied: "His Majesty elevated me from the common streets, allowing me to face south and be called a ruler; this is my good fortune. "In the matter of Xingyang, I failed to die for you and was captured by Xiang Ji; this is one crime." "When the enemy attacked Mayi, I could not hold out firmly and surrendered the city to them; this is a second crime." "Now I have turned around to serve as an enemy general, leading troops against you for the sake of one day's life; this is a third crime." "Zong and Li had not committed even one crime, yet they died tragic deaths; now I have three crimes against His Majesty and yet wish to survive in the world—this is why Wu Zixu failed in Wu." "Now I hide and take refuge among the mountain valleys, begging for alms from the southern barbarians morning and night. My longing to return is like a paralytic who does not forget to stand up or a blind man who does not forget to see—it simply cannot be done." So they proceeded to battle. General Chai sacked Cenhe and beheaded King Xin of Han.

## 7

When Xin fled to the Xiongnu, he went together with the crown prince; and when they arrived at Tuidang City, a son was born. He was thus named Tuidang. The Crown Prince of Han also had a son and named him Ying. In the fourteenth year of Emperor Xiaowen's reign, Tuidang and Ying led their followers to surrender to Han. Han granted Tuidang the title Gonggao Hou and Ying the title Xiangcheng Hou. During the Wu-Chu rebellion, Gonggao Hou's achievements surpassed those of all other generals. The title was passed down from father to son until it reached his grandson; when the grandson had no sons, the marquisate was lost. Ying's grandson lost the marquisate due to disrespectful behavior. Tuidang's collateral descendant Han Yan was favored and held a high position, becoming wealthy and prominent in his time. His younger brother Shuo was twice granted a title and several times called general; he eventually became Marquis of Andao. His son Dai, after more than one year, died by law. Some years later, Shuo's grandson Ceng was granted the title Longe Hou, continuing the lineage of Shuo.

## 8

Lu Wan was a native of Feng and lived in the same village as Emperor Gaozu. Lu Wan's parents were close friends with Emperor Gaozu's Taishang Huang. When they each had sons, Gaozu and Lu Wan were born on the same day; neighbors brought sheep and wine to celebrate both families. When Gaozu and Lu Wan grew up, they studied together and were once again close friends. The villagers admired the two families for their mutual affection, as their sons were born on the same day and remained close friends in adulthood; they once again brought sheep and wine to congratulate both households. When Gaozu was still a commoner, whenever he had legal troubles and needed to hide, Lu Wan often followed him in and out, accompanying him through all circumstances. When Gaozu first rose up at Pei, Lu Wan followed as a retainer and became a general upon entering Hanzhong; he often served in the imperial court. He followed Gaozu eastward to attack Xiang Ji and, as Taiwei, constantly accompanied him. He had access to the inner chambers, received clothes, bedding, food, drink, rewards, and gifts; no court official dared compare with him. Even Xiao He and Cao Shen were only treated with formal courtesy due to their duties, but in terms of closeness and favor, none could rival Lu Wan. Wan was granted the title Chang'an Hou. Chang'an, formerly Xianyang.

## 9

In winter of the fifth year of Han, after defeating Xiang Ji, Gaozu ordered Lu Wan to lead a separate force and join Liu Jia in attacking Wang Gongwei, King of Linjiang; they defeated him. In the seventh month he returned and joined forces to attack Yan Wang Zang Tu, who surrendered. After Emperor Gaozu had pacified the empire, seven feudal kings were not of the Liu clan. He wanted to make Lu Wan a king, but this caused dissatisfaction among his ministers. After capturing Zang Tu, he issued an edict to the generals and marquises, ordering them to select among his ministers those with merit for appointment as King of Yan. The ministers knew the emperor wanted to make Lu Wan king, so they all said: "Taiwei Chang'an Hou Lu Wan has constantly followed His Majesty in pacifying the empire and has contributed the most; he is fit to be King of Yan." The emperor issued an edict granting permission. In the eighth month of the fifth year of Han, they thus established Lu Wan as King of Yan. Among the feudal kings, none was more favored than King Yan.

## 10

In autumn of the eleventh year of Han, Chen Xi rebelled in Dai territory. Emperor Gaozu went to Handan to attack Xi's forces, and King Yan Wan also attacked from the northeast. At that time, Chen Xi sent Wang Huang to seek help from the Xiongnu. King Yan Wan also sent his minister Zhang Sheng to the Xiongnu with word that Xi's forces had been defeated. When Zhang Sheng arrived at the Xiongnu, Zhuang Cha's son Yan, who had fled to the Hu and was in exile there, met Zhang Sheng and said: "The reason you are so valued by Yan is because of your familiarity with Hu affairs. "The reason why Yan has survived for so long is that the feudal lords have repeatedly rebelled, and wars have continued without a decisive outcome. "Now you for Yan wish to quickly destroy Xi and others; once they are all eliminated, it will be your turn next. You too will become captives." "Why don't you advise Yan to delay the attack on Chen Xi and instead make peace with the Hu?" If matters are handled leniently, you will be able to rule Yan for a long time; if Han suddenly becomes urgent in its actions, it can still ensure the safety of your state." Zhang Sheng considered this advice reasonable and secretly ordered the Xiongnu to assist Xi in attacking Yan. King Yan Wan suspected Zhang Sheng of conspiring with the Hu, so he submitted a memorial requesting that Zhang Sheng's entire clan be executed. When Sheng returned, he fully explained why he had done what he did. King Yan realized the truth and thus falsely condemned another person, freeing Sheng's family so that he could serve as a spy for the Xiongnu. He secretly sent Fan Qi to Chen Xi's forces with instructions to prolong their escape and keep the war unresolved.

## 11

In the twelfth year of Han, when attacking Peng Ju, Chen Xi often led troops in Dai. Han sent Fan Kuai to attack and behead Xi. Some of his subordinate generals surrendered and reported that King Yan Wan had sent Fan Qi to communicate plans with Chen Xi's forces. Emperor Gaozu sent envoys to summon Lu Wan, but Wan claimed illness. The emperor then sent Piyang Hou Shen Shi ji and the Minister of Justice Zhao Yao to welcome King Yan, while also investigating his attendants. Wan became even more frightened and secluded himself, saying to his favored ministers: "Among those who are kings but not of the Liu clan, only I and Changsha remain. "In previous years in spring, Han executed Huaiyin; in summer, he killed Peng Yue—both were plans of Empress Dowager Lü. "Now the emperor is ill and has entrusted authority to Empress Dowager Lü." "Empress Dowager Lü, a woman, wants exclusively to use these matters to execute kings of non-Liu lineages and great meritorious ministers." Thus he claimed illness again and refused to go. His attendants all fled into hiding. The conversation was somewhat overheard, and Piyang Hou heard about it and returned to report everything in detail to the emperor, who became even angrier. They also captured a Xiongnu defector, who reported that Zhang Sheng had fled to the Xiongnu and was serving as an envoy for Yan. At this, the emperor said: "Lu Wan has indeed rebelled!" He sent Fan Kuai to attack Yan. King Yan Wan led all his palace attendants, family members, and several thousand cavalry to stay at the foot of the Great Wall, waiting for an opportunity. He hoped that when the emperor recovered from illness, he could personally enter to apologize. In April, Emperor Gaozu died, and Lu Wan then led his followers in fleeing into the Xiongnu, who appointed him as Donghu Lu Wang. Wan was often invaded and deprived by the barbarians, and he frequently longed to return. After living there for more than a year, he died among the Hu people.

## 12

During Empress Dowager Gao's reign, Lu Wan's wife and children fled to surrender to Han. Coincidentally, the empress dowager was ill and could not receive them; they were housed in the Yan residence, as plans had been made for a banquet to see them. Emperor Gaozu eventually died before he could meet with them. Lu Wan's wife also fell ill and died.

## 13

In the sixth year of the middle reign of Emperor Xiaojing, He Zhi, a grandson of Lu Wan and son of the Eastern Hu King, surrendered to Han and was enfeoffed as Yagu Hou.

## 14

Chen Xi was a native of Wanqu, but it is unknown how he originally came to follow the emperor. In the winter of the seventh year of Emperor Gaozu's reign, King Yan Wan rebelled and entered into alliance with the Xiongnu. When the emperor returned to Pingcheng after his campaign, he enfeoffed Xi as a marquis, appointing him as Minister of State of Zhao in charge of overseeing the border troops of Zhao and Dai; all the frontier forces were placed under his command.

## 15

Xi often requested leave to pass through Zhao, and when he did so, the Minister of State Zhou Chang saw that Xi was followed by over a thousand carriages of guests; all official residences in Handan were filled. The way Xi treated his guests and commoner friends was always humble, showing deference to them. When Xi returned to Dai, Zhou Chang requested permission to enter and see the emperor in person. After seeing the emperor, he fully reported that Xi's retinue of guests was very large and that for several years now, Xi had been independently commanding troops in the outer regions, which caused concern about possible rebellion. The emperor then ordered someone to investigate illegal activities involving property and other misconduct of Xi's guests residing in Dai, many of which were linked back to Xi. Xi became frightened and secretly instructed his guests to send envoys to Wang Huang and Man Qiuchen. In the seventh month of the tenth year of Emperor Gaozu's reign, Grandfather Emperor Tai Shang Huang died, and envoys were sent to summon Xi, but Xi claimed to be very ill. In September, he joined forces with Wang Huang and others in rebellion, proclaiming himself King of Dai, and raided Zhao and Dai.

## 16

Upon hearing this, the emperor pardoned all officials and commoners in Zhao and Dai who had been deceived or forced into rebellion by Xi. The emperor personally went there and arrived at Handan, where he said joyfully: "Xi did not take the southern bank of Zhang River or defend Handan to the north; I now know that he is incapable of much." The Minister of State of Zhao submitted a report requesting the execution of the governor and military commander of Changshan, saying: "Changshan has twenty-five cities; when Xi rebelled, twenty were lost." The emperor asked: "Did the governor and the military commander rebel?" They replied: "No, they did not rebel." The emperor said: "Then it is a matter of insufficient strength." He pardoned them and reinstated them as governor and military commander of Changshan. The emperor asked Zhou Chang: "Does Zhao have any strong men who could be appointed as generals?" Zhou Chang replied: "There are four such people." The four men paid their respects, and the emperor scolded them, saying: "You brats can be generals?" The four men were ashamed and bowed low. The emperor enfeoffed each of them with a thousand households and appointed them as generals. Those around him advised: "They did not follow the emperor into Shu and Han or participate in the campaign against Chu; their merits have not been widely recognized. Now, what merit do they have to be enfeoffed?" The emperor said: "That is not for you to know!" "When Chen Xi rebelled, all areas north of Handan were under his control. I issued urgent summons to raise troops from across the empire, but none have arrived; now only the soldiers in central Handan remain." "How could I not cherish these four thousand households to enfeoff these four men, just to comfort the youth of Zhao!" They all said: "That is right." The emperor then asked, "Who are Chen Xi's generals?" They replied: "Wang Huang and Man Qiuchen; they were all former merchants." The emperor said, "I know them well." He then offered a thousand gold each for the capture of Huang and Chen.

## 17

In winter of the eleventh year, Han forces beheaded Chen Xi's generals Hou Chang and Wang Huang at Qu Ni, defeated his general Zhang Chun at Liaocheng, and captured over ten thousand enemy heads. Grand Marshal Bo entered to pacify Taiyuan and Dai regions. In December, the emperor personally attacked Dongyuan, but Dongyuan did not surrender; its soldiers cursed at him. After Dongyuan surrendered, those who had cursed the emperor were beheaded, and those who had not cursed were branded. He changed the name of Dongyuan to Zhen Ding. Wang Huang and Man Qiuchun's subordinates, who had accepted the rewards for capturing them, were all captured alive. Because of this, Chen Xi's army was soon defeated.

## 18

The emperor returned to Luoyang. The emperor said: "Dai is located north of Changshan; Zhao controls it from the south, which is distant." He therefore enfeoffed his son Heng as King of Dai, with the capital at Zhongdu. Both Dai and Yanmen were placed under the jurisdiction of Dai.

## 19

In winter of the twelfth year of Emperor Gaozu, Fan Kuai's soldiers pursued and beheaded Xi at Lingqiu.

## 20

Grand Historian said: "Han Xin and Lu Wan were not from families long known for accumulated virtue and good deeds. They seized power through opportunistic schemes, using deceit and force to achieve success. Because they happened upon the early days of Han's establishment, they were able to receive fiefdoms and sit in state as sovereign rulers." Internally, they were suspected of growing too powerful; externally, they relied on the Hu and Mo for support. As a result, their position became increasingly precarious, their circumstances dire, and their wisdom exhausted—eventually fleeing to the Xiongnu! How pitiable is that! Chen Xi was a native of Liang. In his youth, he often expressed admiration for Marquis Wei. When he later served as a general defending the frontier, he attracted guests and treated scholars with humility, gaining a reputation that exceeded his actual merits. Zhou Chang suspected him, and many flaws began to surface; fearing disaster would befall himself, he listened to the advice of wicked men and thus fell into rebellion. Alas, how tragic! Indeed, the influence of plans and strategies in determining a person's success or failure is profound!
