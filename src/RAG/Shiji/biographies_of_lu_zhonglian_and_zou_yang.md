# Biographies of Lu Zhonglian and Zou Yang

## 1

Lu Zhonglian was a native of Qi. He loved grand and extraordinary strategies, but refused to hold office or take on official duties, preferring instead to uphold lofty principles of integrity. He traveled in Zhao.

## 2

During the reign of King Xiaocheng of Zhao, King Qin sent Bai Qi to defeat the Zhao army at Changping, killing more than forty thousand soldiers. The Qin forces then advanced eastward and encircled Handan. King of Zhao was terrified, and the armies sent by other feudal lords to rescue him dared not fight against the Qin forces. King Anli of Wei dispatched General Jin Bi to rescue Zhao, but fearing the Qin, he halted at Dangyin and did not advance. King Wei sent the guest general Xin Yuanyan to secretly enter Handan, and through Pingyuan Jun he told King Zhao: "The reason why Qin urgently surrounds you is because previously they competed with King Mian of Qi for supremacy as emperor, but later both abandoned their imperial titles; Now Qi [King Mian] has become even weaker, and at present only Qin is powerful in the world. This is not necessarily about greed for Handan; rather, their intention is to once again demand recognition as emperor. "If Zhao truly sends envoys to honor King Zhao of Qin as emperor, the Qin will surely be pleased and withdraw their troops." Pingyuan Jun hesitated without making a decision.

## 3

At this time, Lu Zhonglian happened to be traveling in Zhao. When he encountered the Qin's siege of Zhao and heard that Wei's general wanted Zhao to honor Qin as emperor, he visited Pingyuan Jun and asked: "What is to be done about this matter?" Pingyuan Jun said: "How dare I, Sheng, speak of such matters! We previously lost forty thousand troops outside, and now the enemy is encircling Handan from within and we cannot drive them away. King Wei of Wei sent a guest general, Xin Yuanyan, to urge Zhao to recognize Qin as emperor, and now he is here. "How dare I speak about such matters!" Lu Zhonglian said: "At first, I regarded you as a virtuous nobleman of the world; now only do I realize that you are not such a person. "Where is Liang's guest Xin Yuanyan?" "I shall go to confront him on your behalf and send him back." Pingyuan Jun said: "I will serve as an introducer and bring you before him, Master." Pingyuan Jun then introduced Xin Yuanyan and said: "There is a Master Lu Zhonglian in the eastern states, and now he happens to be here. I will serve as an introducer for you to meet him." Xin Yuanyan said: "I have heard of Master Lu Zhonglian, a man of lofty virtue from the state of Qi. "I am but a subject, with duties to perform on behalf of my lord; I do not wish to meet Master Lu Zhonglian." Pingyuan Jun said: "I have already revealed his presence." Xin Yuanyan agreed.

## 4

Lu Zhonglian met Xin Yuanyan but said nothing. Xin Yuanyan remarked: "I see that all those living within this besieged city have some request of Pingyuan Jun; now, observing your noble appearance, I see that you have no request of Pingyuan Jun. Why then do you remain for so long in this besieged city without leaving?" Lu Zhonglian said: "The world regards Bao Jiao as a man who died without receiving praise, but this is incorrect. Common people do not understand; they act for their own sake alone. That Qin state is one that abandons rites and righteousness in favor of rewarding military achievements, using coercion to control its soldiers and treating its people like captives. If they were to openly declare themselves emperor and impose their rule upon the world, then I would rather leap into the East Sea and die. I cannot bear to be a subject of theirs. "The reason why I came before you, General, is to help Zhao."

## 5

Xin Yuanyan asked: "Master, how do you intend to assist them?" Lu Zhonglian said: "I will make Liang and Yan help them; Qi and Chu have already been assisting." Xin Yuanyan replied: "As for Yan, I shall request to follow your plan; as for Liang, I am a native of Liang myself. How can you possibly make Liang assist them?" Lu Zhonglian said: "Liang has not yet seen the harm caused by Qin's claim to imperial status, that is all. If Liang could see the dangers of Qin claiming the title of emperor, then they would certainly assist Zhao."

## 6

Xin Yuanyan asked: "What are the dangers of Qin's claim to be emperor?" línea Lu Zhonglian said: "In the past, King Wei of Qi once practiced benevolence and righteousness, leading all the feudal lords to pay homage to Zhou. Zhou was poor and weak; no other feudal lord paid it homage, yet Qi alone did so. After more than a year, King Lie of Zhou died, and Qi was the last to arrive. Zhou became angry and sent an envoy to Qi saying: "Heaven has collapsed and the earth has split; the Son of Heaven has descended from his throne. Because a minister from the eastern vassal state arrived late, he was beheaded." King Wei of Qi became furious and said: "Hmph! Your mother is a servant!" In the end, he was laughed at by all under heaven. Therefore, when Zhou was alive they paid homage to him, but after his death they insulted him—truly, one cannot bear such demands. The Son of Heaven is like that by nature; there is nothing strange about it."

## 7

Xin Yuanyan said: "Have you never seen a servant, Master? When ten people follow one person, is it because their strength is not enough or their intelligence inferior? It is out of fear." Lu Zhonglian said: "Alas! Is Liang to be compared with Qin as a servant?" Xin Yuanyan said: "Indeed." Lu Zhonglian said: "I will make King Qin boil and mince the king of Liang into meat paste." Xin Yuanyan looked displeased and said: "Ah, your words are truly excessive, Master! How can you possibly make King Qin boil and mince Liang's king into meat paste?" Lu Zhonglian said: "Of course. I shall explain it to you. In the past, Jiuhou, Ehou, and King Wen were three ministers of King Zhou. Jiuhou had a son who was handsome, so he presented him to King Zhou; but King Zhou found the boy repulsive and had Jiuhou minced into meat. Ehou strongly protested, arguing passionately on Jiuhou's behalf, so King Zhou had Ehou dried and preserved as jerky. King Wen heard of this and sighed deeply, so King Zhou imprisoned him in Youli's prison for a hundred days, intending to have him die. Why did they all hold the title of king together and yet end up being treated like meat or jerky?" When King Mian of Qi went to Lu, Ywei Zi held the reins and followed him, asking the people of Lu: "How will you receive our lord?" The people of Lu said: "We shall treat your lord with ten taolao." Ywei Zi asked: "From where did you get the courtesy to receive our lord?" Our lord is the Son of Heaven. When the Son of Heaven tours his realm, feudal lords must vacate their halls, hand over keys and locks, tuck up their robes with one arm folded across the chest, hold a shuttle in their hands, and serve meals beneath the hall. Only after the Son of Heaven has eaten may they withdraw to attend court." The people of Lu threw down their keys and did not actually admit him. Unable to enter Lu, he proceeded toward Xue, passing through Zou for passage. At that time, the ruler of Zou had died. King Mian wanted to enter and pay his respects, so Ywei Zi told the orphaned heir of Zou: "When the Son of Heaven pays condolences, the host must double the coffins, set up a northern-facing position in the south, after which the Son of Heaven faces south to offer condolences." The ministers of Zou said: "If this must be done, we shall fall upon our swords and die." Thus, they dared not enter Zou. The ministers of Zou and Lu, while the rulers were alive could not serve or support them, and after their deaths could not offer funeral gifts or shrouds. Yet they still wanted to impose the rites of the Son of Heaven upon Zou and Lu; however, the ministers of Zou and Lu did not actually accept this. Now Qin is a state with ten thousand chariots, and Liang is also a state with ten thousand chariotes. Both hold states with ten thousand chariots and each holds the title of king. If one sees a single battle victory and wishes to follow by recognizing them as emperor, this would make the ministers of the three Jin states less than servants or concubines from Zou and Lu. Moreover, if Qin continues to claim imperial status without restraint, they will also replace the high ministers of other feudal lords. They will remove those they consider unworthy and appoint those they regard as virtuous, discard those they dislike and promote those they favor. They will also make their daughters and scheming concubines become consorts of feudal lords. Residing in the palace of Liang. How can King of Liang remain at ease and do nothing? And how will you, General, retain your former favor?"

## 8

At this, Xin Yuanyan rose and bowed twice in gratitude, saying: "Initially I regarded you as an ordinary man; only today do I realize that you are a true gentleman of the world. I shall leave now and dare not speak again about honoring Qin as emperor." The Qin generals heard of this, and withdrew their troops fifty li. At that time, Prince Wuji of Wei seized General Jin Bi's army to rescue Zhao and attacked the Qin forces, so the Qin troops withdrew.

## 9

At this time, Pingyuan Jun wanted to bestow a title upon Lu Zhonglian; Lu Zhonglian declined three times and finally refused it. Pingyuan Jun then held a banquet, and when the wine was flowing freely, he rose to approach Lu Zhonglian and presented him with one thousand gold pieces as a gift of longevity. Lu Zhonglian smiled and said: "What is valued in gentlemen of the world is that they relieve others' troubles, resolve difficulties, and untangle disputes without expecting anything in return. If one accepts payment for such actions, it is the business of a merchant; I cannot bear to do that." Thus he declined Pingyuan Jun's offer and left, never again appearing in his presence for the rest of his life.

## 10

More than twenty years later, a Yan general captured the city of Laicheng. Some people in Laicheng slandered him to Yan, and fearing punishment, the Yan general held onto Laicheng for defense and dared not return. Qi's Tian Dan attacked Laicheng for more than a year; many soldiers died, yet the city still did not fall. Lu Zhonglian then wrote a letter, tied it to an arrow, and shot it into the city as a message for the Yan general. The letter read:

## 11

I have heard that a wise man does not defy the times and abandon profit, a brave warrior does not die without preserving his reputation, and a loyal minister does not put himself before his lord. Now you act out of a moment's anger, disregarding the fact that King Yan will be left without ministers—this is not loyalty; you die and lose Laicheng, yet your authority remains untrusted by Qi—this is not bravery; your failure brings ruin to your reputation, leaving no legacy for future generations to praise—you are not wise. A man lacking in these three virtues will never be a minister under the world's rulers, nor will he be recorded by eloquent scholars; therefore, wise men do not calculate such actions and brave warriors are unafraid of death. Now is the time for life or death, honor or disgrace, wealth or poverty, high status or low rank—such moments will not come again. I hope you carefully consider your choice and do not follow the crowd.

## 12

Moreover, the State of Chu is attacking Qi's Nanyang, and Wei is attacking Pinglu. Yet Qi has no intention of defending its southern border because it considers the loss of Nanyang to be a small harm compared to the great benefit of gaining Jibei; therefore, they have made their decision carefully. Now that Qin has sent its troops down, Wei dares not look eastward; the balance of power favors Qin, and the situation in the State of Chu becomes perilous; Qi abandoned Nanyang, cut off its western territory, and secured Jibei—this decision was still made with careful calculation. Moreover, Qi's determination to settle the matter at Laicheng is certain; you must not hesitate any longer. Now that both Chu and Wei have withdrawn from Qi, yet Yan's reinforcements do not arrive. With the full strength of Qi's army, without any constraints from the world, yet holding out for a year in Laicheng with its exhausted defenses, I see clearly that you will not succeed. Moreover, the State of Yan is in great turmoil; its rulers and ministers have lost their strategy, and both high and low are confused. Li Fu led an army of 100,000 men but suffered five defeats abroad, and with a powerful state of ten thousand chariots, it was encircled by Zhao. Its territory has been reduced and its ruler trapped, becoming the laughingstock of all under heaven. The state is exhausted and plagued with disasters; the people have no one to place their hopes in. Now you are using the weary people of Laicheng to resist the full might of Qi's army—this is like the defense of Mozi. You are making your soldiers eat human flesh and cook bones, yet still they have no intention to retreat—this is the army of Sun Bin. Such a feat can be seen by all under heaven. Nevertheless, for your sake, it would be better to preserve your chariots and armor and return them to Yan. Returning with intact chariots and armor will surely please King Yan; returning unharmed to your homeland, the soldiers and people will welcome you as if seeing their parents; your associates will raise their arms in admiration and speak of you throughout the world—your achievements will be clearly recognized. Above, you can assist the young ruler to control his ministers; below, you can nurture the people and provide support for eloquent scholars. By correcting the state's customs, your fame and achievements will be established. Do you have no intention of abandoning Yan and forsaking this world to travel eastward into Qi? You could divide the land, establish a fief, and grow as wealthy as Tao or Wei. Your descendants would be called "gus" (lords), living on alongside Qi for generations—this is yet another plan. These two plans offer both fame and substantial rewards; I hope you carefully weigh them and choose one wisely.

## 13

Moreover, I have heard that those who fear small losses cannot achieve great fame, and those who resent minor humiliations cannot accomplish great deeds. In the past, Guan Yiwu shot Duke Huan of Qi in the hook of his belt—this was an act of treason; he abandoned Prince Jiu and did not die for him—this showed cowardice; he was bound in shackles—a disgrace. With these three actions, he would never be a minister to the world's rulers nor accepted by his hometown. Had Guanzi remained imprisoned and not returned to Qi, had he died without returning, then his name would have been no more than that of a disgraceful man with ignoble conduct. Even slaves and captives would be ashamed to share the same name as him, let alone the common people! Guanzi did not feel ashamed of being in chains, but was ashamed that the world remained in disorder; he did not feel ashamed for not dying for Prince Jiu, but felt shame that his authority was not trusted by the feudal lords. Thus, despite these three transgressions, he became the leader among the Five Hegemons, achieving a reputation so high it shone across neighboring states. Cao Zi served as a general of Lu and suffered three defeats in three battles, losing 500 li of territory. Had Cao Zi not reconsidered, had he not changed his course of action and instead cut his throat in death, then he would have been no more than a defeated general captured by the enemy. Cao Zi abandoned the shame of three defeats and retreated to consult with Duke Lu. Duke Huan summoned the rulers of all under heaven and convened with feudal lords. Cao Zi, entrusted merely with a sword, stood firm on the sacrificial platform to challenge Duke Huan's intentions, his expression unchanging, his words and tone composed. In one day he recovered what had been lost in three battles; the world was shaken, the feudal lords astonished, and his authority extended even over Wu and Yue. These two men were not incapable of achieving minor integrity or avoiding small losses; they simply believed that to die and lose their lives, cutting off their descendants and leaving no legacy, without establishing fame or achievements, would be unwise. Therefore, they abandoned resentment from momentary anger to establish a name that would last their entire lives; they set aside the rigid adherence of temporary indignation and established enduring achievements for generations. Thus, their accomplishments rival those of the Three Kings, and their fame endures as long as heaven and earth exist. I hope you will choose one of these paths to follow.

## 14

The Yan general, upon reading Lu Zhonglian's letter, wept for three days and remained indecisive. He wanted to return to Yan but feared punishment due to existing suspicions; he wanted to surrender to Qi, yet he had killed and captured many people from Qi, fearing that after surrendering, he would still be humiliated. He sighed deeply and said, "Better I kill myself with my own sword than let others do it to me." And so he committed suicide. With Laicheng in chaos, Tian Dan proceeded to massacre the city's defenders. Upon returning, he spoke highly of Lu Zhonglian and wished to bestow a title upon him. Lu Zhonglian fled into seclusion by the sea and said, "I would rather be poor and lowly while freely pursuing my will than accept wealth and honor at the cost of bowing to others."

## 15

Zou Yang was a native of Qi. He traveled to Liang and associated with figures such as Master Zhuang Ji, an old acquaintance from Wu, and Mei Sheng of Huaiyin. He submitted a letter to recommend himself between Yang Sheng and Gongsun Gui. Yang Sheng and others envied Zou Yang and slandered him before King Xiao of Liang. King Xiao was furious, handed him over to the officials, and intended to execute him. Zou Yang, while traveling as a guest, was captured on false charges and feared dying with an unjust reputation. Thus, from prison he submitted a letter saying:

## 16

I have heard that loyalty is always rewarded and faithfulness never doubted; I used to believe this, but now it seems merely empty words. In the past, Jing Ke admired Prince Dan of Yan for his righteousness; a white rainbow pierced the sun, and the prince feared it. Wei Sheng advised King Qin on the matter of Changping; when Tai Bai (the planet Venus) eclipsed Mao, King Zhaoxiang doubted him. When such extraordinary omens change the heavens and earth yet faithfulness remains unappreciated by two rulers, is this not sorrowful! Now I have given my utmost loyalty and sincerity, fully expressed all my views in hopes of being understood. Yet those around me failed to see clearly, and suddenly I was subjected to interrogation by the officials, doubted by the world. This is as if Jing Ke and Wei Sheng were to rise again, yet Yan and Qin still would not understand. I hope Your Majesty will carefully examine this matter.

## 17

In the past, Bian He presented a precious gem to King Chu, and was punished by having his feet amputated; Li Si devoted himself wholeheartedly, yet Emperor Huhai subjected him to the most brutal punishment. Therefore, Ji Zi feigned madness and Jie Yu withdrew from society, fearing such misfortunes. I hope Your Majesty carefully considers the intentions of Bian He and Li Si, rather than following the ears of King Chu and Emperor Huhai. Let me not become a laughingstock to Ji Zi and Jie Yu. I have heard of Bi Gan, whose heart was ripped out, and Wu Zixu, who was placed in a leather bag and drowned. I did not believe it at first, but now I understand. I hope Your Majesty will examine this carefully and show me some mercy.

## 18

A proverb says: "There are those who have grown old together yet remain as strangers, while others become lifelong friends at first meeting." Why is this so? It depends on whether one truly knows the other. Therefore, in the past, Fan Yuqi fled from Qin to Yan and offered Jing Ke's head for Prince Dan's cause; Wang She left Qi for Wei, where he committed suicide before the city walls to repel Qi and preserve Wei. Wang She and Fan Yuqi were not new to Qi and Qin, nor old friends of Yan and Wei. Yet they left the two states and died for their lords because their actions aligned with their principles and their devotion to righteousness was boundless. Therefore, Su Qin was not trusted by all under heaven but became a loyal retainer of Yan; Bai Gui lost six cities in battle yet for Wei he captured Zhongshan. Why is this so? Because there was genuine understanding between them. When Su Qin served as prime minister of Yan, the people of Yan slandered him to the king. The king drew his sword in anger but then fed him a fast horse; Bai Gui became prominent in Zhongshan, yet the people of Zhongshan slandered him to Duke Wen of Wei. In response, Duke Wen threw a night-glowing jade disc at him. Why is this so? Because the two rulers and their ministers had hearts laid bare in mutual trust—how could such a bond be shaken by idle words!

## 19

Therefore, whether beautiful or ugly, when women enter the palace they face jealousy; whether virtuous or not, when men of talent enter court they are envied. In the past, Sima Xi had his knees broken in Song but eventually became prime minister of Zhongshan; Fan Jiu suffered broken ribs and teeth in Wei yet ultimately became the Marquis Ying. These two men both adhered to certain principles, abandoned private interests of factions, and held isolated positions; thus, they could not avoid falling victim to jealous individuals. Therefore, Shentu Di drowned himself in the river, and Xu Yan carried stones into the sea. They could not be accepted by society; they refused to act dishonorably or curry favor, nor did they form factions in court to sway the ruler's mind. Therefore, Bai Li Xi begged for food on the road, yet Duke Mu entrusted him with state affairs; Ning Qi fed oxen beneath a cart, yet Duke Huan placed the country in his hands. Did these two men rely on official positions at court or seek praise from those around them before the two rulers trusted and employed them? Their hearts were moved, their actions aligned; they became as close as glue and lacquer. Even brothers could not separate them—how then could the words of many people confuse them? Therefore, partial listening breeds corruption, and sole reliance on one person leads to chaos. In the past, Lu listened to Ji Sun's arguments and drove Confucius away; Song trusted Zi Han's plan and imprisoned Mozi. With the eloquence of Kong and Mo, they could not protect themselves from slander and flattery, and thus both states fell into peril. Why was that? Because the voices of many can melt gold, and accumulated slander can destroy bones. Therefore, Qin employed You Yu, a Rong person, and dominated Central China; Qi used Meng Tuo, a Yue person, to strengthen the power of King Wei and King Xuan. Were these two states bound by convention, constrained by worldly opinion, or swayed by biased words? They listened openly and observed impartially, thus earning their names in the world of their time. Therefore, when intentions align, even Hu and Yue peoples become brothers—this is exemplified by You Yu and Meng Tuo of the Yue. When intentions do not align, even close relatives are driven out and not welcomed back—this is seen in Zhu, Xiang, Guan, and Cai. If a ruler today could truly follow the principles of Qi and Qin, rather than imitate the listening habits of Song and Lu, then the Five Hegemons would be unworthy of mention, and the Three Kings would easily be surpassed.

## 20

Therefore, a sage ruler awakens to reality, abandons Zi Zhi's mentality, and is thus not deceived by the virtue of Tian Chang; he enshrines the descendants of Bi Gan, repairs the tomb of pregnant women, and thus restores his achievements throughout the world. Why is that? Because he desires goodness without satiety. The Duke Wen of Jin forgave his enemies and became a powerful hegemon among the feudal lords; Duke Huan of Qi employed his enemy, and thus unified and stabilized the world. Why? Because they were kind-hearted, earnest, and sincere in their hearts; such virtues cannot be feigned with empty words.

## 21

As for Qin's adoption of Shang Yang's laws, it weakened Han and Wei in the east, made its military strong throughout the world, yet ultimately led to his being torn apart by chariots; Yue employed the strategies of Daifu Zhong, subdued the powerful Wu state, dominated Central China, and yet ultimately saw him executed. Therefore, Sun Shuao left his position as prime minister three times without regret, and Yuling Zizhong declined the post of San Gong to become a gardener for others. If a ruler today truly could abandon arrogance, harbor thoughts of gratitude, open his heart and show sincerity, offer loyalty and kindness, share both adversity and prosperity with others, and never withhold from scholars, then Jie's dogs could be made to bark at Yao, and Zhidi's followers could be made to attack You; How much more so with the authority of a ten-thousand-chariot state and the resources of a sage king? If that is the case, then Jing Ke's drowning of seven families or Yao Li's burning his wife and children—how could such things even be worth mentioning!

## 22

I have heard that a bright pearl of the moon, or a luminous jade disk, if cast in darkness upon someone on the road, would cause no one but to draw their sword and glance suspiciously. Why? Because they arrive without prior connection or reason. Twisted wood with deep roots, round and strange in shape, yet made into a vessel for the ruler of ten thousand chariots. Why? Because those around him first prepared a place for it. Therefore, if something arrives without prior connection, even though it may be the Zhuihou pearl or a night-glowing jade disk, resentment will still arise and virtue will not be recognized. Therefore, if someone speaks in advance on its behalf, even a dead tree or rotting stump can achieve merit and never be forgotten. Now, among the commoners in humble circumstances throughout the world, those who are poor and lowly—though they may possess the methods of Yao and Shun, hold the eloquence of Yi Yin and Guan Zhong, or harbor the loyal intentions of Long Peng and Bi Gan—and wish to serve their contemporary ruler with utmost loyalty, yet if they lack prior connections or introductions, even though they exhaust their wisdom in seeking to express sincerity and assist the ruler's governance, the sovereign will inevitably show signs of suspicion, drawing his sword and casting wary glances. Thus, commoners cannot even attain the status of a dead tree or rotting trunk.

## 23

Therefore, sage rulers govern the world and manage customs, standing alone in their transformation above the potter's wheel, unswayed by vulgar or disorderly words, and undeterred by the voices of the many. Therefore, Emperor Qin Shi Huang trusted the words of Zhong Shuzi Meng Jia and believed Jing Ke's arguments, yet a dagger was secretly drawn; King Wen of Zhou hunted by the rivers Jing and Wei, brought back Yu Shang, and thereby became king over all under heaven. Thus, Qin trusted those around him and was killed; Zhou employed the obscure and unknown and became king. Why? Because they were able to transcend the constraints of conventional speech, ride beyond the confines of local opinion, and alone perceive the clear and open path of truth.

## 24

Now, rulers are submerged in flattery and bound by the constraints of court etiquette, causing free-spirited men to be treated no differently from oxen alongside fine steeds—this is why Bao Jiao was enraged at society and refused the pleasures of wealth and honor.

## 25

I have heard that those who enter court in splendid attire do not allow profit to defile righteousness, and those who polish their reputations do not let desire harm conduct. Therefore, when the county was named Shengmu, Zengzi refused to enter; when a town bore the name Chaoge, Mozi turned his carriage around. Now, if one wishes to make the broad-minded men of the world bow before the weighty authority and submit to the prestige of rank, then they will turn their faces in disgraceful conduct to serve flatterers and seek favor with those around the ruler. In that case, scholars would merely hide in caves and thickets, how could they possibly offer complete loyalty and rush to appear at court! When the letter was presented to Prince Xiao Wang of Liang, he had the man released, and ultimately made him a distinguished guest.

## 26

Grand Historian said: Although Lu Lian's intent did not conform to great righteousness, I still admire his position as a commoner who lived freely and boldly, unyielding before feudal lords, engaging in discourse of the age, and challenging the authority of ministers. Zou Yang's words, though not lacking in boldness, still contain comparisons and analogies that are truly sorrowful. He can indeed be called upright and unbent; therefore, I have included him among the biographies.
