# Hereditary House of Relatives

## 1

Since ancient times, emperors who received their mandate and emperors of later generations who maintained the dynasty through peaceful means did not achieve success solely because of their internal virtue; it was also due to assistance from relatives on their mother's side. The rise of the Xia dynasty was due to Tushan, while Jie's downfall was caused by Mu Xi. The rise of the Yin (Shang) dynasty was due to You Song, and Zhou's death was because he favored Daji. The rise of the Zhou dynasty was due to Jiangyuan and Tai Ren, while King You's capture resulted from his indulgence in Bao Si. Therefore, the Yi Jing (Book of Changes) begins with Qian and Kun, the Shi Jing (Book of Songs) starts with Guan Ju, the Shu Jing (Book of Documents) praises Li Jiang, and Chunqiu censures those who do not personally go to welcome their bride. The relationship between husband and wife is the great moral bond of human society. In rites, none are used with more earnestness than in marriage. When music is harmonious, the four seasons are in harmony; changes between yin and yang govern all things. How can one not be cautious? People are able to promote the Way, but they cannot do anything about fate. How profound is the love between spouses! A ruler cannot obtain it from his subjects, a father cannot obtain it from his son; how much less can those of lower status? After joyful union, there may still be no descendants. Even if descendants are produced, one might still not attain a happy ending; is this not determined by fate? Confucius rarely spoke of fate; it was indeed difficult to discuss. Without understanding the transformations between the visible and invisible realms, how could one recognize human nature and destiny?

## 2

Grand Historian said: Before the Qin dynasty, records were still very brief; detailed accounts cannot be obtained. After the Han dynasty was founded, Lü Eyu became Emperor Gaozu's official empress, and her son became crown prince. In later years, when Lü Eyu's beauty faded and the emperor's affection waned, Empress Qi of the 戚 family gained favor; her son Yurui was nearly replaced as crown prince several times. After Emperor Gaozu died, Empress Lü exterminated the 戚 clan, executed King Zhao, and only those in the emperor's harem who had never been favored or kept at a distance remained unharmed.

## 3

Empress Lü's eldest daughter was the wife of Zhang Ao, Marquis of Xuanping; Zhang Ao's daughter became Empress of Emperor Hui. Empress Dowager Lü, because of her close relationship, tried every possible means to have a son; she ultimately had no children and fraudulently took the child of someone from the imperial harem as her own. After Emperor Hui's death, the empire was newly stabilized and had not been long at peace; there was no clear heir to the throne. Therefore, they honored the maternal relatives and enfeoffed various members of the 吕 clan as kings to serve as support; they also made Lady Lü Lu's daughter empress of the young emperor, hoping to consolidate their power firmly. However, it was of no avail.

## 4

After Empress Dowager Gao died, she was buried together with Emperor Gaozu in the Changling Mausoleum. Lü Lu, Lü Chan and others feared punishment and plotted rebellion. The ministers acted against them; Heaven guided the course of events, and in the end, the 吕 clan was destroyed. Only Empress of Emperor Hui was placed to reside in Beigong Palace. They welcomed and enthroned Prince Dai, who became Emperor Wen; he honored the ancestral temple of the Han dynasty. Was this not Heaven's will? If it were not the Mandate of Heaven, who could have achieved this?

## 5

Empress Dowager Bo's father was a native of Wu, surnamed Bo. During the Qin dynasty, he had an affair with Lady Wei, a woman from an old family related to the former King of Wei, and they gave birth to Bo Ji. However, Bo An died in Shanyin, so he was buried there.

## 6

When the feudal lords rebelled against the Qin dynasty, Wei Bao was enthroned as King of Wei and Lady Wei sent her daughter into the Wei palace. Lady Wei's relative Xu Fu, a physiognomist, examined Bo Ji and said she would give birth to an emperor. At that time, Xiang Yu was confronting King Huai of Han at Yingyang, and the fate of the world had not yet been decided. Initially, Wei Bao allied with Han against Chu; but upon hearing Xu Fu's prophecy, he secretly rejoiced and defected from Han to remain neutral, instead forming an alliance with Chu. loom The Han sent Cao Can et al. to capture and subdue King Wei Bao, converted his state into a commandery, and Bo Ji was assigned to the weaving quarters. After Bao's death, King of Han entered the weaving quarters and saw that Bo Ji was beautiful; he ordered her to be taken into the imperial harem. For more than a year, however, she did not receive his favor. When Bo Ji was young, she had been close friends with Guan Fu Ren and Zhao Zier; they made a pact saying: "Whoever attains honor first must not forget the other." Later, Lady Guan and Zhao Zier were favored by King of Han before Bo Ji. While King of Han was seated at the Chenggao Pavilion in Henan Palace, these two beauties laughed about Bo Ji's earlier pact. King of Han heard this and asked them the reason; they truthfully explained everything to him. King of Han felt deeply moved, took pity on Bo Ji, and summoned her for his favor that very day. Bo Ji said: "Last night I dreamed of a blue dragon lying upon my abdomen." Emperor Gaozu said, "This is an omen of great fortune. I will help you fulfill it." After one night together, she gave birth to a son, who later became King Dai. Thereafter, Bo Ji rarely saw Emperor Gaozu.

## 7

After Emperor Gaozu's death, all the favored concubines, including Empress Qi and others, were imprisoned by Empress Dowager Lü out of anger; they could not leave the palace. However, because Bo Ji had rarely been favored by Emperor Gaozu, she was allowed to leave and accompany her son to Dai, becoming the Tai Wang Taihou (Grand Empress Dowager of Dai). Bo Ji's younger brother, Bo Zhao, followed her to Dai.

## 8

King Dai reigned for seventeen years when Empress Dowager Gao passed away. The ministers discussed the choice of a new emperor and were wary of the powerful maternal relatives from the 吕 clan. They all praised the Bo family for their kindness and virtue, so they welcomed King Dai to be enthroned as Emperor Wen. The empress dowager was then re-designated as Empress Dowager, and her younger brother Bo Zhao was enfeoffed as Hou of Zhi.

## 9

Bo Taihou's mother had also died earlier and was buried north of Linyang. At this time, they posthumously honored Bo An as Lingwen Hou. In Kuaiji Commandery, a tomb estate was established for 300 families; officials down to the level of cheng were assigned to guard and maintain the tomb, offering food and sacrifices in accordance with regulations at the ancestral shrine. A similar estate for Lady Lingwen Hou was also established north of Linyang, following the same rites and ceremonies as those for Lingwen Hou. Empress Dowager Bo considered her maternal family to be descendants of the former King of Wei. Since she had lost her parents at an early age, those from the Wei clan who had supported Empress Dowager Bo were summoned and restored; they received rewards and honors according to their closeness in relation to her. Only one person of the Bo family was enfeoffed as a marquis.

## 10

Empress Dowager Bo died in the second year after Emperor Wen's reign, which was also the second year before Emperor Jing took the throne; she was buried at Nanling. Since Empress Lü had been buried together with Emperor Gao in Changling, a separate tomb was specially built for her near the Baling Mausoleum of Emperor Wen.

## 11

Empress Dowager Dou was a native of Guanjin, Qinghe, Zhao Commandery. During the reign of Empress Dowager Lü, Lady Dou entered the palace as a daughter of an upright family to serve the empress dowager. The empress dowager distributed palace women among the kings, five each; Lady Dou was included in this group. Lady Dou's family lived in Qinghe, and she wished to be assigned near Zhao where her home was. She requested the officer in charge to send a eunuch official with this message: "I must be placed among those assigned to Zhao." The eunuch forgot about her request and mistakenly placed her in the group assigned to Dai. After the records were submitted, an imperial edict approved it, and she was to proceed accordingly. Lady Dou wept bitterly, resenting the eunuch for his mistake; she did not want to go but was forced by others and finally agreed. When she arrived at Dai, King Dai favored Lady Dou alone; they had a daughter named Piao, then later two sons. The queen of King Dai gave birth to four sons. Before the King of Dai was enthroned as emperor, his queen had already died. When King Dai became emperor, all four sons born to the former queen fell ill and died one after another. Several months after Emperor Wen took the throne, the ministers requested that a crown prince be appointed; Lady Dou's eldest son was the oldest and thus made crown prince. Lady Dou was enfeoffed as empress, and her daughter Piao became the Chang Gongzhu (Grand Princess). The following year, his younger son Wu was enfeoffed as King of Dai; later he was transferred to Liang and became King Xiao Wang of Liang.

## 12

Empress Dou's mother died early in life and was buried at Guanjin. At this time, Empress Dowager Bo issued an edict to the officials, posthumously honoring Lady Dou's father as Ancheng Hou and her mother as Ancheng Fu Ren. An estate with 200 households was ordered to be established in Qinghe; officials down to the rank of cheng were appointed to guard and maintain it, following the regulations similar to those for the Lingwen Estate.

## 13

Empress Dou had an elder brother named Dou Changjun and a younger brother named Dou Guangguo, courtesy name Shaoshen. When Shaoshen was about four or five years old, his family was poor and he was kidnapped and sold; his family did not know where he had been taken. He was passed through more than ten households and eventually ended up in Yiyang, where his master made him work cutting charcoal in the mountains. One evening, over a hundred people slept along the riverbank; when the bank collapsed, it buried all those sleeping there to death, but Shaoshen alone escaped unharmed. After divining that he would become a marquis in several days, he followed his family to Chang'an. He heard that Empress Dou had recently been enthroned and learned that her family lived in Guanjin, surnamed Dou. Although he had left when still very young, Guangguo remembered the name of his county and surname. He also often picked mulberry leaves with his elder sister, which became a sign for identification; thus, he submitted a petition to the court stating his case. Empress Dou informed Emperor Wen of this matter. The emperor summoned him for an audience and questioned him; Guangguo explained everything in detail, and it turned out to be true. The emperor then asked again what other evidence could verify this? He replied, "When my sister left me to the west, she bid farewell to me in a roadside inn. She asked for water to wash my hair and requested food for me before leaving." At this, Empress Dou held him and wept, her tears streaming down in both eyes. The attendants on either side of the throne fell to their knees and wept as well, helping Empress Dou mourn. He was richly rewarded with land, houses, and money; his uncles were enfeoffed, and the family settled in Chang'an.

## 14

Jiang Hou, General Guan, and others said: "We have not died; our lives now depend on these two people." These two men come from humble origins and cannot do without carefully selected mentors, attendants, and guests; this is also a matter of great importance, just like the 吕 clan affair. Therefore, they chose virtuous and upright men with moral integrity to live among them. Because of this, Dou Changjun and Shaoshen became modest gentlemen who dared not behave arrogantly toward others because of their noble status.

## 15

Empress Dou fell ill and lost her sight. Emperor Wen favored Lady Shen of Handan and Lady Yin, but neither had children. After Emperor Wen died and Emperor Jing took the throne, Guangguo was enfeoffed as Marquis of Zhangwu. Changjun had already died before this; his son Pengzu was enfeoffed as Nanpi Hou. During the rebellion of Wu and Chu, Dou Taihou's cousin by marriage, Dou Ying, who was proud of his chivalrous nature, led troops into battle. He was enfeoffed as Weiqi Hou due to military merit. In total, three members of the Dou family were enfeoffed as marquises.

## 16

Empress Dowager Dou favored the teachings of Huangdi and Laozi; as a result, Emperor Jing, the crown prince, and all members of the Dou clan had to study the works of Huangdi and Laozi and respect their doctrines.

## 17

Empress Dowager Dou died six years after Emperor Jing (the sixth year of the Jianyuan era) and was buried together with him at Baling. Her will decreed that all the gold, money, and possessions from the eastern palace be given to her daughter-in-law Chang Gongzhu Piao.

## 18

Empress Dowager Wang was a native of Huili; her mother was named Zang'er. Zang'er, the daughter of the former King of Yan, Zang Tu's descendant. Zang'er married Wang Zhong of Huili and bore a son named Xin and two daughters. After Zhong died, Zang'er remarried into the Tian family of Changling and bore two sons, Fen and Sheng. Zang'er's eldest daughter had married Jin Wangsun and already bore a daughter. Zang'er consulted diviners, who said that both of her daughters would become noble. In order to make the two daughters remarkable, she therefore took them away from the Jin family. The Jin family was angry and refused to let go of her, so Zang'er sent her into the palace of the crown prince. The crown prince favored and loved her, and she bore him three daughters and one son. While the child was still in her womb, Lady Wang had a dream of the sun entering her arms. She told the crown prince about it, and he said, "This is an omen of great fortune." Before she gave birth, Emperor Wen died, and Emperor Jing ascended the throne. Lady Wang then bore a son.

## 19

Previously, Zang'er had also sent her younger daughter, Erxu, into the palace; Erxu bore four sons.

## 20

When Emperor Jing was crown prince, Empress Dowager Bo arranged for a woman from the Bo family to be his wife. After Emperor Jing ascended the throne, he enfeoffed this consort as Empress Bo. The empress had no children and was not favored. After Empress Dowager Bo died, Empress Bo was deposed.

## 21

Emperor Jing's eldest son was Rong, whose mother was Lady Li. Lady Li was a native of Qi. Rong was made crown prince. Chang Gongzhu Piao had a daughter and wanted to give her as a consort. Lady Li was jealous, and many of Emperor Jing's favored consorts had been introduced to the emperor by Chang Gongzhu. These women were more favored than Lady Li, so she grew increasingly resentful and angry. She refused Chang Gongzhu's proposal and declined it. Chang Gongzhu wanted to give her daughter to Lady Wang, who agreed. Chang Gongzhu became angry and daily slandered Lady Li to Emperor Jing, saying: "Lady Li, when meeting with the emperor's favored consorts and concubines, often has her attendants spit on their backs while performing occult arts of witchcraft." Because of this, Emperor Jing began to resent Lady Li.

## 22

Emperor Jing once fell ill and was unhappy, so he entrusted his sons who had been enfeoffed as kings to Lady Li and said: "After I die, take good care of them." Lady Li was angry, refused to respond, and spoke disrespectfully. Emperor Jing was furious but restrained his anger and did not act on it immediately.

## 23

Chang Gongzhu often praised the virtues of Lady Wang's son, and Emperor Jing also found him virtuous. Moreover, there was still the omen of the sun from his previous dream; thus, a decision had not yet been made. Lady Wang knew that Emperor Jing resented Lady Li and that his anger was still unresolved, so she secretly sent someone to urge the ministers to enfeoff Lady Li as empress. After completing his report on official matters, the Grand Minister said: "As the saying goes, 'a son is honored because of his mother, and a mother is honored because of her son.' Now the crown prince's mother has no title; she should be enfeoffed as empress." Emperor Jing became angry and said, "Is this what you think is appropriate to say?" He then had the Grand Minister investigated and executed, and deposed the crown prince as King of Linjiang. Lady Li became even more furious and resentful; she was not allowed to see the emperor and died from grief. Eventually, Lady Wang was enfeoffed as empress, her son became crown prince, and the empress's brother Xin was enfeoffed as Gai Hou.

## 24

After Emperor Jing died, the crown prince succeeded to the throne as emperor. The mother of Empress Dowager Wang, Zang'er, was honored as Lady Pingyuan Jun. Tian Fen was enfeoffed as Wu'an Hou and Tian Sheng as Zhouyang Hou.

## 25

Emperor Jing had thirteen sons; one became emperor, and the remaining twelve were all enfeoffed as kings. Erxu died early, but her four sons were all enfeoffed as Kings. Empress Dowager Wang's eldest daughter was known as Princess Pingyang, the second as Princess Nangong, and the third as Princess Linlv.

## 26

Gai Hou Xin loved drinking alcohol. Tian Fen and Tian Sheng were greedy and skillful in literary expression. Wang Zhong had died early; he was buried in Huili, posthumously honored as Gong Hou, and two hundred households were assigned to maintain his ancestral shrine. After Lady Pingyuan Jun died, she was buried with the Tian family at Changling, and her tomb estate was established on a scale equal to that of Gong Hou. Empress Dowager Wang died sixteen years after Emperor Jing, in the fourth year of the Yuanshuo era, and was buried together with him in Yangling. In total, three members from Empress Dowager Wang's family were enfeoffed as hou (marquises).

## 27

Empress Wei, courtesy name Zifu, was born into humble circumstances. Her family's surname was Wei, and they were from the fief of Marquis Pingyang. Zifu served as a singer in the household of Princess Pingyang. When Emperor Wu first ascended the throne, for several years he had no sons. Princess Pingyang selected more than ten daughters from good families and prepared them in her household with adornments. After performing a purification ritual at Bashi, Emperor Wu passed by the residence of Princess Pingyang on his return journey. Princess Pingyang presented her attendants who were beautiful women for him to see. The emperor was not pleased. After drinking, the singers performed; the emperor looked on and was particularly fond of Wei Zifu. On that day, when Emperor Wu got up to change his clothes, Lady Zifu happened to be waiting in the chamber for clothing and was favored by him. When the emperor returned to sit down, he was very pleased. He bestowed a thousand catties of gold upon Princess Pingyang as a gift. Princess Pingyang then presented Zifu to the emperor, who sent her into the palace. As Zifu got on the carriage, Princess Pingyang clasped her back and said: "Go now; eat heartily and be strong. Encourage yourself!" "When you become noble, do not forget me." After more than a year in the palace, she was no longer favored. Emperor Wu selected palace women who were not suitable for service and dismissed them back to their families. Wei Zifu was among those seen, wept bitterly, and requested to be dismissed from the palace. The emperor took pity on her, favored her again, and she became pregnant; thus, her status and favor grew increasingly high. He summoned Zifu's elder brother Wei Changjun and younger brother Wei Qing as Attendants-in-Ordinary (Shizhong). Later, Lady Zifu became greatly favored and was highly cherished; in total, she bore three daughters and one son. Her son's name was Ju.

## 28

Initially, when the emperor was crown prince, he married a daughter of Chang Gongzhu as his consort. When he became emperor, this consort was enfeoffed as Empress; her surname was Chen, and she had no children. The emperor's becoming heir to the throne was largely due to the efforts of Grand Chang Gongzhu, and thus Empress Chen became arrogant and proud. When she heard that Wei Zifu had become greatly favored, she was furious; on several occasions, she nearly died from anger. The emperor became even more angry. Empress Chen practiced occult arts of female witchcraft, and her actions were somewhat discovered; thus, she was deposed, and Wei Zifu was enfeoffed as the new empress.

## 29

Empress Chen's mother, Grand Chang Gongzhu, was the elder sister of Emperor Jing. She repeatedly rebuked her niece, Princess Pingyang, saying: "Had it not been for me, the emperor would never have become heir to the throne; yet now he has abandoned my daughter. How could he be so ungrateful and forget his roots?" Princess Pingyang replied, "She was deposed simply because she had no children." Empress Chen spent a total of nine million coins on physicians in her quest for a child, but ultimately bore none.

## 30

After Wei Zifu was enfeoffed empress, Wei Changjun had already died; thus, the emperor appointed Wei Qing as a general. After achieving military success against the Xiongnu, he was enfeoffed as Changping Hou. Wei Qing's three sons, still in swaddling clothes, were all enfeoffed with the title of Liehou (Marquis). Additionally, Wei Zifu's so-called elder sister, Wei Shaoer, bore a son named Huo Qubing. Because of his military achievements, he was enfeoffed Guanjun Hou and given the title of Biaoqi General. Wei Qing was known as the Da Jiangjun (Grand General). The emperor enfeoffed Empress Wei's son Ju as crown prince. Members of the Wei family rose to prominence through military achievements; in total, five individuals were enfeoffed with marquisates.

## 31

Later, as Empress Wei's beauty faded, Lady Wang of Zhao became favored and bore a son who was enfeoffed King of Qi.

## 32

Lady Wang died early. Meanwhile, Lady Li of Zhongshan was favored and bore one son who became King of Changyi.

## 33

Lady Li died early; her elder brother, Li Yannian, gained favor through his musical talents and was given the title Xielv. Xielv was a former musician or performer. Both brothers were executed for corruption, and their entire clan was exterminated. At that time, their eldest brother Guangli was the Ershi General who had campaigned against Dayuan. He escaped punishment and returned; after Emperor Wu had exterminated the Li family, he later took pity on them and enfeoffed Guangli as Hou of Haixi.

## 34

Two sons born to other concubines were enfeoffed as King of Yan and King of Guangling, respectively. Their mother was not favored and died from grief.

## 35

After Lady Li's death, there were other consorts such as Consort Yin who gained favor in turn. However, they all came from the ranks of performers and entertainers; women from families of officials or marquises with fiefs could not be matched to a sovereign.

## 36

Master Chu said: When I was serving as an officer, I asked Zhongli Sheng, who was well-versed in the old customs and traditions of the Han dynasty. He said: "Empress Dowager Wang had a daughter before she entered the palace; her father was Jin Wangsun." Jin Wangsun had already died. After Emperor Jingdi's death and before Emperor Wudi ascended the throne, Empress Dowager Wang was alone. At that time, Han Wangsun, whose name was Yansu, gained the emperor's favor and took the opportunity to inform Emperor Wudi in private that Empress Dowager Wang had a daughter living in Changling. Emperor Wudi said: "Why was this not mentioned earlier!" He then sent envoys to go ahead and inspect the situation at her home. Emperor Wudi personally went to fetch her. The procession blocked the road; vanguard cavalry with banners emerged from Hengcheng Gate, and the imperial carriage rushed to Changling. When the procession reached a small market to the west and entered the neighborhood, the gate of the district was closed. The guards suddenly forced it open, and the imperial carriage proceeded directly into this neighborhood until it stopped outside Jin's family residence. Emperor Wudi ordered elite cavalry to surround their house in case they fled; he personally went to retrieve her but could not find her inside. He immediately dispatched his attendants and court officials to enter the residence and search for her. The family was frightened, and the girl hid under a bed inside the house. They were helped out of the house and ordered to perform a formal bow in greeting. Emperor Wudi got off his carriage and wept, saying: "Oh!" "Dear elder sister, why have you hidden yourself so deeply!" The emperor ordered a secondary carriage to carry her and then turned the procession around in haste, heading straight back to Changle Palace. Orders were given at each gate along the way to record their passage so that they could be granted an audience with Empress Dowager Wang. Empress Dowager Wang said: "The emperor must be tired; where have you come from?" The emperor replied: "Today, I found my sister at Changling and brought her along." He turned and said: "Pay respects to the Empress Dowager!" Empress Dowager Wang asked: "Are you a certain person?" She replied, "Yes." Empress Dowager Wang wept for her, and the woman also knelt on the ground crying. Emperor Wudi presented wine and offered a toast to his sister, gave her ten thousand coins, three hundred slaves and servants, one hundred qing of public land, and a luxurious mansion as gifts. Empress Dowager Wang declined, saying: "This has cost the emperor too much." At this time, they summoned Princess Pingyang, Princess Nangong, and Princess Linlv to come together for an audience with their sister, who was then given the title Xiucheng Jun. She had one son and one daughter. The son was titled Xiucheng Zizhong, while the daughter became a queen consort of a feudal prince. These two children were not members of the Liu clan; therefore, Empress Dowager Wang took pity on them. Xiucheng Zizhong was arrogant and indulgent, bullying officials and commoners alike; everyone suffered from his behavior.

## 37

Wei Zifu was enfeoffed empress. Her younger brother, Wei Qing (courtesy name Zhongqing), was granted the title of Changping Hou as Grand General. He had four sons; his eldest son, Kang, became a marquis' heir apparent and served regularly in the imperial court as an attendant, enjoying great favor. His three younger brothers were also enfeoffed as marquises, each receiving 1,300 households: the first was Hou of Yian, the second Hou of Fagan, and the third Hou of Yichun. Their family's prestige shook the entire realm. People across the land sang: "Do not rejoice at having a son, nor be angry at having a daughter; have you not heard that Wei Zifu rules the world!"

## 38

At this time, Princess Pingyang was a widow and should have married a marquis according to protocol. The princess discussed with her attendants which of the marquises in Chang'an would be suitable as a husband, and all suggested that Grand General Wei Qing was the best choice. The princess laughed and said: "He is from my family, often commanding horsemen to accompany me in and out; how could he possibly be my husband?" Those around her replied: "Now the Grand General's elder sister is empress, and his three sons are marquises; their wealth and prestige have shaken the entire realm. How could you possibly find someone to match him?" Thus, the princess agreed to the marriage. She informed Empress Wei, who then relayed it to Emperor Wudi; an imperial decree was issued for General Wei to marry Princess Pingyang.

## 39

Master Chu said: A man can transform like a dragon. The Classic says: "A snake transforms into a dragon, but its pattern does not change; a family becomes a state, yet its surname remains unchanged." When a man attains wealth and honor at the right time, all misfortunes vanish; his brilliance and glory shine forth. How could his humble past possibly burden him!

## 40

During Emperor Wudi's reign, Consort Yin was a favored lady-in-waiting. Xing Fu ren was known by the title Xing'e; people commonly called her "Xinge." The rank of Xinge was equivalent to that of a central official with an annual salary of 2,000 dou; the rank of Ronghua was equivalent to an official with a salary of 1,000 dou per year; and the rank of Concubine Yin was equal to that of a marquis. Concubine Chang followed this path from being a jieyu to becoming empress.

## 41

Consort Yin and Consort Xing were both favored at the same time, but an imperial decree forbade them from meeting each other. Consort Yin personally requested Emperor Wudi, expressing her wish to see Consort Xing; the emperor agreed. The emperor then ordered other consorts and attendants to dress up, along with dozens of palace attendants, pretending to be Consort Xing coming forward. Consort Yin went forward to see them and said: "This is not the real Consort Xing." The emperor asked, "How do you know this?" She replied: "By looking at her appearance and figure, she is not worthy to be in the presence of an emperor." Hearing this, Emperor Wudi then issued a decree ordering Consort Xing to wear her old clothes and come forward alone. Consort Yin saw her from afar and said: "This is the real one." At this, she lowered her head in sorrow and wept bitterly, lamenting that she was not as good as Consort Xing. A proverb says: "When a beautiful woman enters the house, it becomes an enemy to an ugly one."

## 42

Master Chu said: One need not bathe in rivers or seas; what matters is removing dirt; a horse need not be a Qiji to be valuable; what counts is its ability to run well; a scholar need not come from an illustrious family; what matters is that he understands the Way; a woman need not be of noble lineage; what counts is her virtue and beauty. The Classic says: "A woman may be beautiful or ugly, but jealousy will arise once she enters the inner chambers; a man may be virtuous or unworthy, but envy will emerge when he enters court." A beautiful woman is the enemy of an ugly one. How true this is!

## 43

The Lady of Gouyi, surnamed Zhao, was a native of Hejian. She gained Emperor Wudi's favor and bore one son, who became Emperor Zhao. Emperor Wudi was seventy years old when he fathered Emperor Zhao. When Emperor Zhao ascended the throne, he was only five years old.

## 44

After Crown Prince Wei was deposed, no new crown prince had yet been appointed. King Yan Dan submitted a memorial requesting to return his fief and serve as an imperial guard in the palace. Emperor Wudi was furious, and immediately beheaded his envoy at the northern gate.

## 45

The emperor resided in Ganzhuang Palace and summoned painters to depict the scene of Duke Zhou carrying King Cheng. At this point, the court officials realized that Emperor Wudi intended to appoint his youngest son as heir. A few days later, the emperor rebuked Lady Gouyi. The lady removed her hairpins and earrings and kowtowed in submission. The emperor said: "Take her away, send her to the Yecheng prison!" As she turned back to look at him, the emperor said: "Hurry up and go; you will not live!" The lady died in Yunyang Palace. At that time, a sudden wind blew up dust, and the common people felt sorrowful and moved. Envoys carried her coffin at night to bury her there, marking the location with a seal. Later, when the emperor was alone and asked his attendants: "What do people say about this?" The attendants replied: "People are saying that since you intend to appoint her son as heir, why did you remove his mother?" The emperor said: "Indeed. This is not something those foolish common people can understand." In the past, the reason why states became chaotic was because the ruler was young and his mother was strong-willed. A dominant queen dowager living alone would become arrogant, indulge in licentiousness, and act as she pleased, with no one able to restrain her. "Have you not heard of Empress Dowager Lü?" Therefore, among those who bore sons for Emperor Wu, regardless of whether they were male or female, their mothers were all punished and died; can this not be called the wisdom of a sage! This clear-sighted long-term planning for future generations is certainly beyond what shallow-minded and foolish Confucian scholars can achieve. To bestow the posthumous title "Wu" (Martial) upon him, was this not well deserved!
