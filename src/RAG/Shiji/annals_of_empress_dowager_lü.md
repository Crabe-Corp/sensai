# Annals of Empress Dowager Lü

## 1

Lü Taohou was a consort of Gaozu when he was obscure; she bore Emperor Xiaohui and the empress dowager Lu Yuan. When Gaozu became King of Han, he acquired Qi Ji of Dingtao and favored her; she bore Prince Yurui, the Marquis of Zhao. Emperor Xiaohui was kind and weak; Gaozu considered him unlike himself, and often wanted to depose the crown prince, replacing him with Qi Ji's son Yurui, who resembled Gaozu. Qi Ji was favored and often accompanied the emperor on his tours to Guandong; she wept day and night, wishing for her son to be made crown prince in place of the current one. Lü Hou was older and often stayed behind as regent; she rarely saw the emperor, becoming increasingly estranged. After Yurui was made King of Zhao, he had been considered as a replacement for the crown prince on several occasions; it was only due to the ministers' objections and the strategies of Liu Hou that the crown prince remained unremoved.

## 2

Lü Hou was a resolute and determined person; she assisted Gaozu in securing the empire, and many of the high-ranking ministers were executed through her efforts. Lü Hou had two brothers, both of whom became generals. Her elder brother Zhoulv Hou died in service; his son Lü Tai was enfeoffed as the Marquis of Li, and his son Chan as the Marquis of Jiao; Her younger brother Lü Shizhi was made the Marquis of Jiancheng.

## 3

On the Jiachen day of the fourth month, twelfth year of Gaozu's reign (April 25, 196 BC), he died in the Changle Palace; the crown prince succeeded to the throne as emperor. At that time, Gaozu had eight sons: his eldest son Fei, who was the elder brother of Emperor Xiaohui but not of the same mother; Fei was King of Qi; The rest were Emperor Xiaohui's brothers: Qi Ji's son Yurui was King of Zhao; Fu Ren Bo's son Heng was King of Dai; sons of various consorts, Zi Hui was King of Liang, Zi You as King of Huaiyang, Zi Chang as King of Huainan, and Zi Jian as King of Yan. Gaozu's younger brother Jiao was the King of Chu; his nephew Bi was the King of Wu. Fuliang, a non-Liu clan official and son of Fanjun Wu Rui, was made the King of Changsha.

## 4

Lü Hou bore the deepest resentment toward Qi Fu Ren and her son, the King of Zhao; thus she ordered Qi Fu Ren to be imprisoned in Yongxiang and summoned the King of Zhao. The envoys returned three times, when Zhou Chang, the Sui Wang Xiang and Marquis of Jianping, said to the envoy: "Gao Di entrusted me with the King of Zhao; the King is still young. I have secretly heard that the Empress Dowager resents Qi Fu Ren, and wants to summon the King of Zhao and execute him together; I dare not send away the king." "The king is also ill and cannot obey the imperial decree." Lü Hou became very angry, so she sent someone to summon the Sui Xiang. After the Sui Xiang was summoned to Chang'an, they then had someone again call for the King of Zhao. The king came, but had not yet arrived. Emperor Xiaohui was compassionate and kind; knowing the Empress Dowager's anger, he personally went to Bashi to meet the King of Zhao, brought him into the palace, and took care of his daily life and meals himself. The empress dowager wanted to kill him but could not find an opportunity. In the twelfth month of the first year of Emperor Xiaohui's reign (January 18, 194 BC), the emperor went out early in the morning to hunt. The King of Zhao was young and could not rise early. Upon hearing that he was alone, the empress dowager sent someone to give him poisoned wine to drink. By dawn, Emperor Xiaohui had returned and found the King of Zhao already dead. Thus they moved Prince You of Huaiyang to become the new King of Zhao. In summer, an imperial decree was issued posthumously bestowing the title Lingwu Hou on the father of Marquis Li. The empress dowager then cut off Qi Fu Ren's hands and feet, removed her eyes, cauterized her ears, gave her drugs to make her mute, and made her live in a privy; she was called "ren zhi." After several days, the empress dowager summoned Emperor Xiaohui to view the "human pig." Emperor Xiaohui saw it, asked about it, and then realized that it was Qi Fu Ren; he wept bitterly and fell ill, remaining bedridden for over a year. He sent someone to plead with the empress dowager saying: "This is not something a human should do." "I am your son, and I will never be able to govern the empire." Because of this, Emperor Xiaohui drank daily for pleasure and did not attend to government affairs, hence his illness.

## 5

In the second year (193 BC), King Yuwang of Chu and King Daohui of Qi both came to court. In the tenth month, Emperor Xiaohui dined with King of Qi in the presence of the empress dowager; considering King of Qi as his elder brother, he seated him above himself, following the etiquette of a family gathering. The empress dowager became angry and ordered two cups of poisoned wine to be placed before them, instructing the King of Qi to rise and offer a toast. The King of Qi rose; Emperor Xiaohui also stood up, took a cup, and wished to drink together in offering the toast. Fearing this, the empress dowager herself got up and poured Emperor Xiaohui's wine. The King of Qi was suspicious; thus he dared not drink but pretended to be drunk and left. Upon inquiry, he learned that the wine had been poisoned; fearing for his life, the King of Qi thought he could not escape Chang'an and became anxious. Shi, an official from Qi's Neishi, advised the king: "The empress dowager has only Emperor Xiaohui and Princess Luyuan. Now you have more than seventy cities, while the princess receives food from just a few cities. "If your Majesty sincerely offers one commandery to the empress dowager as a place for Princess Luyuan's bath and meals, she will surely be pleased, and you will have no worries." Thus the King of Qi offered the Junyang commandery, honoring the princess as empress dowager. Lü Hou was pleased and agreed to it. A banquet was then held at the Qi residence, where they drank joyfully; after finishing, the King of Qi returned home. In the third year, construction of Chang'an city began; by the fourth year it was half completed, and in the fifth and sixth years the city walls were finished. The feudal lords came to attend the gathering. In October, they offered congratulations at court.

## 6

On the Wuyin day of the eighth month, seventh year (August 15, 189 BC), Emperor Xiaohui died. When mourning was announced, the empress dowager wept but no tears fell. Zhang Bixiang, the son of Liu Hou and a Shizhong, who was fifteen years old, said to the prime minister: "The empress dowager had only Emperor Xiaohui; now he has died, yet she weeps without sorrow. Do you know why?" The prime minister asked, "What is the reason?" Bixiang said: "The emperor had no strong sons; the empress dowager fears you. Now you should request to appoint Lü Tai, Lü Chan, and Lü Lu as generals, commanding the northern and southern armies. All of the Lü clan should enter the palace and hold positions in central government affairs; if this is done, then the empress dowager will feel at ease, and you may be fortunate enough to escape disaster." The prime minister followed Bixiang's plan. The empress dowager was pleased, and her weeping became sorrowful. From this point on, the power of the Lü family began to rise. A general pardon was then granted throughout the empire. On the Xichou day of the ninth month (October 13, 189 BC) he was buried. The crown prince ascended to the throne as emperor and paid homage at Gao Temple. In the first year, all orders came from the empress dowager.

## 7

The empress dowager assumed authority and decreed, discussing her intention to enfeoff members of the Lü clan as kings; she consulted the Right Chancellor Wang Ling. Wang Ling said: "Gao Di made a white horse oath, stating 'If anyone other than the Liu clan is enfeoffed as king, all under heaven shall rise against him.'" "To now enfeoff the Lü clan violates this agreement." The empress dowager was displeased. She then consulted the Left Chancellor Chen Ping and Marquis of Jiang Hou Zhou Bo. Bo and others replied: "Gao Di secured the empire, enfeoffing his kinsmen; now that the empress dowager holds authority, it is entirely appropriate to enfeoff her relatives of the Lü clan." The empress dowager was delighted and dismissed court. Wang Ling rebuked Chen Ping and Jiang Hou, saying: "At the beginning, when we made a blood oath with Gao Di, were you not present?" "Now that Gao Di has died and the empress dowager, a woman, holds power, you all follow her desires and betray our oath by wanting to enfeoff the Lü family. How can you face Gao Di in the netherworld?" Chen Ping and Jiang Hou said: "In confronting her directly and arguing in court, I am not as good as you; But when it comes to preserving the state and securing the Liu family's succession, you are also no match for us." Wang Ling had nothing to reply. In the eleventh month, the empress dowager wished to remove Wang Ling; she appointed him as Taifu of the emperor and stripped him of his prime ministerial authority. Wang Ling then fell ill and was dismissed from office, returning home. She then appointed the Left Chancellor Ping as Right Chancellor and made Shen Shiji of Piyang Hou the new Left Chancellor. The Left Chancellor did not handle official matters, but was ordered to oversee within the palace, functioning like a Langzhong Ling. Shiji had long been favored by the empress dowager and often held authority; all public officials and ministers decided matters through him. They then posthumously honored the father of Marquis Li as Douwu Wang, intending to gradually enfeoff members of the L ü clan as kings.

## 8

In April, the empress dowager intended to enfeoff members of her Lü clan; she first granted the title of Bocheng Hou to Wu Ze, a Langzhong Ling and one of Gaozu's meritorious ministers. Princess Luyuan died; she was posthumously honored with the title Empress Dowager Luyuan. Her son Yan became King of Lu. The father of the King of Lu was Zhang Ao, Marquis of Xuanping. King Daohui's son Zhang of Qi was enfeoffed as Zhu Xu Hou; the daughter of Lü Lu was given to him in marriage. Qi's prime minister Shou was made Pingding Hou. Yan of the Shaofu became Wu Hou. They then enfeoffed Lü Zhong as Pei Hou, Lü Ping as Fuliu Hou, and Zhang Mai as Nangong Hou.

## 9

The empress dowager wished to enfeoff the L ü clan; first, she made Qiang, a son of Emperor Xiaohui from his concubines, King of Huaiyang; Bu Yi as King of Changshan; Shan as Xiangcheng Hou; Chao as Zhi Hou; and Wu as Huguan Hou. The empress dowager hinted to the ministers, who then requested that Marquis Li Lü Tai be enfeoffed as King of L ü; the empress dowager agreed. Marquis Kang Hou Jiancheng, Shi Zhi, died; his heir was found guilty of a crime and deposed. His younger brother Lü Lu was then enfeoffed as Huling Hou to continue the lineage of Marquis Kang Hou. In the second year, King Changshan died; his younger brother Shan, Marquis Xiangcheng, became the new King of Changshan and was renamed Yi. In November, King Lü Tai died; he was posthumously honored as 肃 Wang, and the crown prince Jia succeeded him as king. In the third year (187 BC), there were no significant events. In the fourth year, Lü Xu was enfeoffed as Linguang Hou, Lü Ta as Yu Hou, Lü Gengshi as Zuiqi Hou, and Lü Fen as Lü Cheng Hou; five chancellors of feudal lords were also enfeoffed.

## 10

When the daughter of Marquis Xuanping became Empress of Emperor Xiaohui, she had no son; pretending to be pregnant, she took a concubine's child and named it as her own, killing the mother, and established this child as crown prince. After Emperor Xiaohui died, the crown prince was enthroned as emperor. When the emperor grew up, he heard that his mother had been killed and that he was not a true son of the empress. He then remarked: "How could the Empress kill my mother and name me?" "I am not yet strong, but when I become so, I will make a change." The empress dowager heard of this and became worried that he might cause trouble, so she confined him in Yongjuan Palace, announcing that the emperor was seriously ill and forbidding anyone from visiting. The empress dowager said: "Whoever rules the world and governs for the lives of all the people should cover them as the sky covers, accommodate them as the earth supports. With joy in his heart to pacify the common people, the people will be pleased to serve their ruler; mutual joy and harmony bring about good governance of the world. "Now, for a long time the emperor has not recovered from his illness; he is confused and disoriented. He cannot continue to succeed in upholding ancestral rites and sacrifices at the temple of our ancestors. Therefore, it would be inappropriate to entrust him with ruling the world; we must replace him." All the ministers kowtowed and said: "The Empress Dowager has deeply considered the well-being of all the people under heaven, ensuring the stability of the ancestral temple and state. We ministers prostrate ourselves in obedience to your decree." The emperor was deposed from his position, and the empress dowager had him confined and killed. On the Bingchen day of the fifth month (June 15, 184 BC), King Yi of Changshan was enthroned as emperor and renamed Hong. The reason a first year was not proclaimed is because the empress dowager controlled all affairs of the realm. Zhi Hou Chao was enfeoffed as King of Changshan. The office of Taiwei (Grand General) was established, and Marquis Jiao Bo became the Taiwei. In August of the fifth year, King Huaiyang died; his younger brother Wu, Marquis Huguan, was enfeoffed as the new King of Huaiyang. In October of the sixth year, the empress dowager said that King Lü Jia was arrogant and indulgent in his conduct; he was deposed. Tai, younger brother of 肃 Wang Lü Tai, was enfeoffed as new King of Lü. In summer, a general pardon was granted throughout the empire. Qi Daohui's son Xingju was enfeoffed as Dongmu Hou.

## 11

In the first month of the seventh year, the empress dowager summoned King You of Zhao. You had married a daughter of the Lü clan as his queen, but he did not love her; instead, he loved another concubine. The wife from the Lü family became jealous and angry, then slandered him to the empress dowager, falsely accusing him of crimes and saying: "How can the Lü clan possibly hold a kingship! "After Empress Dowager passes away in her hundredth year, I will surely attack them." The empress dowager was furious and thus summoned King Zhao. When King Zhao arrived, he was lodged in a residence but not received; guards were ordered to surround him and prevent contact with food. Some of his ministers secretly sent him food, but they were caught and punished. As King Zhao grew hungry, he sang: "The Lü clan holds power while the Liu dynasty is in peril; lords are forced under pressure to give me their daughters as my wife." "My wife, jealous and malicious, falsely accuses me of wrongdoing; this scheming woman has disrupted the state while the ruler remains unaware." "I have no loyal ministers—why then am I abandoned by my country?" "I shall end my life in the wilderness, and may Heaven uphold justice!" "Alas! It is too late to regret; better to die early than live on." "To be a king yet starve to death—who will pity me!" "The Lü clan has gone against reason; I entrust Heaven with my vengeance." On the Dingchou day (July 13, 180 BC), King Zhao was confined and died; he was buried with commoner rites among the tombs of ordinary people in Chang'an.

## 12

On the Jichou day (July 25, 180 BC) there was a solar eclipse; daylight turned dark. The empress dowager disliked this and felt unhappy, so she said to those around her: "This omen is about me."

## 13

In February, King Huai of Liang was transferred as the new King of Zhao. lineno King Lü Chan was moved to become King of Liang; however, King Liang did not go to his fief but instead served as the emperor's Taifu (Grand Tutor). The imperial son Tai, Marquis Pingchang, was enfeoffed as king of Lü. The fief of Liang was renamed Lü, and the former Lü was renamed Jichuan. The empress dowager's younger sister, Lü Xu, had a daughter who married Liu Ze, Marquis Yingling; Liu Ze became the Grand General. The empress dowager enfeoffed members of the Lü clan, and fearing that after her death General Liu might become a threat, she made Liu Ze King of Langya to pacify his mind.

## 14

When King Huai of Liang moved to be enfeoffed as the King of Zhao, he felt unhappy in his heart. The empress dowager married a daughter of Lü Chan to Queen Dowager of Zhao. All the officials accompanying the queen were from the Lü clan; they seized power and secretly watched over King Zhao, who could not act freely. The king had a concubine he loved dearly, but the queen had her poisoned to death. King Zhao then composed four chapters of poetry and ordered musicians to sing them. The king was deeply sorrowful, and in June he committed suicide. When the empress dowager heard about this, she believed that King Zhao had been led astray by women and abandoned ancestral rites; thus, she deposed his heir.

## 15

Marquis Xuanping Zhang Ao died; his son Yan was enfeoffed as Duke of Lu, and Ao was posthumously honored with the title Marquis Luyuan.

## 16

King Dai declined and expressed a wish to remain in charge of guarding the borders of Dai.

## 17

Taifu Chan, Chancellor Ping and others said: Wu Xin Hou Lü Lu is the highest-ranking marquis, holding the first position; they requested that he be enfeoffed as King Zhao. The empress dowager approved this and posthumously honored Lu's father, Kang Hou, as King Zhaozhu of Zhao. In September, King Ling of Yan died; he had a son by one of his concubines. The empress dowager ordered the child to be killed. With no heir left, the fief was abolished. In October of the eighth year, Dongping Hou, son of 肃 Wang Lü Tai named Lü Tong, was enfeoffed as Yan Wang. Tong's younger brother, Lü Zhuang, was made Dongping Hou.

## 18

In the middle of March, Empress Dowager Lü performed a purification ritual. On her return journey passing through Zhi Dao, she saw an object resembling a dark dog that clung to the sleeve of the empress dowager. Suddenly, it vanished and was never seen again. When divined about this event, it was said that the spirit of King Zhao Ru Yi was causing trouble. Empress Dowager Gao then fell ill with an injury to her sleeve area.

## 19

Empress Dowager Gao, seeing that her young grandson, Marquis Luyuan Yan, had lost his parents at an early age and was now alone and vulnerable, enfeoffed two sons of Zhang Ao's former concubine: Chi as Xindu Hou and Shou as Lechang Hou, to assist and support Marquis Luyuan Yan. She also enfeoffed Zhang Shi, a zhongdaiyezhe (middle-ranking palace official), as Jianling Hou and Lü Rong as Zhuci Hou. All the chancellors and assistants of the zhongguan (palace eunuchs) were enfeoffed as Guannei Hou, each receiving a fief of 500 households.

## 20

In the middle of July, Empress Dowager Gao's illness worsened; she then ordered King Lu Lü Lu to serve as Supreme General and command the Northern Army. King Lü Chan would take charge of the Southern Army. Empress Dowager Lü warned Chan and Lu: "Emperor Gaozu had already pacified the world, and made an agreement with his ministers that 'anyone who becomes a king not of the Liu clan will be attacked by all under heaven.'" "Now members of my Lü family are kings, but the ministers do not accept this." "If I die, the emperor is still young and the ministers may cause trouble." "You must hold onto your troops to guard the palace. Be careful not to attend my funeral, lest you fall into others' control." On the Xinsi day (August 16, 180 BC), Empress Dowager Gao died. Her will decreed that each feudal king be granted a thousand gold pieces, and generals, chancellors, marquises, and officials were to receive gold according to their ranks. A general pardon was issued throughout the empire. King Lü Chan was appointed as prime minister, and his daughter of Lu Lu became empress.

## 21

After Empress Dowager Gao had been buried, Zuo Chengxiang Shen Shi Ji was appointed as the emperor's Taifu.

## 22

Zhu Xu Hou Liu Zhang was strong and powerful; Dongmu Hou Xingju was his younger brother. Both were the younger brothers of King Ai of Qi, residing in Chang'an. At that time, the Lü clan held power and plotted to cause trouble; however, they feared the former ministers of Emperor Gaozu such as Jiao and Guan, so they dared not act. Zhu Xu Hou's wife was a daughter of Lü Lu; she secretly learned about their plot. Afraid that they would be executed, she secretly sent someone to inform her brother, King Qi, urging him to raise an army and march westward to eliminate the Lü clan and take power. Zhu Xu Hou wished to cooperate with the ministers from within the palace as a response. King Qi wanted to raise troops, but his chancellor refused to allow it. On the Bingwu day of August (August 29, 180 BC), Prince Qi wanted to send someone to kill his chancellor. Chancellor Zhao Ping rebelled instead and raised troops to surround the prince. The prince then killed his chancellor and launched an army eastward, falsely seizing Langya Wang's forces and leading them westward. This account is recorded in the biography of King Qi.

## 23

King Qi sent letters to the feudal kings, saying: "Emperor Gaozu pacified the world and enfeoffed his sons and brothers as kings. King Daohui was made King of Qi. "After King Daohui died, Emperor Xiaohui sent Liu Hou Liang to enfeoff me as the new King of Qi." "After Emperor Xiaohui's death, Empress Dowager Lü took control. Now in her old age, she listened to the Lü clan who seized power, deposed the emperor and replaced him with another. They also had three kings of Zhao killed one after another, destroyed Liang, Zhao, and Yan, enfeoffing members of their own family as kings instead, and divided Qi into four parts." Loyal ministers advised against this, but the emperor was confused and refused to listen. "Now that Empress Dowager L ü has died, though the emperor is young and vigorous, he cannot yet govern the empire; thus, he must rely on ministers and feudal lords." Yet the L ü clan has again arbitrarily elevated their ranks, gathered troops to display authority, and coerced marquises and loyal ministers. They have issued false imperial decrees to command the empire, thus endangering the ancestral temple. "I shall lead my troops in to eliminate those who should not be kings." The Han court heard of this, and Chancellor Lü Chan and others sent Yingyin Hou Guan Ying to lead troops against them. When Guan Ying arrived at Xingyang, he plotted and said: "The L ü clan holds military power in Guanzhong and intends to endanger the Liu family while establishing themselves as rulers. "If I defeat Qi now and return with a report, this will only strengthen the L ü clan's resources." He therefore remained stationed in Xingyang and sent envoys to King Qi and the feudal lords, urging them to form an alliance and wait for a change in the L ü clan's situation so they could jointly eliminate them. King Qi heard of this and withdrew his troops back west to await an agreement.

## 24

Lü Lu and Lü Chan wanted to start a rebellion in Guanzhong, but inwardly they feared Jiao Hou and Zhu Xu. Outwardly, they were afraid of the armies from Qi and Chu, and also worried that Guan Ying might betray them. They wished to wait until Guan Ying's forces joined with those of Qi before acting, yet remained indecisive. At this time, King Jichuan Tai, King Huaiyang Wu, and King Changshan Chao were nominally younger brothers of the young emperor; as well as Marquis Luyuan, the granddaughter of Empress Dowager Lü. All were still young and had not yet taken up residence in their fiefs, remaining in Chang'an. Prince Zhao Lu and Prince Liang Chan each commanded troops stationed at the northern and southern armies, respectively; they were all members of the L ü clan. The marquises and ministers had no confidence in their own safety.

## 25

Grand Marshal Jiao Hou Bo could not enter the army to command troops. Marquis Quzhou Li Shang was old and ill, and his son Ji had a good relationship with Lü Lu. Jiao Hou therefore conspired with Chancellor Chen Ping to send someone to kidnap Li Shang. They ordered his son Ji to go and deceive Lü Lu, saying: "Emperor Gaozu and Empress Dowager Lü jointly established the empire. The nine kings of the Liu clan and the three kings of the L ü clan were all decided upon by the ministers; these matters have already been announced to the feudal lords, who all believe they are appropriate. "Now that the empress dowager has died, the emperor is young, and you are wearing the seal of King Zhao. You do not hasten to your fief to defend its borders, yet remain as a general in command of troops here, which causes suspicion among the ministers and feudal lords." "Why don't you return your seal and hand over your troops to the Grand Marshal?" "Please have Prince Liang return the seal of Chancellor and make an alliance with the ministers before going to your fief. The army from Qi will surely withdraw, allowing the ministers peace of mind. You can then rest easy as a king ruling over thousands of li; this is in the interest for generations to come." Lü Lu believed and accepted their plan, intending to return his general's seal and hand over his troops to the Grand Marshal. He sent someone to inform Lü Chan and other senior members of the L ü family, some of whom thought it advantageous while others did not; their plan remained indecisive without a final decision. Lü Lu trusted Li Ji and at the time was going out with him for hunting excursions. He passed by his aunt, Lü Xu, who became very angry and said: "You are a general abandoning your troops; the L ü clan will have no place to go now." She then took out all her jewels, treasures, and valuables and scattered them in the hall, saying: "Do not let these be kept by others."

## 26

The Left Chancellor Shi Qi was dismissed.

## 27

On the Gengshen day of the eighth month, Pingyang Hou Zhu, acting as Grand Censor, met with Chancellor Chan to discuss affairs. Langzhongling Jia Shou, who had come from Qi, rebuked Chan repeatedly, saying: "You did not go to your fief early enough; now that you wish to leave, is it still possible?" He informed Chan in detail about Guan Ying's alliance with Qi and Chu, who intended to eliminate the L ü clan, and urged Chan to hurry into the palace. Pingyang Hou had overheard these words and hurriedly reported them to the Chancellor and Grand Marshal. The Grand Marshal wanted to enter the northern army but was denied entry. Xiangping Hou Tong still held the imperial seal and credentials. He then ordered someone to hold the credentials and falsely admit the Grand Marshal into the northern army. The Grand Marshal again ordered Li Ji and Dianke Liu Jie to go first and persuade Lü Lu, saying: "The emperor has appointed the Grand Marshal to command the northern army and wishes for you to return to your fief. Quickly return your general's seal and depart; otherwise, disaster will soon arise." Lü Lu believed that Li Ji was not deceiving him and thus removed his seal, handing it over to Dianke, while transferring command of the troops to the Grand Marshal. The Grand Marshal led them into the military gate and issued an order within the army, saying: "Those who support the L ü clan should bare their right shoulder; those who support the Liu clan should bare their left." The soldiers all bared their left shoulders in support of the Liu clan. By the time the Grand Marshal arrived, General Lü Lu had already removed his general's seal and left; thus, the Grand Marshal took command of the northern army.

## 28

However, there was still the southern army. Pingyang Hou heard of this and informed Chancellor Chen Ping about Lü Chan's plot; Chancellor Chen Ping then summoned Zhu Xu Hou to assist the Grand Marshal. The Grand Marshal ordered Zhu Xu Hou to oversee the military gate. He also ordered Pingyang Hou to inform the Weiwei: "Do not allow Chancellor Chan into the palace gates." Lü Chan did not know that Lü Lu had already left the northern army, so he entered Weiyang Palace intending to start a rebellion. He could not gain entry through the palace gates and paced back and forth in confusion. Pingyang Hou, fearing he would be unable to prevail, hurriedly informed the Grand Marshal. The Grand Marshal still feared he could not overcome the L ü clan and dared not openly declare their execution, so he sent Zhu Xu Hou to say: "Hurry into the palace to protect the emperor." Zhu Xu Hou requested troops, and the Grand Marshal gave him over a thousand soldiers. They entered Weiyang Palace gate and proceeded to confront Chan in the court courtyard. At dusk, they attacked Chan. Chan fled, and a strong wind suddenly arose in the sky; as a result, his attendants became disoriented and no one dared to fight. They chased after Chan and killed him inside the latrine of the Langzhongfu officials' quarters.

## 29

After Zhu Xu Hou had killed Chan, the emperor ordered a court official to hold credentials and go to commend Zhu Xu Hou. Zhu Xu Hou wanted to seize the credentials, but the court official refused. Zhu Xu Hou then forced him into a carriage and, using the credentials as authority, rushed away and beheaded Lü Gengshi, the Weiwei of Changle Palace. He returned, galloped into the northern army, and reported to the Grand Marshal. The Grand Marshal rose and bowed in congratulations to Zhu Xu Hou, saying: "Our only concern was Lü Chan; now that he has been eliminated, the empire is at peace." He then sent people to capture all male and female members of the L ü clan; without regard for age, they were all executed. On the Xinyou day, Lü Lu was captured and beheaded, while Lü Xu was beaten to death. They sent someone to execute King Yan Lu Tong and deposed King Lu Yan. On the Renxu day, Emperor Taifu Shi Qi was reinstated as Left Chancellor. On the Wuchen day, King Jichuan Wang Liang was relocated, and Zhao Youwang's son Sui was appointed as King of Zhao. They sent Zhu Xu Hou Zhang to inform King Qi about the execution of the L ü clan members and ordered him to disband his troops. Guan Ying's forces also disbanded from Xingyang and returned.

## 30

The ministers secretly plotted among themselves, saying: "The young emperor and Kings Liang, Huaiyang, and Changshan are not the true sons of Emperor Xiaohui. "Empress Dowager Lü used deceit to name the son of another, killed his mother, and raised him in the palace so that Emperor Xiaohui would believe he was his own. She then made him emperor as well as a number of kings, thereby strengthening the L ü clan." "Now that we have already eliminated all members of the L ü family and placed those they installed in power, if these individuals continue to hold authority, our own lives will be at risk." "We should instead select among the kings the one who is most virtuous and establish him as emperor." Some said, "King Dohui of Qi was the eldest son of Emperor Gaozu. Now his legitimate son is King of Qi. Tracing back to the root, he is the legitimate grandson of Emperor Gaozu and should be made emperor." The ministers all said: "The L ü clan, through their in-law family's wickedness, nearly endangered the ancestral temple and disrupted the loyal ministers. Now King Qi's mother's family is the Si (Jun) clan; Si Jun is a wicked man." "If we make King Qi emperor, it will be like supporting another L ü clan again." They wanted to appoint the Prince of Huainan but considered him too young and his mother's family also wicked. It was then said: "Prince Dai is currently the emperor's living son, the eldest one, and he is kind, filial, and broad-minded." "The empress dowager's family, the Bo clan, are cautious and virtuous people." "Furthermore, establishing the eldest is in accordance with tradition; his reputation for kindness and filial piety is known throughout the empire. This would be convenient." They then secretly conspired to send someone to summon Prince Dai. Prince Dai sent envoys to decline politely. They returned twice, and then Prince Dai traveled in a procession of six carriages. On the last day of the ninth month, on the Jiyou day, he arrived at Chang'an and stayed at Dai Di. The ministers all went to pay homage, presented the imperial seal to Prince Dai, and jointly honored him as emperor. Prince Dai repeatedly declined, but after the ministers insisted, he finally accepted.

## 31

Dongmu Hou Xingju said: "I had no part in 诛 ing the L ü clan; I request to clear out the palace." He then entered the palace with Taifu Ruyin Hou Teng Gong and said to the young emperor: "You are not of the Liu clan; you should not be on the throne." He then turned around and ordered those holding halberds at his side to lower their weapons and disperse. A few people refused to put down their weapons, but when the Director of Eunuchs Zhang Ze gave instructions, they also lowered their arms. Teng Gong then summoned an imperial carriage and took the young emperor out. The young emperor asked, "Where are you taking me?" Teng Gong said: "We will take you to a residence." They placed him in the residence of the Shaofu. They then presented an imperial procession and went to Prince Dai's residence to welcome him. A report was sent saying: "The palace has been carefully cleared." That evening, Prince Dai entered Weiyang Palace. Ten court officials holding halberds guarded the main gate, saying: "The emperor is here. Who are you and why do you enter?" Prince Dai then spoke to the Grand Marshal. The Grand Marshal went to explain, and the ten court officials all lowered their weapons and left. Prince Dai then entered and began to govern. That night, officials divided their tasks and executed King Liang, King Huaiyang, King Changshan, and the young emperor in their residences. Prince Dai was enthroned as emperor. He died in the twenty-third year of his reign and received the posthumous title Emperor Xiaowen.

## 32

Grand Historian said: During the reign of Emperor Xiaohui and Empress Dowager Gao, the common people were relieved from the hardships of warring states. Both ruler and subjects desired peace through non-action; thus, Emperor Xiaohui merely held court while Empress Dowager Gao governed as a female sovereign. Policies did not originate outside her private chambers, yet the empire remained peaceful. Punishments were rarely used and criminals became rare. The people focused on farming, and clothing and food supplies flourished.
