# Hereditary House of Chancellor Cao

## 1

Pingyang Hou Cao Can was a native of Pei. During the Qin dynasty, he served as a yuyuan in Pei's prison, while Xiao He was the chief official; they were both prominent officials in the county.

## 2

When Gaozu first rose as the Lord of Pei, Cao Can followed him as Zhong Juan. He led troops to attack Hulu and Fangyu, attacked Qin Jian Gong's army, and utterly defeated them. They marched eastward to Xue, and fought against the governor of Si Shui's forces at the western part of Xue Guo. He attacked Hulu again and captured it. He was transferred to serve as the defender of Fangyu. When Fangyu rebelled and submitted to Wei, he attacked them. Feng rebelled in support of Wei, so he launched an attack on it. He was granted the title of Qi Da Fu. He attacked Qin Si Ma Yi's army east of Dang, defeated them, captured Dang, Hufu, and Qishan Zhi. He then attacked the area west of Xiayi up to Yu, where he engaged Zhang Han's chariot and cavalry forces. He attacked Yuanqi and Kangfu, leading the first assault. He was promoted to Wu Da Fu. Moving north to relieve the siege of A, he fought against Zhang Han's army, broke through their lines, and pursued them as far as Puyang. He attacked Dingtao and captured Linji. He marched south to relieve Yongqiu. He attacked Li You's army, defeated it, killed Li You, and captured one Qin marquis. When the Qin general Zhang Han defeated and killed Xiang Liang, Lord Pei and Xiang Yu withdrew eastward. King Huai of Chu appointed Lord Pei as the magisterial official of Dang Jun, commanding its troops. At this time, Cao Can was enfeoffed with Zhice and given the title Jiancheng Jun. He was later promoted to Qi Gong, under the jurisdiction of Dang Jun.

## 3

Later, he followed in attacking the forces of the Magistrate of Dong Jun and defeated them south of Chengwu. He attacked Wang Li's army at Nanchengyang, then again attacked them at Gangli, where he utterly defeated them. He pursued the retreating enemy forces westward to Kaifeng, attacked Zhao Bin's army, defeated them, and laid siege to Zhao Bin within the city of Kaifeng. Moving westward, he attacked Yang Xiong's forces at Quyu, defeated them, and captured one Qin Si Ma and one Yu Shi. He was promoted to Zhigui. Accompanying the attack on Yangwu, he captured Xuanyuan and Goushi, cut off the river crossings, then returned to strike Zhao Bin's forces north of Shi, defeating them. Accompanying the southern attack on Chou, he fought against Nanyang's governor Yi at the east of Yangcheng Guo, broke through enemy lines, captured Wan, captured Yi, and completely pacified Nanyang Jun. Accompanying forces westward to attack Wuguan and Yaoguan, he captured both passes. Previously attacking the Qin army at the south of Lantian, then striking again at night from the north, he utterly defeated the Qin forces. Thus they arrived at Xianyang and destroyed the Qin dynasty.

## 4

When Xiang Yu arrived, Lord Pei was appointed as King of Han. King of Han enfeoffed Cao Can as Hou of Jiancheng. Accompanying him to Hanzhong, he was promoted to general. He accompanied the return campaign to pacify the Three Qins, initially attacking and capturing Bian, Gudao, Yong, and Li. He attacked Zhang Ping's forces at the south of Haozhi, defeated them, laid siege to Haozhi, and captured Rangxiang. He fought against the Three Qin armies east of Rang and Gaoli, defeating them. He again laid siege to Zhang Ping, who fled from Haozhi. ⭐ ⭐ He then attacked Zhao Bin and Neishi Bao's forces, defeating them. Moving eastward, he captured Xianyang, which was renamed to Xincheng. Cao Can commanded troops defending Jingling for twenty days, when the Three Qin forces sent Zhang Ping and others to attack him. Cao Can led an offensive and utterly defeated them. He was granted a fief in Ningqin with land revenue. Cao Can, as general, led troops to lay siege to Zhang Han at Feiqiu. As Zhong Wei, he accompanied King of Han out through Linjin Guan. They reached Hedong, captured Xiuwu, crossed Weijin, and moved east to attack Long Ju and Xiang Ta at Dingtao, defeating them. Moving eastward, they captured Dang, Xiao, and Pengcheng. They attacked Xiang Ji's army, but the Han forces were heavily defeated and fled. Cao Can, in his capacity as Zhong Wei, laid siege to and captured Yongqiu. Wang Wu rebelled at Huang, while Cheng Chu rebelled in Yan; Cao Can went to attack them and completely defeated both. Zhu Tian Hou rebelled at Yanshi, so he advanced to defeat and capture it. He attacked Yu Ying at Kunyang and pursued him as far as Ye. Returning, he attacked Wuqiang and proceeded to Xingyang. From the time Cao Can served as general and Zhong Wei from Hanzhong, accompanying the campaign against various feudal lords until Xiang Yu's defeat, returning to Xingyang, it had been a total of two years.

## 5

In the second year of Gaozu's reign (3 AD), he was appointed as Acting Left Chancellor and stationed troops in Guanzhong. After more than a month, King Wei Pei rebelled; as Acting Left Chancellor, Cao Can separately led Han Xin eastward to attack Sun Su's forces at Dongzhang, where they were greatly defeated. He then attacked Anyi and captured Wei general Wang Xiang. He fought King Pei at Quyang, pursued him to Wuyuan, and captured King Pei alive. ⭐ ⭐ They captured Pingyang, took King Pei's mother, wife, and children, and completely pacified the territory of Wei, a total of fifty-two cities. He was granted land revenue in Pingyang. Accompanying Han Xin, he attacked the forces of Xia Yue, Chancellor of Zhao, at the east of Wu, utterly defeating them and beheading Xia Yue. Han Xin, together with the former King of Changshan Zhang Er, led troops down Jingxing to attack Chengan Jun, while ordering Cao Can to return and lay siege to Qi General, a separate Zhao commander, within Wucheng. Qi General fled from the city; he was pursued and beheaded. He then led his troops to Ao Cang, where King of Han was stationed. After Han Xin had defeated Zhao and been appointed Chancellor, he moved eastward to attack Qi. Cao Can served under Han Xin as Right Chancellor, attacking and defeating the forces at Lixia in Qi, thus capturing Linzi. He then returned to pacify Jibei Jun, attacking Zhuo, Tayin, Pingyuan, Ge, and Lu. Later, he accompanied Han Xin to attack Long Ju's forces at Shangjiami, where they utterly defeated them, beheaded Long Ju, and captured his general Zhou Lan. They pacified Qi, capturing more than seventy counties in total. He captured Tian Guang's former chancellor Tian Guang, the defending chancellor Xu Zhang, and the former Jiaodong general of Qi, Tian Ji. When Han Xin was made King of Qi, he led his troops to Chen and joined with the King of Han to defeat Xiang Yu, while Cao Can remained behind to pacify any remaining areas in Qi that had not yet submitted.

## 6

After Xiang Ji's death, the empire was pacified; King of Han became emperor, and Han Xin was transferred to be King of Chu, while Qi became a jun. Cao Can returned the seal of the Han chancellorship. Emperor Gao Di appointed his eldest son, Yi, as King of Qi and Cao Can as Chancellor of Qi. In the sixth year of Gaozu's reign, he was granted a marquis title and received a fief charter from the emperor, ensuring that his descendants would inherit it without interruption. He was granted 16,300 households in Pingyang as his fief, with the title "Pingyang Hou," replacing all previous land revenues.

## 7

As Chancellor of Qi, he attacked Zhang Chun's forces under Chen Xi and defeated them. When Peng Lu rebelled, Cao Can, as Chancellor of Qi, accompanied Douhuai Wang with an army of 120,000 chariots and cavalry to join Gaozu in attacking Peng Lu's forces, where they were heavily defeated. They marched south as far as Qi, then returned to pacify Zhuyi, Xiang, Xiao, and Liu.

## 8

Cao Can's achievements: He captured two kingdoms and 122 counties; He captured two kings, three chancellors, six generals, one Da Mo Ao, one jun governor, one Si Ma, one Hou, and one Yu Shi.

## 9

In the first year of Emperor Xiaohui's reign, the law regarding feudal lords' chancellors was abolished, and Cao Can was instead appointed as Chancellor of Qi. When Cao Can served as chancellor in Qi, it governed seventy cities. When the empire had just been pacified, Douhuai Wang was still young. Cao Can summoned all the elders and scholars to ask how best to settle and stabilize the people. He consulted over a hundred Confucian scholars from Qi's old customs; each gave different advice, and Cao Can did not know which to adopt. He heard that Gai Gong from Jiaoxi was skilled in the Huanglao school of thought, so he sent generous gifts to invite him. After meeting Gai Gong, Gai Gong explained that the key to governing was tranquility and clarity, allowing the people to settle naturally. He elaborated on this principle in detail. Cao Can then moved out of the main hall and lodged Gai Gong there. He governed primarily using Huanglao philosophy, so during his nine years as chancellor of Qi, the state remained peaceful and stable, earning him great acclaim as a virtuous chancellor.

## 10

In the second year of Emperor Xiaohui's reign, Xiao He died. Upon hearing this news, Cao Can informed his attendants to prepare for travel, saying, "I am about to go in as chancellor." Not long after, envoys indeed summoned Cao Can. Before leaving, he instructed his successor as chancellor: "Treat the administration of justice and markets in Qi with care; be cautious not to disturb them." The new chancellor asked, "Is there no greater task than this?" Cao Can replied, "Not quite. The courts and markets are where people of all kinds gather; if you now disturb them, where will the dishonest find refuge? That is why I placed this as my priority."

## 11

In his early days of obscurity, Cao Can had been on good terms with Xiao He; But when they both became generals and chancellors, a rift developed between them. Yet as Xiao He neared death, the only person he recommended as his successor was Cao Can. When Cao Can succeeded Xiao He as chancellor of Han, he made no changes to policies and strictly followed the regulations established by Xiao He.

## 12

He selected officials from the commanderies and states who were not skilled in literary expression but were upright, substantial, and elderly men of integrity; he then summoned them to serve as Chancellor's clerks. Officials who used elaborate or severe language and sought fame, were always dismissed by him. He drank pure wine day and night. Ministers, officials of lower ranks, and guests who saw Cao Can doing nothing would all come to him with something to say. When they came, Cao Can would serve them pure wine and keep them waiting. If they wanted to say something, he would give them more to drink until they were drunk and then let them go; no one ever got the chance to speak with him about anything, and this became a regular practice.

## 13

The chancellor's residence had an inner garden adjacent to the officials' quarters; every day from there came drinking songs and loud laughter. The attendants of the chancellor found this annoying but did not know what to do, so they invited Cao Can for a stroll in the garden. When he heard the officials singing and shouting while drunk, his attendants hoped that Chancellor would summon them for an inquiry. Instead, Cao Can fetched wine, arranged a seat, and began to drink as well, singing and laughing along with them in harmony.

## 14

Whenever Cao Can saw minor faults committed by others, he would deliberately conceal and cover them up; thus, there were no incidents in the chancellor's office.

## 15

Cao Can's son, Cao Zhu, was an official of the rank Zhongdafa. Emperor Hui was puzzled that the chancellor did nothing and wondered, "Could it be he thinks I am not capable enough?" He then told Cao Zhu, "When you return home, try to casually ask your father: 'High Emperor has recently passed away and left the court. The emperor is still young and inexperienced. As chancellor, you drink every day without attending to any matters of state—how can this be good for worrying about the affairs of the world?'" "But do not mention that I told you this." After returning home from a bath and hair-washing, Cao Zhu waited on his father and privately advised him as instructed. Cao Can became angry and beat Cao Zhu with a stick two hundred times, saying, "Go back to serve the emperor. Matters of state are not for you to discuss." When Cao Can went to court, Emperor Hui reprimanded him and said, "What kind of father-son relationship do you have with your son Cao Zhu? Earlier I sent him to advise you." Cao Can removed his hat and apologized, saying, "May the emperor himself judge—between His Majesty's wisdom and martial virtue, which is greater than that of High Emperor?" The emperor replied, "How could I dare to compare myself with the late emperor!" Cao Can asked, "Does Your Majesty think I am as capable and virtuous as Xiao He?" The emperor said, "You seem to be less capable than he was." Cao Can replied, "Your Majesty is right. High Emperor and Xiao He established the empire, and their laws and decrees are already clear. Now Your Majesty merely needs to sit in state with ease, while I and others hold our posts and follow these regulations without deviation—would that not be sufficient?" Emperor Hui said, "Well spoken. You may rest now!"

## 16

Cao Can served as chancellor of Han for three years. He died and was posthumously honored with the title Yi Hou. His son Cao Zhu succeeded him as marquis. The people sang of them: "Xiao He established laws, clear and precise like a straight line; Cao Can followed in his footsteps, preserving without deviation. He upheld tranquility and clarity; the people found peace and unity."

## 17

Marquis Pingyang Cao Zhu served as Grand Inquisitor during the reign of Empress Dowager Gao. When Emperor Xiaowen ascended the throne, he was relieved of his title as marquis. After reigning for twenty-nine years, he died and was posthumously given the title Jing Hou. His son Cao Qi succeeded as marquis; after seven years in office, he died and received the posthumous title Jian Hou. His son Cao Shi inherited the title of marquis. At that time, he married Princess Pingyang; they had a son named Cao Xiang. Cao Shi fell ill with leprosy and returned to his fiefdom. After reigning for twenty-three years, he died and received the title Yi Hou posthumously. His son Cao Xiang succeeded as marquis. Cao Xiang married Princess Wei Chang; they had a son, Cao Zong. After reigning for sixteen years, he died and posthumously received the title Gong Hou. His son Cao Zong succeeded as marquis. In the middle of the second year of Zhenghe, Cao Zong was implicated in the case of the crown prince and executed; his fiefdom was abolished.

## 18

Grand Historian said: Chancellor Cao Can's numerous achievements in capturing cities and fighting battles were due to his partnership with Marquis Huaiyin. After Han Xin was eliminated, among the marquises who had achieved success in their campaigns, only Cao Can retained his reputation. As chancellor of Han, Cao Can governed with tranquility and restraint, fully aligning with the Daoist principle of non-action. Yet after the people had suffered under Qin's harsh rule, Cao Can allowed them to rest and practice non-interference; thus, all under heaven praised his virtue.
