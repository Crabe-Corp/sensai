# Chen Qi Shijia

## 1

Chen Hugong Man was a descendant of Emperor Shun of Yu. In the past, when Shun was an ordinary person, Yao married him to his two daughters. They lived in Gui Rui, and their descendants later took this as their clan name, adopting the surname Gui. After Shun died, Yu inherited the empire from him, and Shun's son Shangjun was enfeoffed as a feudal lord. During the time of the Xia Hou dynasty, their lineage was sometimes lost and at other times continued. When King Wu of Zhou defeated the Yin 纣, he sought out descendants of Shun and found Gui Man. He enfeoffed him in Chen to uphold the ancestral rites for Emperor Shun; this was Hugong.

## 2

After Hugong died, his son Shen Gong Xi Huo succeeded as ruler. After Shen Gong died, his younger brother Xianggong Gao Yang succeeded as ruler. After Xianggong died, the son of Shen Gong, Tu, was installed as ruler; this was Xiaogong. After Xiaogong died, his son Shengong Yu Rong succeeded as ruler. Shengong was in power during the reign of King Li of Zhou. After Shengong died, his son Yougong Ning succeeded as ruler.

## 3

In the twelfth year of Yougong's reign, King Li of Zhou fled to Zhi.

## 4

In the twenty-third year, Yougong died and his son Ligong Xiao succeeded as ruler. In the sixth year of Ligong's reign, King Xuan of Zhou ascended the throne. In the thirty-sixth year, Ligong died and his son Wugong Ling succeeded as ruler. Wugong died in the fifteenth year of his reign; his son Yigong Yue succeeded as ruler. In this year, King You of Zhou ascended the throne. Yigong died in his third year, and his younger brother Pinggong Xie succeeded as ruler. In the seventh year of Pinggong's reign, King You of Zhou was killed by the Quanrong, and the Zhou dynasty moved its capital eastward. Qi began to be listed as a vassal state.

## 5

In the twenty-third year, Pinggong died and his son Wengong Yu succeeded as ruler.

## 6

In the first year of Wengong's reign, he took a woman from Cai as wife, and they had a son named Tuo. In the tenth year, Wengong died and his eldest son Huangong Bao succeeded as ruler.

## 7

In the twenty-third year of Huangong's reign, Duke Yin of Lu first ascended to power. In the twenty-sixth year, Wei killed its ruler Zhouyu. In the thirty-third year, Lu assassinated its ruler Duke Yin.

## 8

On the Jia Xu to Ji Chou days of the first month in the thirty-eighth year, Huangong Bao died. Huangong's younger brother Tuo, whose mother was a woman from Cai, was supported by the people of Cai who killed Wu Fu and Huangong's crown prince Mian in order to install Tuo as ruler; this was Lai Gong. During Huangong's illness, chaos arose and the people scattered, so they had to return twice.

## 9

In the second year of Lai Gong's reign, his son Jingzhong Wan was born. The Grand Historian of the Zhou dynasty passed through Chen, and Duke Lai of Chen had him divinate using the Yijing. The hexagram obtained was Guan turning into Pi: "This is 'Observing the Light of a Nation,' which is auspicious for serving as an official in the king's court." Will this mean that he will succeed Chen and possess its state? Not here, but in a foreign land? It will not be him himself, but his descendants. If it is in a foreign land, it must be the Jiang surname. The Jiang surname is descended from Taiyue. Nothing can have two great powers; as Chen declines, will this one flourish?"

## 10

Lai Gong took a woman from Cai. The woman from Cai and the people of Cai conspired together, and Lai Gong frequently went to Cai for immoral conduct. In the seventh year, Lai Gong was killed by three younger brothers of Huangong's crown prince Mian: the eldest named Yue, the middle named Lin, and the youngest named Chujiu. They conspired with the people of Cai to lure Lai Gong with beautiful women and then, together with the Cai people, killed him and installed Yue as ruler; this was Ligong. Ligong was a son of Huangong. Five months after Ligong ascended the throne, he died and his middle brother Lin succeeded as ruler; this was Zhuanggong. Zhuanggong died in the seventh year of his reign, and his younger brother Chujiu succeeded as ruler; this was Xuangong.

## 11

In the third year of Xuangong's reign, King Wu of Chu died, and from then on, Chu began to grow strong. In the seventeenth year, King Hui of Zhou married a woman from Chen as his empress.

## 12

In the twenty-first year, Xuangong's concubine who was favored gave birth to a son named Kuan. He wanted to make him heir and thus killed his crown prince Yukou. Yukou had always loved Lai Gong's son Wan, who feared that the disaster would reach him and so fled to Qi. Duke Huang of Qi wanted to appoint Chen Wan as an official, but Wan said: "A wandering subject is fortunate enough to be spared from carrying burdens on his back; this is the grace of your lordship. I dare not accept a high position." Duke Huang appointed him as head of the artisans. Yizhong of Qi wished to marry his daughter to Jingzhong Chen and divined it. The omen said: "This is called 'The phoenix in flight, its harmonious cries resound.'" A descendant of Gui will be nurtured by the Jiang clan. After five generations, it shall flourish and reach the rank of chief minister. "After eight generations, none will surpass it."

## 13

In the thirty-seventh year, Duke Huang of Qi attacked Cai and defeated them; He invaded southern lands to attack Chu, reaching Zhao Ling, then returned, passing through Chen. Chen's official Yuan Taotu disliked the passage of their forces through Chen and deceived Qi into taking an eastern route. The eastern route was poor, so Duke Huang became angry and arrested Yuan Taotu of Chen. In this year, Duke Xian of Jin killed his crown prince Shen Sheng.

## 14

In the forty-fifth year, Xuangong died and his son Kuan succeeded as ruler; this was Muguang. In the fifth year of Muguang's reign, Duke Huang of Qi died. In the sixteenth year, Duke Wen of Jin defeated the Chu army at Chengpu. In this year, Muguang died and his son Gonggong Shuo succeeded as ruler. In the sixth year of Gonggong's reign, Crown Prince Shangchen of Chu assassinated his father King Cheng and took the throne in his place; this was King Mu. In the eleventh year, Duke Mu of Qin died. In the eighteenth year, Gonggong died and his son Linggong Pingguo succeeded as ruler.

## 15

In the first year of Linggong's reign, King Zhuang of Chu ascended the throne. In the sixth year, Chu attacked Chen. In the tenth year, Chen and Chu made peace.

## 16

In the fourteenth year, Linggong and his officials Kong Ning and Yixingfu all had affairs with Xia Ji. They wore her clothes to play games in court. Die Ya advised, "If the ruler and officials indulge in licentiousness, what will the people imitate?" Linggong told the two men about this, and they requested to kill Die Ya. The ruler did not forbid it, so they killed Die Ya. In the fifteenth year, Linggong dined with the two men at Xia's residence. 略 The ruler joked to the two men and said, "Zhengshu looks like you." The two men replied, "He also resembles you." Zhengshu became angry. After Linggong left the banquet, Zhengshu ambushed him at the stable gate with a crossbow and shot him to death. Kong Ning and Yixingfu both fled to Chu, while Linggong's crown prince Wu fled to Jin. Zhengshu declared himself ruler of Chen. Zhengshu was a former official of Chen. Xia Ji was the wife of Yushu and the mother of Shu.

## 17

In the first winter of Chenggong's reign, King Zhuangwang of Chu, avenging Linggong for Xia Zhengshu, led feudal lords to attack Chen. He told the people of Chen: "Do not be alarmed; I am only punishing Zhengshu." After killing Zhengshu, he annexed Chen and made it a commandery, and all his officials congratulated him. Shen Shushi had been on a mission to Qi and was returning when he alone did not offer congratulations. King Zhuang asked the reason, and he replied: "There is a saying among common people: if one leads an ox through another's field, the owner of the field may seize the ox. Passing through the field is indeed a fault, but to seize the ox—was this not excessive?" Now, Your Majesty declared Zhengshu a traitor who killed his lord and summoned feudal lords to punish him in the name of righteousness. Yet after doing so, you have annexed it for its territory—how then can you command the world?!" This is why I did not offer congratulations." King Zhuang said, "Well spoken." He then welcomed from Jin the crown prince Wu of Duke Ling and installed him as ruler, restoring Chen to its former state; this was Chenggong. When Confucius read the historical records about Chu's restoration of Chen, he said: "How virtuous was King Zhuang of Chu! He valued a single word more than a state with a thousand chariots."

## 18

[20] In the eighth year, King Zhuang of Chu died. In the twenty-ninth year, Chen violated its alliance with Chu. In the thirtieth year, King Gong of Chu attacked Chen. In this year, Chenggong died and his son Aigong Ruolie succeeded as ruler. Because of Chen's mourning period for its new ruler, Chu withdrew its troops.

## 19

In the third year of Aigong's reign, Chu besieged Chen but then released it. In the twenty-eighth year, Gongzi Wei of Chu assassinated his ruler Jiao Ao and declared himself king; this was King Ling.

## 20

In the thirty-fourth year, originally, Aigong married a woman from Zheng. His elder concubine bore Crown Prince Dao Shi, and his younger concubine bore Yan. Two favored concubines also gave birth: the elder one bore Liu, and the younger one bore Sheng. Liu was especially favored by Aigong, who entrusted him to his brother Situ Zhao. When Aigong fell ill, in the third month, Zhao killed Crown Prince Dao and installed Liu as crown prince. Aigong was furious and wanted to execute Zhao; in response, Zhao raised troops to surround and guard Aigong. Aigong then committed suicide by hanging. Zhao eventually installed Liu as ruler of Chen. In the fourth month, Chen sent envoys to Chu. King Ling of Chu heard about the turmoil in Chen and had Chen's envoys executed. He then ordered Gongzi Qiji to raise troops and attack Chen, causing Chen ruler Liu to flee to Zheng. In the ninth month, Chu besieged Chen. In the eleventh month, Chen was destroyed. He appointed Qiji as Gong of Chen.

## 21

When Zhao killed Crown Prince Dao, the prince's son named Wu fled to Jin. Jin Pinggong asked Taishi Zhao, "Will Chen finally perish?" He replied, "Chen is a clan of Zhuanxu. The Chen clan gained power in Qi but ultimately perished." From Mu to Gusu, there was no violation of the mandate. Shun elevated him with a clear virtue. Down to Sui, the descendants preserved it generation after generation. When Hugong came into power, the Zhou dynasty bestowed a surname upon him and ordered that sacrifices be made to Emperor Shun of Yu. Moreover, descendants of those with great virtue are surely honored for a hundred generations." "The dynasty of Yu has not yet ended; perhaps it will continue in Qi?"

## 22

Five years after King Ling of Chu destroyed Chen, Gongzi Qiji of Chu assassinated King Ling and took his place; this was King Ping. In the early years of King Ping's reign, he wished to gain harmony with the feudal lords and thus sought Wu, the son of Crown Prince Dao Shi of Chen, and installed him as ruler of Chen; this was Huigong. After Huigong ascended the throne, he began his reign count from the year Aigong died, leaving a five-year gap in the records.

## 23

In the tenth year, there was a fire in Chen. In the fifteenth year, King Liao of Wu sent Gongzi Guang to attack Chen and captured Hu and Shen before withdrawing. In the twenty-eighth year, King Helu of Wu, together with Zi Xu, defeated Chu and entered Yingting. In this year, Huigong died and his son Huaigong Liu succeeded as ruler.

## 24

In the first year of Huaigong's reign, Wu defeated Chu, occupied Yingting, and summoned the Teng ruler. The Teng ruler wanted to go, but a court official said, "Wu has just gained power; although the King of Chu is gone, he had old ties with Chen. We cannot betray him." Huaigong thus declined on grounds of illness. In the fourth year, Wu again summoned Huaigong. Huaigong was afraid and went to Wu. Wu was angry that he had not come before, detained him, and Huaigong eventually died in Wu. Chen then installed Yue, the son of Huaigong, as ruler; this was Min Gong.

## 25

In the sixth year of Mingong's reign, Confucius came to Chen. King Fuchai of Wu attacked Chen and took three towns before withdrawing. In the thirteenth year, Wu once again attacked Chen; Chen urgently appealed to Chu for help. King Zhao of Chu came to rescue and stationed his army at Chengfu. The Wu forces withdrew. In this year, King Zhao of Chu died in Chengfu. At that time, Confucius was in Chen. In the fifteenth year, Song destroyed Cao. In the sixteenth year, King Fuchai of Wu attacked Qi and defeated it at Ailing; he then sent envoys to summon the Teng ruler. The Teng ruler was afraid and went to Wu as requested. Chu attacked Chen. In the twenty-first year, Tian Chang of Qi assassinated his ruler Jian Gong. In the twenty-third year, Bai Gongsheng of Chu killed Ling Yin Zi Xi and Zi Qi, and attacked King Huai. Ye Gong defeated and drove out Bai Gongsheng, who then committed suicide.

## 26

In the twenty-fourth year, King Huai of Chu restored his rule and led troops northward to attack. He killed Mingong of Chen and thus destroyed Chen and annexed it. In this year, Confucius died.

## 27

Qidonglou Gong was a descendant of the Xia Hou Yu. During the Yin dynasty, his descendants were sometimes enfeoffed and sometimes cut off. King Wu of Zhou defeated Yin Zhout, sought descendants of Yu, and found Donglou Gong. He enfeoffed him at Qi to continue the ancestral rites of the Xia Hou Shi.

## 28

Donglou Gong gave birth to Xilou Gong, who in turn fathered Gongsiku, and Gongsiku fathered Mouqu Gong. Mouqu Gong lived during the reign of King Li of Zhou. Mouqu Gong gave birth to Wu Gong. Wu Gong reigned for forty-seven years before dying, and his son Jinggong succeeded him as ruler. Jinggong died in the twenty-third year of his reign; his son Gonggong succeeded as ruler. Gonggong died after eight years on the throne, and his son Degong became ruler. Degong died in the eighteenth year of his reign, and his younger brother Huangong Gu Rong succeeded as ruler. Huangong died after seventeen years on the throne; his son Xiaogong Gai became ruler. Xiaogong died in the seventeenth year of his reign and was succeeded by his younger brother Wengong Yigu. Wengong died after fourteen years on the throne, and was succeeded by his younger brother Pinggong Yu. Pinggong reigned for eighteen years before dying; his son Diaogong Cheng became ruler. Diaogong died in the twelfth year of his reign, and was succeeded by his son Yinguo Qi. In July, Yinguo's younger brother Sui assassinated him and took the throne; this was Liggong. Liggong died after nineteen years on the throne and was succeeded by his son Mingong Wei. In the fifteenth year of Mingong's reign, King Huai of Chu destroyed Chen. In the sixteenth year, Mingong's younger brother Yalu assassinated him and took his place as ruler; this was Aigong. Aigong reigned for ten years before dying, and Mingong's son Chi succeeded him; this was Chugong. Chugong died in the twelfth year after ascending the throne, and his son Jian Gong Chun became ruler. In the first year of his reign (the forty-fourth year of King Huai of Chu), Qi was destroyed by Chu. Qi fell thirty-four years after Chen's destruction.

## 29

Qi was small and weak, its affairs not worth mentioning.

## 30

The descendants of Shun were enfeoffed at Chen by King Wu of Zhou; until their state was destroyed by King Huai of Chu, they had a separate genealogical record. The descendants of Yu were enfeoffed at Qi by King Wu of Zhou; their state was later destroyed by King Huai of Chu and also had a separate genealogical account. The descendants of Qie became the Yin dynasty, which has its own chronological record (Benji). After the fall of Yin, Zhou enfeoffed their descendants at Song; it was later destroyed by King Min of Qi and also has a separate genealogical record (Shijia). The descendants of Houji became the Zhou dynasty; it was destroyed by King Zhao of Qin and has its own chronological account (Benji). The descendants of Gao Yao were sometimes enfeoffed in Ying and Liu; they were destroyed by King Mu of Chu, but no genealogical records remain. The descendants of Boyi were later enfeoffed at Qi during the reign of King Wu of Zhou, known as Tai Gong Wang; they were destroyed by the Teng clan and have a separate genealogical record (shijia). The descendants of Boyi, during the reign of King Ping of Zhou, were enfeoffed as Qin; they were destroyed by Xiang Yu and have their own chronological record (benji). Chui, Yi, Kui, and Long—nothing is known about where their descendants were enfeoffed; they are not recorded. These eleven men above were all famous ministers of virtue and merit during the transition between Tang and Yu. The descendants of five among them became emperors or kings, while the rest became prominent feudal lords. Teng, Xue, and Zou were small fiefdoms enfeoffed between the Xia, Yin, and Zhou dynasties; they were too insignificant to be listed or discussed.

## 31

During King Wu of Zhou's time, there were still more than a thousand marquises and feudal lords. After the reigns of Kings You and Li, the feudal lords engaged in wars to conquer one another. States such as Jiang, Huang, Hu, and Shen were too numerous to count; thus, they are not recorded in the annals (Shang) [Yun]. Grand Historian said: The virtue of Shun can truly be called supreme! He abdicated in favor of Xia, and his descendants received ancestral sacrifices through three dynasties. When the State of Chu destroyed Chen, Tian Chang seized power in Qi and eventually founded a state; his descendants continued for generations without interruption. Their posterity flourished, and there were never lacking those who held land. By the time of Yu, during the Zhou dynasty they were enfeoffed in Qi, but their status was very low and not worth mentioning. King Huai of Chu destroyed Qi; after that, King Goujian of Yue rose to prominence.
