# Fengshan Shu

## 1

Since ancient times, emperors who received their mandate have never failed to perform the Fengshan sacrifices. There may be those who assume power without corresponding omens, but there has never been an emperor who witnessed auspicious signs and did not proceed to Mount Tai. Even if an emperor received the mandate but his achievements were insufficient, or even if he reached Mount Liangfu but his virtue was not universally harmonious, or harmony was achieved yet daily affairs left no time to spare, therefore such matters are rarely pursued. The Classic says: "If rites are not practiced for three years, the rites will surely be abandoned; if music is not performed for three years, music will certainly fall into disrepair." In times of prosperity in each generation, the Fengshan sacrifices are conducted as a response; when decline sets in, they cease. Those who were distant span more than a thousand years, and those closer in time span several hundred years; therefore, their rituals are now lost and forgotten, and the details cannot be recorded or heard of.

## 2

The Shangshu says: "Shun used the Xuanji yuheng to regulate the seven political affairs." He thus presented offerings to Shangdi, made sacrifices of incense to the Six Ancestries, observed the mountains and rivers, and honored all deities. He collected the five symbols of authority, selected an auspicious month and day, presented them to the Four Peaks and their regional governors, and then returned the symbols. In the second month of the year, he conducted an eastern inspection tour and arrived at Taizong (Mount Tai). Taizong is Mount Tai. He made sacrificial offerings and paid homage to the mountains and rivers in order of rank. He then met with the eastern vassals. The "Donghou" refers to the feudal lords. He harmonized the months and fixed the days, standardized the pitch-pipes, measurements, and weights and measures, revised the five rites, and presented tribute consisting of five kinds of jade, three pieces of silk, two live animals, and one dead animal. lòng In the fifth month, he conducted an inspection tour to Nanyue (the Southern Peak). Nanyue is Hengshan Mountain. In the eighth month, he conducted an inspection tour and arrived at Xiyue (the Western Peak). Xiyue is Huashan Mountain. In the eleventh month, he conducted an inspection tour arriving at Beiyue (the Northern Peak). Beiyue is Hengshan Mountain (in Hebei Province). The rites performed at all these places were the same as those conducted at Taizong. Zhongyue is Songgao Mountain (Mount Song). An inspection tour was conducted once every five years.

## 3

Yu followed this practice. Fourteen generations later, when Emperor Kongjia came to power, he indulged in immoral conduct and favored the gods; as a result, the deities became disrespectful, and two dragons left him. Three generations after that, King Tang attacked Jie; he wished to replace the Shang altar of the Xia dynasty but could not do so, and thus composed a text called "Xia She." Eight generations later, when Emperor Taipu came to power, a mulberry and millet plant grew in the court; by morning it had grown large enough for two people to embrace, causing great alarm. Yi Zhi said: "Omens of evil cannot overcome virtue." Taipu cultivated virtue, and the mulberry and millet plants died. Yi Zhi praised Wu Xian; from this time onward began the rise of Wu Xian's influence. Fourteen generations later, Emperor Wuding appointed Fu Yue as his prime minister; the Yin dynasty revived and was known as Gaozong. A pheasant climbed onto the ear of a tripod cauldron and called out, causing Wuding to be alarmed. Zu Ji said: "Cultivate virtue." Wuding followed his advice, and thus the throne remained in lasting peace. Five generations later, Emperor Wuyi disrespected the gods and was struck by lightning to death. Three generations later, Emperor Zhou indulged in licentiousness and disorder; King Wu of Zhou attacked him. From this we can see that at the beginning, they were always respectful and reverent, but later gradually became careless and neglectful.

## 4

The Zhouli says: "On the winter solstice, sacrifices are made to Heaven at the southern suburbs to welcome the arrival of the longest night; on the summer solstice, offerings are made to the earth deities." In both cases music and dance are used; only then can the gods be properly honored through ritual. The Son of Heaven sacrifices to the famous mountains and great rivers throughout the realm; the Five Peaks are treated with a status equivalent to that of the Three Dukes, the Four Great Rivers as equal to feudal lords, while feudal lords sacrifice only to the famous mountains and major rivers within their own territories. The "Four Great Rivers" are the Yangtze River (Changjiang), Yellow River (Huanghe), Huai River, and Ji River. The Son of Heaven built Mingtang and Biyong; feudal lords constructed Pangong.

## 5

After assisting King Cheng, the Duke of Zhou conducted sacrifices at the southern suburbs, offering Houji as a companion to Heaven, and honored King Wen in the Mingtang temple, offering him as a counterpart to Shangdi. Since Yu's time, the worship of ancestral altars was revived; Houji cultivated crops and harvests, so there were temples dedicated to Houji. The practice of sacrifices at the southern suburbs and ancestral altars has a long history.

## 6

Fourteen generations after the Zhou dynasty overthrew Yin, each successive generation declined further; rites and music were abandoned, feudal lords acted with impunity, and King You was defeated by the Quanrong tribes. The Zhou court then moved east to Luoyi. Duke Xiang of Qin attacked the Rong tribes and rescued the Zhou, thus being first enfeoffed as a feudal lord. After being enfeoffed as a marquis, Duke Xiang of Qin resided in the western frontier. He considered himself the master of the deity Shaohao and built an altar called Xichou to worship the White Emperor; for sacrifices, he used one white horse, one yellow bull, and one ram. Sixteen years later, Duke Wen of Qin conducted a hunting expedition eastward between the Qian and Wei rivers; divination indicated that settling there would be auspicious. Duke Wen dreamed of a yellow snake descending from heaven to the ground, with its mouth stopping at Fuyan. Duke Wen asked Shi Dun about it; Dun said: "This is a sign from Shangdi; you should build an altar to worship it." Thus, they built the Fuchou altar and used three sacrificial animals for sacrifices to the White Emperor at the southern suburbs.

## 7

Before the Fuchou altar was built, there had already been an old Wu Yang Wochou altar near Yong; to the east of Yong was Haochou. All these were abandoned and no longer used for sacrifices. Some say: "Since ancient times, Yongzhou has been high and elevated, a favored place for deities; therefore, altars were established there to worship Shangdi at the southern suburbs, and all temples of various gods gathered here." It is said that sacrifices were once conducted during the time of Huangdi; even in late Zhou times, they still performed sacrifices there." These accounts are not found in classical texts and are not mentioned by learned officials.

## 8

Nine years after the Fuchou altar was established, Duke Wen obtained a stone said to resemble an oracle bone; he built an altar in Beiban Cheng of Chencang to worship it. Sometimes the deity did not appear for a whole year, sometimes it came several times in one year. When it arrived, it was often at night; its light resembled that of a shooting star, coming from the southeast and gathering over the altar city. It appeared like a rooster, its sound echoing through the clouds, causing wild pheasants to call out during the night. They offered one set of sacrificial animals in worship and named it Chenbao.

## 9

Seventy-eight years after the Fuchou was established, when Duke De of Qin came to power, divination advised that he should settle in Yong. The oracle said: "Later generations will drink horse water from the Yellow River," so he made Yong his capital. From this time onward, all temples and altars in Yong flourished. Three hundred sacrificial offerings were conducted at Fuchou. An altar for worshiping Fu was built. Dogs were sacrificed at the four gates of the city to ward off plagues and calamities caused by witchcraft.

## 10

Duke De reigned for two years before dying. Four years later, Duke Xuan of Qin built the Michou altar in Weinan to worship the Green Emperor.

## 11

Fourteen years later, when Duke Miao of Qin ascended the throne, he fell ill and lay in bed for five days without regaining consciousness; when he awoke, he said that he had dreamed of Shangdi, who commanded Duke Miao to bring peace to the chaos in Jin. The court historians recorded and stored it in the archives. Later generations all said that Duke Miao of Qin had ascended to Heaven.

## 12

Guan Zhong said: "In ancient times, seventy-two rulers performed the Fengshan sacrifices on Mount Tai and Mount Liangfu, but I have records of only twelve of them. "In antiquity, Wuhuai Shi conducted the Fengshan rites at Mount Tai, performing the sacrifice at Yunyun;  Shennong performed the Fengshan rites on Mounttai and conducted the sacrifice at Yuyun; Yandi conducted the Fengshan sacrifices on Mounttai, performing his ritual at Yuyun;  Huangdi performed the Fengshan rites onmount Tai and conducted the sacrificeat Tingting; Zhuanshu conducted the Fengshan sacrificeson Mounttai, performing his rituial at Yuyun;  Dikou performed the Fengshan rites on Mt.Tai and conducted the sacrificeat Yuyun;  Yao conducted the Fengshan sacrifices omounttai, performing his ritual at Yuun;  Shun performed the Fengshan rites on Mountai and conducted the sacrificeat Yunyun;  Yu conducted the Fengshan sacrifices o Mounttai, performing his rite at Kuaiji; Tang performed the Fengshan rites omount Tai and conducted the sacrifceat Yunyun;  King Cheng of Zhou performed the Fengshan sacrifices onmounttai, conducting his rite at Sheshou: all these rulers received their mandate before they were able to perform the Fengshan rites." Duke Huan said: "I led a northern campaign against the Shan Rong, passing through Guzhu; I waged war westward against Daxia, crossed the Liusha Desert, tethered my horses and suspended my chariots, ascending Mount Beier; I marched south to Zhaoling and climbed Xiong'er Mountain to overlook the Yangtze and Han rivers. I convened three military parliaments with war chariots and six diplomatic assemblies with ceremonial carriages, united the feudal lords nine times, and once restored order to all under Heaven; no lord dared defy me. "In ancient times, when the Three Dynasties received their mandate, what was there that differed from this?" At this, Guan Zhong realized that Duke Huan could not be dissuaded by words alone and therefore presented him with a practical matter. He said: "In ancient times, the Fengshan sacrifices required the millet from Gaoshang and the grain from Beili as symbols of abundance; the three-ridge reed grass growing between the Yangtze and Huai rivers was used to make sacrificial mats. The one-eyed fish from the East Sea and the two-winged bird from the West Sea arrived, and then fifteen other things came without being summoned. "Now, the phoenix and qilin do not appear; auspicious grains do not grow, while ragweed and thorny grass flourish. Owls and hawks visit frequently. Under such circumstances, how can one dare to perform the Fengshan sacrifice? Is it not impossible?" Thus Duke Huan abandoned his plan. In this year, Duke Miao of Qin welcomed Ji Jun Yiwu into the state of Jin. After that, he placed three successive rulers on the throne in Jin and restored peace to its chaos. Duke Miao reigned for thirty-nine years before dying.

## 13

More than a hundred years later, Confucius discussed the Six Arts and briefly mentioned that over seventy kings who changed dynasties had performed the Fengshan sacrifices at Mounttai and the sacrificeat Mount Liangfu. However, the details of their sacrificial rites were not clearly recorded, making it difficult to speak about them in detail. Someone asked Confucius about the ritual of di, and Confucius said: "I do not know." "If one understands the ritual of Di, then governing the world would be as easy as looking at the palm of one's hand." The Book of Songs says: "When King Zhou was on the throne, Duke Wenwang received his mandate, yet his authority did not reach Mount Tai. King Wu defeated Yin two years later, but before peace had been restored to the world, he died. It was not until King Cheng of Zhou that virtue permeated the realm and approached perfection; his Fengshan sacrifice came close to being appropriate. Later, when subordinate ministers held power, Ji Shi performed a sacrificial rite at Mount Tai, and Confucius criticized him for it.

## 14

At that time, Zhang Hong was advising King Ling of Zhou in matters of ritual. The feudal lords no longer paid homage to the Son of Heaven, and the power of Zhou had waned. Therefore, Zhang Hong emphasized rituals involving spirits and deities, establishing a ceremony known as She Lilv. The "Lilv" refers to the feudal lords who did not come to pay homage. He relied on strange and supernatural phenomena in an attempt to bring the feudal lords back into submission. The feudal lords did not comply, and eventually the people of Jin captured and killed Zhang Hong. From then on, those in Zhou who spoke about strange or supernatural matters began with Zhang Hong.

## 15

More than a hundred years later, Duke Ling of Qin established the Shangzhi at Wuyang to worship the Yellow Emperor; he also established the Xiaozhi there to worship the Yan Emperor.

## 16

Forty-eight years later, Zhou Taishi Dan met Duke Xian of Qin and said: "The state of Qin will first reunite with the House of Zhou. After they part ways again, in five hundred years they shall unite once more; seventeen years after this union, a hegemon or emperor shall arise." Rain of gold fell in Linyang. Duke Xian of Qin, believing this to be an auspicious omen of gold, therefore established a sacrificial site at Qichi in Linyang and worshipped the White Emperor.

## 17

One hundred and twenty years later, Qin destroyed Zhou, and the Nine Tripods of Zhou were brought into Qin. Some say that when the sacred altar at Taichou in Song was destroyed, the tripods sank beneath the Si River near Pengcheng.

## 18

One hundred and fifteen years later, Qin unified all under heaven.

## 19

After Emperor Qin Shi Huang had united the world and declared himself emperor, some said: "The Yellow Emperor received the virtue of earth; yellow dragons and earthworms appeared. "The Xia dynasty received the virtue of wood; a green dragon stopped in the suburbs, and grasses and trees flourished abundantly." "The Yin dynasty received the virtue of metal; silver overflowed from the mountains. "The Zhou dynasty received the virtue of fire, marked by the appearance of red crows." "Now that Qin has replaced Zhou, it is the time of the water virtue. "In the past, Duke Wen of Qin went hunting and captured a black dragon; this was an omen of the virtue of water." Thus, Qin renamed the Yellow River "Desui," took the tenth month of winter as the beginning of the year, favored black in color, used six as a standard for measurement, emphasized the musical pitch Dali, and governed by strict legalism.

## 20

Three years after ascending to the imperial throne, Emperor Qin Shi Huang toured the eastern commanderies and counties, made sacrifices at Zouyishan Mountain, and composed an ode praising the achievements of Qin. At that time, he summoned seventy Confucian scholars and doctors from Qi and Lu to Mount Tai's foot. Some of the Confucian scholars argued: "In ancient times, for the Fengshan sacrifices, simple wicker carts were used to avoid damaging the soil, stones, and vegetation on the mountain; the ground was swept for sacrifice, and mats made of reeds were used, to signify that it was easy to follow." Emperor Qin Shi Huang heard these conflicting opinions and found them difficult to implement; thus he dismissed the Confucian scholars. He then cleared a road for his carriage, ascending from the southern slope of Mount Tai to its summit. There he erected stone inscriptions praising Emperor Qin Shi Huang's virtue and declaring that he had earned the right to perform the Fengshan ceremony. He descended from the northern slope of the mountain, performing the Chanshan sacrifice at Mount Liangfu. The rituals were somewhat based on those used by the Grand Priest in worshipping Heaven at Yong, but the details of the offerings and burials were kept secret, so they could not be recorded for posterity.

## 21

When Emperor Qin Shi Huang ascended Mount Tai, he encountered a sudden storm and heavy rain on the middle slope and took shelter under a large tree. After being dismissed, the Confucian scholars were not allowed to participate in the Fengshan rituals. Upon hearing that Emperor Qin Shi Huang had encountered a storm and rain, they criticized him.

## 22

Thus, Emperor Qin Shi Huang proceeded to tour the eastern coast by sea, performing sacrifices at famous mountains and great rivers as well as the Eight Deities in search of immortals such as Xianmen. The Eight Deities have been venerated since ancient times; some say they were established by Duke Tai Gong onwards. The state of Qi was named "Qi" because it was associated with the heavenly deity Tianqi. The origins of their worship have been lost to history, and no one knows when they began. The Eight Deities: the first was Tianzhu, worshipped at Tianqi. Tianqi is a deep spring of water located beneath the southern suburbs of Linzi Mountain. The second was Dizhu, worshipped at Mount Tai and Mount Liangfu. It is said that Heaven favors the shady; thus, sacrifices to it must be conducted at the base of high mountains and on small hills above them. Such sites were called "zhi"; Earth values brightness; therefore, sacrifices to it must take place at a round altar in the middle of a marsh or lake. The third was Bingzhu, worshipped as Chi You. Chi You's shrine is located in Jian Xiang, Dongping Lu, which lies on the western border of Qi. The fourth was Yin Zhu, worshipped at San Shan. The fifth was Yangzhu, worshipped on Zhifu Island. The sixth was Yue Zhu, worshipped at Laishan Mountain. All of these deities were located in the northern part of Qi, bordering Bohai Sea. The seventh was Ri Zhu, worshipped at Chengshan Mountain. Chengshan protrudes into the sea like a ladle and is located in the northeastern corner of Qi, where it faces the rising sun. The eighth was Sishizhu, worshipped at Langya. Langya lies to the east of Qi and is considered the place where each year begins. Each deity was worshipped with one sacrificial set, but priests and shamans made their own additions or omissions, resulting in varied rituals and offerings of jade and cloth.

## 23

During the reigns of Duke Wei and Duke Xuan of Qi, followers of Zou Zi discussed and recorded the theory of the Five Virtues' cycle from beginning to end. When Qin became emperor, people from Qi presented this theory to him; thus Emperor Qin Shi Huang adopted it. Later, Song Wuji, Zheng Boqiao, Chong Shang, and Xianmen Gao were all from Yan. They practiced the Way of Immortals through alchemy and transformation, relying on supernatural and spiritual matters. Zou Yan became prominent among the feudal lords by expounding on the theory of Yin-Yang and cosmic cycles. However, those alchemists from Yan and Qi along the coast who transmitted his teachings could not fully grasp it. From then on, a large number of strange, eccentric, and flatterers emerged, too numerous to count.

## 24

Since the time of Duke Wei, Duke Xuan, and King Zhao of Yan, envoys were sent into the sea in search of Penglai, Fangzhang, and Yingzhou. These three divine mountains are said to be located in Bohai Sea, not far from the human world; but when misfortune was about to strike, the ships would suddenly be carried away by strong winds. It is said that some people had actually reached these islands, where immortals and the elixir of eternal life were to be found. The animals and beasts there were all white in color, and palaces and gates were made of gold and silver. Before reaching them, they appeared like clouds on the horizon; but upon arrival, it turned out that the three divine mountains were actually submerged beneath the water. Whenever one approached them closely, strong winds would suddenly arise and carry the ship away; no one was ever able to reach them. No ruler of the age failed to be deeply eager for this. When Emperor Qin Shi Huang unified all under heaven and came to the sea, alchemists spoke endlessly about these matters. Emperor Qin Shi Huang believed that by coming to the seaside he might still be too late, so he sent people with young boys and girls into the sea in search of them. The ships were caught at sea by strong winds, and all claimed that they had not yet reached the divine mountains but had merely glimpsed them from afar. The following year, Emperor Qin Shi Huang once again toured by the sea, reaching Langya, passing Mount Heng, and returning via Shangdang. Three years later, he traveled to Jieshi, examined the alchemists who had gone into the sea in search of immortality, and returned via Shangjun. Five years later, Emperor Qin Shi Huang traveled south to Xiangshan and then ascended Kuaiji Mountain. He followed the coast of the sea in hopes of encountering the miraculous elixirs on the three divine islands in the ocean. He failed to find them and, while returning, died at Shachou.

## 25

In the first year of his reign, Second Emperor Qin Er Shi toured eastward to Jieshi and followed the southern coast of the sea. He passed Mount Tai and arrived at Kuaiji Mountain, where he performed sacrifices and inscribed new texts beside the stone steles erected by Emperor Qin Shi Huang in order to highlight the achievements and virtue of his predecessor. That autumn, the feudal lords rebelled against Qin. Three years later, Second Emperor was assassinated.

## 26

Twelve years after the Fengshan ceremony of Qin Shi Huang, the state of Qin fell. The Confucian scholars resented the Qin for burning books and suppressing literature, while common people hated its laws. The whole world rebelled against it. All spread rumors saying: "When Emperor Qin Shi Huang went to Mount Tai, he was struck by a violent storm and could not perform the Fengshan ceremony." Is this not exactly what is meant by "those who attempt such matters without possessing the virtue to do so"?

## 27

In ancient times, the capitals of the Three Dynasties were all located between the Yellow River and Luo River. Therefore, Songgao Mountain was regarded as the central sacred mountain, while the Four Sacred Mountains corresponded to their respective directions, and the Four Great Rivers were all situated in Shandong. When Qin declared itself emperor with its capital at Xianyang, the Five Sacred Mountains and Four Great Rivers all lay to the east. From the Five Emperors to Qin, dynasties rose and fell. Sacred mountains and great rivers were sometimes under feudal lords or under the Son of Heaven; their rituals changed with each generation, differing so much that they could not be fully recorded. When Qin unified the world, it ordered officials in charge of sacrifices to regularly worship Heaven and Earth, sacred mountains and great rivers, as well as spirits and deities that could be systematically arranged.

## 28

Thus, from Xiaoyu to the eastward, there were five sacred mountains with sacrificial rites, and two great rivers with their own shrines. The first was Taishi Mountain. Taishi is Songgao Mountain. Hengshan, Taishan, Kuaijishan, and Xiangshan. The rivers were the Ji River and the Huai River. In spring, sacrifices of dried meat and wine were offered annually; when the ice melted in ponds, they would perform rites accordingly. In autumn, when rivers dried up or froze, and in winter, when channels became blocked, prayers and sacrificial ceremonies were conducted. The sacrifices used one young bull for each offering, with different sets of ritual vessels, jade offerings, and silk gifts for each.

## 29

From Huashan westward, there were seven sacred mountains and four great rivers. They were Hua Mountain and Boshan. Boshan is Cuishan. Yueshan, Qishan, Wu Yue, Hongzhongshan, and Dushan. Dushan is the Wen Mountain in Shu. The river was the Yellow River, with its shrine located at Linjin; the Mian River, worshipped in Hanzhong; Qiaoyuan, worshipped at Chaona; the Yangtze River, worshipped in Shu. They also performed spring and autumn rites for thawing or drying rivers and prayers when channels were blocked, just as was done for the sacred mountains and rivers in the east; and each offering used a young bull with different ritual vessels, jade gifts, and silk offerings. Moreover, the four great shrines at Hongshan, Qishan, Wuyue, and Yueshan all had sacrificial millet offerings.

## 30

The Chenbao Festival was held for seasonal sacrifices. For the Yellow River, sacrificial millet porridge was added to the offerings. All these were located within the domain of Yongzhou, near the Son of Heaven's capital; therefore, one additional carriage and four young horses were added to the offerings.

## 31

The Ba River, Chan River, Changshui River, Feng River, Lao River, Jing River, and Wei River were not great rivers; however, because they were near Xianyang, they all received sacrificial rites comparable to those of sacred mountains and rivers, but without any additional offerings.

## 32

The Qian and Luo rivers, along with minor sites such as Mingze, Puyushan, and Yuexushan, were considered lesser mountains and rivers. They also received annual prayers for blocked channels or thawing ice on rivers, but the rituals did not have to be identical.

## 33

In Yongzhou there were shrines for the sun, moon, San Star, Shen Star, the Northern and Southern Dipper constellations, Yinghuo (Mars), Taibai (Venus), Suixing (Jupiter), Tianxing (Saturn), [Chenxing], the Twenty-Eight Lunar Mansions, Fengbo (Wind God), Yushi (Rain Master), the Four Seas, Nine Ministers, Fourteen Officials, and various other deities such as Zhu Bu, Zhu Yan, and Zhu Qiu. There were more than a hundred temples in total. To the west there were also dozens of shrines. At Hulu, there was a shrine for the Zhou Son of Heaven. At Xiagi, there was a deity of Heaven. The Feng and Hao rivers had the Zhaoming and Son of Heaven's Bichi pools. At (She) [Du] and Bo, there were shrines to the Three Shezhu gods and a shrine to Shouxing; and in Yongzhou's Jiang Temple there was also a Du Zhu deity. Du Zhu, an old Right General of the Zhou dynasty, was among the smallest deities venerated in Qinzhong. Each was worshipped according to seasonal rites.

## 34

Only the four altars of Yongzhou for the Supreme Deities were held in highest reverence, and among them only Chenbao's shrine had a visible effect on the people. Therefore, the four altars in Yongzhou were used for annual prayers in spring and for thawing ice or drying rivers in autumn; winter rites were held when channels became blocked. In May, young horses were offered as sacrifices, and during the middle of each season (the fourth, sixth, eighth, and tenth months), monthly sacrifices were conducted, [and] once a year on the Chenbao Festival. Red-brown horses were used in spring and summer, while chestnut-colored ones were used in autumn and winter. Four young horses were sacrificed at each altar, along with one set of wooden figures of dragons and 栾 carts (luan che), and another set of wooden cart-horse figures. Each was colored according to the respective deity's color. Four yellow calves and four lambs were sacrificed, with jade offerings and silk gifts in specified quantities; all were buried alive or covered without the use of ritual tables or sacrificial vessels. A grand sacrifice at the suburban altar was held once every three years. Qin regarded the tenth month of winter as the beginning of the year, so the Son of Heaven would usually appear at the suburban altar on a certain day in the tenth month to perform rites. He would light torches and offer sacrifices by the side of Xianyang while wearing white robes; the procedures followed those described in the ritual classics. The sacrifices at Xichang and Qizhao were conducted as before, but the Son of Heaven did not personally attend.

## 35

All these shrines were regularly managed by the Grand Priest, who performed sacrifices according to seasonal rites. As for other sacred mountains and rivers or various deities and the Eight Gods, if the Son of Heaven passed by a site, he would offer sacrifices there; but once he left, no further rites were performed. As for shrines of distant deities in outlying commanderies and counties, the local people themselves conducted sacrifices independently; these were not under the jurisdiction of the Son of Heaven's priestly officials. The priestly officials had secret priests who, whenever disasters or omens occurred, would immediately perform sacrifices and prayers to transfer the misfortune onto lower ranks.

## 36

When the Han dynasty rose to power, Emperor Gaozu, when he was still obscure, once killed a great serpent. An object said: "This snake is the son of the White Emperor; its killer is the son of the Red Emperor." When Emperor Gaozu first rose in rebellion, he offered sacrifices at Feng Fenyu She. When he captured Pei and became the Lord of Pei, he worshipped Chiyou and used blood to consecrate drums and banners. He then arrived at Bashi in the tenth month, joined with feudal lords to pacify Xianyang, and was enfeoffed as King of Han. Therefore, he adopted the tenth month as the beginning of the new year, with red being the favored color.

## 37

In the second year, when he marched east to attack Xiang Ji and then returned through the pass, he asked: "Which of the former Qin dynasty's Supreme Deities was worshipped at the altar?" The reply was: "There were four deities—shrines for the White, Blue, Yellow, and Red Emperors." Emperor Gaozu said: "I have heard that Heaven has Five Emperors, but there are only four here. Why is this so?" No one knew the explanation. At this, Emperor Gaozu said: "I now understand; it is waiting for me to complete the five." He then established a shrine for the Black Emperor and named it Beizhao. The officials in charge conducted the sacrifices, but the emperor did not personally attend. He summoned all former Qin priestly officials and reestablished the posts of Grand Priest and Grand Minister, following their original ritual procedures. He also ordered that each county establish a communal shrine for public sacrifices. An edict was issued: "I greatly value religious rites and hold them in deep reverence. Now, the sacrifices to the Supreme Deity as well as sacred mountains, rivers, and other deities that should be worshipped must all be conducted according to their proper seasons with rituals just as before."

## 38

Four years later, when the empire had been pacified, an edict was issued to the Grand Historian ordering Feng to carefully maintain Fenyu She and to offer sacrifices with sheep and pigs on a regular basis in all four seasons. He ordered the priestly officials to establish a shrine for Chiyou in Chang'an. Chang'an was assigned priestly officials, female shamans, and their shrines. The Liang witches worshipped Heaven, Earth, the Sacred Earth of Heaven, the Heavenly Water, and sites such as Fangzhong and Tangshang; the Jin witches worshipped the Five Emperors, Dongjun, Yunzhong [Jun], Siming, Wushe, Wusi, Clansmen, Xianchui and others; the Qin witches worshipped Shezhu, Wubao, Culei and other deities; the Jing witches worshipped Tangxia, Wuxian, Siming, Shimimi and others; and the Jiutian witches worshipped the Nine Heavens. All these deities were venerated with seasonal rites inside the palace. The river witches offered sacrifices to the Yellow River at Linjin, while those of Nanshan worshipped Nanshan in Qinzhong. Qinzhong refers to Emperor Er Shi. Each had its designated (month) [day].

## 39

Two years later, some said that with the rise of Zhou, the city Tai was established and a shrine for Houji was built; to this day, sacrifices are offered there throughout the empire. At that time Emperor Gaozu issued an edict to the Grand Historian: "Order all commanderies, states, and counties to establish shrines for Lingxing. Sacrifices with oxen must be conducted regularly according to seasonal rites."

## 40

In spring of the tenth year of Emperor Gaozu's reign, officials in charge requested that counties regularly offer sacrifices to Sheji (the gods of land and grain) with sheep and pigs during the second [third] month of spring and at the winter solstice; local communities could conduct their own sacrifices according to their means. The imperial decree stated: "Approved."

## 41

Eighteen years later, Emperor Wendi ascended the throne. Thirteen years after his enthronement, he issued an edict: "Now that secret priests transfer misfortunes onto lower ranks, I find this practice most unacceptable. From now on, abolish it."

## 42

At first, sacred mountains and great rivers belonged to feudal lords; each lord's priest conducted sacrifices independently, without oversight from the Son of Heaven's officials. After the states of Qi and Huainan were abolished, an order was given for the Grand Priest to conduct seasonal rites at all former sites as before.

## 43

In this year, an imperial decree stated: "Since I ascended the throne thirteen years ago, thanks to the blessings of our ancestral temples and the gods of land and grain, peace prevails within the realm, and my people suffer no illness. In recent years, harvests have been abundant; yet I am unworthy—how can I deserve such blessings? They are all gifts from the Supreme Deities and various gods. I have heard that in ancient times, those who received blessings for their virtue were expected to repay the merit; I wish to add more shrines and offerings to these deities. The officials in charge proposed adding one carriage for each of the five altars at Yongzhou, along with harnesses and all necessary equipment; one set of wooden carts (yu che) for each of the Xichang and Qizao altars, four yu horses, with harnesses and equipment; for the Yellow River, Qiaoyuan, and Hanshui rivers, add two jade offerings each; and for all other shrines, enlarge the altars and courtyards, increasing jade gifts, silk offerings, ritual tables, and sacrificial vessels according to their rank. Yet those secret priests attribute blessings solely to me, the Son of Heaven, without involving the common people. From now on, priests must offer sincere reverence and make no requests or prayers."

## 44

Gongsunchen of Lu submitted a memorial stating: "Initially, Qin was granted the Water virtue; now Han has received it. Following the cycle of succession, Han should possess the Earth virtue. The manifestation of the Earth virtue is the appearance of a yellow dragon. Therefore, we should reform the calendar and change the imperial colors; yellow should be favored." At that time, Chancellor Zhang Cang was fond of music theory and the calendar; he believed Han to be the beginning of the Water virtue, hence the Yellow River's breach at Jindi was its omen. The year should begin in the tenth month of winter; the color would be black on the outside and red within, corresponding to the virtue. Therefore, Gongsunchen's statement is incorrect. The proposal was rejected. Three years later, a yellow dragon appeared in Chengji. Emperor Wen summoned Gongsunchen and appointed him as a Doctor of Literature, instructing him together with the scholars to draft proposals for reforming the calendar and imperial colors. That summer, an edict was issued stating: "The divine manifestation of a strange creature appeared in Chengji; it caused no harm to the people and brought a year of good harvest. I have offered sacrifices at the suburban altars to Heaven and various deities; the officials in charge of rites should deliberate, without concealing anything that would trouble me." The officials all said: "In ancient times, the Son of Heaven personally performed sacrifices at the summer suburban altar to worship Heaven there; hence it is called 'jiao' (suburban sacrifice)." Thus, in the fourth month of summer, Emperor Wen first performed a suburban sacrifice at the five altars in Yongzhou; he wore red garments.

## 45

The following year, Xin Yuanping of Zhao presented himself to the emperor by observing atmospheric signs and said: "To the northeast of Chang'an there is a divine aura appearing in five colors, resembling a person wearing an official headdress. Some say that the northeast is where eastern deities dwell and the northwest is where western deities are buried." This heavenly omen indicates we should establish a shrine to Heaven, in order to align with these signs." Thus, the Wuyang Temple of the Five Emperors was built; all under one roof, with a single hall for each emperor. Each side had five gates, colored according to the respective emperor's virtue. The offerings and rituals used at this shrine were also like those of the five altars in Yong.

## 46

In the fourth month of summer, Emperor Wu personally paid homage at the confluence of Ba and Wei rivers to perform a suburban sacrifice for the Five Emperors in Wuyang. The temple of the Five Emperors faced south toward the Wei River and was connected to the Puchi Gou waterway in the north. When sacrificial fires were lit, their flames seemed to shine brightly up into the sky. Thus, Xin Yuanping was honored as a senior official and received rewards amounting to thousands of gold pieces. He also ordered the Doctors of Literature and scholars to study the Six Classics, compiling a system for royal governance and planning discussions on imperial tours, sacrifices at Mount Tai, and other rites.

## 47

Emperor Wen passed through Changmen Gate and saw five people on the northern side of the road; he then established an altar for the Five Emperors directly to the north, offering sacrifices with five sets of ritual offerings.

## 48

The following year, Xin Yuanping sent someone carrying a jade cup and submitted a memorial at the palace gate to present it. Ping claimed that the emperor said: "A divine aura of precious jade is coming from beneath the palace gates." After examining it, there was indeed a person presenting a jade cup, which was engraved with the words "Longevity for the Son of Heaven." Ping also claimed, "I have observed the sun shining twice at midday." After a short while, the sun did indeed appear to shine again at midday. Thus, he began to change the seventeenth year into the first year of the new era and ordered a grand public feast throughout the empire.

## 49

Ping said: "The Zhou Tripod was lost in the Si River. Now that the Yellow River has overflowed and connected with the Si, I have observed a golden aura of treasure to the northeast at Fenyin; could it be that the Zhou Tripod will emerge?" "If such an omen appears and is not welcomed, then it will not arrive." Thus, the emperor sent envoys to build a temple in the southern part of Fenyin by the riverbank, intending to perform sacrifices hoping for the emergence of the Zhou Tripod.

## 50

Someone submitted a memorial reporting that all the claims made by Xin Yuanping about divine signs and spirits were fabrications. The emperor ordered officials to investigate Xin Yuanping, who was later executed for his crimes. From then on, Emperor Wen became indifferent to reforming the calendar, imperial colors, and matters concerning divine signs. The temples of Wuyang and Changmen for the Five Emperors were managed by officials in charge of sacrifices, who offered seasonal rites without the emperor personally attending.

## 51

The following year, the Xiongnu repeatedly invaded the borders, prompting military forces to be mobilized for defense. In subsequent years, harvests were poor and yields low.

## 52

Several years later, Emperor Jing ascended the throne. For sixteen years, officials in charge of rituals continued to perform seasonal sacrifices as before, without any new initiatives, until the present Son of Heaven.

## 53

When the current emperor first ascended the throne, he was especially reverent toward ancestral and divine sacrifices.

## 54

In the first year, more than sixty years had passed since the founding of the Han dynasty. The empire was at peace and prosperity; scholars and officials all hoped that the Son of Heaven would perform the Fengshan sacrifice and reform the calendar system. The emperor favored Confucianism, recruiting virtuous and capable men. Zhao Wan and Wang Zang were appointed as high-ranking ministers for their literary talents, intending to discuss establishing a Mingtang (Hall of Illumination) in the southern part of the capital city to receive feudal lords. They had not yet completed their proposals on imperial tours, Fengshan sacrifices, calendar reform, and changes in court colors. At that time, Empress Dowager Dou, who favored Huanglao Daoist philosophy and disliked Confucianism, had agents secretly investigate Zhao Wan and others for corrupt dealings. When summoned to be investigated, Zhao Wan and Wang Zang committed suicide; all their proposed reforms were abandoned.

## 55

Six years later, Empress Dowager Dou passed away. The following year, scholars such as Gongsun Hong were summoned to court.

## 56

The next year, the current emperor made his first visit to Yongzhou and performed a suburban sacrifice at its five altars. Thereafter, he continued to perform such sacrifices every three years. At that time, the emperor sought a divine spirit and resided at Tishi Pavilion in Shanglin Park. The "Divine Lord" was a woman from Changling who, after the death of her son, claimed to have encountered spirits at the tomb of Empress Dowager Wanruo. The people of Wanruo built a shrine for her in their village, and many commoners went there to worship. Pingyuan Jun visited the shrine; afterward, his descendants became honored and prominent. When the current emperor ascended the throne, he offered generous honors and placed her shrine within the palace grounds. It was said that people heard her words but never saw her in person.

## 57

At this time, Li Shaojun also presented methods for altar worship, grain cultivation, and longevity to the emperor, who honored him highly. Li Shaojun had previously been a retainer of Marquis Shenze, in charge of alchemical formulas. He concealed his age and background, often claiming to be seventy years old, and said he could manipulate objects and ward off aging. He traveled widely among the feudal lords, promoting his alchemical methods. He had no wife or children. People heard that he could manipulate objects and achieve immortality, so they gave him gifts; as a result, he always had money, food, and clothing in abundance. Everyone believed that without managing any business or profession he was still wealthy; since no one knew where he came from, people became even more convinced and competed to serve him. Shaojun had a natural talent for alchemy and was skilled at making clever predictions that often proved accurate. Once, while drinking with Marquis Wu'an, there was an elderly man over ninety in attendance. Shaojun said he had once played and hunted with the old man's grandfather. The elder recalled as a child following his own grandfather to that very place and recognized it; everyone at the gathering was astonished. When Shaojun presented himself before the emperor, the emperor showed him an old bronze vessel and asked about it. Shaojun said: "This vessel was placed in the Baiming Hall during the tenth year of Duke Huan of Qi's reign." Later, when they examined its inscription, it indeed proved to be a vessel from Duke Huan of Qi. The entire palace was astounded and regarded Shaojun as a divine being, believing him to be hundreds of years old.

## 58

Shaojun told the emperor: "By worshiping the stove god, one can summon objects. Once summoned, cinnabar sand can be transformed into gold; when golden vessels are used for food and drink, longevity increases. With increased longevity, the immortal beings of Penglai in the eastern seas may appear. If one sees them and performs the Fengshan sacrifice, they will achieve immortality—this was how Emperor Huangdi did it." I once traveled by the sea and met Anqi Sheng, who ate large dates as big as melons. Anqi Sheng was an immortal who resided in Penglai; he would appear to people when conditions were right, and vanish otherwise." Thus, the Son of Heaven began personally worshiping the stove god and sent alchemists to sail into the sea in search of Anqi Sheng and others on Penglai. At this time, people started experimenting with cinnabar, sand, and various medicinal ingredients to transform them into gold.

## 59

After some time, Li Shaojun fell ill and died. The Son of Heaven believed he had transformed into an immortal and did not die, so he ordered Huang Chui and Shi Kuanshu to study his alchemical methods. The search for Anqi Sheng on Penglai yielded no results, yet many eccentric alchemists from Yan and Qi in the coastal regions continued to come forward with tales of divine matters.

## 60

Bo native Miou Ji recommended a shrine for Taiyi, saying: "The most honored celestial deity is Taiyi; the assistants of Taiyi are the Five Emperors. "In ancient times, the Son of Heaven offered sacrifices to Taiyi at the southeast suburban altar in spring and autumn, using a grand sacrifice (tai lao) for seven days. An altar was built with eight passageways opened as spiritual paths." Thus, the Son of Heaven ordered the Grand Priest to establish a shrine for Taiyi in the southeast suburbs of Chang'an, where sacrifices were regularly offered according to Miou Ji's methods. Later, someone submitted a memorial stating: "In ancient times, the Son of Tianzi offered sacrifices with a grand sacrifice to the Three Ones—Tianyi, Diyi, and Taiyi—once every three years." The Son of Heaven approved this proposal and ordered the Grand Priest to oversee these sacrifices at Miou Ji's Taiyi altar, following those methods. Later, another person submitted a memorial stating: "In ancient times, the Son of Heavens regularly performed sacrificial rites in spring to release burdens; sacrifices for Huangdi used one broken mirror and an owl. For Mingyang, a sheep was offered as sacrifice; For Mahang, one green male horse was used; for Taiyi and Dizhang of Zeshanjun, a bull was offered; for Wuyijun, dried fish were used; and for the Yin-Yang Envoys, one bull was offered." The emperor ordered the officials in charge of sacrifices to follow these methods and perform the rites at a shrine near Miou Ji's Taiyi Altar.

## 61

Later, in the imperial park there were white deer; their hides were used as offerings to manifest auspicious signs and thus white gold was produced.

## 62

The following year, during a sacrifice at Yongzhou's suburban altar, a one-horned beast was captured, resembling a qiao. The officials said: "Your Majesty has sincerely observed the suburban sacrifices; Heaven has responded by bestowing a one-horned creature, which is presumably a qilin." Therefore, it was presented at the five altars of Yongzhou, with an additional bull added to each altar for sacrificial burning. White gold was also granted to feudal lords; these signs and omens harmonized perfectly with Heaven's will.

## 63

At this time, King Jibei believed that the Son of Heaven was about to perform the Fengshan sacrifice. He therefore submitted a memorial offering Mount Tai and its surrounding towns; the emperor compensated him with other counties instead. King Changshan had committed a crime and was exiled; the Son of Heaven granted his younger brother the fief at Zhen Ding to continue the ancestral rites, and converted Changshan into a commandery. After this, all Five Sacred Mountains came under imperial jurisdiction (commanderies).

## 64

The following year, Shao Weng of Qi presented his methods for communicating with spirits and ghosts to the emperor. The emperor had a favored concubine, Lady Wang; after her death, Shao Weng claimed that by night he could summon the image of Lady Wang and the stove spirit. The Son of Heaven observed them from behind a curtain. Thus, Shao Weng was appointed as General Wen Cheng and received many rewards and gifts; he was treated with the courtesy due to a guest. Wen Cheng said: "If Your Majesty wishes to communicate with spirits, the palace and your attire do not resemble those of the divine; otherwise, spiritual beings will not come." Therefore, they made a chariot decorated with painted clouds and on appropriate days drove it to ward off evil spirits. They also built the Ganshan Palace, in which a central pavilion was constructed. The walls were painted with images of Heaven, Earth, Taiyi and various deities and spirits; sacrificial implements were placed there to summon heavenly gods. After more than a year, the efficacy of his methods gradually declined, and no divine beings appeared. Then he wrote messages on silk cloth and fed them to a bull, pretending not to know about it, saying that there was something extraordinary in the bull's stomach. After killing the bull and examining its abdomen, they found the silk cloth; the writing on it contained very strange messages. The Son of Heaven recognized his handwriting, questioned him, and indeed found it to be a forgery. Thus, General Wen Cheng was executed in secret.

## 65

After that, they built structures such as the Bailing Tower, copper pillars, and figures of immortals holding the Chenglu nectar.

## 66

The year after Wen Cheng's death, the Son of Heaven fell seriously ill at Dinghu Palace; he summoned every kind of shaman and physician available, but his condition did not improve. You Shui Fa Gen reported that there was a shaman in Shangjun who had fallen ill and through whom spirits and deities descended. The emperor summoned him and established an altar for his rites at Ganshan. When the illness persisted, he sent people to consult the Divine Lord. The Divine Lord said: "Your Majesty need not worry about your illness. It will soon improve; you must make an effort to meet with me at Ganshan." Thus, the emperor recovered and rose from his sickbed. He went in person to Ganshan, where he found that his illness had indeed vanished. A general amnesty was declared, and a temple for the Divine Lord of Longevity was established. Among those honored at the Temple of Longevity, the most exalted deity was Taiyi; his attendants included Da Jin and Si Ming. All followed him. He could not be seen directly, but one could hear his voice, which was indistinguishable from that of an ordinary person. At times he would appear and at times disappear; when he arrived, the wind would suddenly become still and solemn. He resided within a curtained chamber. Sometimes he spoke during the day, but usually at night. The Son of Heaven would undergo purification rites before entering. A shaman served as the host, overseeing food and drink offerings. Thus, his words were carried out by those below. They also established the Temple of Longevity and the Northern Palace, raised feathered banners, arranged sacrificial offerings, to honor the Divine Lord with ritual. What the Divine Lord said was recorded by imperial envoys who transcribed his words; this collection of writings was called "Hua Fa." What he said were things known to the common world, nothing extraordinary or unique; yet alone among them, the Son of Heaven took special delight in it. The matter was kept secret, and no one in later generations knew about it.

## 67

Three years later, the officials stated that the beginning of an era should be based on heavenly omens and auspicious signs, not merely numbered as one or two. The first era was named "Jian," the second, marked by a long comet, was called "Guang," and the third, following the capture of a one-horned beast at the suburban sacrifice, was termed "Shou."

## 68

The following winter, the Son of Heaven performed a sacrifice at Yongzhou. The court discussed: "Now that I have personally offered sacrifices to Heaven, but Earth has not been honored with rites; this would be an incomplete expression of ritual propriety." The officials, together with the Grand Historian and the official in charge of sacrifices, Kuanshu, discussed: "The sacrificial animals for Heaven and Earth should have horns as small as silkworm cocoons or young chestnuts. Now that Your Majesty is personally offering sacrifices to Hou Tu, it would be appropriate to construct five round altars in the marshland for Hou Tu. Each altar should have one yellow bull as a grand sacrifice. After the rites are completed, all offerings must be buried entirely; and those accompanying the ceremony should wear yellow robes." Thus, the Son of Heaven proceeded eastward and established a temple for Hou Tu on the Shui Hill in Fenyin, as proposed by Kuanshu and others. The emperor personally gazed toward the altar and paid homage, following the same rites used for Heaven. After completing the ceremony, the Son of Heaven traveled to Yingyang before returning. Passing through Luoyang, he issued an edict: "The Three Dynasties are long gone; they are distant and difficult to preserve. Therefore, I shall grant thirty li of land to the descendants of Zhou as the Lord Zhounanjun, so that they may continue their ancestral rites." In this year, for the first time, the Son of Heaven began touring the commanderies and counties, gradually approaching Mount Tai.

## 69

That spring, Marquis Yuecheng submitted a memorial concerning Luan Da. Luan Da was a native of Jiaodong Palace; he had previously studied with General Wen Cheng under the same teacher, and later became an official in charge of alchemy for King Jiaodong. Marquis Yuecheng's elder sister was Queen Dowager of Wangwang, who had no children. After King Wangwang died, a son of one of his concubines succeeded to the throne as king. However, Queen Dowager Kang had immoral conduct and did not get along with the new king; they threatened each other with legal charges. Queen Dowager Kang, having heard of Wen Cheng's death and wishing to ingratiate herself with the emperor, sent Luan Da through Marquis Yuecheng to request an audience in order to present his methods. After executing General Wen Cheng, the Son of Heaven regretted his early death and lamented that his methods had not been fully revealed. Upon meeting Luan Da, he was greatly pleased. Luan Da was tall and handsome; he spoke eloquently with many strategies, and boldly made grandiose claims without hesitation. He declared: "I have often traveled to the sea, where I met Anqi and Xianmen." They regarded me as lowly and did not believe me. Moreover, they considered King Wangwang merely a feudal lord, unworthy of receiving my methods. I repeatedly advised King Wangwang, but he did not heed me either. My master said: 'Gold can be transmuted, breaches in the Yellow River can be sealed, elixirs of immortality can be obtained, and immortal beings can be summoned.' Yet I fear that if I suffer the same fate as Wen Cheng, all alchemists will keep their mouths shut and dare not speak of their methods!" The emperor said: "Wen Cheng merely died from eating poisoned liver. If you can truly perfect your methods, what is there that I would not give!" Luan Da said: "My master did not seek anyone; it was others who sought him out. If Your Majesty truly wishes to summon him, you must honor his envoys highly, grant them family ties, treat them with the courtesy due guests, and not belittle them. Let each envoy wear a seal of authority; only then can they serve as intermediaries between mortals and immortals." Whether divine beings would still be willing to descend remains uncertain. Only by showing the utmost respect for their envoys can they possibly be persuaded to appear." Thus, the emperor ordered tests of Luan Da's minor methods; he demonstrated a game of chess in which pieces moved and struck each other on their own.

## 70

At this time, the emperor was greatly concerned about breaches in the Yellow River and had not yet succeeded in transmuting gold; thus he appointed Luan Da as General Wuli. After more than a month, he received four seals: those of the Heavenly Earth General, the Earthly Earth General, and the Datong General. The emperor issued an imperial decree to the Grand Historian: "In ancient times, Yu dredged the Nine Jiang rivers and regulated the Four Dus. Recently, the Yellow River has overflowed onto dry land, and embankments have been constantly under repair. For twenty-eight years that I have ruled over all under Heaven, it seems as if Heaven has sent me a man of talent who can achieve great success. The Book of Changes says, 'A flying dragon,' and the Odes say, 'The goose gradually arrives at the riverbank'; I believe this man may be comparable to those omens. Therefore, bestow a fief of two thousand households upon General Dishi Da as Lord Letong." He was granted the residence and honors befitting a marquis, along with one thousand attendants. Imperial carriages, horses, tents, and various ceremonial objects were provided to fill his household. The emperor also married him to Princess Weichang, bestowing ten thousand catties of gold upon them; the name of his fief was changed to Gongzhu Dangli. The Son of Heaven personally visited General Wuli's residence. Envoys sent to inquire after him and provide supplies were constantly traveling along the roads. From the principal princesses, generals, and ministers down, all set up banquets at his home, presenting gifts and offerings to him. At this time, the Son of Heaven also carved a jade seal inscribed "Tian Dao General," and sent an envoy dressed in feathered robes to stand on white reeds at night. General Wuli similarly wore feathered robes and stood on white reeds to receive the seal, thereby demonstrating that he was not subject to imperial authority. The one who wore the "Tian Dao" seal would serve as an intermediary between the Son of Heaven and the gods of Heaven. Thus, General Wuli often conducted night sacrifices at his home in hopes of summoning divine beings to descend. Though the gods had not yet arrived, many lesser spirits gathered; however, he was somewhat able to control them. Later, he prepared for a journey and traveled eastward into the sea in search of his master. After several months, Luan Da had been awarded six seals and his prestige shook the entire realm; in the coastal regions of Yan and Qi, no one failed to grasp their wrists and claim they possessed secret methods for attaining immortality.

## 71

In the sixth month of that summer, a shaman named Jin from Fenyin, while performing rites at a civilian shrine near the Hou Tu altar in Weishui, noticed an earth formation shaped like a hook. When he dug it up, he found a tripod. This tripod was greatly different from ordinary tripods; its engravings were intricate but bore no maker's mark or inscription. Finding this strange, he reported it to the officials. The official informed Tai Sheng, the governor of Hedong Commandery; Tai Sheng then submitted a report to the imperial court. The Son of Heaven sent envoys to investigate and confirm that the shaman had not used trickery in obtaining the tripod. Then, with proper rites, it was honored at a shrine; the tripod was brought to Ganshan Palace along with the imperial procession and presented as an offering. When they reached Zhongshan, it became warm and sunny, and a yellow cloud formed over the tripod like a canopy. A qiao passed by; the emperor personally shot it with his bow and used its body for sacrifice. Upon arriving at Chang'an, all the high officials, ministers, and scholars discussed and petitioned to honor the tripod as a sacred treasure. The Son of Heaven said, "Recently, the Yellow River overflowed and for several years there have been poor harvests; therefore, I toured to perform sacrifices to Hou Tu, praying that grain may be produced in abundance for the people. Now this year has yielded a bountiful harvest before any report could even be made—why then did the tripod appear?" The officials all said, "We have heard that in ancient times Emperor Tai created a single sacred tripod; the number one signifies unity and represents the ultimate connection of heaven, earth, and all things. Huangdi made three precious tripods, symbolizing Heaven, Earth, and Humanity. Yu collected the metal from nine regions and casted the Nine Tripods. All of them were once used to cook sacrifices for the gods and spirits of Heaven and Earth. When a sage arose, they would flourish; thus, the tripods passed from Xia to Shang. As Zhou virtue declined and the sacred altar of Song was destroyed, the tripod disappeared and remained hidden without being seen. The Ode says: "From hall to foundation, from sheep to ox; Great tripods and small cauldrons, neither boastful nor arrogant—this is the virtue of longevity." Now that the tripod has arrived at Ganshan, it shines with a lustrous transformation like a dragon, bearing boundless auspiciousness. In harmony with this in Zhongshan, yellow and white clouds descended as a canopy; like an animal appearing as an omen, the bow was drawn and arrows shot, gathering at the altar below to offer great sacrifices. Only those who received their mandate to become emperor truly understand its meaning and harmonize with virtue. The tripod should be presented before the ancestors, enshrined in the imperial court, to align with this clear divine response." An edict was issued: "Approved."

## 72

Those who sailed into the sea in search of Penglai claimed that it was not far away, yet could never reach it—perhaps because they failed to see its aura. The emperor then dispatched an official skilled in observing atmospheric signs to watch for the aura of Penglai.

## 73

That autumn, the Son of Heaven visited Yong and was about to perform a suburban sacrifice. Some said, "The Five Emperors were assistants to Taiyi; therefore, Taiyi should be enshrined and the Son of Heaven himself should personally perform a suburban sacrifice to him." The emperor was uncertain and had not yet decided. Gongsun Qing, a native of Qi, said, "This year we obtained the sacred tripod. On the first day of winter (December 21, 110 BC), it was also the winter solstice, just as in the time of Huangdi." Qing had a written record stating: "Huangdi obtained the sacred tripod at Wanqu and consulted Guiyu Qu. Guiyu Qu replied: 'Huangdi received the sacred tripod and a divine staff. In that year, on the first day of winter solstice (December 21), he grasped the pattern of Heaven; thus began an eternal cycle.' Thus, Huangdi welcomed the sun and used his staff to calculate time. Afterwards, every twenty years he would again encounter a winter solstice on the first day of the month; after twenty such cycles, spanning 380 years, Huangdi ascended as an immortal to Heaven." Qing therefore asked Suozhong to submit this report. Suozhong examined the document and found it unorthodox, suspecting that it was fabricated. He replied in refusal: "The matter of the sacred tripod has already been decided; what more is there to do!" Qing then submitted his report through a favored court official. The emperor was greatly pleased and summoned Qing for questioning. Qing replied, "I received this book from Shengong Shen; however, Master Shen has already died." The emperor asked, "Who was Master Shen?" Qing said, "Master Shen was a native of Qi. He associated with Anqi Sheng and received teachings about Huangdi; he had no written records, only this book on the tripod." He said, 'The Han dynasty's rise marks a return to the time of Huangdi.' He also said, "The sage of the Han will be among Gaozu's grandson or great-grandson. When the sacred tripod appears and communicates with divine powers, it is time for the Fengshan sacrifices." Among the seventy-two kings who performed the Fengshan rites, only Huangdi was able to ascend Mount Tai and perform the ceremony. Master Shen said: "The ruler of Han should also ascend Mount Tai for the Fengshan sacrifice; by doing so, he will become an immortal and ascend to Heaven. In Huangdi's time, there were ten thousand feudal lords, and among them, seven thousand received divine blessings for their sacrifices. There are eight famous mountains in the world; three of them lie beyond the borders among the southern and western tribes, while five are within China proper. Among these five mountains in China—Huashan, Shoushan, Taishi, Taishan, and Donglai—they were the ones where Huangdi often traveled and met with spirits. Huangdi fought wars while also learning to become an immortal. He was concerned about the common people who did not follow the right path, so he punished those who denied gods and spirits by beheading them. After more than a hundred years, he finally achieved communion with divine powers. Huangdi performed a suburban sacrifice to the God of Heaven at Yong and stayed there for three months. Guiyu Qu, whose title was Dahong, died and was buried in Yong; this is the so-called Hongzhong. After that, Huangdi convened ten thousand spirits at Mingting. Mingting refers to Ganshan. What is called Huanmen is actually the valley's mouth. Huangdi mined copper from Shoushan and casted a tripod at the foot of Jingshan Mountain. After the tripod was completed, a dragon descended with its beard hanging down to welcome Huangdi. Huangdi rode upon it; more than seventy of his ministers and concubins followed him up, and the dragon ascended into the heavens. The lesser officials who could not ride were left behind; they all grasped at the dragon's beard, but it was torn out and fell to the ground, along with Huangdi's bow. The people looked up as Huangdi ascended to Heaven; they embraced his bow and the dragon's beard in wailing grief. Hence, later generations named that place Dinghu, and called his bow "Wuhao."'" Hearing this, the Son of Heaven said: "Alas! If I could truly become like Huangdi, I would regard leaving behind my wife and children as easily as discarding a worn-out shoe." He then appointed Qing as an imperial attendant and sent him eastward to await divine signs at Taishi Mountain.

## 74

The emperor proceeded to perform a suburban sacrifice in Yong, reaching Longxi and ascending Kungtong Mountain before visiting Ganshan. He ordered the official in charge of rites, Kuanshu and others, to prepare an altar for Taiyi. The design of this altar was modeled after the Taixi Taiyi Altar, with three concentric enclosures. The altars of the Five Emperors surrounded it below, each placed according to its directional association; Huangdi's altar was located in the southwest. Eight paths were cleared for spirits to pass through. The offerings used at the Taiyi altar were identical to those of one zhi altar in Yong, with additional items such as sweet wine, dates, and dried meat. A single black bull was sacrificed to prepare the ritual vessels and sacrificial food. The Five Emperors received only the ritual dishes, sacrificial vessels, and sweet wine as offerings. Below were four areas of land for offering sacrifices to the spirits accompanying the gods and to the Northern Dipper. After the sacrifice, all remaining offerings were burned in a ritual fire. The sacrificial bull was white; a deer stood among it, and pigs were placed within the deer's enclosure, with water poured over them until they were soaked. For sacrifices to the sun, an ox was used; for sacrifices to the moon, a sheep and a pig or boar were offered. The priest and slaughterer of Taiyi wore purple robes with embroidered patterns. Each of the Five Emperors was associated with a specific color; the sun was red, and the moon white.

## 75

On the first day of winter, which also coincided with the winter solstice on Xinsi day (December 21) of the eleventh month, at dawn before full daylight, the Son of Heaven began his suburban sacrifice by paying homage to Taiyi. When offering sacrifices to the sun in the morning and to the moon in the evening, he performed a bowing gesture; and paid homage to Taiyi according to the ritual of the suburban sacrifice at Yong. The official offering the sacrifice declared: "Heaven first bestowed the sacred tripod and divine staff upon the emperor; the cycle of months begins again, ending only to begin anew. The Son of Heaven respectfully bows in homage." He wore yellow on top. The altar was filled with torches, and cooking utensils were placed around it for the sacrificial meals. The officials reported: "There is a light above the altar." Ministers and high officials said, "The Son of Heaven first performed a suburban sacrifice to Taiyi at Yunyang; the officials presented jade offerings and choice sacrificial animals for the ritual. That night, there was a beautiful light, and by daybreak, yellow vapor rose to connect with Heaven." The Grand Historian, Kuanshu of the Rites Office, and others said: "This divine blessing and auspicious omen indicate Heaven's favor; we should therefore establish a grand Taiyi altar at this site to clarify its meaning. Let the Grand Priest oversee it, performing regular sacrifices in autumn and during the winter solstice. The Son of Heaven should perform this suburban sacrifice once every three years."

## 76

That autumn, in preparation for a campaign against the Nanyue kingdom, he made offerings and prayers to Taiyi. He had a banner decorated with male holly, painted depictions of the sun, moon, Northern Dipper, and ascending dragon to symbolize Taiyi and the Three Stars. This was called "Lingqi," or the divine flag. When praying for military success, the Grand Historian would hold up the banner and point it toward the enemy state to be attacked. Meanwhile, General Wuli, who was sent on a mission, dared not go to sea but instead went to Mount Tai for worship. The emperor sent people to follow and investigate; in reality, nothing miraculous was found. General Wuli falsely claimed that he had seen his master, but all the methods he presented were exhausted and mostly ineffective. The emperor then executed General Wuli.

## 77

That winter, Gongsun Qing waited for divine signs in Henan and claimed to have seen the footprints of an immortal on Guoshi City Wall. He also saw a creature resembling a pheasant moving back and forth atop the wall. The Son of Heaven personally visited Guoshi City to inspect the footprints. He asked Qing, "Are you trying to imitate Wencheng or General Wuli?" Qing replied, "Immortals do not seek the favor of sovereigns; it is the sovereign who seeks them. Unless a generous and lenient attitude is adopted toward their ways, the spirits will not come. Matters concerning deities often seem remote or absurd; they can only be achieved after many years of accumulation." As a result, each commandery and kingdom cleared roads and renovated palaces, pavilions, famous mountains, and shrines dedicated to deities in anticipation of the emperor's visit.

## 78

That spring, after the Nanyue Kingdom had been conquered, the emperor favored a court official named Li Yannian for his musical talents. 隆重 The emperor liked it and summoned the Grand Commanders and ministers to deliberate, saying: "Even in common people's ancestral worship there are still dances and music. Now, for sacrifices at the suburbs without music, is this appropriate?" The Grand Commanders and ministers said: "In ancient times, sacrifices to Heaven and Earth were all accompanied by music, and thus the deities could be properly honored." Some said: "The Supreme Emperor once had Su Nu, a celestial maiden, play the fifty-stringed se. Her music was so sorrowful that the emperor could not stop himself from grieving; thus, he ordered her se to be broken into one with twenty-five strings." At that time, after pacifying the Nanyue region, sacrifices and prayers were made to Taiyi and Hou Tu. Music and dance were first introduced into these rituals. More singers were recruited, and from then on, the twenty-five-stringed se and instruments such as konghou, qin, and se came into use.

## 79

The following winter, the emperor remarked: "In ancient times, one first must rally troops and settle military campaigns before proceeding with the Fengshan sacrifices." He then proceeded north on an inspection tour to Suofang, mustering more than 100,000 troops. On his return journey, he paid homage at the tomb of the Yellow Emperor on Qiaoshan Mountain and disbanded the army at Xuru. The emperor said: "I have heard that the Yellow Emperor did not die; now there is a tomb, why is this so?" Someone replied: "The Yellow Emperor had already ascended to heaven as an immortal; his ministers buried only his clothing and hat." After arriving at Ganquan, in preparation for the upcoming ceremonies on Mount Tai, he first performed a classification sacrifice to Taiyi.

## 80

Since obtaining the precious tripod, the emperor discussed with his ministers and scholars about performing the Fengshan sacrifices. The rites and ceremonies for the Fengshan sacrifices were rare, long-forgotten, and no one knew their exact forms. The Confucian scholars therefore collected references from texts such as the Shangshu (Book of Documents), Zhouguan (Rites of Zhou), and Wangzhi (Institutes of the King) regarding the expected rituals for worship and sacrificial oxen. Ding Gong, a native of Qi who was over ninety years old, said: "The Fengshan sacrifices are about uniting the name with immortality. "The First Emperor of Qin could not ascend to perform the sacrifice; if Your Majesty is determined to do so, you should proceed gradually. If there are no winds or rains during the ascent, then you may complete the Fengshan ceremony on the summit." The emperor therefore ordered the Confucian scholars to practice the ritual of sacrificing oxen and draft the procedures for the Fengshan ceremonies. After several years, preparations were nearly complete. The Son of Heaven, having heard the words of Gongsun Qing and the alchemists, learned that from Huangdi onward, those who performed the Fengshan sacrifices all encountered strange phenomena and supernatural powers. He wished to emulate Huangdi in connecting with divine immortals on Penglai Mountain and surpass the world by matching the virtue of the Nine Emperors; he therefore adopted some Confucian doctrines to provide a literary basis for these endeavors. The Confucian scholars, already unable to clarify the details of the Fengshan rituals, were further constrained by their adherence to classical texts like the Shijing and Shangshu and could not offer bold or creative suggestions. The emperor displayed the ritual vessels for the Fengshan sacrifices to the Confucian scholars. Some of them said, "These do not conform to ancient practices." Xu Yan added that "the students and officials at Taichang were less skilled in performing rituals than those from Lu," while Zhou Ba submitted a diagram illustrating the Fengshan rites. As a result, the emperor dismissed Xu Yan and Zhou Ba and completely abandoned the use of all Confucian scholars.

## 81

In March, he proceeded east to Guoshi and performed rites at Zhongyue Taishi Mountain. The officials accompanying the emperor on the mountain heard as if someone were saying, "Ten thousand years!" They asked the emperor about it, but he said nothing. They inquired among those below, and they also remained silent. Therefore, he bestowed a fief of 300 households to maintain the shrine at Taishi Mountain, naming it Chonggao Yi. He then traveled eastward up Mount Tai. At that time, the grass and trees on Mount Tai had not yet sprouted leaves; he therefore ordered people to place stones at the summit of Mount Tai.

## 82

The emperor then proceeded eastward for an inspection tour along the coast, performing rites and sacrifices to the Eight Deities. Thousands of people from Qi submitted memorials speaking about divine omens or strange methods, but none proved effective. He then dispatched even more ships and ordered thousands of people who claimed to know about the divine mountains in the sea to search for immortals on Penglai Island. Gongsun Qing, holding an imperial token of authority, often traveled ahead to await divine mountains. When he arrived at Donglai, he claimed that one night he saw a giant figure several zhang tall; when he approached it, the figure vanished, but its footprints were very large and resembled those of some kind of beast or bird. Some court officials claimed to have seen an old man leading a dog, who said, "I wish to see the great official," but then suddenly disappeared. The emperor saw the large footprints, but did not believe them at first; however, when the court officials mentioned an old man, he greatly believed it to be a divine immortal. He stayed by the sea for some time, providing alchemists with carriages and sending thousands of secret envoys to search for immortals.

## 83

In April, he returned to Fenggao. The emperor reflected that the Confucian scholars and alchemists each gave different accounts of the Fengshan sacrifices, many of which were unorthodox and difficult to implement. The Son of Heaven arrived at Liangfu Mountain and performed rites and sacrifices to Di Zhu (the Earth Deity). On the Yimao day, he ordered Confucian scholars serving as attendants to wear pibian caps and girdles of rank, perform the ritual of shooting an ox, and carry out the ceremonies. He performed the Fengshan sacrifice at the eastern foot of Mount Tai in a manner similar to the suburban rites for worshiping Taiyi. The stone used for the Fengshan sacrifice was two chi wide and nine chi high, with jade tablets containing secret writings placed beneath it. After completing the rites, the Son of Heaven ascended Mount Tai alone with his attendants and the carriage attendant Zi Hou, who also performed a smaller Fengshan sacrifice. All details of these events were kept secret. The next day, he descended from the western side of Mount Tai through Yindao. On the Bingchen day, the Son of Heaven performed the Chanshan sacrifice at the northeastern base of Mount Tai on Susan Mountain, following a ritual similar to that for Hou Tu. The Son of Heaven personally performed all the bows and appearances in yellow robes, with music used throughout the entire ceremony. łoży Between the rivers Jiang and Huai, a single thatch mat with three ridges was prepared as an offering for the deities. The Fengshan sacrifice also incorporated five-colored earth of various kinds to symbolize the unification of all regions. He released rare beasts, flying birds, and white pheasants from distant lands, bestowing them with elaborate rites as offerings. Creatures such as sī cattle, rhinoceroses, and elephants were not used in the sacrifices. All of them arrived at Mount Tai to perform sacrifices to Hou Tu. The Fengshan and ancestral rites were performed; That night, there appeared to be a glow, and during the day, white clouds rose from the site of the sacrifice.

## 84

After returning from the Chanshan ceremony, the Son of Heaven sat in the Mingtang Hall, and his ministers took turns offering their congratulations. The emperor then issued an edict to the Grand Historian: "I, with my humble self, have inherited the supreme position; I am filled with anxiety and fear that I may not be worthy. My virtue is shallow, and I do not fully understand rites and music. I performed sacrifices to Taiyi, and strange lights and auspicious signs appeared as if in response. I was startled by the supernatural phenomena but dared not stop; thus, I ascended Mount Tai for the Fengshan sacrifice, reaching Liangfu Mountain before proceeding with the Chanshan rite at Susan Mountain. I have now renewed myself, and I wish to share this new beginning with the officials and scholars. Therefore, I grant each household of a hundred families one ox and ten dan of wine, and give two bolts of cloth and silk to those over eighty years old as well as orphans and widows. Bo, Fenggao, Sheqiu, and Licheng shall be exempt from this year's taxes and levies. I hereby issue a general pardon for all under heaven, following the decree of amnesty issued on the Yimao day. No further construction or restoration shall be carried out along the route I have traveled. Matters occurring more than two years ago shall not be investigated." He also issued another decree: "In ancient times, the Son of Heaven conducted a tour every five years and performed rituals at Mount Tai; feudal lords had designated places for lodging during their visits. Therefore, I order all feudal lords to establish guesthouses beneath Mount Tai."

## 85

After the emperor had completed the Fengshan sacrifice on Mount Tai without encountering any wind or rain disasters, the alchemists continued to claim that the divine mountains of Penglai and others might be attainable. Encouraged by this, the emperor was delighted at the possibility of meeting them and once again traveled eastward to the seashore in hopes of encountering Penglai. Zi Hou, the carriage attendant, suddenly fell ill and died within a single day. The emperor then departed directly along the coast, traveling north to Jieshi and touring from Liaoxi through the northern frontier regions all the way to Jiuyuan. In May, he returned to Ganquan. The officials reported that the precious tripod had appeared and was named Yuandong; therefore, this year was designated as the first year of Yuanfeng.

## 86

That autumn, a comet appeared in the constellation Dongjing. More than ten days later, another comet appeared in Saneng. Wang Shuo, an expert in observing atmospheric omens, said: "I observed a unique sight of the planet Tianxing emerging like a melon; after a meal's time it re-entered." The officials all said: "Your Majesty has established the Fengshan sacrifices of the Han dynasty; this must be a reward from Heaven in the form of an auspicious star."

## 87

The following winter, he performed the suburban sacrifice to the Five Emperors at Yong. On his return journey, he offered sacrifices and prayers to Taiyi in accordance with ritual. The sacrificial chant said: "The virtue star shines brightly, its radiance a sign of auspiciousness. "The longevity star continues to appear, its light profound and brilliant." "The trustworthy star is clearly visible; the Son of Heaven respectfully bows in homage to the offerings made by the Grand Priest."

## 88

That spring, Gongsun Qing claimed that he had seen a divine being on Donglai Mountain who said, "I wish to meet the Son of Heaven." The emperor therefore visited Guoshi City and appointed Qing as Zhongdawu (Middle Doctor). He then went to Donglai, staying there for several days, but saw nothing except the footprints of a giant. Once again, he sent thousands of alchemists to search for divine beings and strange phenomena as well as to gather medicinal herbs and fungi. This year was marked by a drought. As the emperor had already left without an announced itinerary, he made sacrifices and prayers at Wanli Sha and passed through Mount Tai for worship. On his return journey to Huzi, the emperor personally inspected the river embankment where a breach had occurred and stayed for two days before performing a sacrifice by casting offerings into the water and then departing. He ordered two high-ranking officials to lead troops in repairing the breached river, redirecting two channels back to their original courses as recorded during Yu's time.

## 89

At this time, after the two Yue states had been conquered, a native named Yong Zhi from Yue said: "The Yue people are superstitious and believe in ghosts; their shrines allow them to see ghosts, and these rituals often yield results. "In the past, King Dongou revered ghosts and lived to be 160 years old." "Later generations became careless and disrespectful, which is why they declined and weakened." The emperor then ordered Yue shamans to establish shrines for the Yue ancestral deities, with altars but no ritual platforms. They also worshipped heavenly gods and a hundred ghosts, using chicken bones for divination. The emperor believed in this practice, and the Yue rituals involving chicken bone divination began to be used.

## 90

Gongsun Qing said: "Immortals can be seen, but the emperor always travels in haste, which is why he has not been able to see them. Now, Your Majesty could build an observatory like the one in Guocheng, place meat and dates there, and thus it would be appropriate to invite divine beings." "Moreover, immortals prefer to dwell in high towers." Thus, the emperor ordered that at Chang'an a Feilian Gui Pavilion be built and at Ganquan an Yiyanshou Pavilion be constructed, instructing Qing to hold a ceremonial offering with full preparations while waiting for divine beings. They then built the Tongtianjing Altar, placing sacrificial equipment beneath it in order to summon immortals and divine beings. At this time, a new front hall was built at Ganquan, marking the beginning of the expansion of various palace buildings. In summer, fungi sprouted inside the central chamber of the palace hall. After the emperor had the river blocked and built the Tongtian Altar, when he saw a light in the sky, he issued an edict: "Nine stems of fungi have grown inside the palace at Ganquan; therefore, I grant amnesty to all under heaven and forbid any further labor projects."

## 91

The following year, he launched a campaign against Chaoxian. In summer, there was a drought. Gongsun Qing said: "During the time of Huangdi, the heavens were dry when he performed the Fengshan sacrifices; it took three years for the soil to become moist enough for the sacrificial earth." The emperor then issued an edict saying: "There is a drought in the heavens. Could this be because of the dry Fengshan sacrifice?" Therefore, I order that all under heaven honor and worship the deity Lingxing."

## 92

The following year, the emperor performed a suburban sacrifice at Yong, opened up the Huizhong Road, and toured it. In spring, he arrived at Mingze and returned from Xhe River.

## 93

The following winter, the emperor toured Nanjun, reaching Jiangling before heading eastward. He climbed and performed rites at Tianzhushan in Qian, which was declared as Nanyue (the Southern Sacred Mountain). He sailed along the Yangtze River, departing from Xunyang through Zongyang and passing Penglai, performing rites at its famous mountains and rivers. He traveled north to Langya, reaching the coast along the sea. In the fourth month, he arrived at Fengao and began preparations for the Fengshan sacrifice.

## 94

Initially, when the emperor performed the Fengshan ceremony on Mount Tai, there was an ancient Mingtang (a grand hall for rituals) site at the northeast base of Mount Tai; however, it was located in a perilous and narrow area, not open or suitable. The emperor wished to construct a Mingtang hall near Fengao but had yet to understand its proper design and specifications. Gong Sudai from Jinan submitted a diagram of the Mingtang hall from the time of Huangdi. The diagram of the Mingtang showed one hall with no walls on any of its four sides, covered with thatch and connected by water channels. The circular palace was surrounded by a wall, with an elevated corridor for passage. There was also a tower above it; access to the hall was from the southwest entrance, which was named Kunlun. The emperor would enter through this gate in order to perform sacrifices and worship Heaven. Thus, the emperor ordered the construction of a Mingtang at Fengao on the Wei River, following the design in Dai's diagram. When the emperor conducted the Fengshan sacrifice five years later, he arranged for sacrifices to Taiyi and the Five Emperors on the upper platform of the Mingtang, with ancestral altars for Emperor Gaozu facing them. Sacrifices to Hou Tu were performed in the lower chamber using twenty taoliao (ritual offerings). The emperor entered through the Kunlun passage and for the first time paid homage in the Mingtang according to the ritual of a suburban sacrifice. After the ceremony, sacrificial offerings were burned beneath the hall. The emperor then ascended Mount Tai again and secretly performed sacrifices at its summit. At the foot of Mount Tai, altars were set up for the Five Emperors, each arranged according to their respective directions. Huangdi was placed together with Chidi, and officials attended the sacrifices accordingly. Fires were lit on the mountain top, and all below responded in kind.

## 95

Two years later, on the first day of the eleventh month, Jiazi (the winter solstice), those who calculated the calendar used this as a fundamental reference point. The emperor personally went to Mount Tai and on the first day of the eleventh lunar month, which coincided with Jiazi and the winter solstice, he worshipped Heaven at the Mingtang hall without performing the Fengshan sacrifices. The official offering prayer stated: "Heaven has bestowed upon Emperor Ta yuan shen ce (the great divine strategy), which cycles and repeats endlessly. "The emperor respectfully worships Taiyi." He traveled east to the sea, examining those who had entered the ocean and alchemists seeking divine beings; none proved effective. Nevertheless, he sent out even more people in hopes of encountering them.

## 96

On Yiyou day of the eleventh month (November 15, 129 BC), a fire broke out at Boliang. On Jiawu day of the twelfth month (December 1, 129 BC) the emperor personally performed the Chanshan ceremony at Gaoli and worshipped Hou Tu. Standing by Bohai Sea, he intended to perform distant sacrifices to Penglai and others in the hope of reaching a miraculous realm.

## 97

On his return journey, because of the Boliang fire, the emperor received reports from officials at Ganquan instead of in the capital court. Gongsun Qing said: "Huangdi built the Qingling Altar and, after twelve days of burning sacrifices there, he then constructed the Mingting (Hall of Clarity)." "The Mingting is Ganquan." Many alchemists claimed that ancient emperors had once established their capitals at Ganquan. Later, the emperor again received feudal lords at Ganquan, and guest houses for the feudal lords were built there. Yong Zhi then said: "Among Yue customs, when a fire occurs, the new house must be built larger than before in order to overcome and subdue the misfortune." Thus, they constructed Jianzhang Palace, designed with a scale of ten thousand households and a thousand gates. The front hall was planned to be as tall as Weiyang Palace. To its east stood Fengque, over twenty zhang in height. To its west was Tangzhong, where tigers were kept in enclosures stretching for dozens of li. To the north, a large pool was constructed. Jiantai, over twenty zhang high, was named Taiye Chi (the Great Liquid Pool). Within it were Penglai, Fangzhang, Yingzhou, and Huliang, symbolizing divine mountains, turtles, and fish in the sea. To its south were Yuting (Jade Hall), Bimen (Jade Gate), and other structures associated with great birds. They then built Shenming Tai (Divine Light Altar) and Jinggan Lou (Well-Dry Tower), each over fifty zhang in height, connected by elevated roads.

## 98

In summer, the Han dynasty revised its calendar system, designating the first month as the beginning of the year. Yellow was adopted as the favored color; official titles and seals were changed to use five-character names, marking it as the first year of Taichu. In this year, a western campaign against Dayuan was launched. A massive locust plague occurred. Lady Ding, Luoyang's Yu Chu, and others performed ritual curses against the Xiongnu and Dayuan through their religious practices.

## 99

The following year, officials reported that at Yong's Five Altars there were no prepared sacrificial animals or mature offerings, and the fragrant items required for rituals were incomplete. The emperor then ordered ritual officials to provide calves, sacrificial animals, and offerings at the altars in accordance with the colors and foods associated with overcoming certain elements, using wooden dummy horses as substitutes for foals. Only during the May sacrifice were actual young horses used, as well as in the emperor's personal suburban rituals. For sacrifices at all famous mountains and rivers where foals had previously been required, wooden dummy horses were used instead. Only when the emperor passed through an area would actual foals be used for sacrifice. All other rituals remained as before.

## 100

The following year, the emperor traveled east along the coast to examine matters related to divine beings and immortals, but none proved effective. Some alchemists claimed: "During the time of Huangdi five cities and twelve towers were built to await divine beings at a fixed date, named Yingnian (Welcoming the Year)." The emperor approved their construction according to this plan and named it "Mingnian" (Next Year). The emperor personally performed rituals and worshipped Heaven.

## 101

Gong Sudai said: "Although Huangdi performed the Fengshan sacrifice on Mount Tai, it was actually Fenghou, Fengju, and Qibo who instructed Huangdi to perform the Feng ceremony at Dongtai Mountain and the Chanshan ritual at Fanshan. Only after uniting the divine symbols did he achieve immortality." The emperor had already ordered the preparation of sacrificial equipment and traveled to Dongtai Mountain. However, since Dongtai was small and did not match its reputation, he merely instructed ritual officials to perform ceremonies there without conducting the Fengshan sacrifices. Later, he ordered Dai to oversee sacrificial rites in search of divine beings and objects. In summer, the emperor returned to Mount Tai and performed the five-year sacrifice as before, adding a Chanshan ceremony at Shilu. Shilu was located to the south of the base of Mount Tai. Many alchemists claimed it to be a gate where immortals resided, so the emperor personally performed the ritual there.

## 102

Five years later, he returned once again to Mount Tai for the Fengshan sacrifice. On his return journey, he passed by Heng Mountain and performed a sacrifice there.

## 103

The temples established by the present emperor for worship include Taiyi and Hou Tu, with a personal suburban sacrifice held every three years. The Han dynasty's Fengshan rituals were initiated, and a major Fengshan ceremony was conducted once every five years. The worship of Taiyi, San Yi (Three Ones), Mingyang, Maxing, and Chixing—five deities—and the ritual officials under Kuanshu offered seasonal sacrifices according to set times. In total there were six shrines, all overseen by the Grand Priest (Tai Zhu). As for the Eight Gods and other deities, as well as temples such as Mingnian, Fanshan, and others with various names, sacrifices were performed only when the emperor passed by; once he left, no further rites were conducted. Temples established by alchemists were independently managed by their founders. When these individuals died, the shrines ceased to be maintained and were no longer overseen by official ritual officials. All other temples remained as they had been before. Since the emperor performed his Fengshan sacrifice, twelve years later he returned and paid homage to all Five Great Mountains and Four Major Rivers. However, the alchemists' efforts to await divine beings or search for Penglai by sailing into the sea ultimately proved fruitless. As for Gongsun Qing, who was sent to await divine beings, he still used the footprints of giants as an excuse, yet there were no real results. The emperor became increasingly weary and skeptical of the alchemists' strange and impractical claims, yet he still maintained a loose connection with them, hoping to encounter one who might be genuine. From this time onward, the number of alchemists speaking about divine shrines and spiritual matters increased even more, yet their effectiveness was clearly nonexistent. Grand Historian said: I followed the emperor on his tour to perform sacrifices at the shrines of Heaven and Earth, as well as at famous mountains and rivers, including the Fengshan ceremonies. I entered Shou Gong to serve at the shrine and listen to discussions about divine matters, thoroughly examining the intentions of alchemists and ritual officials. Thus, I withdrew and compiled a detailed account of all those who had been involved with spirits and deities since ancient times, clearly revealing their inner motives and outward appearances. Later generations of virtuous men will be able to peruse this account. As for the detailed arrangements regarding ritual vessels, jade offerings, and the etiquette of presenting and responding with wine, these are matters entrusted to the appropriate officials.
