# Biographies of Tian Shu

## 1

Tian Shu was a native of Jingcheng, Zhao. His ancestors were descendants of the Tian clan of Qi. Shu was fond of swords and studied the Huanglao school of thought under Master Yueju Gong. Shu was a man who took pride in his integrity and liked to associate with various officials. The people of Zhao recommended him to Zhao Xiang Zhao Wu, who spoke highly of Shu to King Zhang Ao of Zhao. King Zhao appointed him as a Langzhong (attendant-official). After several years, he remained upright, incorruptible, and fair. King Zhao regarded him as virtuous but had not yet promoted him.

## 2

At this time, Chen Xi rebelled in Dai. In the seventh year of the Han dynasty, Emperor Gaozu went to suppress him and passed through Zhao. King Zhang Ao of Zhao personally served food on a tray, showing great courtesy, but Emperor Gaozu sat with his legs spread out like a winnowing basket and scolded him. At that time, Zhao Xiang Zhao Wu and dozens of others were all angry. They said to King Zhang: "Your Majesty has already observed every courtesy toward the emperor. Now he treats you in this manner; we ministers request to rise in rebellion." King Zhao bit his finger until it bled and said, "My ancestors lost the state. Without His Majesty's grace, I and my ministers would have perished like insects." How could you say such things!" "Do not utter another word!" At this, Guan Gao and others said: "The king is a man of virtue; he will not betray kindness." They finally secretly conspired to assassinate the emperor. However, their plot was discovered before it could be carried out, and the Han court issued an edict to arrest King Zhao and his ministers who had conspired in rebellion. At this time, Zhao Wu and others all committed suicide, but only Guan Gao was arrested. At that time the Han court issued a decree: "Anyone from Zhao who dares to follow the king will be punished by the death of three generations." Only Meng Shu, Tian Shu and more than ten others wore yellow clothes, shaved their heads, and put on iron collars, claiming to be the king's slaves, and followed King Zhao Ao to Chang'an. After Guan Gao's case was cleared up, King Zhao Ao was released and demoted to the title of Duke Xuanping. He then recommended Tian Shu and more than ten other men. The emperor summoned them all for an audience, spoke with them, and found that none of the Han court ministers could surpass them. The emperor was pleased and appointed them all as governors of commanderies or ministers to feudal lords. Shu served as the governor of Hanzhong for more than ten years. At this time, Empress Dowager Gao died and the Lu clan rose in rebellion; the ministers suppressed them and enthroned Emperor Xiaowen.

## 3

After Emperor Xiaowen was enthroned, he summoned Tian Shu and asked him: "Do you know the most virtuous men in the empire?" Shu replied: "How could I be sufficient to know them!" The emperor said, "You are a man of virtue yourself and should know them." Shu bowed his head to the ground and said: "Former governor Meng Shu of Yunzhong is a man of virtue." At that time, Meng Shu had been dismissed from office because the Hu tribes had launched a large-scale raid into the frontier and committed banditry; Yunzhong was especially affected. The emperor said: "The late emperor had appointed Meng Shu as governor of Yunzhong for more than ten years, yet the Hu tribes once launched a raid and Meng Shu failed to hold out firmly, resulting in hundreds of soldiers being killed in battle without cause. Is it true that a man of virtue would kill so many people?" "Why then do you say Meng Shu is a man of virtue?" Shu kowtowed and replied: "This is precisely why Meng Shu is considered a man of virtue. When Guan Gao conspired to rebel, the emperor issued an explicit decree: anyone in Zhao who dared follow King Zhang would be punished by execution of three generations. Yet Meng Shu shaved his head, put on an iron collar, and followed King Zhang Ao wherever he went, willing to die for him. How could he have known that he would later become the governor of Yunzhong! When Han and Chu were at war, their soldiers became exhausted and worn out. At that time, the Xiongnu ruler Modu had just subdued the northern tribes and came to harass the borders. Meng Shu knew his soldiers were exhausted but could not bear to speak out; as a result, the soldiers volunteered to fight at the city walls against the enemy like sons for their father or brothers for their elder brother, which is why hundreds died. How could Meng Shu have deliberately driven them into battle!" This is precisely why Meng Shu deserves to be called a man of virtue." At this, the emperor said: "How virtuous Meng Shu is!" He was then recalled and reappointed as governor of Yunzhong.

## 4

Several years later, Shu lost his official post due to legal violations. Prince Xiao Wang of Liang had sent men to kill the former prime minister of Wu, Yuan Ang. Emperor Jingdi summoned Tian Shu to investigate in Liang, and he fully uncovered the matter before returning with a report. Emperor Jingdi asked: "Did Prince Xiao Wang of Liang do it?" Shu replied: "I commit a capital crime! He did." The emperor asked, "Where is the evidence of this matter?" Tian Shu said: "Your Majesty should not be concerned with matters in Liang. The emperor asked, "Why?" Shu said: "If Prince Xiao Wang of Liang is not punished for his crimes, it would mean the Han laws are not enforced; if he is executed according to law, then Empress Dowager will lose her appetite and be unable to sleep soundly. This worry lies with Your Majesty." Emperor Jingdi greatly admired him and appointed him as the prime minister of Lu.

## 5

When Tian Shu first arrived in Lu as prime minister, more than a hundred people came forward to accuse the prince of taking their property. Tian Shu selected twenty ringleaders among them and flogged each of them fifty times, while giving the rest twenty strokes each. He angrily said to them: "Is the prince not your lord? How dare you speak against your lord!" Prince of Lu, upon hearing this, was greatly ashamed and sent money from the central treasury to have the prime minister repay them. The prime minister said: "If Your Highness took it and now orders me to return it, this would make the prince appear wicked while I am seen as virtuous. I will not repay it on your behalf." Thus, the prince repaid them in full.

## 6

Prince of Lu enjoyed hunting, and the prime minister often accompanied him into the hunting grounds. Whenever this happened, the prince would have the prime minister rest in a pavilion while he went on with his hunt; when the prime minister returned, he would always sit outside the hunting ground waiting for the prince, regardless of the weather. The prince sent people several times to invite the prime minister to rest, but he never did so, saying: "My lord is exposed in the hunting grounds; how can I alone go indoors!" Because of this, Prince of Lu no longer went on large-scale hunting expeditions.

## 7

After several years, Shu died in office. The state of Lu offered a hundred gold pieces as an offering, but his youngest son Ren refused it, saying: "I will not allow a hundred gold to damage my ancestor's reputation."

## 8

Ren became a retainer of the Wei General due to his strength and vigor, and several times followed him in campaigns against the Xiongnu. The Wei General recommended Ren, who was then appointed as a Langzhong. After several years, he served as the chief clerk of a high-ranking official (erqianshi) under the prime minister before losing his post. Later on, he was sent to investigate and supervise the Three He commanderies. When the emperor made an eastern tour, Ren presented a report with eloquence that pleased him, and was appointed as the Commander of Jingfu. More than a month later, the emperor promoted him to the position of Sizhi (Inspector). After several years, he was implicated in an incident involving the crown prince. At that time, the Left Chancellor personally led troops and ordered Sizhi Tian Ren to be in charge of closing and guarding the city gates. For allowing the crown prince to escape, he was handed over to officials and executed. Ren mobilized his troops, but the commandery official Che Qianqiu of Changling reported a rebellion by Ren to the authorities, and thus Ren's entire family was executed. Jingcheng is now located in Zhongshan Kingdom.

## 9

The Grand Historian said: Confucius once remarked, "If one resides in a state, he must know its administration"—was it referring to Tian Shu! Loyalty did not forget virtue; the wise ruler's excellence was used to correct mistakes. Ren and I were good friends, so I have discussed them together.

## 10

Chu Xiansheng said: When I was a Langzhong, I heard that Tian Ren had once been on good terms with Ren An. Ren An was a native of Yingyang. He lost his father at an early age and was poor; he worked as a cart driver for others, traveling to Chang'an, where he stayed. He sought employment as a minor official but had no connections or opportunities, so he registered himself on the list of commoners. Wugong is a small county in the western border area of Fufeng; Gu Kou and Shudao are located near the mountains. An thought that Wugong was a small county with no powerful families, and it would be easy to rise in status. So he stayed there and took over the position of a minor official responsible for guarding a market town. Later he became a Tingzhang (a village head). When the people of the county went out hunting together, Ren An often divided up the deer, pheasants, and hares fairly among them. He arranged for the elderly and children to take their share first while the strong and vigorous took what was easier to obtain. Everyone was pleased and said: "There is no harm in this; Young Qing of Ren distributes things justly and has wisdom and strategy." The next day, they gathered again, with hundreds attending the meeting. Young Qing of Ren said: "Why hasn't Son Jia come?" Everyone was surprised at how quickly he recognized him. Later, he was appointed as Sanlao (a local elder), recommended for a position of close contact with the people, and then promoted to an official in charge of a district with a salary rank of three hundred dou, where he governed the people. He was dismissed from office for failing to properly prepare accommodations during an official tour.

## 11

He then became a retainer of General Wei, where he met Tian Ren; they both served as retainers and worked under the same office, united in heart and affection for one another. These two men were poor and had no money to offer the general's household supervisor; as a result, they were ordered to feed and care for sick or ill-treated horses. The two men shared the same bed, and Ren secretly said: "What an ignorant household supervisor!" Ren An replied: "Even the general does not know people well; how much less so this household supervisor!" General Wei, accompanied by these two men, visited Princess Pingyang. The princess's household ordered the two to sit at a table with the horse attendants; in response, the two men drew their swords and cut up the mat, sitting separately instead. Everyone in the princess's household was surprised and disliked this behavior but dared not scold them.

## 12

Later, an imperial decree was issued to select retainers of General Wei for appointment as Langzhong. The general selected those among the retainers who were wealthy and well-provided, ordering them to prepare saddled horses, crimson robes, and jade-hilted swords in preparation for a court presentation. At this time, the learned official Shaofu Zhao Yu came to visit General Wei; the general summoned the selected retainers and showed them to Zhao Yu. Zhao Yu questioned them in turn, but among more than ten men, not one was familiar with official matters or possessed wisdom and strategy. Zhao Yu said: "I have heard that beneath a general's gate there must be men of general-like qualities. The Classic says, 'To know the ruler, look at those he sends; to know a son, look at his friends.' Now that an imperial decree has been issued to recommend retainers of the general, it is intended to observe whether the general can select virtuous and capable men who are both learned and martial. Now you merely select the sons of wealthy families to present, yet they have no wisdom or strategy; it is like dressing wooden puppets in brocade and embroidery. What will be done about this?" At this, Zhao Yu summoned all more than a hundred retainers of General Wei and questioned them in turn. He found Tian Ren and Ren An suitable and said: "Only these two men are fit; the rest are not usable." General Wei saw that these two were poor and felt it was unfair. After Zhao Yu left, he told the two men: "Each of you should prepare a yue elephant crimson robe." The two replied together: "Our families are poor and have no such equipment." General Wei became angry and said, "Now that the two of you claim to be poor by birth, why do you say such words?" "You are haughty as if trying to shift virtue onto me. Why is this so?" The general had no choice but to submit their names for imperial knowledge. An imperial decree summoned the retainers of General Wei; these two men appeared before the emperor, who issued an edict to inquire about their abilities and strategies, ranking them accordingly. Tian Ren replied: "Holding the drumstick and standing at the gate of the army, making scholars and officers willing to die in battle—on this I am not as good as Ren An." Ren An replied: "To resolve doubts and suspicions, determine right from wrong, manage official affairs, and ensure the people have no grievances—on this I am not as capable as Ren." Emperor Wu laughed heartily and said: "Well said." He ordered Ren An to guard the Northern Army, and Tian Ren to oversee the border farmland and grain stores along the Yellow River. These two men thus established their reputations throughout the land.

## 13

Later, Ren An was appointed as Cishi of Yizhou, and Tian Ren became Chongxiang Zhangshi.

## 14

Tian Ren submitted a memorial stating: "Many of the commandery governors throughout the empire are corrupt and pursue unjust profits, especially in the Three He. I request to first investigate and report on the Three He." "The commandery governors of the Three He all rely on influential court favorites and are related to the Three Dukes; they have no fear or restraint. It is appropriate to first rectify the Three He as a warning to corrupt officials throughout the empire." At that time, the commandery governors of Henan and Hedong were brothers and nephews of Du, the Yushi Daifu; the governor of Hedong was a descendant of Shi, the Shishang. At that time, nine members of the Shi family held posts as Erqianshi officials; their power and prestige were at its height. Tian Ren repeatedly submitted memorials about this matter. Du, the Daifu, and members of the Shi family sent envoys to apologize, saying to Tian Shaoqing: "We did not dare to speak ill; we only hope that you will not falsely accuse us." After Tian Ren investigated the Three He, all three commandery governors were handed over to officials and executed. When Tian Ren returned to report his mission, Emperor Wu was pleased and praised him for being capable of not fearing powerful adversaries. He appointed Tian Ren as Chongxiang Sizhi, and Tian's authority reverberated throughout the empire.

## 15

Later, when the Crown Prince encountered military affairs, the Chancellor personally led troops and assigned the Sizhi to oversee the city gates. The Sizhi considered that the Crown Prince was a close blood relative, and did not wish to be too closely involved in matters between father and son. Thus he passed by the various mausoleums without stopping. At that time, Emperor Wu was at Ganshan and sent Yushi Daifu Pou Jun to reprimand the Chancellor: "Why did you allow the Crown Prince?" The Chancellor replied, saying, "I ordered the Sizhi to guard the city gates, but he allowed the Crown Prince passage." The matter was reported to the emperor for his knowledge, and an order was issued to arrest and detain the Sizhi. The Sizhi was handed over to officials, who executed him.

## 16

At that time, Ren An served as the Northern Army's messenger and protector of troops. The Crown Prince stopped his carriage outside the southern gate of the Northern Army compound and summoned Ren An, giving him a token to command the troops and order them into action. Ren An bowed in acceptance of the token, entered, and then shut the gate without emerging. Emperor Wu heard about this and thought: "Ren An must have pretended to be righteous; he did not transmit the matter, but why?" Ren An had beaten and humiliated a minor official in charge of finances at the Northern Army. The minor official submitted a memorial to report this, stating that Ren An had accepted the Crown Prince's token and said: "I hope you will give me those who are well-favored." When the memorial was brought to the emperor's attention, Emperor Wu said: "This is an old official who, upon seeing military affairs arise, wished to sit back and observe the outcome. Seeing which side prevails, he wants to align with them—this shows a divided loyalty." "Ren An has committed many crimes deserving of death, yet I have often spared his life. Now he harbors deceit and shows an unfaithful heart." Ren An was handed over to the officials, who executed him. When the moon is full, it wanes; when things reach their height of prosperity, they decline—this is a constant law of heaven and earth. To know advancement but not retreat, to remain in wealth and honor for too long—misfortunes accumulate and become calamities. Therefore, Fan Li's departure from Yue, his refusal of official rank, ensured that his name was passed down through later generations and never forgotten for a thousand years—how could one possibly rival this! Later generations should take caution.
