# Monthly Table of the Period Between the Qin and Chu Dynasties

## 1

The Grand Historian, reading about the time between the Qin and Chu dynasties, said: "Initially, the uprising began with Chen She." Brutality led to the fall of the Qin dynasty, beginning with the Xiang clan; Restoring order and punishing tyrants, pacifying the realm, ultimately ascending to imperial authority, was accomplished by the Han dynasty. Within five years, commands changed hands three times. Since the beginning of humanity, there has never been a case where imperial authority changed so rapidly as this.

## 2

In the past, when the Yu and Xia dynasties rose to power, they accumulated virtue and merit over several decades. Their moral influence reached the people; they administered state affairs while being tested by Heaven, and only then did they assume their positions. The kings Tang and Wu came to power because their ancestors, Qi and Houji, practiced benevolence and righteousness for more than ten generations. Unexpectedly, over eight hundred feudal lords gathered at Mengjin without being summoned; yet they still considered it not the right time, and only later did they proceed with deposing and killing the tyrant. The Qin dynasty began with Duke Xiang, became prominent under the reigns of Duke Wen and Duke Miao, and after the rule of Duke Xian and King Xiao, gradually devoured the six warring states like silkworms eating mulberry leaves. After more than a hundred years, it was not until Emperor Qin Shi Huang that they were able to unify all the feudal lords. To achieve unification through virtue as in those earlier cases, or by force as in this case, it seems that achieving such unity is indeed extremely difficult.

## 3

After the Qin declared themselves emperor, they were troubled by ceaseless warfare, which was due to the existence of feudal lords. Therefore, they did not grant even a square inch of land as fiefs; they destroyed famous walled cities, melted down weapons, and eliminated outstanding individuals in an effort to ensure peace for ten thousand generations. Yet the rise of royal authority began in the common streets; alliances formed to campaign and punish tyranny surpassed even those of the Three Dynasties. The Qin's prohibitions, intended to prevent such things, proved instead merely to provide resources for the virtuous to clear away difficulties. Therefore, those who rose up in anger became mighty rulers of the world; how could it be that one cannot become a king without land? Is this what is referred to in the records as the "great sage"? Was it not Heaven? Was it not Heaven! Who but a great sage could have been worthy of receiving this mandate and becoming emperor?
