# Biographies of Ji Bu and Luan Bu

## 1

Ji Bu was a native of Chu. He valued righteousness and chivalry, and had a reputation in Chu. Xiang Ji sent him to command troops, and he repeatedly put King Han in difficult situations. After Xiang Yu was destroyed, Emperor Gaozu offered a reward of one thousand gold pieces for Ji Bu's capture, and anyone who dared to shelter or hide him would be punished along with his three generations. Ji Bu took refuge in the Zhou family in Puyang. The Zhou family said, "The Han are urgently seeking General Ji Bu; his trail will soon reach my home. If the general is willing to listen to me, I dare to offer a plan;" if not, I would rather commit suicide first." Ji Bu agreed. They shaved Ji Bu's head and fastened an iron collar around his neck, dressed him in a coarse hemp robe, placed him inside a wide willow cart, and along with dozens of their household attendants, took him to sell him at Zhu Jia's residence in Lu. Zhu Jia recognized that it was Ji Bu and bought him, placing him on a farm. He warned his son, "Matters concerning the fields must be listened to by this slave; you must share your food with him." Zhu Jia then rode a light carriage to Luoyang and met Duke Ruyin, Teng Gong. Duke Teng kept Zhu Jia for several days of drinking together. 藟 He then said to Duke Teng, "What great crime has Ji Bu committed that the emperor is seeking him so urgently?" Duke Teng said, "Ji Bu repeatedly put Your Majesty in difficult situations during Xiang Yu's time; the emperor resents him, so he must capture him." Zhu Jia said, "Do you think Ji Bu is what kind of person?" He replied, "A virtuous man." Zhu Jia said, "Ministers serve their respective lords; Ji Bu served Xiang Ji, which was his duty. "Should all the ministers of the Xiang clan be executed?" "Now that His Majesty has just gained control over the empire, if he pursues one person solely out of personal resentment, how will this demonstrate his broad-mindedness to the people throughout the land!" Moreover, if a virtuous man like Ji Bu is being pursued so urgently by the Han, he will either flee north to the Hu or south to the Yue. To be jealous of a valiant man and thus provide him as an asset to enemy states is precisely why Wu Zixu was able to whip the tomb of King Jing of Chu. "Why don't you calmly speak on his behalf to His Majesty?" Duke Ruyin, Teng Gong, knew in his heart that Zhu Jia was a great chivalrous man and suspected that Ji Bu was hiding at his residence. He thus agreed and said, "Yes." When an opportunity arose, he indeed spoke as Zhu Jia had advised. The emperor then pardoned Ji Bu. At that time, many officials praised Ji Bu for his ability to transform rigidity into flexibility; Zhu Jia also became famous in his generation because of this. Ji Bu was summoned for an audience, expressed gratitude, and the emperor appointed him as a Langzhong (attendant official).

## 2

During the reign of Emperor Xiaohui, he served as Zhonglangjiang. The Chanyu once sent a letter that insulted Empress Dowager Lü, showing no respect. Empress Dowager Lü was greatly angered and summoned the generals to discuss it. General Fan Kuai said, "I wish to command ten thousand troops and march freely through the territory of the Xiongnu." All the generals flattered Empress Dowager Lü's wishes and said, "Indeed." Ji Bu said, "Fan Kuai is worthy of being beheaded!" "When Emperor Gaozu led more than 400,000 troops, he was trapped in Pingcheng. Now how can Kuai claim to lead ten thousand men and roam freely through the Xiongnu territory? This is nothing but a face-saving lie!" Moreover, it was because of conflicts with the Hu that Chen Sheng and others rose in rebellion. Now, when wounds from past wars have not yet healed, Kuai dares to flatter openly and wishes to stir up unrest across the land." At that time, all in the hall were frightened. The Empress Dowager dismissed court and thus no longer discussed attacking the Xiongnu.

## 3

Ji Bu served as governor of Hedong. During the reign of Emperor Xiaowen, someone praised his virtue; Emperor Xiaowen summoned him and wanted to appoint him as Yushi Daifu (Grand Censor). However, others said he was bold and prone to drinking, making it difficult to work closely with him. When Ji Bu arrived, he stayed at the guesthouse for a month before being dismissed. Ji Bu then said, "I have no merit yet received undeserved favor; I serve in Hedong under the burden of an official post. Your Majesty has summoned me without cause—there must be someone who has deceived Your Majesty about me; now that I have arrived, no duties were assigned to me and I was dismissed—there must be someone who slandered me. Your Majesty summoned me because of one person's praise and dismissed me due to another's slander. I fear that those with insight throughout the empire will take this as an opportunity to judge Your Majesty." The emperor fell silent in shame and, after a long pause, said, "Hedong is one of my vital regions; that was why I specially summoned you." Ji Bu then took his leave to return to his post.

## 4

Cao Qiuseng, a native of Chu, was an eloquent man who often sought power and wealth through connections. He served influential figures such as Zhao Tong and maintained good relations with Dou Changjun. Ji Bu heard about this, wrote a letter to advise Dou Changjun, saying, "I have heard that Cao Qiuseng is not an upright man; do not associate with him." When Cao Qiuseng returned home, he wanted to write a letter requesting an audience with Ji Bu. Dou Changjun said, "General Ji does not favor you; you should not go." Cao Qiuseng insisted on writing the letter and proceeded to visit. He had someone send the letter first, and Ji Bu indeed became greatly angered and waited for Cao Qiuseng's arrival. When Cao Qiuseng arrived, he immediately bowed to Ji Bu and said, "There is a saying among the people of Chu: 'A hundred catties of gold are not as valuable as one promise from Ji Bu.' How did you earn such a reputation in Liang and Chu?" Moreover, I am a native of Chu, and so are you. I have promoted your name throughout the world; is that not significant?" "Why then do you reject me so strongly!" Ji Bu was greatly pleased, invited him in, kept him for several months as a distinguished guest, and gave him generous gifts upon his departure. The reason why Ji Bu's reputation became even more renowned was because Cao Qiuseng promoted it.

## 5

Ji Bu's younger brother, Ji Xin, was renowned for his boldness in Guanzhong. He treated people with respect and care; as a chivalrous man, he commanded the loyalty of thousands of miles of territory, and scholars all vied to die for him. Once, after killing someone, he fled to Wu and took refuge with Yuan Si. He served Yuan Si as a son would serve his father, while treating figures such as Guan Fu and Ji Fu as younger brothers. Once serving as Zhong Sima (Middle Horseman), even General Zhi Wu dared not fail to show him respect. Many young men often secretly used his name for their own purposes. At that time, Ji Xin was renowned in Guanzhong for his bravery, while Ji Bu was famous for his word of honor.

## 6

Ji Bu's maternal younger brother, Ding Gong, served as a general under the state of Chu. Ding Gong, serving Xiang Yu, pursued and cornered Emperor Gaozu west of Pengcheng. When their forces engaged in close combat and the emperor was in dire straits, he turned to Ding Gong and said, "How could two virtuous men find themselves at odds?" At this, Ding Gong withdrew his troops, allowing King Han to escape. After Xiang Wang was destroyed, Ding Gong paid a visit to Emperor Gaozu. Emperor Gaozu paraded Ding Gong before the troops and said, "Ding Gong was disloyal as a minister to Xiang Wang; it was precisely Ding Gong who caused Xiang Wang to lose the empire." He then beheaded Ding Gong, saying, "Let this serve as a warning for future ministers not to follow in Ding Gong's footsteps!"

## 7

Luan Bu was a native of Liang. In his early days, when King Liang Wang Peng Yue was still an ordinary person, he once traveled with Luan Bu. He was poor and destitute, working as a hired laborer in Qi, serving as a guarantor for an alcohol seller. After several years, Peng Yue left and became a bandit in the Ju Ye region; meanwhile, Luan Bu was captured and sold into slavery, becoming a slave in Yan. After avenging his master's family, the Yan general Zang Tu recommended him as Du Wei (military officer). Later, when Zang Tu became King of Yan, he appointed Luan Bu as a general. When Zang Tu rebelled against the Han, the Han attacked Yan and captured Luan Bu. King Liang Peng Yue heard about this and petitioned the emperor to request his release, asking for Luan Bu to be appointed as a Liang official.

## 8

While Luan Bu was on an errand in Qi and had not yet returned, the Han summoned Peng Yue, accused him of plotting rebellion, and exterminated his three generations. Later, Peng Yue's head was displayed on a pole in Luoyang, and an imperial decree stated: "Anyone who dares to collect or look at it will be immediately captured." When Luan Bu returned from Qi, he reported his affairs beneath Peng Yue's decapitated head, paid homage to it, and wept. The officials captured Luan Bu and reported the matter to the emperor. The emperor summoned Luan Bu, scolded him, saying, "Did you conspire with Peng Yue in rebellion?" "I forbade anyone from collecting or honoring his remains, yet you alone paid homage and wept for him—this clearly shows that you conspired with Peng Yue." "Quickly execute him!" Just as they were about to lead him to the boiling pot, Luan Bu turned and said, "I wish to speak one word before I die." The emperor asked, "What do you have to say?" Luan Bu said, "When Your Majesty was in dire straits at Pengcheng and suffered defeats between Xingyang and Chenggao, King Xiang could not advance westward because King Peng Yue controlled the Liang region and allied with Han to resist the Chu forces. At that time, if King Peng had switched allegiance—had he joined the Chu, then Han would have been defeated; had he supported Han, then Chu would have been crushed. Moreover, at the Battle of Gaixia, without King Peng's assistance, the Xiang clan would not have perished. Now that the empire is at peace, King Peng was granted a fief and invested with a seal; he also wished to pass it down for ten thousand generations. Now, when Your Majesty summoned troops from Liang and King Peng was too ill to respond, you suspected him of rebellion. Yet no clear signs of treachery existed; instead, you executed him over a trivial matter. I fear this will make all meritorious officials feel constantly at risk." "Now that King Peng is dead, I would rather die than live. Please proceed with the execution." At this, the emperor pardoned Luan Bu's crime and appointed him as Du Wei (a military officer).

## 9

During the reign of Emperor Xiaowen he served as prime minister of Yan, rising to the rank of general. Luan Bu then declared, "A person who cannot humble himself and lower his aspirations in times of hardship is not a true man; one who cannot find satisfaction in wealth and honor is not virtuous." Therefore, those who had done him favors were generously rewarded, while those with grudges against him were inevitably dealt with according to the law. During the time of Wu's (actually Chu) rebellion, he was enfeoffed as Yu Hou due to his military achievements and once again served as prime minister of Yan. Between Yan and Qi, people all established altars in honor of Luan Bu, calling them the "Luan Bu Society."

## 10

He died during the fifth year of Emperor Jingdi's Zhong period. His son Bin succeeded him, serving as Taichang (Minister of Rites). However, he failed to meet the regulations regarding sacrificial animals, and his fief was abolished.

## 11

Grand Historian said: Given Xiang Yu's formidable might, Ji Bu was able to gain prominence in Chu for his bravery. On multiple occasions, he personally led troops into battle and captured enemy banners; he could truly be called a valiant warrior. Yet when it came to being subjected to corporal punishment, enduring the humiliation of slavery and still not dying—how low he had fallen! He must have believed in his own abilities, thus enduring humiliation without shame. He wished to prove that he was not yet fully utilized; therefore, he eventually became a famous general of the Han dynasty. Indeed, virtuous men place great value on their lives. Those lowly servants and concubines who commit suicide out of emotion are not truly brave; they simply have no further plans or means to recover. Luan Bu wept for Peng Yue, rushing into the boiling cauldron as if returning home; he truly understood his situation and did not place value on his own life. Even among the most valiant men of ancient times, who could surpass him!
