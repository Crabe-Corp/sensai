# Annals of Xia

## 1

Xia Yu, whose personal name was Wenming. Yu's father was Gun, and Gun's father was Emperor Zhuanxu. Zhuanxu's father was Changyi, and Changyi's father was the Yellow Emperor. Yu was a great-great-grandson of the Yellow Emperor and a grandson of Emperor Zhuanxu. Yu's great-grandfather Changyi and his father Gun were both unable to ascend the imperial throne, serving instead as ministers.

## 2

During Emperor Yao's reign, floods were rampant and overwhelming; the waters surged high, engulfing hills and drowning lowlands, causing great suffering among the people. Emperor Yao sought someone capable of controlling the floods, and both his ministers and the Four Mountains all recommended that Gun was suitable. Yao said: "Gun is a man who disobeys commands and brings ruin to his clan; he cannot be entrusted with this task." The Four Mountains replied: "Among those available, there is no one more capable than Gun. We respectfully request that the Emperor give him a trial." Thus, Yao heeded the Four Mountains' recommendation and appointed Gun to control the floods. łó After nine years, the floods still did not subside, and his efforts achieved no success. Therefore, Emperor Yao sought a new person to replace him and found Shun. Shun was appointed to an official position, assumed the duties of the Son of Heaven, and conducted imperial tours. Upon inspecting Gun's efforts to control the floods and finding them ineffective, Shun had Gun executed at Yushan. People throughout the land all considered Shun's punishment of Gun to be just. Thus, Shun appointed Gun's son Yu and ordered him to continue his father's unfinished task.

## 3

After Emperor Yao died, Emperor Shun asked the Four Mountains: "Is there anyone capable of completing the noble tasks left by Emperor Yao and assigning them to official positions?" They all replied, "Bo Yu is the Minister of Works; he can complete the great achievements left by Emperor Yao." Shun said, "Ah, indeed!" He ordered Yu: "You must pacify the waters and control the land. Strive diligently to accomplish this." Yu bowed deeply in gratitude, offering the position to Xi, Houji, and Gao Yao. Shun said: "Then go forth and attend to your duties."

## 4

Yu was a man who was quick-witted, resourceful, and diligent. His virtue was consistent; his benevolence made him lovable, and his words were trustworthy. He used the pitch of his voice as a standard for musical scales, his body as a measure for length, and these standards were established accordingly; With steady diligence and dignified composure, he became the model of order and discipline.

## 5

Yu then worked with Yi and Houji, carrying out the Emperor's command. He ordered feudal lords and common people to mobilize laborers for land reclamation, traveled through the mountains setting up markers, and surveyed high mountains and great rivers. Yu was distressed by the fact that his father Gun had failed in his task and thus suffered punishment. Therefore, he toiled diligently and endured great mental strain, spending thirteen years outside his home, passing by his family's gate but not daring to enter. He lived in simple clothing and meager food, offering filial devotion to the spirits of heaven and earth. He kept his palace modest and frugal, directing resources instead toward building irrigation canals and ditches. On land he traveled by chariot; on water by boat; through mud by sledge; and over mountains by wooden shoes. With a plumb line in his left hand and compasses in the right, he carried with him the knowledge of the four seasons. He opened up the Nine Provinces, connected the Nine Routes, constructed embankments around the Nine Lakes, and measured the Nine Mountains. He ordered Yi to distribute rice to the common people for planting in low-lying, moist areas. He commanded Houji to provide the people with food that was difficult to obtain. When there was a shortage of food, he regulated surpluses and distributed them among the people, thus balancing resources among the feudal lords. Yu then traveled to assess the suitability of each region's land for tribute offerings and took into account the convenience provided by mountains and rivers.

## 6

Yu began his journey from Jizhou. Jizhou: After controlling the Hukou region, he managed Liang and Qi. After completing the work on Taiyuan, he reached Yueyang. The achievements of Qianhuai extended as far as Hengzhang. Its soil was white loam. The tribute was of the highest grade with some variation; the land quality was medium. The regions of Chang and Wei were already under control, and Dalu had been properly cultivated. The Niao Yi people offered furs as tribute. Between the right side of Jieshi and the sea.

## 7

Between the Ji River and the Yellow River was Yan Zhou: The Nine Rivers had been channeled, Leixia had become a lake, the Yong and Ju rivers converged together. Mulberry fields were cultivated for silkworms; thus, people could descend from their hillside dwellings to live on level land. Its soil was black and fertile, with grass growing luxuriantly and trees arranged in rows. The land quality was medium to low; the tribute was of moderate grade. It took thirteen years before it became uniform. Its tribute offerings were lacquer and silk, carried in bamboo baskets with woven patterns. Transportation was by boat along the Ji and Ta rivers, connecting to the Yellow River.

## 8

Between the Sea and Mount Dai was Qing Zhou: The Youyi people had already been pacified; the Wei and Ziyi rivers were properly channeled. Its soil was white and fertile, with extensive salt flats along the seashore; its land was saline and difficult to cultivate. The land quality ranked as medium between high and low grades; the tribute level was moderate to high. Its tribute offerings included salt and fine hemp cloth, with a variety of marine products. From the mountain valleys of Dai came silk, flax, lead, pine trees, and peculiar stones; the Lai Yi people provided livestock, carried in baskets made of Yanshi silk. Transportation was by boat along the Wen River, connecting to the Ji River.

## 9

Between the Sea and Mount Tai as well as the Huai River was Xu Zhou: The Huai and Yi rivers were controlled, while Meng and Yu regions were cultivated. Daye had already been tamed; the eastern plains were now level. Its soil was red and fertile, with gradually growing grass and trees covering it. The land quality ranked high to medium; the tribute level was medium. Its tribute offerings included five-colored soil. From the valleys of Yu came Xia Di; from Yiyang, rare paulownia trees; from Sibin, floating stone chimes; and from the Huai Yi people, pearls, fish, and other aquatic products. The tribute was carried in baskets made of black silk with white patterns. Transportation was by boat along the Huai and Si rivers, connecting to the Yellow River.

## 10

Between the Huai River and the Sea was Yang Zhou: Penglai had already been tamed, and it was where the Yangniao birds dwelled. The Three Rivers had already flowed into their channels; Zhenze Lake reached a state of stability. Bamboo and arrow plants were widely distributed. Its grass was lush, its trees tall; the soil was muddy. The land quality ranked low to very low; the tribute level varied between lower and higher grades. Its tribute offerings included three types of metal, as well as jadite, kun stone, bamboo, and arrow plants. It also offered teeth, hides, feathers, and yak tails. The island Yi people contributed woven plant fibers; the tribute was carried in baskets with embroidered shells, packed with tangerines, pomelos, and tin offerings. It connected the Yangtze River to the sea, as well as the Huai and Si rivers.

## 11

Between Jing and Hengyang was Jing Zhou: The Yangtze and Han rivers all flowed toward the sea as their ultimate destination. The Nine Rivers were well regulated; the Tuo and Cen rivers had been channeled; Yun Tu and Meng regions were under control. Its soil was muddy. The land quality ranked medium to low; the tribute level ranked high to medium. Its tribute offerings included feathers, yak tails, teeth, and hides; three types of metal; trees such as chun, gan, gua, and bai; grinding stones,砥 stones, arrowheads, and cinnabar. It also contributed bamboo shoots, julu reeds, and ku wood. Three states presented tribute by name: they packed fragrant rush grass in wooden boxes and carried it in baskets with black and red silk tassels. From the Nine Rivers came large turtles as imperial gifts. Transportation was by boat through the Yangtze, Tuo, and Cen rivers, then via the Han River; it crossed the Luo River and reached the southern reaches of the Yellow River.

## 12

Between Jing and the Yellow River was Yu Zhou: The Yi, Luo, Chan, and Jian rivers had already flowed into the Yellow River; Yingbo had been tamed. The Heze region was regulated, as were the Mingdu plains. Its soil was fertile loam of lower quality. The land ranked medium to high in quality; the tribute varied between high and medium grades. Its tribute offerings included lacquer, silk, fine hemp cloth, and ramie fabric; the baskets carried fine flax fibers. Tin offerings also included stone chimes and polishing stones. Transportation was by boat via the Luo River to reach the Yellow River.

## 13

Between Huayang and Heishui was Liang Zhou: Wen and Bo regions had been cultivated; the Tuo and Cen Rivers were channeled. The Cai and Meng areas were pacified, and the He Yi people achieved success. Its soil was dark green loam. The land quality ranked lower to medium; the tribute levels varied among three grades of lower and medium ranks. Its tribute offerings included jade, iron, silver, engraved ornaments, arrowheads, stone chimes, as well as bears, brown bears, foxes, leopards, and woven furs. From the western slopes of Qin, it came via Huan; transportation was by boat through Qian, crossing Mian, and entering Wei River, eventually converging with the Yellow River.

## 14

Between Heishui to the west and the Yellow River was Yong Zhou: The Weak Water had already flowed westward, while the Jing River joined the Wei at its confluence. The Chi and Ju rivers were under control; they converged with the Feng River. Jing and Qi regions had been pacified, extending from Zhongnan to Dunwu as far as Niushu. The plains and lowlands were tamed, reaching as far as Duye. The Three Weis had been measured; the Sanmiao people were now well-ordered. Its soil was yellow loam. The land quality ranked highest; the tribute level was medium to low. Its tribute offerings included jadite, lin stone, and langgan jade. Transportation was by boat from Jishi, reaching the western reaches of Longmen on the Yellow River, where it converged with the Wei at its confluence.  The woven fur offerings came from Kunlun, Xizhi, and Qusou; the western Rong tribes were now in order.

## 15

Controlling the Nine Mountains: From Qian to Qi up to Jing Shan, crossing the Yellow River; From Hukou and Leishou up to Ta Yue; From Dizhu and Xicheng up to Wangwu; From Taihang, Changshan up to Jieshi, reaching the sea; From Xi Qing, Zhuyu, and Niushu up to Ta Hua; From Xionger, Waifang, and Tongbai up to Fuwei; Controlling the Bozhong region, reaching Jing Shan; From Neifang up to Dabie; The southern side of Wenshan reached Hengshan, passed through the Nine Rivers, and extended to Fupianyuan.

## 16

Controlling the Nine Rivers: The Ruoshui River reached Helili, its remaining waters flowing into Liusha. Controlling the Heishui River, reaching Sanwei, and entering the Southern Sea. Controlling the Yellow River from Jishi up to Longmen, southward to Huayin, eastward to Dizhu, then further east to Mengjin. It passed east of Luorei and reached Dapi, then north across Jiangshui to Dalu. To the north it spread into the Nine Rivers; together they formed a reverse-flowing river, entering the sea. Controlling the Yiang River from Bozhong, it flowed eastward as the Han River. Further east became the Canglang waters, passing through San Shi and entering Dabie to the south into the Yangtze River. It converged with lakes in the east forming Pengli, then continued east as the Bei Jiang, entering the sea. Controlling the Yangtze River from Wenshan, it branched eastward into Tuo. Further east it reached Li, passed through the Nine Jiangs, and arrived at Dongling. It then turned northeast to converge with Hui, continued east as Zhongjiang, entering Mei. Controlling the Yan Shui, it flowed east as Ji River and entered the Yellow River. Overflowing into Ying, it emerged north of Taoqiu to the east, then reached He further east, converged with Wen in the northeast, and finally entered the sea in the northeastern direction. Controlling the Huai River from Tongbai, it flowed east to converge with Si and Yi rivers, then entered the sea to the east. Controlling the Wei River from Niushutongxue, it flowed east to meet Feng, then northeast to Jing. It passed through Qi and Ju rivers to the east before entering the Yellow River. Controlling the Luo River from Xionger, it converged northeast with Jian and Chan rivers, then further east with Yi. It entered the Yellow River in the northeastern direction.

## 17

At this time, the Nine Provinces were unified; the four corners of the land had been settled. The Nine Mountains were cleared and traversed, the Nine Rivers cleansed their valleys, the Nine Lakes were already embanked, and the Four Seas converged in harmony. The Six Administrative Regions were well maintained; all lands were surveyed and regulated. Care was taken with financial resources, and taxation followed the standard of three soil grades to determine contributions. The Central Kingdom bestowed land and surnames: "Only those who uphold virtue first shall not resist my course."

## 18

The Son of Heaven's domain was surrounded by a 500-li buffer zone known as the Dianfu region: within 100 li, total produce was assigned; within 200 li, grain husks were submitted; within 300 li, straw and fodder were provided; within 400 li, millet was delivered; and within 500 li, rice was contributed. Beyond the Dianfu region lay another 500-li area known as Houfu: within 100 li were those who collected tribute, within 200 li were officials in charge of states, and within 300 li resided feudal lords. Beyond the Houfu region lay another 500- li area known as Sui Fu: within 300 li was the domain of measuring literary instruction, and within 200 li was where martial defense forces were mobilized. Beyond the Sui Fu region lay another 500 -li area known as Yao Fu: within 300 li were the Yi people, and within 200 miles were the Cai. Beyond the Yao Fu region lay another 50 -li area known as Huang Fu: within 30 li were the Man people, and within 20 miles were those in exile.

## 19

To the east it extended to the sea, to the west reached the Liusha sands; north and south included all regions: the influence of culture and instruction reached as far as the Four Seas. At this time, Emperor granted Yu a black jade tablet to announce his success to all under heaven. Thus the world was at peace and well governed.

## 20

Gaotao established a judicial system to govern the people. In the court of Emperor Shun, Yu, Boyi, and Gaotao conversed before the emperor. Gaotao recounted his plan, saying: "Believe in virtue and morality; deliberate wisely to assist harmony." Yu said, "Indeed. How so?" Gaotao said: "Ah! Be cautious in self-cultivation, think long-term, cultivate harmony among the nine clans, elevate and support the worthy, for what is near can be extended far through oneself." Yu bowed in appreciation of these words, saying: "Indeed." Gaotao said: "Ah! It lies in knowing people and in bringing peace to the people." Yu said: "Ah! If all were like this, only the Son of Heaven would find it difficult. Knowing people is wisdom; being able to appoint officials properly. Being able to bring peace to the people is benevolence, and the common people will cherish it. If one can know people and show benevolence, what need is there to worry about Huan Dou? What need is there to exile You Miao? And what fear should be felt toward those who speak flatteringly with pleasant expressions?" Gaotao said: "Indeed, Ah! There are also nine virtues in conduct; there is virtue to be found in words as well." He said: "At the beginning, one undertakes affairs diligently. Be broad-minded yet cautious, gentle yet resolute, sincere and cooperative, capable yet respectful, adaptable yet determined, upright yet warm-hearted, straightforward yet modest, simple yet incorruptible, firm yet practical, strong yet righteous—those who display these consistent virtues will bring good fortune." Every day, promote the three virtues; from early morning to night, support and illuminate one's household. Daily cultivate reverence and respect for the six virtues, shining with virtue in a nation. If one accepts universally and widely bestows benefits, all nine virtues will be fulfilled; the capable and virtuous will hold office, and all officials will be conscientious and cautious. Do not teach wickedness, licentiousness, or strange strategies. If an unqualified person occupies a position of office, this is called disordering heavenly affairs. Heaven punishes the guilty; thus the five punishments are applied accordingly. "Are my words truly feasible?" Yu said: "Your words are indeed capable of achieving success." Gaotao said: "I have not yet attained understanding; I shall strive to assist and guide."

## 21

Emperor Shun said to Yu, "You also speak wisely." Yu bowed and said; "Ah, what words of mine!" I have only strived diligently day by day." Gaotao challenged Yu, asking: "What do you mean by 'striving diligently'?" Yu said: "The great flood surged to the heavens, vast and overwhelming, engulfing mountains and dikes; the people below were all submerged by water. On land I traveled in a carriage, on water in a boat, through mud on sledges, and over mountains on yokes; I cut trees to clear the way. Yu said: "I worked with Yi to provide rice and fresh food for the common people." To drain the Nine Rivers into the Four Seas, and to dredge ditches and irrigation channels leading to the rivers. Working with Ji, I provided the common people with food that was hard to obtain. When food was scarce, we redistributed surplus to make up for shortages and relocated settlements. The people thus found stability, and all the states were well governed." Gaotao said: "Indeed, this is admirable."

## 22

Yu said: "Ah, Your Majesty! Be cautious in your position of power and find peace in your place. Support virtue, and the whole world will respond accordingly. With a clear mind and shining devotion to await Heaven's command, Heaven shall bestow its great mandate for peace." The emperor said: "Ah, loyal minister! Loyal minister! Ministers are my limbs and eyes. I wish to have the people on all sides; you must assist me in this. I wish to observe the patterns of ancient sages. The sun, moon, stars, and constellations form embroidered garments and colors; you must clarify their meanings. I wish to understand the Six Laws, Five Tones, and Eight Musical Categories, from which all sounds originate; they form the five types of speech. You must listen carefully. When I act rashly, you must correct me. You must not flatter me to my face. Speak ill of me behind my back. Respect the four assisting ministers. All those who are slanderous and favored by flattery, if the virtue of a ruler is truly practiced, all will be purified." Yu said: "Indeed. If the Son of Heaven does not act at the right time, and if good and evil are treated equally, there will be no achievement."

## 23

The emperor said: "Do not be like Dan Zhu, who was arrogant and loved idleness; do not travel on water in boats for pleasure, or indulge in licentiousness at home, which will lead to the extinction of your lineage. "I am unable to follow such a path." Yu said: "I (on the Xinren day) married Tushan, [on the Xinren and Gua Jia days], gave birth to Qi. I did not indulge in fatherhood, which is why I could accomplish the task of controlling water and land." Assisting in establishing the Five Regions, reaching as far as five thousand li; twelve military divisions were organized for each province. The outer regions extended to the Four Seas; all areas established Five Leaders, and each path contributed its achievements. The You Miao people remained stubborn and did not accept their due contributions. The Son of Heaven must remember this." The emperor said: "Promote my virtue, and that will be the order of your achievements."

## 24

Gaotao thus revered Yu's virtue and ordered the people to all follow Yu as a model. Those who do not act according to these words will be punished accordingly. The virtue of Shun was greatly illuminated.

## 25

At this time, Ku performed music; ancestors and forebears arrived. The feudal lords vied to yield precedence. Birds and beasts soared and danced. The Xiao Shao music was played in nine movements. Phoenixes came with dignified grace, all creatures followed in dance, and officials worked in harmony and trust. The emperor composed a song saying: "Ascending to Heaven's mandate, it is the right time and the critical moment." He sang: "Limb and arm, how joyful! The head of state rises! All officials work in harmony!" Gaotao bowed with clasped hands and kowtowed, raising his voice to say: "Remember this! Lead by initiating good deeds. Be cautious in your laws; be reverent!" He then composed another song, saying: "The head of state is enlightened! The limb and arm are virtuous! All affairs are prosperous!" (Shun) also sang: "The head of state is preoccupied with trivialities! The limb and arm are lazy! All matters fall into decline!" The emperor bowed and said, "Indeed. Go forth with reverence!" At this time, all under heaven revered Yu's clear standards of measurement and music, regarding him as the sovereign deity of mountains and rivers.

## 26

Emperor Shun recommended Yu to Heaven, appointing him as his successor. In the seventeenth year, Emperor Shun died. After completing three years of mourning, Yu declined to accept power and withdrew from Yangcheng, avoiding Shun's son Shangjun. All the feudal lords under heaven abandoned Shangjun and paid homage to Yu. Yu thus ascended the throne of Son of Heaven, facing south to rule over all under heaven; his dynasty was named Xiahou, and his surname was Si.

## 27

Emperor Yu ascended the throne and recommended Gaotao for promotion, intending to entrust him with governance; however, Gaotao died. The descendants of Gaotao were enfeoffed in Ying and Liu, or some were located in Xu. Afterward, Yi was promoted and entrusted with governance.

## 28

In the tenth year, Emperor Yu went on an eastern inspection tour and died upon reaching Kuaiji. He passed the throne to Yi. After completing three years of mourning, Yi yielded the throne to Qi, the son of Emperor Yu, and withdrew to live on the southern side of Jishan Mountain. Yu's son Qi was virtuous, and all under heaven looked forward to him. After Yu died, although the throne was passed to Yi, Yi had assisted Yu for a short time and thus did not enjoy widespread acceptance among all under heaven. Therefore, the feudal lords abandoned Yi and paid homage to Qi, saying: "He is our lord, the son of Emperor Yu." Thus, Qi ascended the throne as Son of Heaven and became known as Xiahoudi Qi.

## 29

Xiahoudi Qi was Yu's son, whose mother was a daughter of the Tushan clan.

## 30

The You Hu clan did not submit; Qi attacked them, and a great battle took place at Gan. Before the battle, he composed the "Gan Oath" and summoned the Six Ministers to reaffirm it. Qi said: "Alas! You six officials, I swear to inform you: The You Hu clan showed arrogance and insulted the Five Elements; they neglected the Three Correct Principles. Heaven therefore cut off their mandate. Now I am merely carrying out Heaven's punishment together with you. If the left does not attack from the left, if the right does not attack from the right, and if you do not follow orders, if the charioteer does not control his horse properly, you will also be considered as having disobeyed commands. Those who obey orders shall receive rewards at the Ancestral Altar; those who disobey will be punished at the Earth God's altar, and I will enslave or execute you." Thus, the You Hu clan was annihilated. All under heaven came to pay homage.

## 31

Xiahoudi Qi died, and his son Emperor Taikang ascended the throne. Emperor Taikang lost the kingdom; his five younger brothers waited by Luorui and composed "The Songs of the Five Sons."

## 32

Taikang died, and his brother Zhongkang ascended the throne; he was known as Emperor Zhongkang. During Emperor Zhongkang's reign, Xi and He indulged in wine and idleness, neglecting the seasons and disrupting the calendar. Yin went to punish them, composing "The Expedition of Yin."

## 33

Zhongkang died, and his son Emperor Xiang ascended the throne. Emperor Xiang died, and his son Emperor Shaokang ascended the throne. Emperor Shaokang died, and his son, Emperor Yu, ascended the throne. Emperor Yu died, and his son Emperor Huai ascended the throne. Emperor Huai died, and his son Emperor Mang ascended the throne. Emperor Mang died, and his son Emperor Die ascended the throne. Emperor Die died, and his son Emperor Bujiang ascended the throne. Emperor Bujiang died, and his younger brother Emperor Jiong ascended the throne. Emperor Jiong died, and his son Emperor Jin ascended the throne. Emperor Jin died, and Emperor Bujiang's son Kongjia was enthroned; he became known as Emperor Kongjia. When Emperor Kongjia ascended the throne, he favored occult practices and deities, engaging in licentious and disorderly conduct. The virtue of the Xiahou clan declined, and feudal lords rebelled against them. Heaven sent down two dragons, one male and one female. Kongjia could not feed them, as he had no descendants of the Hualong clan to care for them. After the Tao Tang clan declined, one of its later descendants named Liu Lei studied dragon taming from the Hualong family and served Emperor Kongjia with this skill. Kongjia granted him the surname "Yulong" (Dragon Ruler) and bestowed upon him the fief of Shiw ei's descendants. One female dragon died, and Liu Lei served its meat to the Xiahou ruler. When the Xiahou ruler sent someone to inquire about it, Liu Lei was afraid and fled from his position.

## 34

Emperor Kongjia died, and his son Emperor Gao ascended the throne. Emperor Gao died, and his son Emperor Fa ascended the throne. Emperor Fa died, and his son Di Lükui ascended the throne; this was King Jie. During Emperor Jie's reign, since Kongjia's time, many feudal lords had already rebelled against the Xia dynasty. Jie did not cultivate virtue but used military force to harm the people; the common folk could no longer endure it. He summoned Tang and imprisoned him at Xiatai, but later released him. Tang cultivated virtue; all the feudal lords returned to his allegiance. Thus, Tang led an army to attack Xia Jie. Jie fled to Mingtiao and was exiled there until he died. Jie said to others: "I regret not having executed Tang at Xiatai; otherwise, I would not have come to this pass." Tang then ascended the throne of Son of heaven and replaced the Xia dynasty as ruler over all under heaven. Tang enfeoffed descendants of the Xia clan, a practice that continued until the Zhou dynasty, when they were granted fiefs in Qi.

## 35

Grand Historian said: Yu belonged to the Si surname. Afterward, his descendants were enfeoffed and adopted their fiefdoms as surnames; thus there arose the Xiahou clan, You Hu clan, Nan clan, Zhen Xun clan, Tongcheng clan, Bao clan, Fei clan, Qi clan, Zeng clan, Xin clan, Ming clan, and Zhen (clan) Ge clan. Confucius corrected the Xia calendar; many scholars passed down "Xia Xiao Zheng" as a record of it. Since the time of Yu and Xia, tribute and taxation systems were already well established. Some say that Yu convened feudal lords in southern Jiang, where he died after completing his achievements and was buried there; the place was named Kuaiji. Kuaiji means "accounting" or "reckoning."
