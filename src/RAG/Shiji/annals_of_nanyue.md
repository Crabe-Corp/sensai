# Annals of Nanyue

## 1

The King of Nanyue, You Tu, was a native of Zhen Ding and bore the surname Zhao. During the Qin dynasty, it had already unified all under heaven and roughly pacified Yangyue. It established the commanderies of Guilin, Nanhai, and Xiang, and settled exiled convicts there to live together with the Yue people for thirteen years. You Tu was appointed as the magistrate of Longchuan in Nanhai during the Qin dynasty. When the Second Emperor's reign, the Nanhai military officer Ren Xiao fell ill and was about to die. He summoned Zhao Tu, the magistrate of Longchuan, and said: "I have heard that Chen Sheng and others have started a rebellion. The Qin dynasty has been tyrannical; all under heaven suffer from its rule. Xiang Yu, Liu Ji, Chen Sheng, Wu Guang, and others in various commanderies and states have each raised armies and gathered followers to contend for dominance over the world. Central China is in turmoil, and it remains uncertain where stability will be found. Outstanding men are abandoning the Qin and establishing themselves as rulers." Nanhai is remote, and I fear that bandit forces may invade this area. I want to raise an army to cut off the new road as a precaution, waiting for changes among the feudal lords; however, my illness has worsened. Moreover, Panyu is backed by mountainous terrain and faces the South China Sea. Stretching thousands of miles from east to west, it has many Chinese people who can assist one another; this region could also become a ruler over its own state. There are no senior officials in the commandery worthy of discussion, so I summoned you to inform you." Ren Xiao then passed on his official seal and documents to Zhao Tu, who assumed the duties of Nanhai military officer. After Ren Xiao died, Zhao Tu immediately issued proclamations to the Hengpu, Yangshan, and Huangxi Passes, saying: "Bandit forces are about to arrive. Quickly cut off the roads and gather troops for self-defense!" He then gradually used legal means to execute the senior officials appointed by the Qin, appointing his followers as acting magistrates. After the fall of the Qin dynasty, Zhao Tu attacked and annexed the commanderies of Guilin and Xiang, declaring himself King Wu of Nanyue. After Emperor Gaozu had pacified all under heaven, he was weary from the toil of governing China and thus released Zhao Tu without punishing him. In the eleventh year of the Han dynasty, Emperor Gaozu sent Lu Jia to formally appoint Zhao Tu as King of Nanyue. He granted him a covenant seal and established diplomatic relations, aiming to pacify the Baiyue peoples and prevent them from becoming a threat along the southern frontier, where they bordered Changsha.

## 2

During the reign of Empress Dowager Gao, officials requested to ban the trade of iron tools at border markets with Nanyue. Zhao Tu said: "Emperor Gaozu appointed me and allowed trade between our peoples. Now Empress Dowager is listening to the slander of ministers, treating the southern barbarians as different, and cutting off the exchange of goods. This must be a plan by King Changsha, who wants to rely on China's support to attack and destroy Nanyue in order to annex it and claim the credit for himself." Therefore, Zhao Tu declared himself Emperor Wu of Nanyue and launched a military campaign to attack border towns of Changsha. He defeated several counties before withdrawing. Empress Dowager Gao sent General Longyu Hou Zao to attack him. However, the season was hot and humid, causing a severe outbreak among the soldiers, so the army could not cross the mountains. After more than a year, Empress Dowager Gao died, and the military campaign was immediately halted. Zhao Tu then used his military strength to dominate the borders, bribing Minyue, Xiuou, and Luoyue with wealth in order to subjugate them. His rule stretched over more than ten thousand li from east to west. He then rode in a yellow-roofed carriage with the imperial standard on its left, claimed imperial authority, and equated himself with China's emperor.

## 3

In the first year of Emperor Xiaowen, as he began to pacify all under heaven, envoys were sent to inform the feudal lords and foreign tribes about his succession from the previous emperor, conveying the great virtue of the new reign. He then established a town to guard Zhao Tu's ancestral tomb in Zhen Ding, with officials appointed to conduct annual and seasonal sacrifices. He summoned Zhao Tu's close relatives, bestowing high official positions and generous gifts upon them as a sign of favor. An edict was issued ordering Chancellor Chen Ping and others to recommend someone suitable for a mission to Nanyue. Chen Ping recommended Lu Jia of Haoshi, who had previously been experienced in diplomatic missions to Nanyue during the reign of the former emperor. The court then summoned Lu Jia and appointed him as Taizhong Dafu, sending him on the mission. He thus rebuked Zhao Tu for having declared himself emperor without even sending a single envoy to report it. When Lu Jia arrived in Nanyue, the King was very frightened and wrote a letter of apology, stating: "I, Zhao Tu, an old man among the southern barbarians, humbly submit to Your Majesty. In previous years, Empress Dowager separated Nanyue from China, which led me to secretly suspect that Minister Changsha had slandered me. Moreover, I heard rumors that Empress Dowager had executed all of my relatives and dug up and burned my ancestors' tombs; thus, out of desperation, I abandoned restraint and violated the borders of Changsha." Moreover, the southern regions are low and damp, inhabited by various barbarian tribes. To the east, the Minyue people with a thousand warriors call themselves kings; to the west, the Ou Luo Luoguo also claim royal titles. "As an old subject, I rashly assumed the imperial title merely for my own amusement; how could I dare to report this to Your Majesty, the Son of Heaven!" He then kowtowed in apology and expressed his wish to remain a vassal subject forever, fulfilling his duties by offering tribute. At this, he issued an order throughout the country: "I have heard that two great powers cannot coexist, and two sages do not live in the same age. "The Son of Heaven is a wise emperor. "From now on, I will abandon the imperial title and remove the yellow-roofed carriage and left-standard." When Lu Jia returned with his report, Emperor Xiaowen was greatly pleased. Thus, by the time of Emperor Xiaojing, Nanyue submitted as a vassal state and sent envoys to pay homage and request audiences at court. However, within their own country, the people of Nanyue still secretly retained their former titles and names. When they sent envoys to the Son of Heaven, however, they followed imperial protocol as vassal lords would. He died in the fourth year of Jianyuan.

## 4

Zhao Tu's grandson, Hu, became King of Nanyue. At this time, the King of Minyue, Ying, raised an army to attack Nanyue's border towns. In response, Hu sent a memorial to the court stating: "Both Yue states are vassal subjects; they must not raise arms against each other without authorization." "Now that Minyue has raised an army to invade my territory, I dare not retaliate with force. Only the Son of Heaven can issue a decree on this matter." The Son of Heaven commended Nanyue for its righteousness in upholding its duties and respecting the agreement, so he raised an army, dispatched two generals to punish Minyue. Before the troops had crossed the mountains, Ying's younger brother Yushan killed him and surrendered to the Han forces. The campaign was then halted.

## 5

The Son of Heaven sent Zhuang Zhu to convey his message to King Hu of Nanyue, who kowtowed and said: "Your Majesty has raised an army for my sake to punish Minyue; I am unworthy and cannot repay such virtue with my life!" He sent his crown prince Yingqi to serve as a palace guard in the imperial court. He said to Zhuang Zhu: "My country has just suffered an attack, and envoys are already on their way." "King Hu is preparing day and night for a personal audience with the Son of Heaven." After Zhuang Zhu left, Hu's senior ministers advised him: "The Han raised an army to punish Ying, and this action has already stirred unrest in Nanyue. Moreover, our late king once said that we must serve the Son of Heaven without failing to observe proper etiquette; in essence, one cannot gain favor by merely speaking flatteringly and requesting an audience. "If we go for an audience, we will not be allowed to return—it would spell the end of our state." Therefore, King Hu claimed illness and ultimately did not make the journey to see the Son of Heaven. Some ten or more years later, when King Hu's illness worsened, Crown Prince Yingqi requested permission to return home. After King Hu died, he was posthumously honored with the title "Wenwang."

## 6

Yingqi succeeded to the throne and immediately concealed his ancestor's imperial seal of Emperor Wu. While Yingqi was serving as a palace guard in Chang'an, he married a woman from the Jiu family of Handan and had a son named Xing. After ascending the throne, Yingqi submitted a memorial requesting to appoint his wife from the Jiu family as queen and their son Xing as heir. The Han court repeatedly sent envoys to advise Yingqi, but he still enjoyed exercising arbitrary power over life and death. He feared that upon an audience with the Son of Heaven, he would be required to follow Han laws like the internal feudal lords. Therefore, he continued to claim illness and never went in person for an audience. He sent his son Cigong to serve as a palace guard at court. After Yingqi died, he was posthumously titled "Mingwang."

## 7

The crown prince Xing succeeded to the throne, and his mother became empress dowager. Before she had become Yingqi's concubine, the Empress Dowager once had an affair with An Guoshaoji of Balingle. After Yingqi's death, in the fourth year of Yuanding, the Han court sent An Guoshaoji to advise King Xing and his maternal grandmother, the Queen Dowager, to come to court for an audience on equal terms with the internal feudal lords; They ordered eloquent officials such as Jian Daifu Zhong Jun to deliver the message, and brave warriors like Wei Chen to assist where needed. General Weiyi Lu Bode led troops stationed in Guiyang to await the envoys. King Xing was young, and his maternal grandmother was a native of China who had once been involved with An Guoshaoji. She now resumed her private relationship with him during the mission. The people in the country learned about this, and many no longer supported the Queen Dowager. Fearing that unrest might arise, the Queen Dowager also wanted to rely on Han authority and repeatedly urged King Xing and his ministers to request formal incorporation into the Han empire. Through the envoys, they submitted a memorial requesting treatment on par with internal feudal lords—paying homage once every three years and having border customs abolished. The Son of Heaven approved the request, granting the Prime Minister Lu Jia a silver seal, as well as seals for the posts of Neishi, Zhongwei, and Taifu; other officials could be appointed at their own discretion. They abolished the old punishments of branding and amputation, adopting Han laws instead, placing them on equal footing with internal feudal lords. The envoys remained in Nanyue to oversee and pacify the region. King Xing and his maternal grandmother prepared their attire, packed lavish gifts, and made arrangements for an audience at court.

## 8

The prime minister Lu Jia was now very old, having served under three kings. Over seventy members of his family held official positions as senior magistrates; all the men were married to princesses of Nanyue, and all the women had married sons or brothers from the royal clan. He also maintained connections with Qin Wang of Cangwu. He was highly influential within the country; the Yue people trusted him, and many served as his informants. His popularity among the people surpassed even that of the king. When King Xing submitted a memorial to the Han court, Lu Jia repeatedly advised him against it, but the king did not listen. Lu Jia harbored rebellious intentions and often claimed illness to avoid meeting with Han envoys. The envoys kept a close watch on Lu Jia, but the situation had not yet reached a point where he could be punished. King Xing and his maternal grandmother also feared that Lu Jia might act first, so they held a banquet, using the authority of the Han envoys to plot against Lu Jia. The envoys sat facing east, the Queen Dowager faced south, and King Xing faced north. Lu Jia and his ministers all sat facing west, seated in attendance for the banquet. Lu Jia's younger brother was a general, commanding troops stationed outside the palace. As the wine was served, the Queen Dowager asked Lu Jia: "The incorporation of Nanyue into Han is in our country's interest. Yet you, Chancellor, have found it inconvenient—why so?" She did this to provoke the envoys' anger. The envoys hesitated and refrained from taking action, so no one dared to move against Lu Jia. When Lu Jia noticed that his informants were not present as expected, he immediately rose and left the banquet. The Queen Dowager was furious and wanted to stab Lu Jia with a spear, but King Xing stopped her. Lu Jia then left the palace, ordered his brother's troops to return to their barracks, claimed illness, and refused to see King Xing or the envoys. He secretly conspired with senior ministers to rebel. King Xing had no intention of killing Lu Jia, and knowing this, Lu Jia delayed his rebellion for several months without taking action. The Queen Dowager had immoral conduct; the people did not support her, and she wanted to kill Lu Jia alone but lacked the strength to do so.

## 9

The Son of Heaven learned that Lu Jia had defied King Xing, and since both the king and his maternal grandmother were weak and unable to control him, the envoys hesitated without taking decisive action. Moreover, since King Xing and the Queen Dowager had already submitted to Han rule, it was believed that only Lu Jia's rebellion existed, which was not sufficient reason to launch a military campaign. The court therefore planned to send Zhuang Can with two thousand men as envoys. Zhuang Can said: "If we go in good faith, a few people would be enough; if we go with force, two thousand men will not be sufficient." His argument was rejected, and the Son of Heaven dismissed Zhuang Can. A brave warrior named Han Qianqiu, former magisterial officer of Jibei, declared passionately: "This tiny Nanyue has the king and queen dowager in support; only Chancellor Lu Jia is a threat. I wish to lead two hundred brave warriors and will surely behead Lu Jia as my report." Thus, the Son of Heaven dispatched Han Qianqiu and the Queen Dowager's younger brother Jiu Le with two thousand men to enter Nanyue territory. Lu Jia and others then openly rebelled, issuing a decree throughout the country: "The king is young. The queen dowager is a native of China, and she has been involved in an affair with the envoys. She wants to submit entirely to Han rule and plans to take all our late kings' treasures to offer as tribute to the Son of Heaven for her own favor. She will bring many people along on the journey to Chang'an and sell them into slavery." "She seeks only short-term profit for herself, without regard for the Zhao family's state or considering the long-term interests of future generations." With this as justification, Lu Jia and his brother led their troops to attack and kill King Xing, the Queen Dowager, and the Han envoys. They sent messengers to inform Qin Wang of Cangwu and the various commanderies and counties, proclaiming King Mingwang's eldest son, Yue Wizi Shuyang Hou Jiande, as the new king. Meanwhile, Han Qianqiu's forces entered Nanyue and captured several small towns. Later, the Nanyue people cleared a path and provided supplies for them; but before they reached Panyu by forty li, the Nanyue forces attacked Han Qianqiu et al. and completely annihilated them. They sent people to place the seal of the Han envoys' staff in a wooden case and put it on the frontier, while sending flattering words to apologize for their offense, and mobilized troops to guard key strategic locations. At this time, the Son of Heaven said: "Although Han Qianqiu achieved no success, he was still the outstanding leader among the vanguard troops." He posthumously enfeoffed his son Yannian as Hou of Cheng'an. 龙头 Jiu Le, whose sister was the Queen Dowager and who first expressed a desire to submit to Han rule, had his son Guangde enfeoffed as Hou of Longkang. Then an edict was issued: "When the Son of Heaven is weak, feudal lords wield power and criticize ministers who do not punish rebels. Now Lu Jia, Jiande and others have rebelled and declared themselves rulers with ease. I hereby order ten thousand naval troops from the southern regions of Jianghuai to go and punish them."

## 10

In autumn, Yuanding Year Five, Weiwei Lu Bode was appointed as Fu Bo General, marching out from Guiyang and advancing down the Hui River; Zhuque Duyi Yang Pu was appointed as Lou Chuan General, departing from Yuzhang and advancing down the Hengpu River; Two former Guiyi Yue Hou were appointed as Ge Chuan and Xia Li Generals, departing from Lingling; some advanced down the Lishui River, others reached Cangwu; The Son of Heaven ordered Chiyi Hou to mobilize convicts from Ba and Shu, raise an army from Yelang, and descend the Zangke River: all forces were to converge at Panyu.

## 11

In winter, Yuanding Year Six, the Lou Chuan General led an elite force to first capture Xunxia, broke through Shimen, seized Nanyue's grain stored on boats, and then advanced further forward, weakening the enemy's vanguard, and waited with tens of thousands of troops for Fu Bo General. Fu Bo General led convicts, but the journey was long and he arrived late; when he joined up with Lou Chuan's forces, there were only over a thousand men. They then advanced together. The Lou Chuan force led the way and reached Panyu. Jiande and Lu Jia both defended the city from within its walls. The Lou Chuan forces chose a convenient location for themselves, occupying the southeast side; the Fu Bo forces occupied the northwest side. As night fell, the Lou Chuan forces defeated the Nanyue troops and set fire to the city. The Nanyue people had long heard of Fu Bo's reputation, and at dusk they did not know how many soldiers he commanded. Fu Bo then established a camp and sent envoys to invite those willing to surrender, granting them seals of office and allowing them to go back and persuade others. The Lou Chuan forces fiercely attacked and burned the enemy, inadvertently driving them into Fu Bo's camp. By dawn, all within the city had surrendered to Fu Bo. Lu Jia and Jiande had fled by night with several hundred of their followers into the sea, escaping westward on boats. Fu Bo further inquired among the captured high-ranking surrendering officials to learn where Lu Jia had gone, and sent men after him. Because of this, the former jiao wei Sima Su Hong captured Jiande and was enfeoffed as Hou Hanchang; the Yue Lang Dujī captured Lu Jia and was enfeoffed as the Hou of Lincai.

## 12

Cangwu Wang Zhao Guang, a relative of the Nanyue royal family by shared surname, upon hearing that Han troops had arrived, together with the Yue commandery official Jieyang Ling Ding, voluntarily submitted to Han rule; the Yue Guilin Jian Juweng persuaded Ouluo to submit to the Han: all were granted the title of Hou. The forces of Ge Chuan and Xia Li generals, as well as those raised by Chiyi Hou from Yelang had not yet descended when Nanyue was already pacified. Thus, the region became nine commanderies. Fu Bo General was further enfeoffed with additional fiefs. The Lou Chuan General's troops, for their role in breaching the defenses, were granted the title of Jiangliang Hou. From the time You Tu first became king, five generations and ninety-three years passed before the state was destroyed.

## 13

Grand Historian said: King You Tu's rule originally stemmed from Ren Xiao. He encountered the early Han dynasty, when it was newly established, and was listed among the feudal lords. LONGLV's defeat by a wetland plague allowed You Tu to grow increasingly arrogant. The conflict between Ouluo and Nanyue caused instability in the region. When Han troops approached the border, Ying Qi entered court to pay homage. Later, when the state was lost, it was due to Jiu's wife; Lu Jia's petty loyalty caused You Tu to have no descendants. The Lou Chuan forces followed their own desires, becoming complacent and arrogant, thus losing direction; Fu Bo, in dire straits, became wiser and more resourceful, turning misfortune into fortune. The transformation between success and failure is like the correction of a mistake with ink.
