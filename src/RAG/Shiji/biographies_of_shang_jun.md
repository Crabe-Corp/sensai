# Biographies of Shang Jun

## 1

Shang Jun was a minor branch of the noble family in Wei, whose given name was Yang and surname was Gongsun; his ancestors originally belonged to the Ji clan. Yang was young and fond of legalist doctrines, serving as Zhongshu Zi under Gongshu Zuo, the prime minister of Wei. Gongshu Zuo knew he was virtuous but had not yet recommended him. When Gongshu fell ill, King Hui of Wei personally visited to inquire about his condition and said, "If General Gongshu's illness becomes untreatable, what will become of the state?" Gongshu said, "My Zhongshu Zi Gongsun Yang is young but possesses extraordinary talent; I wish Your Majesty would entrust the entire state to him." The king remained silent. As the king was about to leave, Gongshu dismissed the attendants and said, "If Your Majesty does not listen to or employ Yang, you must kill him; do not allow him to leave the borders." The king nodded in agreement and left. Gongshu Zuo summoned Yang and apologized, saying, "Earlier today the king asked who could be made prime minister; I recommended you, but the king's expression showed he did not agree with me." "I first considered my lord and then my subject, so I told the king that if you were not employed, he should kill you." "The king agreed with me." "You may leave quickly now, or you will soon be captured." Yang said, "If that king cannot follow your words to appoint me as an official, how could he possibly follow your words to kill me?" He ultimately did not leave. After King Hui left, he said to his attendants, "General Gongshu is very ill. It's pitiful; he wanted me to entrust the state to Gongsun Yang. Wasn't that absurd!"

## 2

After Gongshu died, Gongsun Yang heard that King Xiao of Qin had issued an edict to the country seeking virtuous men who could help restore Duke Mu's achievements and reclaim lost eastern territories. Thus, he went west into Qin and sought an audience with King Xiao through Jing Jian, one of the king's favored ministers. After King Xiao met Wei Yang, they discussed matters for a long time; however, King Xiao often fell asleep and did not listen. After the meeting ended, King Xiao was angry with Jing Jian and said, "Your guest is merely a fool; how could he be of any use!" Jing Jian rebuked Wei Yang for this. Wei Yang said, "I advised the king with the Way of the Son of Heaven, but his mind was not enlightened." Five days later, King Xiao again requested to see Wei Yang. Wei Yang met with King Xiao again; the king became more attentive, but still did not grasp the main points. After the meeting ended, King Xia once again rebuked Jing Jian, and Jing Jian also rebuked Wei Yang. Wei Yang said, "I advised the king about the Way of Kingship but he still did not understand." "Please allow me to see him again." Wei Yang met with King Xiao once more, and the king found his advice good but still did not employ it. After the meeting ended, he left. King Xiao said to Jing Jian, "Your guest is capable; we can discuss matters with him." Wei Yang said, "I advised the king on the Way of Hegemony, and his intention was to employ it already. "If he truly sees me again, I will know what to do." Wei Yang once again met with King Xiao. The king conversed with him, not realizing that his knees had moved forward on the mat. They spoke for several days without tiring of each other's company. Jing Jian asked, "What is it that has captured my lord the king's attention? "My lord the king is very pleased." Wei Yang said, "I advised my lord on the way of emperors and kings compared to the Three Dynasties, but he replied, 'That is too distant; I cannot wait.' "A virtuous ruler should achieve fame in his own lifetime and be renowned throughout the world; how could he idly wait for dozens or hundreds of years to become an emperor or king?"' Therefore, I advised him with strategies for a strong state, and he was greatly pleased. "Yet it is difficult to compare one's virtue to that of the Yin and Zhou dynasties."

## 3

After King Xiao employed Wei Yang, Yang wanted to enact legal reforms but feared criticism from all under heaven. Wei Yang said, "Hesitation in action brings no reputation; hesitation in undertaking tasks yields no achievement. Moreover, those who possess extraordinary conduct are naturally criticized by the world; those with unique insight and foresight will inevitably be mocked by the common people. The foolish are ignorant of completed affairs, while the wise foresee matters before they arise. The people cannot be involved in planning from the beginning but can rejoice in success once it is achieved. Those who discuss supreme virtue do not conform to vulgar customs, and those who accomplish great feats do not seek counsel from the masses. Therefore, a sage, if it can strengthen the state, does not adhere to old laws; if it can benefit the people, he does not follow traditional rites." King Xiao said, "Well said." Gan Long said, "Not necessarily. A sage does not change the people to teach them; a wise ruler does not alter laws to govern." By teaching according to the nature of the people, one achieves success without great effort; governing by existing laws allows officials to be familiar with them and the people to feel secure." Wei Yang said, "What Long has said is merely conventional opinion. Ordinary people are content with old customs, and scholars are immersed in what they have heard. With these two types of people, it is sufficient to hold office and uphold the law; however, one cannot discuss matters beyond legal reform with them. The Three Dynasties achieved kingship through different rites, and the Five Hegemons attained hegemony by following different laws. Wise men create laws; foolish men are bound by them; virtuous people reform rites, while the unworthy remain confined to them." Du Zhi said, "Unless there is a benefit of one hundred percent, do not change laws; unless the achievement is tenfold, do not replace instruments. "Following ancient laws avoids mistakes; adhering to rites prevents deviation." Wei Yang said, "There is no single way to govern a well-ordered world; what benefits the state need not follow ancient laws. Therefore, Tang and Wu did not follow antiquity yet became kings, while Xia and Yin did not reform rites and perished. Those who oppose ancient ways should not be condemned, and those who merely follow rites are not particularly commendable." King Xiao said, "Well said." He appointed Wei Yang as Zuo Shuzhang and finally established the legal reforms.

## 4

The order required the people to be organized into groups of ten and five, with mutual supervision and collective punishment for crimes. Those who did not report criminals were beheaded at the waist; those who reported them received rewards equal to those for killing an enemy's head, while those concealing criminals were punished as severely as deserters. For families with two or more adult sons who did not separate into different households, their taxes would be doubled. Those with military merit were awarded the highest ranks according to their achievements; those who fought in private brawls received punishments of varying severity depending on the seriousness of the offense. Those who diligently engaged in farming and weaving, producing more grain and cloth, would be exempted from corvée labor. Those who pursued commerce for profit or were lazy and became poor would have their families taken as state slaves. Members of the royal clan without military merit could not be recorded in the family register. The ranks and grades of nobles were clearly defined, with land, houses, concubines, and clothing allocated according to household status. Those with merit would be honored and distinguished; those without merit, even if wealthy, could not enjoy luxury or splendor.

## 5

After the edict was prepared but not yet announced, fearing that the people would not believe it, they erected a wooden pole three zhang tall at the southern gate of the capital market square and offered ten gold pieces to any citizen who could move it to the northern gate. The people found this strange, and no one dared to move it. They then announced again, "Those who can move it will be given fifty gold pieces." One person moved the pole, and immediately received fifty gold pieces to demonstrate that there would be no deception. Finally, they issued the decree.

## 6

After one year of implementing the order among the people, thousands of Qin citizens in the capital complained about the initial decree being inconvenient. At this time, the crown prince violated the law. Wei Yang said, "The failure to enforce laws begins with those in high positions breaking them." He was about to punish the crown prince according to law. The crown prince is the heir of the sovereign and could not be punished directly, so his tutor Gongzi Qian was subjected to corporal punishment, and his teacher Gongsun Jia was branded. The next day, the people of Qin all rushed to comply with the decree. After ten years of implementation, the people of Qin were greatly pleased; lost items on roads went unclaimed, there were no thieves in the mountains, and every household had enough for their needs. The people became brave in public battles but timid in private fights; villages and towns were well governed. Some of the Qin people who had initially complained about the decree now claimed it was convenient. Wei Yang said, "These are all people who disrupt order and customs," and relocated them all to border cities. After that, no one among the people dared to discuss or criticize the decree.

## 7

Thus, he was appointed as Daliangzao. He led troops to besiege Anyi of Wei and forced it to surrender. After three years, he constructed the Jique palace complex in Xianyang, and Qin moved its capital from Yong to there. He also issued an order prohibiting fathers, sons, brothers, and other family members from sleeping together in the same room. He consolidated small towns and villages into counties, appointing county magistrates and assistants, establishing a total of thirty-one counties. He established field boundaries by opening up qianmo and fengjiang for farmland, resulting in fairer land taxes. He standardized the measures of buckets, scales, weights, and lengths. Four years later, Gongzi Qian violated an agreement again and was punished with a nose-cutting penalty. After five years, the people of Qin became wealthy and powerful; the Son of Heaven presented sacrificial meat to Duke Xiao, and all feudal lords offered their congratulations.

## 8

The following year, Qi defeated the Wei army at Maling, captured its Crown Prince Shen, and killed General Pang Juan. The following year, Wei Yang advised Duke Xiao of Qin, saying: "Qin's relationship with Wei is like a person suffering from an internal illness; either Wei will swallow up Qin or Qin will swallow up Wei. "Why is that?" Wei occupies the western part of a strategic pass, with its capital Anyi and bordering Qin across the Yellow River while exclusively enjoying the benefits of Shandong. When it is strong, Wei invades westward into Qin; when it is weak, it retreats eastward and consolidates its territory. Now that Your Majesty is so wise and virtuous, the state relies on you to flourish. Yet last year Wei suffered a major defeat at Qi's hands, and the feudal lords have turned against it; we should take advantage of this opportunity to attack Wei. If Wei cannot withstand Qin, it will definitely move eastward. Once it moves eastward, Qin can occupy the solid terrain of the Yellow River and mountains, facing east to control the feudal lords. This would be a great imperial enterprise." Duke Xiao considered this advice reasonable and appointed Wei Yang as general to lead an attack on Wei. Wei sent Gongzi Ang to command its forces against them. After the armies had confronted each other, Wei Yang sent a letter to Gongzi Ang, the Wei general, saying: "Initially I was on good terms with you, and now we are both generals for our respective states. I cannot bear to fight against you. We should meet in person, swear an oath of peace, enjoy a banquet together, and withdraw our troops so as to ensure peace between Qin and Wei." Gongzi Ang of Wei also considered this reasonable. After the meeting and oath, during a banquet, Wei Yang ambushed and captured Gongzi Ang with hidden soldiers. He then attacked the Wei army, completely defeating it before returning to Qin. After suffering repeated defeats at the hands of Qi and Qin, Wei Huiwang found his state's treasury exhausted and territory shrinking day by day. Fearing for his safety, he sent envoys to cede the western bank of the Yellow River region to Qin as a gesture of peace. And thus Wei abandoned Anyi and moved its capital to Daliang. Liang Huiwang said, "I regret not following Gongshu Zuo's advice." After defeating Wei and returning to Qin, Wei Yang was granted fifteen fiefs in the regions of Gu and Shang by the state, and he was given the title "Shang Jun."

## 9

During his ten years as prime minister of Qin, many members of the royal family and aristocrats resented him. Zhao Liang visited Shang Jun. Shang Jun said, "I was able to meet you because of Meng Langao; now I would like to form a friendship with you. Is that acceptable?" Zhao Liang replied, "I do not dare to wish for such an honor." Confucius once said: 'Those who promote the virtuous and support them will advance, while those who gather the unworthy and claim kingship will fall.' I am unworthy, so I dare not accept your offer. I have heard it said: 'To occupy a position that does not belong to you is called greed for rank, and to possess a title that does not belong to you is termed greed for reputation.' If I were to listen to your words and accept this friendship, I would fear that I am being greedy for rank and reputation. Therefore, I dare not obey the command." Shang Jun asked, "Do you disapprove of my governance in Qin?" Zhao Liang said: "To listen to opposing views is called wisdom; to examine oneself is called clarity; and to overcome one's own shortcomings is called strength. Yu Shun once said: "Even the humblest can be respected." "You would do better to follow Yu Shun's way of humility and need not ask me at all." Shang Jun said: "Initially, Qin followed the customs of the Rong and Di tribes, where fathers and sons had no distinction and lived together in the same room." Now I have reformed their customs, distinguishing between men and women, greatly constructing palaces like Ji Que, making Qin's court resemble that of Lu or Wei. You observe my governance in Qin—do you think I am wiser than Wu Gu Daifu?" Zhao Liang said: "The hide of a thousand sheep is not as valuable as the fur of one fox; the nodding approval of a thousand people is not worth the forthright advice of a single upright man. King Wu was forthright and thus prospered, while King Zhou of Yin was stubbornly silent and so perished. "If you do not oppose King Wu's example, then I request to speak honestly all day without fear of punishment—would that be acceptable?" Shang Jun said: "There is a saying: words of appearance are ornamental, words to the point are substantial, bitter words are like medicine, and sweet words are like illness. "If you truly agree to speak honestly all day, your words would be my medicine." "I shall serve you, Master. Why then do you refuse?"! Zhao Liang said: "Wu Gu Daifu was a man from the remote regions of Jing. He heard of Duke Mu of Qin's virtue and longed to meet him, but lacking funds for the journey, he sold himself as a servant in Qin, wearing coarse clothes and feeding cattle. After one year, Duke Mu learned of him, elevated him from beneath the oxen to a position above the common people, and no one in Qin dared to look down on him. As prime minister of Qin for six or seven years, he led eastern campaigns against Zheng, installed three successive rulers in Jin, and once rescued Jing from a disaster. He issued decrees within the state's borders, and the Ba people sent tribute; he extended virtue to the feudal lords, and eight Rong tribes submitted. Yuyu heard of this and requested an audience by knocking on the city gate. When Wu Gu Daifu served as prime minister of Qin, he did not ride in a carriage when tired and did not use an umbrella to shield from the heat. When traveling within the state, he did not take a chariot or carry weapons; his achievements were stored in government archives, and his virtue was passed down for generations. After Wu Gu Daifu's death, men and women in Qin wept; children did not sing songs, and those pounding grain no longer chanted as they worked. This was the virtue of Wu Gu Daifu. Now, when you gained favor with King Qin through a favorite official Jing Jian as your patron, this is not the way to earn a good reputation. As prime minister of Qin without concerning yourself with the people's welfare but instead greatly constructing palaces and halls, this is not how to achieve true merit. You punished the teachers of the crown prince with branding, and inflicted harsh punishments on the people, causing suffering—this is accumulating resentment and breeding disaster. The influence of moral instruction upon the people is deeper than decrees; the people's emulation of their superiors acts more swiftly than commands. Now, you have changed policies abruptly and externally, which is not a way to provide proper instruction. You now sit facing south as ruler, calling yourself "I alone," daily punishing the noble sons of Qin. The Book of Songs says: 'Even a rat has form; but a man without propriety—how can such a man not die soon?' Judging by this verse, it is not the way to ensure longevity. Gongzi Qian has kept his doors closed and remained indoors for eight years already, and you have also killed Zhu Huan and branded Gongsun Jia. The Book of Songs says: 'He who gains the people prospers; he who loses them collapses.' These several actions are not ways to gain the support of the people. When you go out, dozens of carriages follow behind yours; armed soldiers ride in the following chariots. Strong men with broad shoulders serve as outriders, while warriors holding spears and halberds run alongside your carriage. If even one of these things is lacking, you will not leave. The Book of Documents says: 'He who relies on virtue prospers; he who depends on force perishes.' Your danger is as fleeting as morning dew—how can you still wish to prolong your life and add years? Then why not return the fifteen fiefs, cultivate a garden in obscurity, advise King Qin to honor reclusive virtuous men, care for the elderly and orphans, respect fathers and elder brothers, reward those with merit, and honor the virtuous? This would bring you some peace. If you still greedily cling to the wealth of Shangyu, bask in the favor of Qin's court, and accumulate resentment among the people, when King Qin one day abandons his guests and no longer holds court, how could the state of Qin deal with someone like you? Would it be a minor matter? Your downfall would come in an instant." Shang Jun did not follow this advice.

## 10

Five months later, Duke Xiao of Qin died and the crown prince ascended the throne. Gongzi Qian's followers accused Shang Jun of plotting rebellion, and officials were dispatched to arrest him. Shang Jun fled to the gate of a pass, where he intended to stay at an inn. The innkeeper did not recognize him as Shang Jun and said: "According to Lord Shang's laws, anyone who lodges a guest without proper identification will be punished." Shang Jun sighed and said: "Alas! Could the drawbacks of my laws have reached such a point!" He fled to Wei. The people of Wei resented his betrayal of Gongzi Ang and the destruction of their army, so they refused to accept him. Shang Jun wanted to go to another state. The people of Wei said: "Lord Shang is a criminal from Qin. Now that Qin is strong and its criminal has entered Wei, if we do not return him, it will be unacceptable." So they handed him over to Qin. After returning to Qin, Shang Jun fled to Shang Yi, gathered his followers and local troops, and led them northward to attack Zheng. Qin sent forces after Shang Jun, killing him at Minchi in Zheng. King Hui of Qin had Lord Shang drawn and quartered by chariots as a public warning, declaring: "None is more rebellious than Shang Yang!" He then exterminated the entire family of Lord Shang.

## 11

Grand Historian said: "Shang Jun was by nature a harsh and ruthless man. Observing his desire to advise Duke Xiao with strategies for imperial rule, he relied on empty rhetoric and did not match the substance of such ambitions. Moreover, he gained favor through a favored official; once in power, he punished Gongzi Qian, deceived Wei's general Ang, and ignored Zhao Liang's advice. This is sufficient to reveal Lord Shang's lack of mercy. I once read the writings of Lord Shang on opening and closing borders, agriculture, and warfare, which were similar to his actions in life. In the end, he received a bad reputation in Qin; indeed there was reason for it!
