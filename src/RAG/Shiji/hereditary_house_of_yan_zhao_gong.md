# Hereditary House of Yan Zhao Gong

## 1

Zhao Gong Shi was of the same surname as the Zhou dynasty; his surname was Ji. When King Wu of Zhou destroyed Zhou, he enfeoffed Zhao Gong at Beiyan.

## 2

During the reign of King Cheng, Zhao Wang was one of the Three Dukes: from Shanxi to the west, Zhao Wang administered it; from Shanxi to the east, Duke Zhou administered it. Since King Cheng was still young, Duke Zhou assumed regency, took charge of state affairs, and ascended the throne; Zhao Gong doubted this arrangement, so he composed "Jun Shi." "Jun Shi" did not please Duke Zhou. Duke Zhou then said, "In the time of King Tang there was Yi Yin, who received authority from the Supreme Heaven; in the reign of Taiwu, there were men like Yi Zhi and Chen Hu, who received authority from Shangdi; Wuxian managed the royal household; in the time of Zu Yi, there was a man named Wuxian; in the reign of Wu Ding, there was one such as Gan Ban: they all maintained and upheld these traditions, preserving peace and order for Yin." At this, Zhao Gong became pleased.

## 3

Zhao Gong's governance of the western regions won great harmony among the people. Zhao Gong toured the villages and towns, where there was a tang tree; he decided lawsuits and handled government affairs beneath it. From marquises and dukes down to commoners, each found their proper place, with none failing in their duties. After Zhao Gong died, the people missed his governance and cherished the tang tree, refraining from cutting it down; they sang praises of him, composing the poem "Gan Tang."

## 4

From Zhao Gong onward, through nine generations, came Duke Hui. Yan Duke Hui was in power during the time when King Li of Zhou fled to Zhi and the Regency Period occurred.

## 5

After Duke Hui died, his son Duke Li succeeded him. In this year, King Xuan of Zhou first ascended the throne. In his twenty-first year, Duke Li, Zheng Huan Gong was initially enfeoffed at Zheng. In the thirty-sixth year, Duke Li died and his son Duke Qing succeeded him.

## 6

In his twentieth year, King You of Zhou was licentious and corrupt, and was murdered by the Quanrong. Qi began to be ranked among the feudal lords.

## 7

In his twenty-fourth year, Duke Qing died and his son Duke Ai succeeded him. Duke Ai died in the second year of his reign; his son, Duke Zheng, took over. In the thirty-sixth year of his reign, Duke Zheng died and his son Duke Miao succeeded him.

## 8

In the seventh year of Duke Miao's reign was also the first year of Duke Yin of Lu. He died in his eighteenth year, and his son, Duke Xuan, took over. Duke Xuan died in the thirteenth year of his reign; his son became Duke Huan. In the seventh year, Duke Huan died and his son Zhuanggong ascended to the throne.

## 9

In the twelfth year of King Zhuang's reign, Duke Huan of Qi began to dominate as a hegemon. In the sixteenth year, he joined Song and Wei in attacking King Hui of Zhou. King Hui fled to Wen, and they installed King Hui's younger brother Tui as king of Zhou. In the seventeenth year, Zheng captured Yan Zhongfu and brought King Hui back to Zhou. In the twenty-seventh year, the Shan Rong invaded us; Duke Huan of Qi came to Yan's rescue and then proceeded northward to attack the Shan Rong before returning. The Yan ruler escorted Duke Huan of Qi beyond the border, and as a result, Duke Huan ceded to Yan the territory up to where Yan had reached, instructing Yan to jointly present tribute to the Son of Heaven according to its duties during the reign of Cheng Zhou; he also ordered Yan to resume implementing the laws of Zhao Gong. In his thirty-third year, he died and his son Xianggong became ruler.

## 10

In the twenty-sixth year of King Xiang's reign, Duke Wen of Jin convened the Jiantu assembly and claimed hegemony. In his thirty-first year, the Qin army was defeated at Xiao Pass. In the thirty-seventh year, Duke Mu of Qin died. In his fortieth year, King Xiang died and Huan Gong took over.

## 11

Huan Gong died in the sixteenth year of his reign and Xuan Gong succeeded him. Xuan Gong died in the fifteenth year of his reign, and Zhao Gong took over. Zhao Gong died in the thirteenth year, and Wu Gong was enthroned. In this year, Jin eliminated the three Xie ministers.

## 12

Wu Gong died in his nineteenth year and Wen Gong took over. In the sixth year of King Wen's reign, he died and Yi Gong succeeded him. In the first year of King Yi's reign, Cui Zhu of Qi assassinated his ruler, Duke Zhuang. He died in the fourth year and his son Hui Gong took over.

## 13

In the first year of Hui Gong's reign, Gao Zhi of Qi fled to Yan. In the sixth year, King Hui had many favored concubines. The king wanted to remove the ministers and establish Concubine Song as his successor; however, the ministers jointly executed Concubine Song. Fearing for his life, King Hui fled to Qi. In the fourth year, Gao Yan of Qi went to Jin and requested a joint campaign against Yan to depose its ruler. King Ping of Jin agreed and joined forces with Qi to attack Yan, deposing King Hui. King Hui died upon arriving in Yan. Yan enthroned Dao Gong.

## 14

Dao Gong died in the seventh year of his reign, and Gong Gong took over. Gong Gong died in the fifth year of his reign, and Ping Gong succeeded him. The Jin royal house declined, and the Six Ministers began to grow powerful. In his eighteenth year of reign, King Helu of Wu defeated Chu and entered Yingting. He died in the nineteenth year, and Jian Gong took over. Jian Gong died in his twelfth year of reign; Xian Gong succeeded him. Zhao Mang of Jin besieged Fan and Zhonghang at Chaoguo. In the twelfth year, Tian Chang of Qi assassinated his king Jian Gong. In the fourteenth year, Confucius died. In his twenty-eighth year, Xian Gong died and Xiao Gong took over.

## 15

In the twelfth year of Xiao Gong's reign, Han, Wei, and Zhao destroyed Zhi Bo, divided his territory, and the Three Jin states became powerful.

## 16

In the fifteenth year, King Xiao died and Cheng Gong took over. King Cheng died in his sixteenth year; Min Gong succeeded him. Min Gong died in the thirty-first year of his reign, and Li Gong took over. In this year, Han, Zhao, and Wei were officially recognized as feudal lords.

## 17

In the thirtieth year of King Li's reign, Yan defeated Qi at Linying. King Li died and Huan Gong succeeded him as ruler. Huan Gong died in his eleventh year, and Wen Gong took the throne. In this year, Duke Xian of Qin died. Qin became increasingly powerful.

## 18

In the nineteenth year of King Wen's reign, King Wei of Qi died. In the twenty-eighth year, Su Qin first came to see him and advised King Wen. King Wen gave him carriages, horses, gold, and silk so that he could go to Zhao, where Duke Su of Zhao employed him. He thus formed an alliance with the six states as a vertical coalition leader. King Hui of Qin gave his daughter in marriage to Yan's crown prince.

## 19

In the twenty-ninth year, King Wen died and the crown prince took over; this was Yi Wang.

## 20

At the beginning of Yi Wang's reign, King Xuan of Qi took advantage of Yan's mourning period to attack us and captured ten cities; Su Qin advised Qi to return the ten cities back to Yan. In the tenth year, the ruler of Yan became a king. Su Qin had an affair with King Wen of Yan's queen, and fearing punishment, he advised the king to send someone to Qi as a double agent in order to sow discord within Qi. Yi Wang reigned for twelve years before dying; his son Yan Kui succeeded him.

## 21

After King Yan Kui took the throne, the people of Qi killed Su Qin. While Su Qin was in Yan, he formed a marital alliance with Zi Zhi, the prime minister; meanwhile, Su Dai and Zi Zhi were close friends. After Su Qin's death, King Xuan of Qi again employed Su Dai. In the third year of Yan Kui's reign, together with Chu and the Three Jin states they attacked Qin but failed to achieve victory and returned. Zi Zhi served as prime minister of Yan, held great power and prestige, and made all major decisions. Su Dai was a Qi envoy to Yan; King Yan asked him, "What is King of Qi like?" He replied, "He will certainly not become a hegemon." King Yan asked, "Why?" He replied, "Because he does not trust his ministers." Su Dai wanted to provoke King Yan into honoring Zi Zhi. Thus, King Yan greatly trusted Zi Zhi. Zi Zhi then gave Su Dai a hundred gold pieces and followed his instructions.

## 22

Lu Mao Shou advised King Yan, "It would be better for you to cede the country to your prime minister Zi Zhi." People say Yao was a virtuous ruler because he offered the world to Xu You, who refused it. Thus, Yao gained the reputation of ceding the world without actually losing power over it. Now, if you offer your kingdom to Zi Zhi, he will certainly refuse; thus, you would be acting in the same way as Yao." King Yan therefore entrusted his country to Zi Zhi, who thereby gained great power. Some said, "Yu recommended Yi, but later appointed Qi's people as officials." And when he grew old, he considered Qi's people to be unfit to rule the world and passed power on to Yi. Later, Qi and his faction attacked Yi, seizing power from him. The world said that Yu's name was passed on to Yi, but in reality he allowed Qi to take it himself. "Now Your Majesty says you have entrusted the country to Zi Zhi, yet all officials are from the crown prince's faction; this is merely giving the name of power to Zi Zhi while in reality it is the crown prince who holds authority." King Yan therefore collected all seals from officials ranked above three hundred stones and submitted them to Zi Zhi. Zi Zhi sat facing south, acting as king, while Kuai grew old and no longer participated in politics. Instead, he became a subject, with all state affairs decided by Zi Zhi.

## 23

After three years, the country fell into great chaos, and the people were terrified. General Shi Bei and Crown Prince Ping plotted to attack Zi Zhi. linha The generals said to King Min of Qi, "Take advantage of this opportunity to intervene; we will surely defeat Yan." King Qi therefore sent someone to say to Crown Prince Ping of Yan, "I have heard of the virtue of the crown prince; you are going to set aside private interests and establish public justice, rectify the relationship between ruler and minister, and clarify the positions of father and son." "My country is small and insufficient to take a leading or following role." "Nevertheless, it will be entirely up to the crown prince to give orders." The crown prince therefore gathered his faction and a crowd; General Shi Bei surrounded the palace, attacking Zi Zhi but failing to succeed. General Shi Bei and the common people counterattacked Crown Prince Ping; General Shi Bei was killed and his body displayed publicly. The conflict lasted for several months, with tens of thousands dead; the people were terrified, and public morale collapsed. Meng Ke said to King Qi, "Now is the time to attack Yan, a moment comparable to that of Wen and Wu; it must not be missed." The king therefore ordered Zhang Zi to lead the armies from the five capitals, along with the forces from Beidi, to attack Yan. The soldiers did not fight, and the city gates were left unbarred; King Kuai of Yan died, and Qi achieved a great victory. Two years after the fall of Zi Zhi in Yan, the people of Yan jointly installed Crown Prince Ping as ruler; he became known as King Zhao of Yan.

## 24

After the fall of Yan, King Zhao ascended the throne. He humbled himself and offered generous rewards to attract virtuous men. He said to Guo Wei, "Qi took advantage of my country's chaos and attacked Yan. I am fully aware that Yan is small and weak, insufficient to exact revenge." "Nevertheless, if I can truly find virtuous men with whom to govern the country and avenge my ancestors' disgrace, that would be my wish." "If you, sir, see anyone suitable, you may personally serve him." Guo Wei said, "If the king truly wishes to attract virtuous men, he should start with me. "Those who are more virtuous than I will certainly not be deterred by a thousand li!" Thus, King Zhao built Guo Wei a new palace and treated him as his teacher. Yue Yi came from the state of Wei, Zou Yan from Qi, and Ju Xin from Zhao; scholars vied to go to Yan. King Zhao of Yan shared hardships and joys with the people after King Kuai's death.

## 25

After twenty-eight years, Yan had become wealthy and prosperous; its soldiers were eager for battle. Thus, King Zhao appointed Yue Yi as supreme general and joined forces with Qin, Chu, and the three Jin states to attack Qi. The Qi army was defeated, and King Min fled abroad. Yan's forces pursued the retreating enemy alone, advancing all the way to Linzi, where they seized all of Qi's treasures and burned its palaces and ancestral temples. The only cities in Qi that did not fall were Liao, Ju, and Ji Mo; all others belonged to Yan for six years.

## 26

In the seventh year of King Wucheng's reign, Tian Dan of Qi attacked us and captured Zhongyang. In the thirteenth year, Qin defeated Zhao at Changping with more than forty thousand casualties. In the fourteenth year, King Wucheng died and his son, King Xiao, ascended the throne.

## 27

In the first year of King Xiao's reign, the Qin forces surrounding Handan withdrew. He died in the third year and was succeeded by his son, the current King Xi.

## 28

In the fourth year of King Xi's reign, King Zhao of Qin died. King Yan ordered his prime minister Li Fu to establish friendly relations with Zhao and presented five hundred gold pieces as a wine offering to King Zhao. Returning, he reported to King Yan, "All the strong men of Zhao have died at Changping; their orphans are not yet grown. We can attack them." The king summoned Yue Jian, Lord of Changguo, and asked him about it. He replied, "Zhao is a country constantly at war; its people are experienced in military affairs. It cannot be attacked." The king said, "I can attack one with five times the force." He replied, "No, it is not advisable." King Yan became angry, and all his ministers believed the attack was feasible. The king finally raised two armies, with 2,000 chariots; Li Fu led one to attack Gao, while Qing Qin attacked Dai. Only the official Jiang Qu said to King Yan, "Having established a treaty and exchanged friendly relations with another state, we gave five hundred gold pieces for their king's wine. Now that our envoy has returned and we attack them instead, this is inauspicious; the campaign will not succeed." King Yan did not listen and personally led a contingent of troops to follow. Jiang Qu pulled on King Yan's sash to stop him, saying, "Your Majesty must not go in person; if you do, the campaign will surely fail." The king kicked him with his foot. Jiang Qu wept and said, "I am not doing this for myself, but for Your Majesty!" The Yan army reached Songzi. Zhao sent Lin Po to command its forces and defeated Li Fu at Gao. Yue Cheng defeated Qing Qin (Yue Cheng) at Dai. Yue Jian fled to Zhao. Lin Po pursued them for more than five hundred li and laid siege to their capital. The Yan people requested peace, but the Zhao people refused unless Jiang Qu was sent to negotiate. Yan's prime minister Jiang Qu went to conduct the negotiations. Zhao accepted Jiang Qu's mediation and lifted the siege on Yan.

## 29

In the sixth year, Qin destroyed Eastern (Western) Zhou and established the Sanchuan Commandery. In the seventh year, Qin captured thirty-seven cities of Zhao including Yuci, and Qin established the Taiyuan Commandery. In the ninth year, King Zheng of Qin ascended the throne for the first time. In the tenth year, Zhao sent Lin Po to lead an attack on Fanyang and captured it. King Xiao Cheng of Zhao died, and King Dao Xiang ascended the throne. Zhao sent Yue Cheng to replace Lin Po, but Lin Po refused and attacked Yue Cheng. Yue Cheng fled, and Lin Po went into exile in Daliang. In the twelfth year, Zhao sent Li Mu to attack Yan and captured Wusui and Fangcheng. Ju Xin had previously lived in Zhao and was on good terms with Pang Juan, but later fled to Yan. Yan saw that Zhao had repeatedly been troubled by Qin, and with Lin Po gone and Pang Juan now in command, wanted to take advantage of Zhao's weakness to attack it. They consulted Ju Xin, who said, "Pang Juan is easy to deal with." Yan sent Ju Xin to lead an attack against Zhao, but Zhao sent Pang Juan to counterattack. Pang Juan captured twenty thousand Yan soldiers and killed Ju Xin. Qin captured twenty cities from Wei and established the Dongjun Commandery. In the nineteenth year, Qin captured nine cities of Ye in Zhao. King Dao Xiang of Zhao died. In the twenty-third year, Crown Prince Dan, who had been a hostage in Qin, fled back to Yan. In the twenty-fifth year, Qin captured and executed King An of Han and established the Yingchuan Commandery. In the twenty-seventh year, Qin captured King Qian of Zhao and destroyed the state of Zhao. Prince Jia of Zhao declared himself king of Dai.

## 30

Yan saw that Qin was about to destroy the six states, and its troops were approaching Yishui; disaster was imminent for Yan. Crown Prince Dan secretly nurtured twenty valiant men and sent Jing Ke to present a map of the Dukang region to Qin, using it as an opportunity to assassinate King Qin. King Qin discovered the plot, killed Jing Ke, and dispatched General Wang Jian to attack Yan. In the twenty-ninth year, Qin attacked and captured our city of Ji; King Yan fled and moved to Liaodong, where he beheaded Dan and presented his head to Qin. In the thirtieth year, Qin destroyed Wei.

## 31

In the thirty-third year, Qin captured Liaodong and captured King Xi of Yan; thus Yan was finally destroyed. In this year, Qin general Wang Ben also captured King Jia of Dai.

## 32

Grand Historian said: Zhao Gong Shi can truly be called a benevolent man! Even the mulberry trees and willows still think of it, how much more so for its people? Yan (North) [Outer] was pressured by the Man and He tribes externally, and sandwiched between Qi and Jin internally; situated among powerful states in a difficult position, it was the weakest of all, nearly destroyed several times. Yet its ancestral temple continued to be honored with sacrifices for eight or nine hundred years, and among the Ji surname states it was the last to perish—was this not due to the virtue of Zhao Gong?
