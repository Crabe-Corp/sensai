# Biographies of Hilarious Characters

## 1

Confucius said: "The Six Arts are one in governance." Rites regulate people, music fosters harmony, the Book of Documents conveys historical events, poetry expresses meaning, the Yijing inspires transformation through divination, and the Spring and Autumn Annals upholds righteousness. Grand Historian said: The Dao of Heaven is vast and boundless—how great it is! Thoughtful words that subtly hit the mark can also resolve disputes.

## 2

Chunyu Kun was a groomsman in the state of Qi. He was not more than six chi and seven cun tall, witty and eloquent, frequently sent as an envoy to the feudal lords, yet never suffered humiliation. During King Wei of Qi's reign, he enjoyed riddles and liked to indulge in excessive pleasures and all-night drinking sessions, becoming addicted and neglecting state affairs, leaving governance to high-ranking officials and ministers. All government offices were in chaos, while the feudal lords encroached upon Qi simultaneously; the state was on the verge of peril and collapse, a matter of morning or evening. No one among his attendants dared to remonstrate with him. Chunyu Kun addressed him through a riddle, saying: "There is a great bird in the kingdom; it has perched in your palace for three years without flying or singing. Do you know what kind of bird this is?" The king said: "If this bird does not fly, then it will; once it flies, it soars to the heavens; if it does not sing, then it will; when it sings, it astonishes people." Thus he summoned the magistrates of seventy-two counties and districts for court, rewarded one person, executed another, and mobilized his troops to go out. The feudal lords were greatly startled and returned the territories they had invaded from Qi. His authority was maintained for thirty-six years. The account is recorded in the biography of Tian Wan's family.

## 3

In the eighth year of King Wei's reign, the state of Chu launched a large-scale military campaign against Qi. King Qi sent Chunyu Kun to the state of Zhao to request reinforcements, carrying a hundred jin of gold and ten sets of carriages and horses. Chunyu Kun laughed loudly toward the sky, his hat ribbon snapping from the force. The king asked: "Are you saying this is too little?" Kun replied: "How dare I!" The king asked, "Is there a reason for your laughter?" Kun said: "Just now, when I came from the east, I saw by the roadside a man performing a ritual to pray for good harvests. He held one pig's trotter and a bowl of wine, and prayed: 'Let my small fields be filled with crops; let my carts overflow with grain; may five grains flourish in abundance, filling our homes.'" I laughed because I saw that the offering he presented was meager yet his desires were extravagant." Thus King Wei of Qi increased the gifts, providing a thousand yu of gold, ten pairs of white jade discs, and one hundred sets of carriages and horses.  Kun took his leave and departed for Zhao. King Zhao gave him ten thousand elite troops and a thousand armored chariots. When the state of Chu heard about this, they withdrew their army at night.

## 4

King Wei was greatly pleased and held a banquet in the rear palace, summoning Kun to bestow wine upon him. He asked: "Master, how much can you drink before becoming drunk?" Kun replied: "I become drunk after drinking one dou of wine, and also get drunk after drinking ten dou." King Wei said: "Master, if you get drunk after only one dou, how can you possibly drink ten dou?" "May I hear your explanation for this?" Kun said: "When I am given wine before the king, officials of law stand by my side and the imperial censors are behind me. Out of fear, I bow low and drink nervously; in such a case, I become drunk after just one dou." If I am serving a close relative who has distinguished guests, I must wear formal attire and sit upright in respectful posture while waiting for wine to be served. At such times, if I receive occasional leftovers from the wine, raise my cup to offer blessings, and do this repeatedly, I will become drunk after no more than two dou. If it is among friends and companions, after a long time without meeting, we suddenly see each other again, joyfully exchanging old stories and sharing private feelings, then I can drink five or six dou before becoming drunk. If it is a gathering in the neighborhood, where men and women sit together, wine flows slowly as they play games of liubo and pot-throwing, forming groups to invite each other to drink, holding hands without penalty, gazing at one another with no restraint—before there are dropped earrings and behind, lost hairpins—I secretly enjoy such occasions. On these days, I can drink eight dou before getting drunk for the second or third time. As night falls and the wine flows freely, we gather closely together with cups in hand. Men and women sit on the same mat; shoes are mixed up in disorder. Cups and plates lie scattered about. When the candles in the hall go out, the host keeps me while seeing off the guests. Robes loosen at the collar, faint fragrances of perfume waft through the air. At such a moment, my heart is most joyful, and I can drink one shi. Hence it is said: Excessive drinking leads to disorder; excessive joy leads to sorrow; all things are like this. Words should not be taken to extremes, for when they reach the extreme, decline follows." This was a veiled remonstration. King Qi said: "Well spoken." He then discontinued his all-night drinking sessions and appointed Kun as the official in charge of entertaining envoys from the feudal lords. At banquets hosted by members of the royal family, Kun was often present at their side.

## 5

More than a hundred years later, in the state of Chu there was You Meng.

## 6

You Meng was an entertainer from ancient Chu. He was eight chi tall, eloquent and witty, often using humor and jest to offer veiled remonstrations. During the reign of King Zhuang of Chu, there was a horse he loved dearly. He dressed it in embroidered garments and housed it under a luxurious pavilion, placed it on fine mats, and fed it dried dates and meat. When the horse died of overfeeding, he ordered his ministers to mourn for it and wished to bury it in a coffin with the rites reserved for a high-ranking official. His attendants argued against this, saying that it was inappropriate. The king issued an order: "Anyone who dares to remonstrate about the horse will be put to death." You Meng heard of this and entered the palace gate. He wept loudly toward the heavens. The king was startled and asked why he was doing this. You Meng said: "The horse is what the king loves. Given that Chu is such a vast and powerful state, there is nothing you cannot obtain; yet to bury it with the rites for an official seems too meager. I suggest we use the rites reserved for a ruler." The king asked: "How should this be done?" You Meng replied: "I request that we use carved jade for the coffin, ornate cypress wood for the outer case, and precious woods such as pian, feng, and yuzhang to make the supporting beams. Mobilize armored soldiers to dig the grave; have the elderly and weak carry soil. Let the states of Qi and Zhao stand in attendance before it, while those of Han and Wei guard its rear. Offer a grand sacrifice with three animals (ox, sheep, pig), and grant a fief of ten thousand households to support this." When the feudal lords hear about this, they will all know that Your Majesty values horses more than people." The king said: "Have I made such a grave mistake as this?" What should I do about it?" You Meng said: "Please allow me to bury the horse as one would a domestic animal. Use a cooking stove as the outer case and copper pots as the coffin. Accompany it with ginger and dates, place wood orchid leaves beneath it, offer rice for sacrifice, wrap it in firelight, and bury it within people's bellies." Thus the king ordered that the horse be given to the imperial kitchen for cooking, so as not to let this matter become widely known throughout the world.

## 7

The prime minister of Chu, Sun Shuao, knew that You Meng was a man of virtue and treated him kindly. When he fell ill and neared death, he instructed his son: "After my death, you will certainly become poor and destitute. If you go to see You Meng and tell him that you are the son of Sun Shuao." Several years later, his son was poor and destitute, cutting firewood for a living. He met You Meng, who said to him: "I am the son of Sun Shuago." "My father was about to die and instructed me that if I became poor, I should go see You Meng." You Meng said: "You need not go far away. He immediately dressed in Sun Shuao's robes and hat, clasped hands with him, and spoke as if he were the prime minister himself. After more than a year, You Meng resembled Sun Shuao so closely that King of Chu and his attendants could not tell the difference. King Zhuang held a banquet, at which You Meng stepped forward to offer a toast. King Zhuang was greatly astonished and thought that Sun Shuao had been reborn; he wanted to appoint him as prime minister. You Meng said: "Please allow me to return home and consult with my wife. I will be ready for the position in three days." King Zhuang agreed. Three days later, You Meng returned. The king asked: "What did your wife say?" Meng said: "My wife advised me to be cautious and not accept it, for the position of prime minister in Chu is not worth holding. When Sun Shuao served as prime minister of Chu, he gave his wholehearted loyalty and upheld integrity in governing the state, enabling King Zhuang to become a hegemon. Now that he is dead, his son has not even a place to stick a cone; he is poor and destitute, chopping firewood just to support himself. To be like Sun Shuao would mean certain death." He then sang: "Living in the mountains and tilling fields is hard; it is difficult to obtain food. When one rises to become an official, those who are greedy and base accumulate wealth without concern for shame or disgrace. When they die, their families grow rich; yet fearing accusations of corruption and legal violations, they commit crimes through deceit and incur grave punishment, resulting in the death of themselves and the destruction of their family. How can one be a greedy official!" Thinking of being an upright official, abiding by the law and fulfilling one's duties, yet still dying without ever daring to act improperly. How can one be an upright official!" The prime minister Sun Shuao upheld integrity until his death, yet now his wife and children are poor and destitute, gathering firewood to eat. It is not worth it!" Thus King Zhuang apologized to You Meng and summoned Sun Shuao's son, bestowing upon him the fief of Qinqi with four hundred households to maintain his family's ancestral rites. The descendants continued for ten generations without interruption. This is an example of knowing how to speak at the right time.

## 8

More than two hundred years later, the state of Qin had You Zhan.

## 9

You Zhan was a court entertainer and a short-statured dwarf in the Qin court. He was good at making humorous remarks, yet his words aligned with great principles. During the reign of Emperor Qin Shi Huang, when a banquet was held and it rained heavily, those standing on duty under the palace steps were all soaked and chilled. You Zhan saw this and felt compassion for them, saying to them: "Would you like a break?" Those on duty at the steps all said: "That would be most welcome." You Zhan said: "I will call you, and you must quickly respond with 'Yes.'" After a short while, from the hall above came the cry of offering longevity wishes and shouting "Ten thousand years!" You Zhan stood by the railing and shouted loudly: "Guards at the steps!" The guards replied, "Yes." You Zhan said: "Although you are tall, what good is it? Fortunately, it is raining and you can stand here. I am short, but fortunately I may rest." Thus Emperor Qin Shi Huang allowed the guards at the steps to take turns resting.

## 10

Emperor Qin Shi Huang once proposed an idea to greatly expand his hunting grounds, extending eastward to the Hangu Pass and westward to Yong and Chencang. You Zhan said: "Good. Release many animals and beasts into it; if bandits come from the east, let the deer and antelope charge at them—it will be sufficient." Emperor Qin Shi Huang therefore abandoned the plan.

## 11

When the Second Emperor ascended the throne, he also wanted to coat the city walls with lacquer. You Zhan said: "Good. Although the Son of Heaven has not spoken, I will certainly request it." Coating the city walls with lacquer would indeed be burdensome and costly for the people, but what a fine idea! With the lacquered city so smooth, bandits coming will not be able to climb up. Even if one wishes to do it, it would be easy enough to apply the lacquer; however, it is difficult to build shade structures for drying." Thus Emperor Er Shi laughed at this and abandoned the plan because of it. Not long after, Emperor Er Shi was killed; You Zhan returned to Han, and died several years later.

## 12

Grand Historian said: Chunyu Kun laughed loudly toward the sky, and King Wei of Qi acted recklessly. You Meng shook his head and sang, and a woodcutter was thus enfeoffed. You Zhan shouted urgently by the railing, and the guards at the steps were allowed to take turns resting halfway through their shift. Was this not also remarkable!

## 13

Master Chu said: I am fortunate to have become a Lang official through my knowledge of classics, and I enjoy reading the anecdotes from my wife's family. Without hesitation or modesty, I have composed six chapters of humorous and amusing stories, compiled them on the left. They may be read and appreciated to express ideas, for future generations of those who take interest in such matters. Reading them will delight the mind and startle the ears, adding to the three chapters by the Grand Historian above.

## 14

During Emperor Wu's reign, there was a favored court entertainer named Guo She Ren; although his words and expressions did not conform to great principles, they made the Son of Heaven pleased. When Emperor Wu was young, Lady Mother of Dongwu Hou often raised him; when the emperor grew up, he referred to her as "Da Rumen." She would visit court once or twice a month. When she came to court, an imperial decree was issued ordering the favored minister Ma Youqing to give Lady Men fifty bolts of silk as a gift and also provide her with food and provisions for sustenance. Lady Men submitted a memorial stating: "I have some public land; I wish to request permission to cultivate it." The emperor said: "Does Lady Men want this?" And he granted it to Lady Men. Whatever Lady Men requested, the emperor never failed to listen. An imperial decree was issued allowing Lady Men to ride in a carriage along the royal road. At that time, all the high-ranking officials and ministers showed respect for Lady Men. The descendants and servants of Lady Men's family were arrogant and violent in Chang'an, blocking the way to pull down people's carriages and horses, seizing their clothes. This came to the emperor's attention, but he could not bear to bring them before the law. The officials requested that Lady Men's family be relocated and settled at a frontier location. The emperor approved the proposal. Lady Men was to enter and appear before him in person for a farewell audience. Before meeting the emperor, Lady Men first saw Guo She Ren and wept bitterly. Guo She Ren said: "When you go in to bid farewell, walk quickly but look back several times." Lady Men followed his advice and took her leave, walking briskly while glancing back many times. Guo She Ren suddenly shouted at her angrily: "Hey! Old woman! Why don't you hurry up!" The emperor is already grown; does he still need your milk to survive?" Why look back so often!" At this, the Son of Heaven felt pity and sorrow for her, and issued an edict to stop the relocation of Lady Men, punishing those who had slandered her with exile.

## 15

During the reign of Emperor Wu, there was a man from Qi named Dongfang Shuo. He loved ancient texts and classics, and had widely studied various anecdotes passed down in his wife's family. When Dongfang Shuo first arrived in Chang'an, he went to the Office of Public Vehicles to submit a memorial, which consisted of no fewer than three thousand slips. The two officials at the Office of Public Vehicles had to hold and carry his memorial together, barely able to manage it. The Son of Heaven read the memorial from top to bottom; whenever he paused, he would mark the place with an "Yi" symbol. It took him two months to finish reading it. An imperial decree was then issued appointing Dongfang Shuo as a Lang official, and he often served in attendance at the Son of Heaven's side. He was frequently summoned to appear before the emperor for conversation; the Son of Heaven never failed to be pleased. At times, the emperor would personally grant him food in his presence. After finishing his meal, he stuffed all the leftover meat into his robe and took it away, soiling his clothes completely. He was often given bolts of silk and cloth, which he would carry off with a pole. Using the money and goods bestowed upon him, he took young women from Chang'an as his concubines. He would typically take a wife for about a year before discarding her and taking another. All the money he had been given was spent on these women. The officials and Langs around the Son of Heaven often called him a "madman." When the Son of Heaven heard this, he said: "If Dongfang Shuo were to behave without such actions while in office, how could any of you compare with him!" Dongfang Shuo arranged for his son to be appointed as a Lang and also served as an Attendant Envoy, often holding a staff when sent on diplomatic missions. While walking in the palace hall, one of the Langs said to him: "Everyone considers you mad." Dongfang Shuo replied: "Men like me are precisely those who avoid the world while remaining in court. Ancient people, on the other hand, withdrew from society deep into the mountains." At one time, while seated among others at a banquet and after drinking heavily, he lay on the ground and sang: "I have sunk into the mundane world; I hide from society within Jinma Gate. The palaces and halls can shelter one from the world and preserve one's life—why must one retreat to deep mountains beneath a hut of weeds?" Jinma Gate was the gate of the Office of Eunuchs; there were bronze horses beside the gate, hence it was called "Jinma Gate."

## 16

At that time, when scholars and learned men gathered beneath the palace to debate, they jointly challenged him by saying: "Su Qin and Zhang Yi each served a ruler of a powerful state with ten thousand chariots, attained positions as high-ranking ministers, and their descendants benefited from their achievements. Now you, Master Dongfang, study the teachings of past kings and emulate the righteousness of sages; you recite poetry, classics, and writings from a hundred schools of thought, too numerous to count. You have recorded them on bamboo slips and silk, believing yourself unparalleled in the realm—this can surely be called extensive knowledge and eloquence. Yet, having devoted all your strength and loyalty to serve the sage emperor for many years, even decades, you have only attained the position of a Shilang official, with no higher rank than that of an officer bearing a halberd. Might there still be some faults in your conduct? What is the reason for this?" Dongfang Shuo said: "This is something you cannot fully understand. That was one era, and this is another; how can they be compared!" In the time of Zhang Yi and Su Qin, the Zhou dynasty was in great decline; the feudal lords did not pay homage to the Son of Heaven. Power struggles and the pursuit of authority dominated, with states capturing each other through military force, forming twelve rival powers without a clear victor or loser. Those who gained talented individuals became strong, while those who lost them perished. Hence, their strategies were accepted and implemented; they attained positions of honor, and their descendants enjoyed lasting glory. It is not the same now. Now, the sage emperor is on the throne; virtue spreads throughout the land. The feudal lords submit in reverence, and his authority deters all four barbarian regions. He extends his influence beyond the Four Seas as if it were a mat beneath him, secure like an overturned basin. Under heaven, harmony prevails, with all united into one family. Initiating actions or undertaking endeavors is as effortless as moving something from one's palm. What difference exists between the virtuous and the unworthy? Now, with such a vast empire and numerous scholars and commoners, those who exhaust their energy to present arguments and strategies, rushing in from all directions, are too many to count. They strive earnestly for righteousness but suffer hardship over food and clothing, some even losing their homes. If Zhang Yi and Su Qin were to be born in this age alongside me, they would not even manage a position as a clerk; how could they dare hope for the rank of Shilang or Changshi?" The Classic says: "When there is no calamity in the world, even a sage has nowhere to apply his talents; when harmony prevails between ruler and subjects, even the virtuous have no opportunity to achieve merit." Therefore it is said: "When times change, affairs also change." Nevertheless, how can one not strive to cultivate virtue? The Book of Songs says: "Drums and bells resound in the palace; their sound is heard beyond its walls. "A crane cries from a marsh nine miles away, yet its voice reaches the heavens." If one can cultivate virtue, what need is there to fear lack of honor! Grand Duke Tai Gong personally practiced benevolence and righteousness for seventy-two years. When he met King Wen, his teachings were accepted; he was enfeoffed in Qi, where his lineage endured for seven hundred years without interruption. This is why scholars strive day and night to study diligently and practice the Way, never daring to cease their efforts. The recluses of today, though not employed in their time, stand upright and independent, living alone in solitude. Looking upward to Xu You and downward to Jieyu, they share the same strategy as Fan Li and match the loyalty of Wu Zixu. In times of peace and harmony, they support righteousness together; yet few companions or disciples are found, which is their natural state. What doubt can you have in me!" At this, the various sages fell silent and had no response.

## 17

In the back chamber of Jianzhang Palace, within a dense grove of Chinese parasol trees, there emerged an entity whose appearance resembled that of a deer. This was reported to Emperor Wu, who went in person to inspect it. He asked the officials around him who were knowledgeable in affairs and versed in Confucian classics, but none could identify it. The emperor summoned Dongfang Shuo to examine it. Shuo said, "I know what it is. I request that Your Majesty grant me a generous serving of fine wine and millet rice before I will speak." The emperor decreed, "So be it." Afterward, he said again, "There are several qing of public land with fish ponds and reed marshes in a certain place. If Your Majesty were to grant them to me, then Dongfang Shuo will speak." The emperor decreed again, "So be it." At this, Shuo finally agreed to speak and said, "This is what is called the Zouya. Distant lands will soon come to submit in righteousness, and thus the Zouya has appeared first." Its teeth are uniform front and back, even without any protrusions; hence it is called the Zouya." A year later, King Hunxie of the Xiongnu indeed led ten thousand men to surrender to Han. The emperor then bestowed upon Master Dongfang Shuo a great amount of money and wealth.

## 18

Until old age, when Dongfang Shuo was about to die, he advised: "The Book of Songs says, 'Busily buzzing green flies alight on the fence. Kind and gentle gentlemen, do not believe slanderous words." Slander has no bounds; it brings chaos to all four corners of the land.'" I hope Your Majesty distances himself from cunning flatterers and rejects slanderous words." The emperor said, "Now then, is Dongfang Shuo really so full of good advice?" He found it strange. Not long after, Dongfang Shuo indeed fell ill and died. The Classic says: "When a bird is about to die, its cry is sorrowful; when a person is about to die, his words are kind." This is what it means.

## 19

During Emperor Wu's reign, the great general Wei Qing was the elder brother of Empress Wei and was enfeoffed as Marquis of Changping. He followed the army to attack the Xiongnu, reaching the Yuwu River before returning. He captured and killed many enemies; upon his return with merit, the emperor decreed a reward of one thousand catties of gold. As the general was leaving the palace gate, Dongguo Xiansheng of Qi, a scholar-priest awaiting imperial summons at Gongche, blocked his carriage in the road and bowed to greet him, saying, "I wish to report something." The general stopped his carriage, and Dongguo Xiansheng spoke from the side of the carriage: "Lady Wang has recently gained favor with the emperor, but her family is poor. Now that General receives a thousand catties of gold, if you truly give half of it to Lady Wang's relatives, the Son of Heaven will surely be pleased upon hearing this. This is what is called an extraordinary strategy and a convenient plan." General Wei thanked him, saying: "Master, you have kindly advised me with this convenient plan. Please instruct me further." Thus General Wei presented five hundred catties of gold as a gift for Lady Wang's relatives. Lady Wang reported this to Emperor Wu. The emperor said, "The great general did not know about doing this." He was asked where he had received the plan, and he replied, "From the scholar-priest Dongguo Xiansheng awaiting imperial summons." The emperor summoned Dongguo Xiansheng and appointed him as Commandant of a commandery. Dongguo Xiansheng had long awaited an imperial summons at Gongche, enduring poverty and hunger, his clothes tattered and his shoes incomplete. Walking in the snow, his shoes had tops but no soles; his feet were entirely exposed to the ground. People along the road laughed at him, and Dongguo Xianshen replied: "Who could walk in snow like this? Let people look closely; what is on top are my shoes, but the parts below that resemble human feet?" When he was later appointed to a position of 2000-dan rank and left the palace gate wearing a green silk sash, he went out to thank his host. Thus, those who had served together as officials awaiting imperial summons lined up at the capital's outer gate to see him off with ancestral rites. His glory and splendor on the road made his name renowned in his time. This is what is called a man wearing coarse clothes but carrying great treasures within. When he was poor and destitute, no one paid him any attention; but when he became powerful, people vied to attach themselves to him. A proverb says: "Judging a horse by its thinness leads to error; judging a man by his poverty also leads to mistake." Is this not what is meant?

## 20

Lady Wang fell seriously ill, and the Son of Heaven personally went to visit her, asking: "You should become a king. Where would you like your fief to be?" She replied, "I wish to reside in Luoyang." The Son of Heaven said, "That is not possible. Luoyang has the Wu Warehouse and Ao Granary; it controls a strategic pass and is the throat of the empire. Since the previous emperor, by tradition no king has been established there. However, among the states east of the Passes, none is greater than Qi; you may be made King of Qi." Lady Wang struck her head with her hand and cried out, "How fortunate!" After Lady Wang died, she was eulogized as "The Dowager Empress of Qi passed away."

## 21

In the past, King Qi sent Chunyu Kun to present a crane to Chu. As he left the city gate, the crane flew away along the road; holding only an empty cage and fabricating a story, Chunyu Kun went to see King Chu and said: "King Qi sent me to present this crane. While passing over water, I could not bear for it to be thirsty, so I let it drink. But when I turned around, it flew away." I was about to stab my abdomen and strangle myself in despair. Yet I feared that people might criticize our king for causing a man to harm himself over the matter of an animal. Cranes are feathered creatures, and many look alike; I wanted to buy one to replace it, but that would be dishonesty toward our king. I wished to flee to another state and escape, yet I was pained by the thought of causing discord between our two sovereigns. Therefore, I have come to confess my fault and bow in acceptance of punishment from Your Majesty." King Chu said: "Excellent! King Qi must indeed have loyal men like this!" He gave him generous rewards, twice the value of the crane.

## 22

During Emperor Wu's reign, they summoned Taihou, the governor of Beihai, to appear at the emperor's temporary residence. There was a scholar-official named Wang Xiansheng who volunteered to accompany the governor, saying, "I will be of benefit to you," and the governor agreed. The various officials in the office, including the Gongcao, said: "Master Wang is fond of alcohol and speaks much but says little that is true; I fear he cannot accompany you." The governor said, "Since Master wishes to go, we cannot refuse him." And so they went together. When they arrived at the palace, he waited for summons at the gate of the imperial office. Master Wang used his money to buy wine, drinking with the guards and attendants each day until drunk, paying no attention to his governor. The governor entered and knelt in homage. Master Wang said to the door attendant: "Kindly call my lord for me, so that he may speak from inside the gate at a distance." The door attendant called out to the governor. The governor came, looked ahead and saw Master Wang. Master Wang said: "If the Son of Heaven asks you how you governed Beihai to have no thieves or bandits, what will your reply be?" The governor replied: "I selected virtuous talents and assigned them according to their abilities; I rewarded those who excelled and punished the unworthy." Master Wang said: "Answering in this way is self-praise and claiming personal merit; it will not do. I wish you would reply that it was not my own ability, but entirely the result of Your Majesty's divine power and martial might." The governor said: "Yes." They were summoned in and brought to the steps of the palace. An imperial decree asked, "What did you do to govern Beihai so that no thieves or bandits arose?" The governor knelt and replied: "It was not my ability, but entirely due to the divine power and martial might of Your Majesty." Emperor Wu laughed heartily and said, "Ah! How rare it is to hear such words from a man of virtue and praise them!" From whom did you receive this advice?" The governor replied: "I received it from the scholar-official Wang Xiansheng." The emperor asked, "Where is he now?" The governor replied: "He is outside the palace office gate." An imperial decree was issued, appointing Master Wang as Shuiheng Cheng and appointing the governor of Beihai as Shuiheng Duwei. The Classic says: "Fine words can be sold in the market, and noble conduct can elevate others." "Gentlemen part with words of wisdom, while petty people part with wealth."

## 23

During the reign of Emperor Wen of Wei, Xi Men Bao was appointed as the magistrate of Ye. Bao went to Ye and met with the elders, asking them about the hardships endured by the people. The elders said: "We suffer because we must offer a bride to the River God, and that is why we are poor." Bao asked for the reason, and they replied: "The three elders of Ye and the magistrates have long collected taxes from the people every year. They collect several hundred thousand coins, using twenty or thirty thousand to offer a bride to the River Lord, while sharing the rest with the fortune-tellers and witches." At that time, when a witch saw a girl from a poor family who was beautiful, she would say the girl was destined to be the River Lord's bride and immediately arrange for her to be betrothed. They would bathe and wash her, dress her in new silk and fine gauze robes, have her stay at home in seclusion, and observe rituals of purification; a shrine was built by the river for ritual preparation, with crimson curtains hung up, and the girl would reside inside. They also prepared oxen, wine, food, and meals, lasting more than ten days. They would then decorate her as if she were a bride, placing her on a bed and mattress like those for a wedding, and let her float in the river. At first, they floated along for dozens of li before finally sinking. Families with beautiful daughters were afraid that the fortune-teller or witch would offer them to the River Lord, so many parents took their daughters far away and fled. Because of this, the city became increasingly depopulated, and people were impoverished; this practice had been going on for a very long time. The local saying among the people was, 'If we do not offer a bride to the River Lord for him to take, then when the water comes it will flood and drown our people,' they said." Xi Men Bao said: "When the time comes to offer a bride for the River Lord, I hope that the three elders, the witches and fortune-tellers, as well as the respected elders will accompany the girl to the river. Please kindly inform me when this happens; I too shall go to see her off." They all said: "Yes."

## 24

When the time came, Xi Men Bao went to meet them by the river. The three elders, officials, influential local leaders, and respected elders from the neighborhood all gathered, along with two or three thousand common people who had come to watch. The witch was an old woman, already seventy years of age. She brought with her about ten young female disciples, all dressed in plain silk robes, standing behind the old witch. Xi Men Bao said: "Call forth the bride for the River Lord so that I may see whether she is beautiful or ugly." They immediately brought out the girl from behind the curtain and led her forward. Bao looked at her and then turned to the three elders, witches, and respected elders, saying: "This girl is not beautiful. Please trouble the old witch to go in and inform the River Lord; find a more beautiful girl and send her later." He immediately ordered his officers and soldiers to seize the old witch and throw her into the river. After a while, he said: "Why has the old witch been gone so long? Hurry up and send one of her disciples to replace her!" They then threw another of the witch's disciples into the river. After a while, he said: "Why has the disciple been gone so long? Send another one to hurry her along!" They then threw yet another disciple into the river. A total of three disciples were thrown in. Xi Men Bao said: "The old witch's disciples are young women and cannot deliver the message. Please trouble one of the three elders to go in and report this." They then threw the three elders into the river. Xi Men Bao, with a brush tucked in his hair and bowing deeply as if holding up a jade tablet, stood facing the river for a long time. The elders and officials who were watching nearby were all terrified. Xi Men Bao turned around and said: "Since the old witch and the three elders have not returned, what shall we do about it?" He wanted to send one more official or influential local leader into the river to urge them. They all kowtowed, their foreheads striking the ground so hard that blood flowed down and their faces turned ashen gray like a corpse. Xi Men Bao said: "Alright, let us wait for them just a little while longer." After a short while, Bao said: "The official may rise now. It seems the River Lord has kept his guests for too long; you may all disperse and go home." The officials and people of Ye were greatly frightened, and from then on, no one dared to speak of offering a bride for the River Lord again.

## 25

Xi Men Bao immediately mobilized the people to dig twelve canals, leading river water into the fields for irrigation; all the farmland was then flooded with water. At that time, the people found it somewhat burdensome and troublesome to work on the canals and were unwilling to do so. Bao said: "The people may be happy with a completed project, but they cannot be involved in considering its beginning. Now, although the elders and younger generations may suffer from my actions, after a hundred years they will hope that their descendants remember what I have said." Until now, all have benefited from the water resources, and the people are well-provided for and prosperous. The twelve canals crossed the main road, and when the Han dynasty was established, senior officials considered that the bridges of the twelve canals crossing the main road were too close together, which they deemed unacceptable. They wanted to merge the canal waters and combine three channels into a single bridge at the location of the main road. The elders and people of Ye refused to listen to the senior officials, saying that it was something done by Magistrate Xi Men; they believed the model established by a virtuous ruler could not be changed. In the end, the senior officials had to accept and leave things as they were. Therefore, Xi Men Bao's governance of Ye became renowned throughout the land; his benefits flowed to future generations without end. Who could say he was not a virtuous official!

## 26

The Classic says: "Zi Chan governed Zheng, and the people dared not deceive him; Zi Jian governed Danfu, and the people could not bear to deceive him; "Xi Men Bao governed Ye, and the people dared not be deceitful." Among these three men, whose talent and virtue was the greatest? Those who understand governance should be able to distinguish between them.
