# Biographies of Confucius's Disciples

## 1

Confucius said, "There were seventy-seven disciples who received instruction and mastered the teachings," all of whom were men of exceptional talent. Virtue and conduct: Yan Yuan, Min Ziqian, Ran Boniu, Zhong Gong. Administration and affairs: Ran You, Ji Lu. Speech and eloquence: Zai Wo, Zi Gong. Literature: Zi You, Zi Xia. Shi was eccentric; Shen was dull-witted; Chai was obtuse; You was impetuous; Hui often found himself in poverty. Zi Ci did not accept his destiny but engaged in commerce, and he frequently predicted market trends accurately.

## 2

The person Confucius held in the highest esteem: from the Zhou dynasty, it was Laozi; in Wei state, Qu Boyu; in Qi state, Yan Pingzhong; in Chu state, Lao Laizi; in Zheng state, Zi Chan; in Lu state, Meng Gongchuo. He often praised Zang Wenzhong, Liu Xiaohui, Tongdi Bohua, and Jieshan Ziran; all of these were predecessors of Confucius and did not live in his time.

## 3

Yan Hui was a native of Lu, courtesy name Zi Yuan. He was thirty years younger than Confucius.

## 4

Yan Yuan asked about ren (benevolence). Confucius said, "Exercise self-discipline and return to propriety, and the whole world will then embody benevolence."

## 5

Confucius said, "How virtuous is Hui!" "A single bamboo basket of food, a gourd of water, living in a humble lane—others could not bear the hardship, but Hui did not change his joy." "Hui appears to be dull; "yet when he withdrew and examined his private conduct, it was sufficient for enlightenment. Hui is not dull." "When employed, I act; when discarded, I hide away. Only you and I possess this quality!"

## 6

Hui was twenty-nine years old when his hair turned completely white and he died young. Confucius wept bitterly, saying, "Since I had Hui, my disciples have become even more devoted."

## 7

Duke Ai of Lu asked, "Among your students, who is the most diligent in study?" Confucius replied, "There was a disciple named Yan Hui who loved learning. He did not transfer his anger to others, nor did he repeat the same mistake." "Unfortunately, he died young and prematurely; there is none like him now."

## 8

Min Sun, courtesy name Ziqian. He was fifteen years younger than Confucius.

## 9

Confucius said, "How filial is Min Ziqian!" "People do not find fault with the words of his parents and brothers." He did not serve as an official under a high-ranking noble, nor did he accept salary from a corrupt ruler. "If there is anyone who will revive me, it must be in Wenshang."

## 10

Ran Geng, courtesy name Boniu. Confucius regarded him as a man of virtue and conduct.

## 11

Boniu suffered from a serious illness. Confucius went to visit him, holding his hand through the window and said, "This is fate!" "This person of virtue yet afflicted with such an illness—this is indeed fate!"

## 12

Ran Yong, courtesy name Zhonggong.

## 13

Zhonggong asked about governance. Confucius said, "When going out of the door, treat everyone as a distinguished guest; when governing the people, act as if conducting a great sacrifice." "In the state, there should be no resentment; in one's family, there should also be no resentment."

## 14

Confucius considered Zhonggong to possess virtue and conduct, saying, "Yong could serve as a ruler facing south [i.e., in an official capacity]."

## 15

Zhonggong's father was of low status. Confucius said, "The calf of an ox used for plowing has a red coat and well-formed horns. Though one may wish not to use it, would the mountains and rivers abandon it?"

## 16

Ran Qiu, courtesy name Ziyou, was twenty-nine years younger than Confucius.  He served as an administrator for the Ji family.

## 17

Ji Kangzi asked Confucius, "Is Ran Qiu a man of ren (benevolence)?" Confucius said, "In a fief with a thousand households or a noble house with a hundred chariots, Qiu can be entrusted to manage its taxes and levies. "As for whether he is a man of benevolence, I cannot say." Ji Kangzi asked again, "Is Zi Lu a man of benevolence?" Confucius replied, "Like Qiu."

## 18

Qiu asked, "When one hears a principle, should he act on it immediately?" Confucius said, "Yes, act upon it." Zi Lu asked, "When one hears a principle, should one act on it immediately?" Confucius said, "With father and elder brothers still alive, how could you possibly act upon what you hear immediately!" Zi Hua found this strange and asked, "May I ask why the same question received different answers?" Confucius said, "Qiu is hesitant, so I encouraged him." "You is overly bold, so I held him back."

## 19

Zhong You, courtesy name Zilu, was a native of Bian. He was nine years younger than Confucius.

## 20

Zi Lu had a coarse nature, loved bravery and strength, and harbored an upright and defiant spirit. He wore the crest of a rooster on his hat and hung a pig's tusk as ornamentation, and he was disrespectful toward Confucius.  Confucius gradually guided Zi Lu with rituals; later, Zi Lu changed into the robes of a scholar and presented himself as a disciple through the recommendation of Confucius' students.

## 21

Zi Lu asked about governance. Confucius replied, "Lead by example, and work diligently." He requested further instruction. Confucius said, "Be tireless."

## 22

Zi Lu asked, "Do gentlemen value bravery?" Confucius said, "Righteousness is the highest." "A gentleman who values bravery without righteousness will bring chaos; a petty person who values bravery without righteousness becomes a bandit."

## 23

Whenever Zi Lu heard something, if he had not yet put it into practice, he was afraid of hearing more.

## 24

Confucius said, "One who can resolve a case with just one sentence—this must be You!"  "Zi Lu's courage surpasses mine, but he lacks the ability to choose wisely." "As for Zi Lu, I fear he will not die a natural death." "Wearing a ragged coat of coarse wool and standing among those dressed in sable and fox fur without feeling ashamed—this is Zi Lu, isn't it!" "Zi Lu has entered the hall [of learning], but he has not yet reached the inner chamber."

## 25

Ji Kangzi asked, "Is Zhong You a man of benevolence?" Confucius said, "In a state with a thousand chariots, he can be entrusted to manage taxes and levies; but whether he is benevolent, I do not know."

## 26

Zi Lu enjoyed accompanying Confucius on his travels, and encountered Chang Ju, Jie Ni, and the old man with a bamboo staff.

## 27

Zi Lu served as an administrator for Ji Shi. Ji Sun asked, "Can Zi Lu be called a great minister?" Confucius said, "He can be considered a capable official."

## 28

Zi Lu served as the magistrate of Pu and bid farewell to Confucius.  Confucius said, "Pu has many strong men, and it is difficult to govern." "Still, I will tell you: With reverence and respect, one can control bravery; With leniency and uprightness, one can lead the people; "With reverence, integrity, and calmness, one can serve those above."

## 29

Initially, Duke Ling of Wei had a favored concubine named Nanzi. Duke Ling's crown prince Kuaiqiu offended Nanzi and, fearing punishment, fled the country. After Duke Ling died, his wife wished to install Gongzi Ying as ruler. Ying refused, saying, "The son of the exiled crown prince, Zhe, is still alive."  Thus, Wei installed Zhe as ruler; he became known as Chugong. Chugong reigned for twelve years, during which his father Kuaiqiu remained outside the state and was not allowed to return. Zi Lu served as a local administrator under Kong Kui, an official of Wei state. Kuaiqiu then conspired with Kong Kui to rebel, planning to enter Kong Kui's residence and thus attack Chugong along with his followers. Chugong fled to Lu, and Kuaiqiu entered the capital and took power; he became Duke Zhuanggong.  While Kong Kui was rebelling, Zi Lu happened to be outside the city when he heard of it and rushed back. He encountered Zigao exiting the gates of Weicheng, who said to Zi Lu, "Duke Chugong has already left; the city gate is now closed. You may as well return and avoid taking an unnecessary risk." Zi Lu said, "One who eats his food should not shy away from danger." Zigao eventually left. When a messenger entered the city and the gates opened, Zi Lu followed him in. Zi Lu went to see Kuaiqiu, who was with Kong Kui on a terrace. Zi Lu said, "Why do you need Kong Kui?" "Please allow me to capture and kill him." Kuaiqiu did not listen. Zi Lu then wanted to set the terrace on fire, and Kuaiqiu became frightened. He sent Shi Qi and Hu Yan to attack Zi Lu, striking and breaking Zi Lu's sash. Zi Lu said, "A gentleman, even when dying, should not let his hat fall from his head." He then fastened his sash and died.

## 30

When Confucius heard of the chaos in Wei, he said, "Alas! You have died!" Soon after, it turned out that Zi Lu had indeed died. Therefore, Confucius said: "Since I gained You as a disciple, I have not heard harsh words in my ears." At that time, Zigong was serving as an envoy of Lu to Qi.

## 31

Zai Yu, courtesy name Ziwo. He was eloquent and skilled in argumentation. After receiving instruction, he asked: "Is a mourning period of three years not too long?" "If gentlemen do not practice rites for three years, the rites will surely be ruined; if they do not engage in music for three years, music will certainly collapse." "The old grain has already been consumed, the new grain has just come in; the fire-drilling for different seasons changes, and a year is sufficient." Confucius asked: "Do you feel at ease with that?" He replied, "Yes." "If you are at peace with it, then do so. "A gentleman in mourning finds sweet food tasteless and music unpleasing; therefore, he does not partake." After Zai Wo left, Confucius said: "Ziwo is lacking in benevolence! "A child lives for three years before being weaned from his parents' arms. "Therefore, the mourning period of three years is a universally accepted principle throughout the world."

## 32

Zai Yu was sleeping during the day. Confucius said: "Decayed wood cannot be carved, and a wall of dungy earth cannot be whitewashed."

## 33

Zai Wo asked about the virtues of the Five Emperors. Confucius said: "You are not the kind of person to whom I can speak of this."

## 34

Zai Wo became a marquis of Linzi, and conspired with Tian Chang in rebellion, which led to the extermination of his clan. Confucius was ashamed of him.

## 35

Duanmu Ci, a native of Wei, courtesy name Zigong. He was thirty-one years younger than Confucius.

## 36

Zigong was eloquent and skillful in argument, but Confucius often dismissed his arguments. He asked: "Between you and Hui, who is superior?" Zigong replied: "How dare I compare to Hui!" "Hui can understand ten things from one, while I can only grasp two from one."

## 37

After receiving instruction, Zigong asked: "What kind of person am I?" Confucius said: "You are a vessel." Zigong asked, "What kind of vessel?" Confucius said: "A hulian."

## 38

Chen Ziqin asked Zigong, "Where did Zhongni learn his knowledge?" Zigong said: "The Way of Wen and Wu has not fallen to the ground; it is in people. The virtuous recognize its greater aspects, while the less virtuous understand only its minor ones. No one lacks the Way of Wen and Wu. "Therefore, our teacher learns from everywhere; how could he have a fixed master?"! Ziqin asked again: "Whenever Confucius arrives in a state, he always hears about its administration. Is it because he seeks it out? Or is it given to him?" Zigong said: "Our teacher gains this through his warmth, kindness, respectfulness, humility, and modesty. The way our teacher seeks it is quite different from how others seek it."

## 39

Zigong asked: "What do you think of a person who, though rich, is not arrogant, and though poor, is not servile?" Confucius said: "That is acceptable; but it is better to be poor yet joyful in the Way, and rich yet fond of rites."

## 40

Tian Chang wanted to rebel in Qi but feared the families Gao, Guo, Bao, and Yan. Therefore, he moved his troops with the intention of attacking Lu. Confucius heard about this and said to his disciples: "Lu is the land of our ancestors' graves, the country of our parents. The state is in such danger; why have none of you stepped forward?" Zi Lu volunteered to go out, but Confucius stopped him. Zizhang and Zishi also requested to go, but Confucius did not approve. Zigong asked for permission to go, and Confucius agreed.

## 41

So Zigong went forward, arrived in Qi, and advised Tian Chang: "Your attack on Lu is a mistake. Lu is a difficult country to attack. Its walls are thin and low, its territory narrow and poorly drained; its ruler is foolish and lacking in benevolence, its ministers insincere and useless, and its soldiers and people dislike warfare. This is not a state with which you can wage war. łoż Tian Chang would be better off attacking Wu instead. "Gou, its walls are high and thick, its territory vast and deep; its armor is strong and new, its soldiers well-chosen and well-fed. Its finest weapons and elite troops are all concentrated there, and it is guarded by capable officials. This makes Wu an easy target." Tian Chang became angry and changed his expression, saying: "What you find difficult is what others consider easy; what you call easy is precisely what others find hard. Why do you advise me this way?" Zigong said: "I have heard that when danger lies within, one attacks the strong; when danger lies outside, one attacks the weak. Now your danger is internal. I have heard that you were granted three fiefs but failed to establish them, because some of the high ministers do not obey you. łoż Now, if you destroy Lu to expand Qi, win a battle and thereby become arrogant before the ruler, weaken the state to elevate your ministers, yet your own merit is not recognized, then your relationship with the ruler will grow increasingly distant. This means that you would be arrogant toward the ruler above and indulging the ministers below; to seek thus to accomplish great achievements will be difficult. "When a minister is arrogant toward the ruler, he becomes unrestrained; when his subordinates are arrogant, they will compete. This means you would be at odds with your sovereign above and in conflict with your ministers below." In this way, your position within Qi will become perilous. Therefore, it is said that you should attack Wu instead. "If you attack Wu and fail, the people will die in external battles, and your ministers' power will be drained internally. In this way, there would be no strong minister to oppose you above, nor any blame from the populace below; then you alone would hold control over Qi." Tian Chang said: "Well said. "Nevertheless, my troops have already been deployed against Lu; if I now turn to attack Wu, the ministers will suspect me. What should I do?" Zigong said: "You may hold your troops back and refrain from attacking. I will go to King Wu of Wu, persuade him to rescue Lu and attack Qi; then you can use your forces to meet them." Tian Chang agreed, and sent Zigong southward to see King Wu of Wu.

## 42

Zigong said: "I have heard that a true king does not allow a dynasty to perish, and a hegemon has no formidable rival. A weight of a thousand jin can be moved by the addition of mere zhu or liang." "Now, with a great state like Qi, which has ten thousand chariots, attacking a small state like Lu, which has only a thousand, and then competing for dominance against Wu, I secretly fear for Your Majesty's safety." Moreover, rescuing Lu would bring you renown; attacking Qi brings great profit. By doing so, you can pacify the feudal lords along the Si River, punish the tyrannical Qi, and subdue the powerful Jin. There is no greater advantage than this. In name you would save or destroy Lu, but in reality, you would weaken the mighty Qi. A wise ruler does not hesitate." King Wu said: "Well spoken. "Nevertheless, I once fought against Yue and drove them to take refuge at Kuaiji. King Goujian of Yue endured hardships and trained his soldiers; he harbors a desire for revenge against me. "You wait here while I attack Yue, then I will listen to your proposal." Zigong said: "Yue's strength is no greater than Lu's, and Wu's power is not superior to Qi's. If Your Majesty abandons Qi to attack Yue, by the time you return, Qi will have already conquered Lu." Moreover, Your Majesty is now using the name of preserving endangered states and reviving extinct dynasties. To attack a small Yue yet fear a powerful Qi would not be an act of courage. A brave man does not avoid danger; a benevolent one does not fall into poverty and hardship; a wise one does not miss the right moment; a true king never allows a dynasty to perish but establishes righteousness. Now, by preserving Yue you demonstrate benevolence to the feudal lords; by rescuing Lu and attacking Qi, you extend your power over Jin. The feudal lords will surely follow one after another in paying homage to Wu, thus achieving hegemony." "Moreover, since Your Majesty must be hostile to Yue, I request permission to go east and see King Goujian of Yue, persuading him to send troops to join you. This will effectively weaken Yue while giving the appearance of following the feudal lords in an attack." King Wu was greatly pleased and sent Zigong to Yue.

## 43

King Goujian of Yue cleared the road and personally met Zigong at the outskirts, accompanying him to his lodging before asking: "This is a remote land; why has such an esteemed official deigned to visit me?" Zigong said: "Recently, I advised King Wu of Wu to rescue Lu and attack Qi. He is eager to do so but fears Yue, saying, 'I will only proceed after defeating Yue.'" If that is the case, then Yue will certainly be destroyed. Moreover, to have no intention of revenge yet allow others to suspect you is foolish; to have the intent for revenge and let others know about it is dangerous; for a matter not yet revealed to become known beforehand is perilous. These three are the great dangers in undertaking any endeavor." Goujian kowtowed and bowed twice, saying: "I once overestimated my strength and fought against Wu. I was trapped at Kuaiji, the pain of which pierced to my bones. Day and night, with dry lips and parched tongue, I have only wished to die side by side with King Wu — that is my sole desire." He then asked Zigong. Zigong said: "King Wu of Wu is fierce and brutal, his ministers can no longer endure it; the state has been exhausted by repeated wars, and the soldiers can no longer bear it; the people resent their ruler, and high officials are in internal turmoil; Wu Zixu was executed for his remonstrations, and Taizai Pi holds power. He follows the king's faults to secure his own interests — this is a corrupt administration of a dying state." Now, if Your Majesty truly sends troops to assist him in fulfilling his ambitions, offers valuable treasures to please his heart, and uses humble words to honor your courtesy, he will certainly attack Qi. If he fails in battle, it will be Your Majesty's good fortune. If he wins the battle, he will surely march his troops to Jin. I request permission to travel north and see the King of Jin, persuading him to join forces in attacking Wu — this will certainly weaken Wu." His sharpest troops will be exhausted by the war with Qi, and his heavily armed forces will be trapped in Jin. Then Your Majesty can strike when they are at their weakest — this is certain to destroy Wu." King Goujian was greatly pleased and agreed. He presented Zigong with a hundred ying of gold, one sword, and two fine spears. Zigong did not accept them and departed accordingly.

## 44

Zigong reported to King Wu: "I respectfully conveyed Your Majesty's words to King Goujian. King Goujian was deeply frightened and said, 'Unluckily, I lost my ancestors at an early age; within, I did not measure myself properly, and incurred punishment from Wu. My army was defeated and my body disgraced, taking refuge in Kuaiji, where my state became a wasteland. It is only by Your Majesty's grace that I am allowed to offer sacrifices on the altar and perform ancestral rites — even in death, I would never forget this kindness! How could I dare to plot anything?'" Five days later, Yue sent a minister named Zhong, who kowtowed before King Wu and said: "I am Minister Zhong, the envoy of my lord Goujian, the vassal servant from the East Sea. I dare to present humble messages to Your Majesty." Now I have secretly heard that Your Majesty is about to uphold great righteousness, punish the strong and rescue the weak, subdue the tyrannical Qi, and pacify the Zhou royal house. Please allow me to mobilize three thousand soldiers within our borders; my lord himself will personally don armor and wield sharp weapons, leading the charge in facing arrows and stones." He then presented twenty suits of armor, axes, the Qulu spear, and the Buguang sword as tribute from his humble servant Zhong, saying: "These are ancestral treasures passed down by my lord. I offer them to celebrate Your Majesty's military officers." King Wu was greatly pleased, and told Zigong: "King Goujian wishes to personally accompany me in attacking Qi. Is this acceptable?" Zigong said: "No." To empty a country of its people, to mobilize all its forces, and then follow their ruler is unjust. You should accept their tribute, agree to their troops, but refuse the presence of their king." King Wu agreed and thus declined King Goujian's personal attendance. Thus, King Wu of Wu mobilized the forces from nine commanderies to attack Qi.

## 45

Zigong then departed for Jin and said to the King of Jin: "I have heard that without prior deliberation, one cannot respond to emergencies; without prior preparation of troops, one cannot defeat the enemy. Now Qi and Wu are about to fight. If they do not prevail in battle, Yue will surely take advantage of their chaos; if they win the battle against Qi, they will certainly march their troops to Jin." The King of Jin was greatly alarmed and asked: "What should we do about this?" Zigong said: "Prepare your troops and rest your soldiers in readiness for them." The King of Jin agreed.

## 46

Zigong left and went to Lu. As expected, King Wu of Wu fought the Qi forces at Ailing and decisively defeated them. He captured seven generals' troops without returning them, and indeed marched his army to Jin, where he met with the Jin forces on the banks of Huangchi. Wu and Jin vied for dominance. The Jin forces attacked them, inflicting a crushing defeat on Wu's army. King Goujian of Yue heard about this and crossed the river to launch a surprise attack on Wu, encamping seven li away from the city. King Wu of Wu learned of it, withdrew from Jin and returned to face Yue at Wuhu. After three battles without victory, the city gates could not be held. Yue then surrounded the royal palace and killed King Fuchai while executing his prime minister. Three years after destroying Wu, Yue turned eastward to establish its hegemony.

## 47

Thus, with one journey by Zigong, Lu was preserved, Qi thrown into chaos, Wu destroyed, Jin strengthened, and Yue made hegemon. Zigong's single mission caused the powers to break each other down; within ten years, five states experienced major changes.

## 48

Zigong was fond of buying up goods when they were undervalued and selling them at the right time for profit. He loved to praise others' virtues but could not conceal their faults. He often served as an official in Lu and Wei, accumulating wealth amounting to thousands of gold pieces, and eventually died in Qi.

## 49

Yanyan was a native of Wu; his courtesy name was Ziyou. He was forty-five years younger than Confucius.

## 50

After receiving instruction, Ziyou became the administrator of Wucheng. When Confucius passed through there, he heard the sound of music and singing. Confucius smiled slightly and said: "Why use a bull's knife to slaughter a chicken?" Ziyou replied: "In the past, I heard from Master that when a gentleman studies the Way, he learns to love others; when a commoner studies the Way, it makes him easier to govern." Confucius said: "My disciples, Ziyou's words are correct. What I said before was merely a joke." Confucius considered Ziyou well-versed in literature and learning.

## 51

Busang, courtesy name Zixia. He was forty-four years junior to Confucius.

## 52

Zixia asked: "What does the verse 'A charming smile, bright eyes full of grace, simplicity made radiant' mean?" Confucius said: "Painting comes after a white background." Zixia asked: "Does this mean that propriety follows?" Confucius said: "Now I can finally discuss the Book of Songs with Shang."

## 53

Zigong asked: "Between Shi and Shang, who is more virtuous?" Confucius said: "Shi tends to overdo it; Shang does not reach enough." Zigong asked: "Then Shi is better, then?" Confucius said: "Excess is as bad as deficiency."

## 54

Confucius told Zixia: "You should be a scholar of virtue, not a petty scholar."

## 55

After Confucius's death, Zixia taught in the western river region and became the teacher of King Wenhou of Wei. When his son died, he wept so bitterly that he lost his sight.

## 56

Zhuansun Shi was a native of Chen; his courtesy name was Zizhang. He was forty-eight years younger than Confucius.

## 57

Zizhang asked about how to obtain official rank and salary. Confucius said: "Listen much, suspend judgment on doubtful matters, speak cautiously about the rest — this will minimize faults; See much, avoid danger in doubtful situations, and proceed carefully with the rest — then you will have few regrets. If your words are free of faults and your actions free of regret, official rank and salary will be found within this."

## 58

On another occasion, while traveling between Chen and Cai, they were in a difficult situation. Zizhang asked about the Way of conduct. Confucius said: "Speak with loyalty and trustworthiness, act with sincerity and respect — even in the most remote barbarian lands, this will hold true; To speak without loyalty or trustworthiness, to act without sincerity or respect — how could such a person be accepted even in their own state!" When standing upright, one sees these principles before them; when riding in a carriage, they appear leaning against the crossbar. Only then can one act properly." Zizhang wrote this down on his sash.

## 59

Zizhang asked: "What must a man of virtue do to be considered truly accomplished?" Confucius asked: "What is it that you mean by 'accomplished'?" Zizhang replied: "One who is surely known in the state and certainly renowned within his family." Confucius said: "That is fame, not true accomplishment. A truly accomplished person has an upright and sincere nature, values righteousness, is perceptive in speech and observant of expressions, considers how to humble himself — such a one will surely be recognized both in the state and within his family. Fame, on the other hand, is achieved by appearing benevolent but acting contrary to it, holding this false image without doubt — such a person will certainly be known in both state and family."

## 60

Zengcan was a native of Nanwu Cheng; his courtesy name was Ziyu. He was forty-six years junior to Confucius.

## 61

Confucius believed he could understand and transmit the Way of filial piety, so he taught him. He composed the Classic of Filial Piety. He died in Lu.

## 62

Dantai Mieming was a native of Wucheng; his courtesy name was Zi Yu. He was thirty-nine years younger than Confucius.

## 63

His appearance was very unprepossessing. He wished to serve Confucius, but Confucius considered his talent shallow. After receiving instruction, he withdrew and cultivated himself; he walked without taking shortcuts, and did not visit high officials unless on official business.

## 64

Traveling south to the River Jiang, he had three hundred disciples following him; his standards for accepting or rejecting things and making decisions were clear, and thus his reputation spread among the feudal lords. Confucius heard of this and said: "I once judged people by their words, which led me to misjudge Zaiyu; judging by appearance, I erred in the case of Zi Yu."

## 65

Mi Buqi, courtesy name Zijian. He was thirty years younger than Confucius.

## 66

Confucius said of Zijian, "What a gentleman he is! If there are no gentlemen in Lu, where could such virtue come from?"

## 67

Zijian served as the magistrate of Shanfu and reported to Confucius, saying: "There are five virtuous men in this state who surpass me; I have learned from them how to govern." Confucius said: "Alas, the territory Zibushi governs is small; if he were entrusted with a larger domain, he would surely achieve much."

## 68

Yuan Xian, courtesy name Zisi.

## 69

Zisi asked about the meaning of shame. Confucius said: "When a state is governed properly, one receives an official stipend. If the state is misgoverned and one still receives a stipend, that is a shame."

## 70

Zisi said: "If one refrains from self-aggrandizement, boastfulness, resentment, and desire — can such a person be considered benevolent?" Confucius said: "It would certainly be difficult to do so; but whether that constitutes benevolence, I cannot say."

## 71

After Confucius died, Yuan Xian withdrew and lived in the wilderness. Zigong served as an official in Wei, where he traveled with a retinue of four horses and many attendants. He pushed through fields of weeds into the humblest alleys to visit Yuan Xian and express his gratitude. Yuan Xian received Zigong in tattered clothes and a worn hat. Zigong was ashamed and said, "Is Master suffering from illness?" Yuan Xian replied: "I have heard that one without wealth is called poor, but one who studies the Way yet cannot practice it is called sick. "As for me, I am poor, not sick." Zigong felt ashamed, left without saying much more, and was forever embarrassed by his hasty words.

## 72

Gongye Chang was a native of Qi; his courtesy name was Zichang.

## 73

Confucius said: "Chang is worthy to be married into a family, even though he is now in chains — it is not his fault." He gave his daughter in marriage to him.

## 74

Nangong Kuo, courtesy name Zirong.

## 75

He asked Confucius: "Yi was good at archery and Ao was skillful in boat handling, yet neither of them died a natural death; why did Yu and Ji personally cultivate the fields and thus came to rule over all under heaven?" Confucius did not answer. After Rong left, Confucius said: "What a gentleman that man is! How noble his virtue!" "When the state is well governed, he will not be neglected; when it is misgoverned, he can avoid punishment and death." After hearing the verse "The blemish on a white jade disk" three times, Confucius gave his nephew's daughter in marriage to him.

## 76

Gongxi Ai, courtesy name Jici.

## 77

lodash Confucius said: "In the world today, there are few with virtue; many serve as household retainers and hold office in the capital city; only Jici has never held an official post."

## 78

Zeng Dian, courtesy name Xi. While serving Confucius, he was asked by Confucius: "Speak of your aspirations." Dian said: "When spring comes and the light clothes are ready, with five or six adults in their caps and six or seven boys, we would bathe in the Yi River, enjoy the breeze at Wuyu, and return singing." Confucius sighed deeply and said: "I am in harmony with Dian!"

## 79

Yan Wuyou, courtesy name Lu. Lu was the father of Yanhui; both father and son once served Confucius at different times.

## 80

After Yanhui died, Yan Lu, being poor, asked Confucius to lend him a carriage for the funeral. Confucius said: "Whether one is talented or not — each of you speaks for your own son. "When my son Li died, he had a coffin but no outer case. I did not walk barefoot to provide one, because as a follower of the high officials, I could not go barefoot."

## 81

Shang Ju was a native of Lu; his courtesy name was Zimu. He was twenty-nine years junior to Confucius.

## 82

Confucius passed the Book of Changes to Ju, who in turn transmitted it to Churen Cangbi Zihong. Hong passed it on to Jiangdong's Jiaozi Yongci, who then passed it to Yan native Zhouzi Jiashu. Shu passed it to Chunyu native Guangzi Chengyu, who gave it to Qi native Tianzi Zhuanghe. He passed it to Dongwu native Wangzi Zhongtong, and Tong finally transmitted it to Zichuan native Yang He. Yang He was appointed as a Hanzhong Daifu (a high official) in the first year of Yuanshuo for his expertise in the Book of Changes.

## 83

Gaochai, courtesy name Zigao. He was thirty years younger than Confucious.

## 84

Zigao was less than five chi tall; he studied under Confucius, who considered him foolish.

## 85

Zilu appointed Zigao as magistrate of Fei Hou, and Confucius said: "You are harming the son of a gentleman!" Zilu replied: "There is a people to govern and state affairs to manage — why must one read books before being considered learned!" Confucius said: "This is why I despise those who are sly and eloquent."

## 86

Qidiao Kai, courtesy name Zikai.

## 87

Confucius sent Kai to take office, but he replied: "I am not yet confident in my ability to do this." Confucius was pleased.

## 88

Gongbo Liao, courtesy name Zizhou.

## 89

Zhou complained about Zilu to Jisun. Zifu Jingbo informed Confucius, saying: "Master, you have indeed been misled in your judgment — Liao is a man; with my strength, I could still execute him publicly in the marketplace." Confucius said: "If the Way is to be practiced, it is determined by fate; if the Way is to perish, that too is determined by fate. What can Gongbo Liao do about fate!"

## 90

Simageng, courtesy name Ziniu.

## 91

Ziniu was talkative and impetuous. He asked Confucius about benevolence, who replied: "A benevolent person speaks with restraint." Ziniu said: "If one speaks with restraint, can that be called benevolence?" Confucius said: "If it is difficult to act upon something, should one not speak of it with restraint!"

## 92

He asked about the gentleman. Confucius replied: "A true gentleman neither worries nor fears." Ziniu said: "If one neither worries nor fears, can that be called a gentleman?" Confucius replied: "If one examines oneself internally and feels no guilt, what is there to worry about or fear!"

## 93

Fan Xu, courtesy name Zichi. He was thirty-six years junior to Confucius.

## 94

Fan Chi asked to learn farming. Confucius said: "I am not as knowledgeable as an old farmer." He then asked to learn horticulture, and Confucius replied: "I am not as skilled as an old gardener." After Fan Chi left, Confucius remarked: "What a petty-minded man Fan Xu is! If those in high positions value propriety, the people will have no choice but to show respect; if those above value righteousness, the people will naturally submit; if those in power uphold trustworthiness, the people will dare not be insincere. If this is so, then the people from all directions will carry their children in swaddling clothes to come here — why would we need farming!"

## 95

Fan Chi asked about benevolence. Confucius said: "To love others." He then asked about wisdom, and the Master replied: "To know people."

## 96

You Ruo was forty-three years younger than Confucius.  You Ruo said: "The function of propriety lies in harmony, which is most precious. The way of the former kings was beautiful precisely for this reason. Whether in small or great matters, if one follows it rigidly, there will be things that cannot be carried out; knowing harmony and seeking only harmony without regulating it through propriety is also unworkable." "Trustworthiness that approaches righteousness means one's words can be fulfilled; reverence that approaches propriety keeps disgrace and humiliation far away; and when one chooses relatives without losing closeness, they can also be considered as a model."

## 97

After Confucius's death, his disciples missed him and longed for his presence. Since You Ruo resembled Confucius in appearance, the disciples together elevated him as their teacher, following him just as they had followed Master Kong. On another day, a disciple came forward and asked: "In the past, when Master Kong was about to travel, he had his disciples bring along rain gear, and indeed it rained later. The disciples asked: 'How did the master know this would happen?' The Master said: "Is it not stated in the Book of Songs?" "When the moon passes by Bi, heavy rain will fall." 'Didn't the moon stay at Bi last night?'" On another day, when the moon stayed at Bi, it did not rain after all. Shang Qu was old and childless; his mother arranged for him to take a wife. Confucius sent him on an errand to Qi, but Shang Qu's mother requested otherwise. Confucius said: "Do not worry; after Shang Qu turns forty, he will have five sons." Indeed, this came to pass. The disciples asked: "How did the Master know this?"" You Ruo remained silent, unable to respond. The disciples then stood up and said: "Master You should step aside; this is not your place!"

## 98

Gongxi Chi, courtesy name Zihua. He was forty-two years younger than Confucius.

## 99

Zihua went on a mission to Qi, and Ran You requested millet for his mother. Confucius said: "Give her six dou." Ran You asked for more, and the Master said: "Give her twenty-four dou." Ran Zi gave her fifty dou of millet. Confucius said: "Chi's journey to Qi was in a carriage drawn by fat horses, and he wore light furs." I have heard that gentlemen provide for urgent needs but do not add to wealth."

## 100

Wuma Shi, courtesy name Ziqi. He was thirty years younger than Confucius.

## 101

Chen Sibei asked Confucius: "Did Duke Zhao of Lu know propriety?" Confucius said: "He knew propriety." After leaving, he bowed to Wuma Qi and said: "I have heard that gentlemen do not favor their own side; are gentlemen also biased?" The ruler of Lu married a woman from Wu as his wife and named her Mengzi. Mengzi belonged to the Ji clan, and it was taboo to address someone by their shared surname; thus she was called Mengzi instead. "If the ruler of Lu knew propriety, who did not know propriety!"" Shi reported this to Confucius, who said: "I am fortunate; if I have made a mistake, people will surely know about it." "It is not proper for a subject to speak of the faults of his ruler or relatives; to conceal such matters is in accordance with propriety.""

## 151

Grand Historian said: Scholars often mention the seventy disciples, and those who praise them sometimes exaggerate their merits, while those who criticize them may damage the truth. Since none of us have seen their actual appearances, when discussing the records of Confucius' disciples, the list from Kongshi's ancient texts is probably most accurate. I have collected and arranged by name all the disciples' names, surnames, and characters mentioned in Analects of Confucius, leaving those in doubt blank.
