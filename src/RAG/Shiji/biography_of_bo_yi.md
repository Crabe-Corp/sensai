# Biography of Bo Yi

## 1

The scholar's collection of written works is extremely extensive, yet he still verifies reliability through the Six Arts. Although the Book of Songs and Book of Documents are incomplete, the writings of Yu and Xia dynasties can still be known. Yao was about to abdicate his position and cede it to Yu of Shun. Between Shun and Yu, all the regional officials recommended him; thus, he was tested in office, holding official posts for several decades. After his achievements became evident, then political authority was conferred upon him. This demonstrated to the world that the great vessel of kingship and the supreme lineage of a sovereign ruler could not be transferred easily, showing how difficult it was to pass on authority over the realm. However, some commentators say that Yao ceded the realm to Xu You, who refused it and fled into seclusion, regarding such an offer as a disgrace. During the Xia dynasty, there were people named Bian Sui and Wu Guang. Why are these individuals mentioned? Grand Historian said: I climbed Mount Ji, and it is said that there was a tomb of Xu You on its summit. Confucius arranged the sequence of ancient benevolent, sage, and virtuous people in great detail, such as Wu Taibo, Bo Yi, and others. Based on what I have heard, Xu You and Wu Guang's righteousness was extremely high; yet their writings are not widely seen. Why is that?

## 2

Confucius said: "Bo Yi and Shu Qi did not dwell on past grievances, so resentment was rarely found in them." "Seeking benevolence and attaining it, what resentment could there be?" I lament the spirit of Bo Yi; upon seeing the extraordinary poem, I find it remarkable. Its biography says:

## 3

Bo Yi and Shu Qi were the two sons of Gudong Jun. Their father wanted to appoint Shu Qi, but after their father's death, Shu Qi yielded to Bo Yi. Bo Yi said: "This is my father's command." He then fled away. Shu Qi also refused to assume the position and fled as well. The people of the state then enthroned their middle son. At that time, Bo Yi and Shu Qi heard that Xi Bo Chang was good at honoring the elderly, so they decided to go there and submit themselves. When they arrived, Xi Bo had already died; King Wu placed a wooden effigy on his chariot, calling it the spirit of Wen Wang, and marched east to attack Zhou. Bo Yi and Shu Qi hurriedly remonstrated by holding the horse's reins, saying: "Your father is dead but not yet buried; you are already waging war. Can this be called filial piety?" "To kill a ruler with the status of a subject, can this be considered benevolence?" Those around King Wu wanted to execute them. Tai Gong said: "These are men of righteousness." They were supported and led away. After King Wu had pacified the chaos of Yin, all under heaven revered Zhou. However, Bo Yi and Shu Qi regarded this as a disgrace; they refused to eat grain from the Zhou dynasty out of righteousness, and retreated into Shouyang Mountain, where they gathered fava beans to eat. When they were starving and on the verge of death, they composed a song. Its words said: "I climbed that western mountain, gathering fava beans." "Replacing violence with violence, they do not know it is wrong." "The eras of Shen Nong, Yu, and Xia have suddenly vanished; where can I return to now?" "Alas! My life is waning!" They eventually starved to death in Shouyang Mountain.

## 4

Judging from this, was their resentment justified or not?

## 5

Some say: "The Way of Heaven is impartial; it always favors the good." If Bo Yi and Shu Qi can be called good people, then isn't that so? Yet with such accumulated benevolence and pure conduct, they died of starvation! Moreover, among Confucius's seventy disciples, only Yan Yuan was recommended by Confucius as the one who loved learning. Yet Yan Hui often faced poverty, eating coarse food without complaint, and yet he died young. How then does Heaven reward good people? Dào Zhí killed the innocent every day, feasted on human flesh, acted cruelly and tyrannically, gathered thousands of followers to roam unchecked across the world, yet ended his days living a long life. What virtue did he follow? These are the most prominent and obvious examples. If we look to recent times, there are those whose conduct deviates from the norm and who deliberately violate taboos; yet they enjoy a life of ease and pleasure, accumulating wealth for generations without end. Or there are those who carefully choose their paths, speak only when appropriate, do not take shortcuts in their actions, and express anger only for just causes; yet they encounter misfortunes and disasters beyond count. I am greatly puzzled: If this is what is called the Way of Heaven, then is it right or wrong?

## 6

Confucius said, "Those whose paths differ need not plan together," meaning that people follow their own aspirations. Therefore he said: "If wealth and honor can be obtained, even if it means being a mere whip-holder in service, I would do it." "If they cannot be obtained, then I will follow what I love." "In the cold of winter, one learns that only cypresses and pines remain green after all other trees have shed their leaves." When the whole world is muddied, only the pure-hearted are revealed. Is it not because their value lies in that and their worthlessness in this?

## 7

"A gentleman laments dying without a reputation being passed down." Jiazi said: "Greedy men sacrifice for wealth, loyal men die for reputation, the boastful perish for power, and common people rely on life." "Those of equal brightness illuminate each other; those of similar kind seek one another." "Clouds follow the dragon, winds follow the tiger. When a sage arises, all things become evident." Although Bo Yi and Shu Qi were virtuous, it was only through Confucius that their names became even more renowned. Yan Yuan, though deeply devoted to learning, gained greater prominence by following in the shadow of a great horse. Those recluses living in caves and rocks often have aspirations and choices like these; their names are buried and forgotten without recognition—how sad! Common people in the lanes and alleys who wish to cultivate virtue and establish a reputation must attach themselves to those of high status; otherwise, how can their deeds be passed down through later generations?
