# Hereditary House of Liu Hou

## 1

Liu Hou Zhang Liang was a native of the state of Han. His grandfather, Kai Di, served as prime minister under King Zhao of Han, Xuanhui Wang, and Xiangai Wang. His father, Ping, served as prime minister to King Li and Duke Doupui. In the twenty-third year of Duke Doupui's reign, Ping died. Twenty years after his death, the Qin dynasty destroyed Han. Zhang Liang was young at that time and had not yet held office in Han. After the fall of Han, Zhang Liang had three hundred family attendants; he did not bury his dead brother and spent all his family wealth to hire assassins to kill the King of Qin in order to avenge Han, because his great-grandfather and grandfather had served as prime ministers for five generations under Han.

## 2

Zhang Liang once studied rites in Huaiyang. He went east to visit Canghai Jun. łów He found a strongman and had him make an iron mace weighing 120 jin. When the Emperor of Qin toured eastward, Zhang Liang and his hired assassin attempted to ambush the emperor at Bolangsandu, but they mistakenly hit a secondary carriage. The Emperor of Qin was greatly enraged and launched an extensive search throughout the land, urgently seeking the assassin, because of Zhang Liang. Zhang Liang then changed his name and surname and fled to hide in Xupu.

## 3

Zhang Liang once strolled leisurely along the banks of Xupu Yi. There, he encountered an old man dressed in a coarse hemp robe who came to where Zhang Liang was and deliberately dropped his sandals below the Yi. He looked back at Zhang Liang and said, "Boy, go down and get my sandals!" Zhang Liang was startled but wanted to strike him. Because the man was old, Zhang Liang forced himself to endure and went down to retrieve the sandals for him. The old man said, "Put them on my feet!" Since Zhang Liang had already retrieved the sandals for him, he knelt and put them on the old man's feet. The old man took his foot to receive the service, smiled, and left. Zhang Liang was greatly surprised and followed with his eyes as he watched him leave. After the old man had walked about a li, he returned and said, "The boy is worth teaching. Five days from now at dawn, meet me here." Zhang Liang found this strange but knelt and replied, "Yes, sir." Five days later at dawn, Zhang Liang went there. The old man was already waiting and angrily said, "I had made an appointment with you, the elder one, yet you are late—why?" He left and said, "Come five days later at dawn." Five days later at the crowing of the rooster, Zhang Liang went. The old man was again already there and angrily said once more, "You are late again—why?" He left and said, "Come five days later even earlier." Five days later, Zhang Liang went before midnight. After a while, the old man also arrived and said happily, "This is how it should be." He took out a scroll of writings and said, "Read this, and you will become the teacher of a king. Ten years from now it shall flourish." "In thirteen years, the boy will see me in Jibei, at the foot of Gucheng Mountain; the yellow stone there is I." Then he left without saying anything else and was never seen again. The next day, Zhang Liang looked at the book and realized it was Tai Gong's Art of War. Zhang Liang considered this extraordinary and often studied and recited it.

## 4

While living in Xupu, Zhang Liang became a patron of chivalry. Xiang Bo had once killed someone and took refuge with Zhang Liang to hide.

## 5

Ten years later, when Chen She and others rose in rebellion, Zhang Liang also gathered more than a hundred young men. Jing Ju declared himself the provisional King of Chu, ruling from Liu. Zhang Liang wanted to go and join him but met Duke Pei along the way. Duke Pei was leading several thousand men, capturing areas west of Xupu, so Zhang Liang joined him. Duke Pei appointed Zhang Liang as the commander of his stables. Zhang Liang often used Tai Gong's Art of War to advise Duke Pei, and Duke Pei found his advice excellent, frequently employing his strategies. When Zhang Liang advised others, they did not understand it. Zhang Liang said, "Duke Pei is probably divinely appointed." Therefore, he followed Duke Pei and did not go to see Jing Ju.

## 6

When Duke Pei went to Xue, he met Xiang Liang. Xiang Liang declared King Huai of Chu as the ruler. Zhang Liang then advised Xiang Liang, saying, "You have already restored the descendants of Chu to power. Among the noble families of Han, Hengyang Jun Chengxian is a capable 公子 and should be made king, which would help strengthen your alliances." Xiang Liang sent Zhang Liang to seek out Han Cheng, and they installed him as the King of Han. Zhang Liang was appointed as the Shentu of Han and, together with King Han, led more than a thousand men westward to reclaim territories in Han. They captured several cities, but the Qin forces would always retake them; thus, they moved back and forth, operating as guerrilla troops in Yingchuan.

## 7

When Duke Pei's forces moved south from Luoyang through Huanyuan, Zhang Liang led his troops to join Duke Pei and captured more than ten cities in Han, defeating the army of Yang Xiong. Duke Pei then ordered King Han Cheng to remain as garrison commander in Yangzhi, while he went south with Zhang Liang, capturing Wan and marching west into Wuguan Pass. Duke Pei wanted to attack the Qin army at Xiaoxia with 20,000 troops. Zhang Liang advised him, saying, "The Qin forces are still strong; we must not be careless. I have heard that their general is the son of a butcher, and such men can easily be tempted by profit." I respectfully request that Duke Pei remain in camp for the time being and send men ahead to prepare food for 50,000 soldiers. Additionally, raise more banners on all the surrounding hills to create a diversionary force. Let Li Shiqi carry valuable treasures to bribe the Qin general." The Qin general indeed defected, wanting to form an alliance and jointly attack Xianyang from the west. Duke Pei was willing to accept this proposal. Zhang Liang said, "This is only the general's desire to defect; I fear his soldiers will not follow him." If they do not obey, it will be dangerous. It would be better to take advantage of their confusion and attack them." Duke Pei then led his troops against the Qin army and completely defeated it. (He) pursued them north to Lantian, where they fought twice more, finally defeating the Qin forces. They then arrived at Xianyang, and the Qin crown prince Ziying surrendered to Duke Pei.

## 8

When Duke Pei entered the palace of Qin, he saw that there were thousands of palaces, curtains, dogs, horses, treasures, and women. He was tempted to stay and reside there. Fan Kuai advised Duke Pei to leave the palace and find another residence, but Duke Pei did not listen. Zhang Liang said: "The Qin dynasty became tyrannical, which is why Duke Pei has reached this position. Now that we have rid the world of its cruel and wicked rulers, we should adopt plain clothes as our attire. Now that we have just entered Qin and are already enjoying its luxuries, this is what is called 'helping the tyrant commit evil.' Moreover, as the saying goes: 'Loyal advice may sound harsh but is beneficial to action; bitter medicine may taste unpleasant but cures illness.' I hope Duke Pei will listen to Fan Kuai's words." Duke Pei then withdrew his troops back to Bashang.

## 9

When Xiang Yu arrived at Hongmen, he intended to attack Duke Pei. Xiang Bo then rode into Duke Pei's camp at night and secretly met Zhang Liang, wanting him to leave with him. Zhang Liang said: "I am here as the envoy of King Han for Duke Pei; now that there is an emergency, it would be unjust to flee." He then fully informed Duke Pei about the situation. Duke Pei was greatly alarmed and asked, "What should I do as a general?" Zhang Liang said: "Does Duke Pei truly want to betray Xiang Yu?" Duke Pei replied, "A minor official advised me to block the pass and not admit any feudal lords, so that I could rule all of Qin's territory as its king. That is why I listened to him." Zhang Liang asked: "Does Duke Pei really think he can resist Xiang Yu?" Duke Pei remained silent for a long time and said, "Indeed I cannot." Now then, what should we do?" Zhang Liang thus firmly invited Xiang Bo. Xiang Bo met with Duke Pei. Duke Pei drank and toasted with him, forming a bond of marriage between their families. He instructed Xiang Bo to explain that Duke Pei had no intention of betraying Xiang Yu, and that the reason for blocking the pass was merely to guard against other bandits. After meeting with Xiang Yu later, the situation was resolved; this account is recorded in the events concerning Xiang Yu.

## 10

In the first month of the first year of the Han dynasty, Duke Pei was made King of Han and granted rule over Ba and Shu. King Han bestowed on Zhang Liang 100 yi of gold and two dou of pearls, which Zhang Liang presented entirely to Xiang Bo. King Han also instructed Zhang Liang to give generous gifts to Xiang Bo and ask for the territory of Hanzhong. Xiang Wang then agreed, and thus King Han obtained the land of Hanzhong. When King Han returned to his fiefdom, Zhang Liang accompanied him as far as Baozhong and was then sent back to Han. Zhang Liang advised King Han, saying: "Why does Your Majesty not burn and destroy the 栈道 (stacked roads) you have passed through? This would show to all under heaven that there is no intention of returning, thus solidifying Xiang Wang's trust in you." King Han then sent Zhang Liang back. On his way, he burned and destroyed the 栈道 (stacked road).

## 11

When Zhang Liang arrived at Han, King Han Cheng did not allow him to return to his fief because Zhang had followed King Han. Instead, he traveled east with him together under Xiang Wang's orders. Zhang Liang advised Xiang Wang, saying: "King Han has burned the 栈道 (stacked roads), showing that he has no intention of returning." He also reported to Xiang Wang about Duke Tian Rong, King of Qi, who had rebelled, sending a letter to inform him. Because of this, Xiang Wang no longer worried about an attack from the west by King Han and instead sent his troops north to attack Qi.

## 12

Xiang Wang still refused to send King Han back to his fief, instead making him a marquis and later killing him in Pengcheng. Zhang Liang fled and secretly returned to King Han, who had already returned and pacified the Three Qins. King Han once again appointed Zhang Liang as Chengxin Hou (Marquis of Chengxin) and ordered him to accompany him eastward in his campaign against Chu. When they reached Pengcheng, King Han's forces were defeated and retreated. When they arrived at Xiayi, King Han dismounted and sat on his horse's saddle to ask: "I wish to abandon the Guan Pass and all lands east of it as if discarding them. Who can I share this achievement with?" Zhang Liang stepped forward and said: "Jiujiang Wang Qing Bu is a fierce general of Chu, and he has a grudge with Xiang Wang; Peng Yue and Duke Tian Rong, the King of Qi, have rebelled in Liang territory: these two men can be urgently dispatched." And among King Han's generals, only Han Xin is capable of being entrusted with major responsibilities and commanding a separate front. If you wish to abandon them, then abandoning these three men will allow us to defeat Chu." King Han then sent Sui He to persuade Jiujiang Wang Bu, and also dispatched envoys to contact Peng Yue. When King Wei Bao rebelled, King Han sent Han Xin to lead troops against him and thereby captured Yan, Dai, Qi, and Zhao. Yet in the end, it was these three men who helped break Chu's forces.

## 13

Zhang Liang often fell ill and never personally commanded troops; he was always a strategist, occasionally accompanying King Han.

## 14

In the third year of Han (205 BCE), Xiang Yu urgently besieged King Han at Yingyang. Fearing and anxious, King Han plotted with Li Shiqi to weaken Chu's power. Li Shiqi said: "In the past, Emperor Tang attacked Jie and enfeoffed his descendants in Qi. King Wu of Zhou attacked Zhou and enfeoffed his successors in Song. Now, the Qin dynasty has abandoned virtue and righteousness, invaded the states of the feudal lords, destroyed their ancestral altars, and extinguished the descendants of the six states, leaving them no place to stand even with a cone. If Your Majesty sincerely restores the descendants of the six states to power, bestows seals upon them all, then their rulers, officials, and common people will surely honor your virtue. They will all look up to you with admiration for righteousness and wish to become your subjects or servants. Once virtue and righteousness have been established, Your Majesty may face south as a hegemon; Chu will surely bow respectfully and come to pay homage." King Han said: "Good. Hasten to have the seals engraved; you may proceed and distribute them."

## 15

Before Li Shiqi could act, Zhang Liang returned from outside and paid his respects. King Han was just about to eat and said: "Zi Fang, come forward! A guest has devised a plan for me to weaken Chu's authority." He informed Zhang Liang of Li Shiqi's proposal and asked, "What do you think about this, Zi Fang?" Zhang Liang said: "Who advised Your Majesty to devise such a plan? Your Majesty's cause is doomed." King Han asked, "Why so?" Zhang Liang replied: "May I borrow the chopsticks from before to outline my strategy for Your Majesty." He said, "In ancient times, Emperor Tang attacked Jie but enfeoffed his descendants at Qi because he could control the fate of Jie's death. Can Your Majesty now control Xiang Ji's life and death?" King Han replied, "Not yet." "This is the first reason it cannot be done. King Wu of Zhou attacked King Zhou and enfeoffed descendants at Song because he was able to obtain Zhou's head. Can Your Majesty now obtain Xiang Ji's head?" King Han replied, "Not yet." "This is the second reason it cannot be done. When King Wu entered Yin, he honored the neighborhood of Shang Rong with a plaque, released Ji Zi from confinement, and enfeoffed Bi Gan's tomb. Now can Your Majesty enshrine the tombs of sages, honor the neighborhoods of virtuous men with plaques, and visit the doors of wise people?" King Han replied: "Not yet." "This is the third reason it cannot be done. He distributed the grain from Juqiao and the money from Lutai to give to the poor. Now can Your Majesty open your treasuries to bestow wealth upon the impoverished?" King Han replied: "Not yet." "This is the fourth reason it cannot be done. After the Yin dynasty's affairs were concluded, King Wu transformed war chariots into ceremonial ones, turned weapons upside down, covered them with tiger skins, and displayed to all under heaven that he would no longer use arms. Now can Your Majesty abandon military force in favor of civil governance and cease using weapons?" King Han replied: "Not yet." "This is the fifth reason it cannot be done. He let his horses rest on the southern side of Mount Hua, showing to all that he had no further ambitions. Now can Your Majesty allow your troops to rest and have no use for them?" King Han replied: "Not yet." "This is the sixth reason it cannot be done. He released oxen in the shade of Taolin, showing that he would no longer accumulate resources for war. Now can Your Majesty release your cattle and cease accumulating supplies?" King Han replied: "Not yet." "This is the seventh reason it cannot be done. Moreover, all the wandering scholars in the world have left their relatives and ancestors' tombs behind, abandoned their old homes, and followed Your Majesty on your travels—only hoping to obtain a small piece of land day by day. Now, if you restore the six states and enfeoff the descendants of Han, Wei, Yan, Zhao, Qi, and Chu, then all these wandering scholars will return to serve their former lords, reunite with their relatives, and go back to their ancestral homes and tombs. With whom will Your Majesty take over the empire?" "This is the eighth reason it cannot be done." Moreover, if Chu remains strong, those six states that are reestablished will once again be swayed by it and follow its lead. How can Your Majesty then make them your subjects?" If you truly adopt this guest's plan, Your Majesty's cause will be lost." King Han dropped his food and spat out the bite in anger, cursing: "Foolish scholar! You nearly ruined my affairs!" He ordered them to quickly destroy the seals.

## 16

In the fourth year of Han (203 BCE), Han Xin defeated Qi and wished to proclaim himself King of Qi, which enraged King Han. Zhang Liang advised King Han; thus, King Han sent Zhang Liang to bestow the seal of kingship upon Han Xin as King of Qi. This account is recorded in the events concerning Huaiyin.

## 17

That autumn, King Han pursued Chu forces to the south of Yangxia but suffered a defeat and took up position at Guling. The feudal lords had promised to arrive but did not come. Zhang Liang advised King Han again, and following his strategy, the feudal lords all arrived as planned. This account is recorded in the annals of Xiang Ji's deeds.

## 18

In the first month of the sixth year of Han, meritorious subjects were enfeoffed. Zhang Liang had never personally achieved any military victories, but Emperor Gaozu said: "Devising strategies within the tent and determining victory from a distance of thousands of miles—this is Zi Fang's achievement. He may choose any thirty thousand households in Qi as his fief." Zhang Liang said: "When I first rose at Xiapi and met Your Majesty at Liu, it was Heaven that placed me in the hands of Your Majesty. If Your Majesty has found my strategies useful and they have occasionally succeeded, I would be content to receive Liu as my fief. I do not dare accept a fief of thirty thousand households." He was then enfeoffed as Liu Hou, together with Xiao He and others.

## 19

[Sixth year] The emperor had already enfeoffed more than twenty of the most meritorious subjects, but for the rest, disputes over their merits continued day and night without resolution, so they could not yet be granted fiefs. The emperor was at Nangong in Luoyang and from the upper corridor observed the generals often sitting together on the sand, talking among themselves. The emperor asked: "What are they discussing?" Liu Hou said, "Does Your Majesty not know? They are plotting rebellion." The emperor said: "Now that the empire is at peace, why would they rebel?" Liu Hou said: "Your Majesty rose from common origins, and it was with these men that you took the empire. Now that Your Majesty is emperor, those enfeoffed are all old friends of Xiao He or Cao Shen whom You love dearly, while those executed are people with whom You had long-standing grudges. Now, as the military officials calculate their merits, they realize that there are not enough fiefs to go around for everyone. These men fear that Your Majesty cannot enfeoff them all and worry that You might suspect past mistakes or have them executed, so they gather together and plot rebellion." The emperor became anxious and asked: "What should be done about this?" Liu Hou said: "Which of the people Your Majesty has always hated is most well known to all your ministers?" The emperor replied: "Yong Chi and I have an old relationship, and he often embarrassed and humiliated me. I want to kill him, but because of his many contributions, I cannot bear to do it." Liu Hou said: "Now, urgently enfeoff Yong Chi first to show the ministers. When they see Yong Chi being granted a fief, each of them will feel reassured and remain loyal." Thus, the emperor immediately held a banquet, enfeoffed Yong Chi as Shifang Hou, and urgently ordered the prime minister and imperial censors to determine merits and proceed with granting fiefs. After the banquet ended, all the ministers were delighted and said: "Even Yong Chi has been made a marquis; we have nothing to fear now."

## 20

Liu Jing advised Emperor Gaozu, saying: "Establish the capital in Guanzhong." The emperor doubted this. The emperor's attendants and ministers were mostly from the east of the Hangu Pass, and many advised him to establish the capital at Luoyang: "Luoyang has Chenggao to its east and Xiaomen to its west, with the Yellow River on one side and the Yi and Luo rivers facing it. Its defenses are also sufficient for reliance." Liu Hou said: "Although Luoyang has these strong defenses, its territory is small, no more than several hundred li in size. The land is poor, and it faces enemies on all four sides; this is not a place suitable for waging war." "Guanzhong has the Xiaohan Pass to its left and Longshu to its right, with fertile plains stretching for a thousand li. To the south it enjoys the abundance of Bashu, and to the north it benefits from the advantages of the Hu grasslands. It is protected on three sides and needs only one side facing east to control the feudal lords." "When the feudal lords are at peace, the He and Wei rivers can transport goods from all over the empire, supplying the western capital." "If there is a change among the feudal lords, one can descend along the river current, which will be sufficient to transport supplies." "This is what is called a golden city stretching for thousands of li and the land of natural wealth. Liu Jing's advice was correct." Thus, Emperor Gaozu departed on that very day and established his capital in Guanzhong to the west.

## 21

Liu Hou followed him into Guan. Liu Hou was prone to illness, so he practiced Dao Yin (guiding techniques) and abstained from eating grains, keeping his doors closed and not going out for more than a year.

## 22

The emperor wanted to depose the crown prince and instead establish Qi Furen's son, Zhao Wang Ru Yi, as heir. Many ministers strongly advised against it, but no decisive resolution was reached. Lady Lu was frightened and did not know what to do. Someone told Lady Lu, "Liu Hou is good at devising strategies; the emperor trusts and relies on him." Lady Lu then sent Chengjian Hou Lü Ze to coerce Liu Hou, saying: "You have always been the emperor's chief strategist. Now that he wants to replace the crown prince, how can you rest easy and do nothing?" Liu Hou said: "In the past, when Your Majesty was often in dire straits, he was fortunate to use my strategies. "Now that the empire is at peace, His Majesty wants to replace the crown prince out of affection. Matters between family members; even if there are a hundred of us like me, what good would it do?" Lü Ze insisted and demanded: "Then devise a plan for me." Liu Hou said: "This matter cannot be resolved by mere words. "There are four people in the world whom Your Majesty has been unable to bring under his control. These four men are now old, and they all consider Your Majesty disrespectful and contemptuous of others; therefore, they have hidden in the mountains and refused to serve as ministers under the Han. "Yet, Your Majesty greatly respects these four men." "Now, if you truly do not hesitate to offer gold, jade, precious stones, and silk, have the crown prince write a letter with humble words, prepare an elegant carriage for his journey, and send eloquent envoys to earnestly invite them, they should come." "Once they arrive, treat them as honored guests and have them accompany the crown prince to court from time to time. When Your Majesty sees them, he will surely be impressed and inquire about them." "If he inquires about them, and learns that these four men are virtuous, it would be a great help." Thus, Lady Lu ordered Lü Ze to send envoys carrying the crown prince's letter with humble words and generous gifts to welcome these four men. The four men arrived and were received as guests at Chengjian Hou's residence.

## 23

In the eleventh year of the Han era, Qing Bu rebelled; the emperor was ill and wanted to send the crown prince as general to go and attack him. The four men said to each other: "We have come here for the purpose of preserving the crown prince. "If the crown prince leads troops, it will be dangerous." They then advised Chengjian Hou: "If the crown prince commands the army and achieves success, his position as heir will not improve. "If he returns without achievement, he will suffer misfortune from this point on." "Moreover, the generals accompanying the crown prince are all experienced tiger-like commanders who once helped Your Majesty pacify the empire. Now sending the crown prince to command them is no different from sending a sheep to lead wolves; they will not be willing to exert themselves for him, and failure is certain." "We have heard the saying, 'A mother's love means her child is held close.' Now, Qi Furen receives Your Majesty's attention day and night, while Zhao Wang Ru Yi often stays by His Majesty's side. The emperor has said, 'I will never allow an unworthy son to rank above my beloved son,' which clearly indicates that Zhao Wang will replace the crown prince." "Why don't you urgently advise Lady Lu to seize the opportunity and weep before His Majesty, saying: 'Qing Bu is a fierce general of the empire who excels in military strategy. Now all these generals are Your Majesty's former equals; if you command them under the crown prince's leadership, it is no different from sending sheep to lead wolves. None will be willing to serve him. Moreover, if Qing Bu hears this, he will march westward without hesitation.'" "Even though the emperor is ill, if he personally boards a decorated carriage and lies down to oversee the campaign, the generals will not dare to withhold their efforts." "The emperor may suffer, but for his wife and children, he will force himself to be strong.'" Thus, Lü Ze immediately visited Lady Lu at night, and Lady Lu seized the opportunity to weep before the emperor as the four men had advised. The emperor said: "I thought this young man was indeed not worthy of being sent, but I will go myself." Thus, the emperor personally led his troops eastward, while the ministers remained to guard the capital and all saw him off at Ba Shang. Liu Hou was ill, but forced himself to rise and went as far as Qu You, where he met the emperor and said: "I should have accompanied Your Majesty, but my illness is severe. "The Chu people are fierce and swift in battle; I beg Your Majesty not to engage them directly in combat." He then advised the emperor, saying: "Appoint the crown prince as general and have him oversee the troops in Guanzhong." The emperor said: "Although you are ill, Zi Fang should remain strong, lie down, and serve as a tutor to the crown prince." At that time, Shusun Tong was the Taifu (Grand Tutor), and Liu Hou served in the duties of Shaofu (Deputy Tutor).

## 24

In the twelfth year of the Han era, after returning from defeating Qing Bu's forces, the emperor's illness worsened and he became even more determined to replace the crown prince. Liu Hou advised against it, but was not listened to; as a result, he fell ill and ceased attending to official duties. Grand Tutor Shusun cited ancient and modern precedents, pleading with his life for the crown prince's position. The emperor pretended to agree but still wanted to replace him. When the imperial tour reached Yan, a banquet was held and the crown prince attended as an attendant. The four men followed the crown prince; they were all over eighty years old, with white beards and eyebrows, and their clothing and hats appeared very dignified. The emperor was surprised and asked: "Who are those men?" The four men stepped forward to answer, each stating their names: Dongyuan Gong, Jueli Xiansheng, Qili Ji, and Xiahuang Gong. The emperor was greatly astonished and said: "I have been seeking you for years, yet you avoided me. Now how come you are following my son?" The four men all replied: "Your Majesty is disrespectful to scholars and often abuses them. We, out of principle, could not endure the humiliation; therefore, we were frightened and went into hiding." "We secretly heard that the crown prince is a kind and filial person, respectful and loving toward scholars. There is no one in the empire who does not long to die for him; thus, we have come here." The emperor said: "I trouble you all and hope that you will continue to assist and protect the crown prince."

## 25

After the four men had finished offering their blessings, they hurriedly left. The emperor watched them leave and then summoned Qi Furen, pointing out the four men to her and saying: "I wanted to replace him, but those four are now supporting him. His wings have already been formed; it will be difficult to move him." "Lady Lu is truly the empress now." Qi Furen wept. The emperor said: "Dance for me in the style of Chu, and I will sing a song from Chu for you." The song went: "Wild geese fly high, soaring thousands of li in one flight. "Their wings are fully grown, and they cross the four seas without hindrance." "Having crossed the four seas, what can one do about it!" "Even if there are arrows and nets, where could they possibly be used now!" After singing several songs, Qi Furen sobbed with tears streaming down her face. The emperor rose and left, ending the banquet. In the end, the crown prince was not replaced; this was due to Liu Hou's original effort in bringing these four men into the situation.

## 26

Liu Hou accompanied the emperor in attacking Dai, devising a brilliant strategy at Maiyi. When Xiao He was appointed as prime minister, Liu Hou often engaged in casual conversations with the emperor about affairs of the empire; there were many such discussions, but since they did not pertain to matters of life and death for the empire, they are not recorded here. Liu Hou then declared: "My family has long served as prime ministers of the state of Han, and when Han fell, I did not hesitate to spend ten thousand gold pieces in order to avenge Han against the powerful Qin. The whole empire was stirred by this action. "Now, using only my eloquence I have become a teacher to an emperor, was enfeoffed as the lord of ten thousand households, and ranked among the marquises. This is the highest honor for one who began as a commoner; it is more than enough for me." "I wish to abandon worldly affairs and follow Chi Songzi in his travels." Thus, he began studying how to abstain from food (bi gu) and practiced Dao Yin techniques to lighten the body. At this time, Emperor Gaozu died. Lady Lu was grateful to Liu Hou and forced him to eat, saying: "A person's life in the world is as fleeting as a white colt passing through a crevice; how could you subject yourself to such hardship!" Liu Hou had no choice but to reluctantly comply and eat.

## 27

Eight years later, Liu Hou died and was posthumously honored with the title Wencheng Hou. His son Bu Yi succeeded him as marquis.

## 28

The old man who had previously given Zi Fang the Tai Gong book at Yishang in Xiapi was later seen thirteen years after this, when Liu Bang passed through Jibei. Indeed, beneath Gu Cheng Mountain, a yellow stone appeared; they took it and preserved it for worship. After Liu Hou's death, he was buried together with the yellow stone (tomb). Every year on the day of the winter solstice, sacrifices were made to the yellow stone at his tomb.

## 29

Liu Hou Bu Yi was found guilty of disrespect in the fifth year of Emperor Xiaowen's reign and had his fief abolished.

## 30

Grand Historian said: "Many scholars claim there are no ghosts or gods, yet they admit the existence of unseen forces." "As for the old man Liu Hou saw who gave him a book, it is indeed strange and remarkable." Emperor Gaozu was rescued from dire straits on many occasions, and Liu Hou was always instrumental in these efforts. Can this truly be said to lack the will of Heaven? The emperor said: "In devising strategies within the tent and determining victory from a distance, I am not as capable as Zi Fang." I had thought he was a tall and extraordinary man; but when I saw his portrait, his appearance resembled that of a woman or a beautiful lady. This is what Confucius meant by saying: "Judging people by their appearances leads to the mistake of Zi Yu." Liu Hou was also such a case.
