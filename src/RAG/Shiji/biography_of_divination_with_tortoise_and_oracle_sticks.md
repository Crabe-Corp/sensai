# Biography of Divination with Tortoise and Oracle Sticks

## 1

The Grand Historian said: Since ancient times, when sage kings were about to establish a state and receive their mandate, or initiate great endeavors, how often did they not treasure divination as an aid for virtue! Before the Tang and Yu dynasties, it is no longer possible to record. Since the rise of the Three Dynasties, each dynasty based itself on auspicious omens. The omen at Tushan followed, thus the Xia dynasty began; the divination for Feiyan was auspicious, so the Yin dynasty rose; the yarrow stalks cast for the hundred grains were favorable, hence King Zhou ruled. When a sovereign decides on matters of doubt, he consults divination and oracles; decisions are made through yarrow stalks and tortoise shells—this is the unchanging principle.

## 2

Even among the southern barbarians, Di, and Qiang peoples, who lack a hierarchy of ruler and subject, there are also divinations for resolving doubts. Some use metal or stone, others use herbs or trees; customs differ from nation to nation. Yet all of them can be used for warfare and attacks, deploying troops in pursuit of victory; each people trusts their own deities to foresee future events.

## 3

I have heard that in the Xia and Yin dynasties, those who wished to consult oracles would take yarrow stalks and turtle shells; once used, they were discarded, for it was believed that a shell kept hidden would lose its power, and yarrow stalks kept long would lose their divinity. By the time of the Zhou dynasty's oracle officials, they often treasured and preserved the yarrow stalks and turtle shells. Moreover, their preferences for large or small items, earlier or later ones, varied; ultimately, they were all considered equal in importance. Some believe that a sage king, when encountering any matter, could make no decision without certainty, and resolve no doubt without insight. The reason such methods of consulting the divine were established was because in later generations, people declined morally; the ignorant did not follow wisdom, each person contented himself with his own way, society fragmented into many factions, and the Way dispersed boundlessly. Therefore, they traced it back to the most subtle essence, seeking clarity through spiritual insight. Some believe that what is naturally endowed in insects and creatures, even a sage cannot contend with. Their indications of auspiciousness or misfortune, distinguishing yes from no, often hit the mark for people. By the time of Emperor Gaozu, they inherited the Qin dynasty's Grand Diviner official position. The empire had just been pacified, and warfare had not yet ceased. When Emperor Xiaohui's reign was brief, Empress Dowager Lü ruled as a female sovereign. Emperors Xiaowen and Xiaojing followed precedent without change, having no time to study or test these matters; even though father and son held the same office generation after generation, much of their refined and profound knowledge was lost over time. Since the current emperor ascended the throne, he has widely opened paths for artistic and scholarly talent, inviting all kinds of learning. Those skilled in a single art were allowed to offer their services; those who stood out as unparalleled or extraordinary were given high rank, without partiality. Within several years, the Grand Diviners gathered in great numbers. At that time, the emperor wished to attack the Xiongnu in the north, expand westward against Dayuan, and subdue the Baiyue in the south; divination was used to foresee omens beforehand, seeking advantage before taking action. When valiant generals advanced with resolve and held fast to their principles, achieving victory there, the yarrow stalks, turtle shells, and auspicious dates also played a role in this success. The emperor paid especially close attention, bestowing rewards as high as tens of millions. Individuals such as Qiu Ziming became so wealthy and favored that they held great influence in the imperial court. To the point where divination, fortune-telling, and methods of exorcising malevolent spirits were used; at times, these witchcraft practices proved somewhat accurate. Ordinary people, because of false omens or fabricated prophecies, were executed on official orders; at will they caused harm, leading to the destruction of families and clans—too numerous to count. All officials were terrified, saying that turtle shells and divination could speak. Later, when the treachery was uncovered, they too were executed along with three generations of their family.

## 4

To cast yarrow stalks to determine numbers, and scorch the turtle shell to observe omens—these transformations are boundless. Therefore, one selects the wise to interpret them; can this not be called a matter of great importance for sages! Zhou Gong divined with three turtle shells, and King Wu recovered from his illness. J 纣 was tyrannical and cruel, yet the great turtle shell did not yield an omen. When Duke Wen of Jin was about to secure King Xiang's position, divination revealed the omen of Huangdi; in the end, he received the command with a red bow. King Xianggong was greedy for 骊 Ji's beauty; divination showed an omen with the image of a mouth, and its calamity eventually lasted through five generations. King Ling of Chu was about to betray the Zhou dynasty; divination with the turtle shell yielded an adverse omen, and in the end he suffered defeat at Qianxi. When omens correspond to sincerity within, and contemporaries discern them clearly from the outside, can this not be called harmony between the two! The virtuous man says that those who lightly regard divination and claim there are no divine spirits are wrong; those who abandon the way of humanity yet place faith in auspicious omens cannot attain the proper will of gods and spirits. Therefore, the Book establishes the method for resolving doubts: among five considerations, divination accounts for two; when there are five interpretations, one follows the majority—this clarifies the principle of having guidance without exclusive reliance.

## 5

When I arrived in Jiangnan, I observed their actions and asked the elders; they said that a turtle of a thousand years would walk upon lotus leaves, and a hundred yarrow stalks would grow from one root. Moreover, in the regions where they were born, there are no tigers or wolves among the beasts, and no poisonous or stinging plants among the grasses. Families living by the river often keep turtles and feed them, believing that they can guide qi (vital energy) and promote health, helping to counteract aging and support longevity—how could this not be true!

## 6

Chu Xiansheng said: I, the subject, have studied classical learning and received instruction from a Doctor of Learning; I studied The Spring and Autumn Annals and was appointed as an officer with high rank. Fortunate to serve in palace guard duty, I entered and left the palaces for more than ten years. I secretly admired the writings of the Grand Historian. The Grand Historian's biography says: "The Three Kings did not use the same turtle shells, and the Four Barbarians had different forms of divination; yet each used them to decide auspiciousness or misfortune. I have briefly examined their essentials, hence this Biographies of Divination and Oracles." I traveled to Chang'an and sought the Biographies of Divination and Oraces in vain, so I went to the Grand Diviner's office and asked learned elders familiar with historical records and past events; I copied down accounts of divination practices and compiled them below.

## 7

I have heard that in ancient times, when the Five Emperors and Three Kings initiated any endeavor, they necessarily first decided matters through yarrow stalks or turtle shells. The text says: "Below there is hidden spirit, above there is dodder; above there are yarrow stalks being crushed, below there is a divine turtle." lington What is called the hidden spirit lies beneath dodder, appearing in the shape of a flying bird. After a new rain has passed, when the sky is clear and still with no wind, at night one should gently remove the dodder from this spot. Then light a torch to illuminate the area; once the fire goes out, mark the location. Surround it with four zhang of new cloth. The next day dig there; if you reach between four chi and seven chi deep, you will find it. If deeper than seven chi, it cannot be found. The hidden spirit is the root of a thousand-year-old pine tree; eating it grants immortality. I have heard that where yarrow grows with a full hundred stalks, there must be a divine turtle guarding it beneath, and above it is often covered by green clouds. The text says: "When the world is at peace and harmony prevails, when the king's Way is realized, yarrow stalks grow a zhang long, with clusters growing in full hundreds of stalks." In the present age, those who gather yarrow cannot meet the standards of ancient times; they cannot find clusters with a hundred stalks each a zhang long. To collect more than eighty stalks where each is eight chi in length is already difficult to obtain. For the people who prefer using hexagrams, gathering at least sixty stalks with each over six chi in length is sufficient for use. The record says: "Whoever can obtain a famous turtle, wealth will return to him; his family is sure to become extremely rich, reaching tens of millions." First is the "Beidou Turtle," second the "Nanchen Turtle," third the "Wuxing Turtle," fourth the "Bafeng Turtle," fifth the "Ershiba Su Turtle," sixth the "Riyue Turtle," seventh the "Jiuzhou Turtle," and eighth the "Yu Turtle": in total, eight named turtles. Each turtle map has a pattern on its belly; these patterns describe what kind of turtle it is. I briefly record their general meanings, without drawing the diagrams. To obtain one of these turtles does not require it to be a full 12 cun long; if common people find one seven or eight cun in length, it is already precious. Now pearls, jade, and treasures, though deeply hidden, must reveal their luster; they must display their divine brilliance—this is what is meant by that! Jade in the mountains makes trees moist; pearls born in deep waters keep their shores from drying—this is due to the nourishing influence they bring. The bright pearl of the moon emerges from rivers and seas, hidden within a clam, guarded by the dragon. When a sovereign obtains it, he will rule the world for long; the Four Barbarians will submit in reverence. Whoever can obtain one hundred yarrow stalks and also find the turtle beneath them for divination will have a hundred predictions, each accurate; this is sufficient to determine auspiciousness or misfortune.

## 8

Divine turtles emerge from rivers; the Lujiang Commandery regularly produces twenty turtles a year, each one zhang and two cun long, which are sent to the Grand Diviner's office. The Grand Diviner then selects auspicious days to remove scales from beneath their bellies. A turtle a thousand years old reaches one zhang and two cun in length. When a sovereign mobilizes an army or appoints generals, he must consult the turtle shell on the temple hall to decide auspiciousness or ill fortune. Now in the High Temple there is a chamber for turtles, where they are stored inside as sacred treasures.

## 9

The text says: "Take the bone from the front leg, drill it and wear it as a pendant; place the turtle in the northwest corner of your room and hang it there. When entering deep mountains or vast forests, you will not be confused." When I was an officer in the palace, I saw Master Wan Bi Shi Zhufang. The text says: "There is a divine turtle in the fine woods of Jiangnan." A "Jialin" is a forest where there are no tigers or wolves, no hawks or owls, no poisonous plants or stinging herbs; wild fires do not reach it, and axes never touch it—this is called Jialin. The turtle resides there, often nesting on fragrant lotus leaves. On its left flank, the inscription reads: "Jiazi Chongguang. He who obtains me shall rise from a commoner to become a ruler; one with land and authority will become an emperor if a feudal lord acquires me." To seek it in the forest where white snakes coil around mulberry trees, one must observe purification rites and wait. Suddenly, it appears as if someone comes to inform you; then pour an offering of wine with other hair strands, and after three nights of seeking, you will obtain it." Viewed in this light, is it not magnificent! How can one fail to revere the divine turtle?

## 10

An old man in the south used a turtle to support his bed's legs. He lived for more than twenty years with it; when the old man died and the bed was moved, the turtle was still alive and had not perished. Turtles can guide vital energy and regulate breath. Someone asked: "If turtles are so divine, then why does the Grand Diviner's office kill live turtles and take their shells?" In recent times, a man by the river obtained a famous turtle and kept it; his family then became greatly wealthy. He discussed with others about sending it away. People advised him to kill it rather than send it away, for releasing it would bring ruin to his family. The turtle appeared in a dream and said: "Send me back into the water; do not kill me." In the end, his family killed it. After killing it, he died himself and misfortune befell his household. The people and the sovereign follow different ways. When common people obtain a famous turtle, its form suggests it should not be killed. According to ancient stories of the past, enlightened kings and sage sovereigns all killed them for use.

## 11

During the reign of King Yuan of Song, a turtle was obtained and also killed for use. I carefully record these events on the left side, so that those interested may observe and choose from them.

## 12

In the second year of King Yuan's reign in Song, a divine turtle was sent by the river to travel to He River and arrived at Quanyang. A fisherman named Yuju cast his net and captured it, imprisoning it. He placed it in a cage. At midnight, the turtle appeared to King Yuan of Song in a dream and said: "I was sent by the River Jiang to travel to He, but your fishing net blocked my path. Quanyang's Yuju captured me, so I could not proceed." I am in distress and have no one to whom I can speak. King, you possess virtue and righteousness; therefore, I come to inform you." King Yuan was suddenly alert and understood. He summoned the Doctor of Learning Wei Ping and asked him, saying: "Tonight I dreamed of a man with an elongated neck and long head, dressed in black embroidered robes and riding a carriage, who appeared to me in my dream and said: 'I was sent by River Jiang to He, but your net blocked my path. Quanyang's Yuju caught me, so I cannot proceed." I am in distress with no one to whom I may speak. King, you possess virtue and justice; thus, I have come to inform you.'" What is this creature?" Wei Ping then took up his staff and rose, looked upward at the moon's light, observed where the Dipper pointed, and determined the day and direction. He used compasses and rulers as assistants, supplemented by scales and weights. The four directions were already fixed, the Eight Trigrams facing each other. Observing its auspiciousness or inauspiciousness, the intermediate creature appeared first. He then replied to King Yuan: "Tonight is the day of Rensi, with the constellation Jiao Niuling in residence at Qiantou. The great confluence of the Yellow River brings together ghosts and spirits to conspire. The Han flows directly north and south, while the Yangtze and Yellow Rivers meet as scheduled; a southerly wind has just arrived, so the envoy of the River Jiang came first. White clouds block the Han River, causing all things to remain behind. The Dipper's handle points at the sun; thus, the envoy should be imprisoned. Dressed in black robes and riding a palanquin carriage, its name is Gui (the turtle). The king quickly sent someone to inquire and search for it." King Yuan said, "Well done."

## 13

Thus the king sent someone to rush and ask the magistrate of Quanyang: "How many fishermen are there? Who is named Yuju?" Yuju caught the turtle, which appeared in a dream to the king; that is why I was sent by him to find it." The magistrate of Quanyang then ordered his officials to check the records and maps, and found that there were fifty-five fishing families on the water; a house upstream was named Yuju. The magistrate of Quanyang said: "Yes." He then rode with the envoy to ask Yuju, saying: "What did you catch while fishing last night?" Yuju said: "At midnight I cast my net and caught a turtle." The envoy asked, "Where is the turtle now?" He said: "It is in a cage." The envoy said, "The king knows you caught the turtle and has therefore sent me to retrieve it." Yuju said: "Yes." He immediately tied the turtle and took it out of the cage to present it to the envoy.

## 14

The envoy loaded it onto his carriage and left through the gate of Quanyang. It was broad daylight, yet nothing could be seen; winds and rain made everything dark and gloomy. Clouds covered it above, displaying five colors of blue and yellow; Thunder and rain arose together, the wind driving them forward. It entered through the Duan Gate and appeared in the eastern chamber. Its body was like flowing water, moist and radiant with light. Looking toward King Yuan, it extended its neck and advanced three steps before stopping; then it retracted its neck and retreated back to where it had been. King Yuan saw this and was surprised; he asked Wei Ping, "The turtle appeared before me, extending its neck to advance. What did it hope for? Retracting its neck again, what does this signify?" Wei Ping replied: "The turtle was in distress and ultimately imprisoned last night. The king possesses virtue and righteousness, so he sent someone to save it. Now it extends its neck forward as a gesture of gratitude; it retracts and retreats because it wishes to leave quickly." King Yuan said: "Well done! How extraordinary for a spirit to be so present; it cannot remain here long; Hurry and prepare the carriage to send off the turtle, do not let it miss its time." Wei Ping replied: "This turtle is a treasure of the world. Whoever first obtains this turtle shall become emperor, and will have ten correct predictions out of ten, winning ten battles out of ten. It was born in deep waters and grew up on yellow earth. It understands the way of Heaven, being clear about ancient times. It has traveled for three thousand years without ever leaving its domain. It maintains peace and stability, moving without exerting force. Its longevity spans heaven and earth; no one knows its limit. It changes with the transformations of things, altering color with each season. It resides in concealment, hiding and not feeding. In spring it is green, in summer yellow; in autumn white, in winter black. It understands yin and yang clearly, discerning the principles of punishment and virtue. It foresees advantages and disadvantages, discerns misfortune and fortune; its words are always correct, its battles always victorious. If the king can treasure it, all the feudal lords will submit. Do not send it away, King, to ensure peace for your state and dynasty." King Yuan said: "This turtle is extremely divine; descended from Heaven, it was trapped in the deep abyss. It endured hardship and distress. Because I am virtuous, it came to me. With great virtue and loyalty, it therefore came to inform me. If I do not send it away, then I am no different from a fisherman. A fisherman desires its flesh; I desire its power. To act thus would be unkind to the people below and lacking in virtue above. If ruler and minister lack propriety, how can blessings arise? I cannot bear it; how could I not send it away!" Wei Ping replied: "Not so. I have heard that great virtue does not seek reward, and a heavy trust is never returned; If Heaven bestows but one refuses to accept, then Heaven will take away the treasure. Now this turtle has traveled throughout the world and returned to its place; it reaches up to the blue sky, down to the mud. It has returned across all nine provinces, never feeling shame or disgrace, and has encountered no delays. Now it has come to Quanyang, where fishermen have shamed and imprisoned it. Even if the king sends it away, the rivers will surely be angry and seek revenge. They consider themselves wronged, so they conspire with spirits. Heavy rains will not cease; the waters cannot be controlled. If it becomes a drought, winds will raise dust and locusts will suddenly appear, causing the people to lose their season for farming. Though the king practices benevolence and righteousness, its punishment must come. There is no other reason; the calamity lies in the turtle. Even if regret follows later, how could it be too late!" Therefore, King, do not send it away."

## 15

King Yuan sighed deeply and said: "To oppose a person's envoy and cut off their plans, is this not cruelty? To take what belongs to others as one's own treasure, is this not greed?" I have heard that whoever gains power through cruelty will surely perish cruelly; whoever seizes by force will certainly achieve no lasting success. Jie and Zhou were cruel and powerful, yet they perished and their states fell. Now if I listen to you, it would mean having no reputation for benevolence or righteousness but following a path of cruelty and force. The rivers are Tang and Wu; I am Jie and Zhou. I have not seen the benefit, yet fear falling into guilt. I am uncertain and hesitant; how can I deal with this treasure? Hurry and prepare the carriage to send away the turtle, do not keep it long."

## 16

Wei Ping replied: "Not so. The king will have no misfortune. Between Heaven and Earth, stones pile up to form mountains. They are high yet do not collapse; the earth thus remains at peace. Therefore, things may appear dangerous but remain safe, or be light in nature yet cannot be moved; People may be loyal and trustworthy but not as effective as those who lie and flatter; some may appear ugly yet are fit for high office, while others may be beautiful and charming yet bring trouble to the people. Unless one is a sage or holy person, none can fully understand this. Spring, autumn, winter, and summer—sometimes hot, sometimes cold. When cold and heat are out of harmony, harmful qi intermingles with evil forces. The same year may have different seasons; this is caused by the time itself. Therefore, spring brings growth and summer promotes flourishing, autumn brings harvest and winter ensures storage. Some act in accordance with benevolence and righteousness; others resort to violence and strength. Violence and strength have their place, while benevolence and righteousness also have their time. All things are thus; they cannot be completely governed. Your Majesty, please listen to me. I shall explain everything in detail. Heaven produces five colors to distinguish between white and black. The earth grows the Five Grains, so that good and evil may be known. People do not know how to distinguish; they are no different from beasts. They live in huts and dwell in caves, knowing nothing of farming. Disasters and chaos throughout the world arise from the intermingling of Yin and Yang. In haste and in turmoil, they are interconnected yet not chosen carefully. Ominous signs appear frequently; these are transmitted as weak omens. Sages distinguish their origins, so that they do not fall into each other's grasp. Beasts have males and females; they are placed in the mountains and plains; Birds have males and females, scattered throughout forests and wetlands; Creatures with shells are placed in valleys and streams. Therefore, to govern the people, cities and walls were built; within there are lanes and alleys, outside there are fields and roads. Men and women in marriage are given land and homes, their houses arranged in order. Genealogies and records were made to distinguish their clans and names. Officials and magistrates were appointed, encouraged by ranks and rewards. They are clothed in silk and hemp, nourished with the Five Grains. They plow and harrow the fields, weed and cultivate them. Their mouths enjoy what they desire, their eyes behold beauty, and their bodies benefit from it. Viewed in this way, without effort one cannot attain such results. Therefore it is said: If a farmer does not work hard, the granary will not be full; If merchants do not strive, they cannot gain profit; If women do not work hard, their cloth and silk will not be fine; If officials do not strive, their authority cannot be established; If a general does not work hard, his soldiers will not obey orders; If marquises and kings do not strive, they will die without leaving a name. Therefore it is said: Striving is the beginning of endeavors, the principle of division, and the order of things. What one seeks through striving will always be attained. Your Majesty believes this to be untrue, yet have you not heard of the jade case and the pheasant that come from Kunshan Mountain; the Pearl of Bright Moon, which comes from the Four Seas; chiseling stones and opening oysters, they are sold in the market; sages obtain them and regard them as great treasures. Where the great treasure is found, that is where the Son of Heaven resides. Now Your Majesty considers yourself violent, yet you are no better than an oyster in the sea; you consider yourself strong, yet you are not more than a stone being chiseled on Kunshan Mountain. To take them brings no blame; to treasure them brings no misfortune. Now the turtle has come of its own accord into a net, and was caught by a fisherman. It spoke to him in a dream, revealing itself as the nation's treasure. Your Majesty, why should you be worried?"

## 17

Yuan Wang said: "Not so. I have heard that remonstrance brings blessings, while flattery is a source of harm. A ruler who listens to flattery is foolish and deluded. Nevertheless, misfortune does not come without cause, nor does blessings arrive without reason. Heaven and Earth combine their energies to produce all kinds of wealth. Yin and Yang have their divisions, never departing from the four seasons, twelve months in total, with solar terms marking each period. Sages understand this thoroughly; thus, they themselves suffer no calamity. A wise ruler who employs it will find that none dare to deceive him. Therefore, it is said: When blessings arrive, they arise from the people themselves; when misfortune comes, it is also brought about by human actions. Misfortune and blessings are the same; punishment and virtue go hand in hand. Sages examine them to discern auspiciousness or misfortune. In the time of Jie and Zhou, they competed with Heaven for merit, obstructed spirits and deities, preventing them from communicating. This was indeed a reign without virtue; flatterers were numerous among the ministers. Jie had a flatterer named Zhao Liang. He taught him to act without virtue, urging him toward greed and ferocity. He imprisoned Tang at Xiatai, and killed Guan Longfeng. Those around him feared death, so they secretly flattered from the side. The state was in danger as precarious as a pile of eggs; yet all said it would not be harmed. They proclaimed joy for ten thousand years, or claimed the bliss had no end. They blocked his ears and eyes, deceiving him with madness and falsehoods. Finally, Tang attacked Jie; he perished and the state was lost. By listening to his flatterers, he alone suffered misfortune. The Spring and Autumn Annals recorded it; the memory remains to this day. Zhou had a flatterer, named Zuo Qiang. He was boastful and sly-eyed, teaching the making of ivory officials. He claimed it would reach Heaven; there were also jade beds. Rhino and jade vessels, ivory chopsticks with which to eat soup. Sages cut open his heart; valiant men severed his shins. Ji Zi feared death, so he let down his hair and pretended to be mad. They killed Zhou's crown prince Li, imprisoned King Wen of Zhou, Chang. He was thrown into a stone chamber, to be kept from dusk until dawn. Yin Jing saved him and fled with him. They entered Zhou territory, where they met Duke Tai Gong Wang. Troops were raised and gathered; they attacked Zhou. King Wen fell ill and died, yet his body was carried along on the campaign. The crown prince Fa took command in his place, assuming the title of King Wu. They fought at Mushe, and defeated Zhou on the southern side of Mount Hua. Zhou could not withstand defeat and fled back; he was cornered in Xianglang. He committed suicide in the Hall of Announcements, his body left unburied. His head was hung from a carriage pole; four horses dragged it along. I think of how this happened, and my intestines feel like boiling water. These men were all rich with the whole world and honored as Son of Heaven, yet they became greatly arrogant. They desired never to tire of their time in power; they took pleasure in lofty undertakings and were greedy, fierce, and arrogant. They did not employ loyalty or trustworthiness but listened to their flatterers; thus they became the laughingstock of all under Heaven. Now my state, situated among the feudal lords, is as insignificant as a single autumn hair. To act improperly would only lead to further disaster and escape!"

## 18

Weiping replied, "Not so. Though the Yellow River is divine and wise, it is not as great as Mount Kunlun; the source of the Yangtze River, though clear and orderly, is not as vast as the Four Seas, yet people still strive to seize its treasures; feudal lords contend for them, thus wars arise. Small states see their destruction, great states face peril; fathers and elder brothers are killed, wives and children taken captive, temples destroyed and nations ruined—all in the struggle for these treasures. To wage war, to attack, and to contend is sheer brutality and strength. Therefore it is said that one should take power through force and might, yet govern with culture and reason; not oppose the four seasons, and must surely be close to virtuous men; harmonize with yin and yang, and have ghosts and spirits as attendants; be connected to Heaven and Earth, and be their friend. The feudal lords would submit as guests, the people numerous and joyful. The state would be at peace, and a new beginning would come with the age. Tang and Wu acted in this way, thus taking the Mandate of Heaven; The Spring and Autumn Annals recorded it, regarding it as a standard for governance. You do not compare yourself to Tang or Wu, yet liken yourself to Jie and Zhou. Jie and Zhou were tyrants who relied on force; they regarded this as the norm. Jie built a palace of tiles, Zhou was cornered at Xianglang. They burned fine silk for divination, and squandered resources on the people [meng]. Taxes were levied without limit, and killings carried out without restraint. They killed people and their livestock, using cowhide to make sacks. They filled the sacks with blood, hung them up for people to shoot at, contending in strength with the Heavenly Emperor. They defied and disrupted the four seasons, offering sacrifices to a hundred ghosts before others. Those who advised were always put to death; flatterers remained at their side. Sages hid themselves, and the common people dared not act. The Mandate of Heaven brought droughts; the state was filled with omens of disaster. Grasshoppers emerged every year, and the five grains failed to mature. The people could not find peace in their dwellings; ghosts and spirits refused offerings. Strong winds arose daily, and daylight turned dark as night. The sun and moon were eclipsed together; their light extinguished without a trace. Stars scattered in chaos, all celestial order broken. Judging from this, how could such a state endure for long! Even without Tang and Wu, the time was already ripe for their downfall. Thus King Tang attacked Jie, and King Wu overthrew Zhou; it was a matter of the times. They became Son of Heaven, their descendants continuing through generations; Their entire lives free from blame, later ages praised them, and this continues to the present day. These were all men who acted in accordance with their times, who observed events and demonstrated strength—thus they succeeded as emperors and kings. Now the turtle oracle is a great treasure, serving sages, passed on to virtuous [kings]. It requires no hands or feet; thunder and lightning carry it forth; Winds and rain deliver it, flowing water carries it along. Only a marquis or king with virtue is worthy to receive it. Now, if Your Majesty possesses virtue and is worthy of this treasure, you would likely dare not accept it; If the king were to send it away, the state of Song will surely suffer blame. Even if regret follows later, it would be too late."

## 19

Yuanwang was greatly pleased and delighted. Thus Yuanwang turned toward the sun in gratitude, bowed twice, and accepted it. They selected a day for purification and fasting; the days Jia and Yi were considered most auspicious. Then they sacrificed white pheasants, as well as black-haired sheep; they poured the blood over the turtle oracle at the center of the altar. They peeled it with a knife, yet its body remained intact without injury. They offered dried meat and wine as offerings to the oracle, laying out its entrails horizontally. With a branch of mugwort for divination, they would surely determine the wound's fate. The patterns revealed their meaning clearly; the inscriptions interwove and welcomed interpretation. They had diviners interpret it, and all that was said proved accurate. The state's great treasure brought blessings, its fame spreading to neighboring regions. They slaughtered an ox and took its hide, which was tanned in the mulberry trees of Zheng. Grasses and trees were all divided up, transformed into armor and weapons. Victory in battle and the seizing of territory—none could rival Yuanwang. During Yuanwang's time, Weiping served as minister to Song; at that time the state of Song was strongest—this was due to the power of the oracle turtle.

## 20

Hence it is said that even a divine being, when reaching its limit, could appear in dreams to Yuanwang, yet could not free itself from the fisherman's trap. Though it could speak ten truths and have them all prove accurate, it could not send a messenger to the Yellow River or return with news from the Yangtze; though wise enough to help people win battles and seize territory, it could not free itself from a blade's edge or escape the suffering of flaying and piercing. Though sage enough to foresee events and appear quickly in visions, it could not make Weiping remain silent. Though its words were always perfectly accurate, the oracle itself ended up bound and crippled; when circumstances proved unfavorable, what good was wisdom or virtue! Virtuous men have constancy; scholars act according to the moment. Therefore, even a wise man may fail to see something, and an attentive ear may miss certain sounds; even if a person is virtuous, he cannot draw a square with his left hand and a circle with his right. Even the brightness of the sun and moon can be obscured by drifting clouds at times. Yi was famed for his archery, yet he could not surpass Hongqu or Fengmen; Yu was renowned as wise and discerning, yet could not prevail over ghosts and spirits. When the pillars of earth break, Heaven naturally lacks beams—how then can one demand perfection from people? Confucius heard this and said: "The divine turtle knows fortune and misfortune, yet its bones remain dry and hollow. The sun, though a symbol of virtue and sovereign over the world, is disgraced by the three-legged crow. The moon, serving as an instrument of punishment and assisting in governance, is devoured by a toad. The hedgehog suffers disgrace at the hands of magpies; even the divine worm Tengshe is imperiled by the Ji Qie. Bamboo has joints and patterns on its exterior, yet inside it is straight and hollow; Pines and cypresses are the elders among all trees, yet they stand as sentinels at gates and alleys. The days of the calendar are not complete; thus there exist "gu" and "xu," representing voids. Gold has flaws, white jade has blemishes. Some matters proceed swiftly; others move slowly. Things are sometimes constrained, yet also have their foundations. There is no absolute order; there is always some disorder as well. People have their virtues, yet also things in which they are not equal to others. How can one possibly be perfectly suited for everything? Can anything in the world ever be truly perfect? Heaven itself is not perfect, so people build houses; they do not complete three tiles but arrange them as a response to Heaven. "There are ranks and gradations in the world—imperfection is what allows things to come into being."

## 21

Chu Xiansheng said: A fisherman cast his net and caught a divine turtle. The turtle appeared in a dream to Song Yuanwang, who summoned the scholar-official Weiping and told him of the vision involving the turtle. Weiping calculated the positions of stars, fixed the dates for the sun and moon, measured balance and dimensions, observed omens of fortune and misfortune; his interpretation of the turtle's markings matched those on other objects. Weiping advised the king to preserve the divine turtle as a precious treasure for the state—this was admirable indeed. In ancient times, divination by yarrow stalks always referred to oracle turtles, because of their esteemed reputation; this practice has a long history. I have recorded and composed this as a biography.

Using divination to seek—complete failure; nothing will be obtained at all ． It is named "Shou Yang Zu Wu Bei Bei Wei ．" The sick person will die ． It is named "Shou Yang Zu Xian Ji Bei Wei ．" Those detained have committed crimes and are guilty of wrongdoing ． It is named "Shou Yang Zu Xian Bei Bei Wei ．" Looking for travelers—will not arrive ； they do not come at all ． It is named "Shou Yang Zu Xiao Ji Bei Wei ．" Travelers proceed ； movement occurs smoothly and successfully ． It is named "Shou Yang Zu Xiao Bei Bei Wei ．" Will they come or not ？ Their arrival remains uncertain ． It is named "Shou Yang Zu Xin Ji Bei Wei ．" Meeting a person—will absolutely never happen ； no meeting at all ． It is named "Shou Yang Zu Xin Bei Bei Wei ．"

## 88

This is Ting Nei Wai Zi Chui, which may be interpreted as "Straightforward Inner and Outer Self-Drop" or a symbol of internal and external decline. It is named "Shou Yang Zu Xu Ji Bei Wei ．" Using divination to seek—unclear outcome ； the result remains obscure and uncertain ． It is named "Shou Yang Zu Xu Bei Bei Wei ．" The illness does not cause death, but recovery will be difficult and slow ． It is named "Shou Yang Zu Yan Ji Bei Wei ．" Those detained are innocent of wrongdoing, yet they find it hard to be released from confinement ． It is named "Shou Yang Zu Yan Bei Bei Wei ．" Will the journey proceed or not ？ Movement remains uncertain ． It is named "Shou Yang Zu Yao Ji Bei Wei ．" Will they come or not ？ Arrival is uncertain ． It is named "Shou Yang Zu Yao Bei Bei Wei ．" Meeting a person—will never happen ； no meeting takes place at all ． It is named "Shou Yang Zu Yi Ji Bei Wei ．" Not auspicious ； inauspicious ． It is named "Shou Yang Zu Yi Bei Bei Wei ．"

## 89

This is Heng Ji Yu Yang Shou Fu, which may be interpreted as "Lateral Auspiciousness Elm Upright Head Bowed" or a symbolic omen of mixed fortune. It is named "Shou Yang Zu Ying Ji Bei Wei ．" Using divination to seek—a difficult attainment ； the desired outcome will be hard to achieve ． It is named "Shou Yang Zu Ying Bei Bei Wei ．" The illness is difficult to recover from, yet death does not occur ． It is named "Shou Yang Zu Wu Ji Bei Wei ．" Detention will be hard to escape from, but no harm or injury will come about ． It is named "Shou Yang Zu Wu Bei Bei Wei ．" One may remain at home in the household and proceed with marrying a wife or giving away a daughter in marriage ． It is named "Shou Yang Zu You Ji Bei Wei ．"

## 90

This is Heng Ji Shang Zhu Zai Zheng Shen Jie She Nei Wai Zi Ju, which may be interpreted as "Lateral Auspiciousness Upper Pillar Bearing Upright Self-Joint Broken Inner and Outer Self-Raise" or a symbolic representation of mixed fortune with internal and external efforts. It is named "Shou Yang Zu You Bei Bei Wei ．" Using divination for an illness—divination indicates the sick person will not die on that day, but will die on another day instead ． It is named "Shou Yang Zu Zhi Ji Bei Wei ．"

## 91

## 92

(Regarding a person's illness) Head bowed and feet deceptive, with outward appearance but no inner substance. It is named "Shou Yang Zu Zhong Bei Bei Wei ．" The sick person consulted the turtle oracle without ceasing; death will come swiftly and urgently. It is named "Shou Yang Zu Hui Ji Bei Wei ．" Divination for a minor loss but major consequences—death does not occur on that day. It is named "Shou Yang Zu Hui Bei Bei Wei ．"

## 93

Head upturned, feet concealed. It is named "Shou Yang Zu Xun Ji Bei Wei ．" Using divination to seek something—will not be obtained; the desired outcome remains unattained. It is named "Shou Yang Zu Xun Bei Bei Wei ．" Those detained are guilty of a crime. It is named "Shou Yang Zu Yu Ji Bei Wei ．" Words spoken by others may cause fear, but no harm will come about. It is named "Shou Yang Zu Yu Bei Bei Wei ．" Will the journey proceed or not? Movement remains uncertain. It is named "Shou Yang Zu Yuan Ji Bei Wei ．" Meeting a person—will not happen; no meeting takes place at all. It is named "Shou Yang Zu Yuan Bei Bei Wei ．"

## 94

The Great Discourse says: The outer aspect refers to others, while the inner aspect refers to oneself; The outer aspect is the female, and the inner aspect is the male. A bowed head signifies worry or sorrow. The great refers to the body, and the small refers to its branches. According to the great principle of divination for illness: if the feet are concealed, life is preserved; but if the feet are open and exposed, death will occur. For a journey: if the feet appear open, arrival occurs; if the feet remain concealed, arrival does not happen. Regarding travel: when the feet are hidden, movement will not occur; but when the feet appear open and exposed, movement proceeds. When seeking something: if the feet are revealed, it is obtained; if they remain concealed, it cannot be attained. For those detained: if their feet are hidden, they will not leave confinement; but if the feet appear open and visible, release is possible. When divining for illness: if death occurs with the feet revealed, it indicates an inner state of elevation but outer decline.
