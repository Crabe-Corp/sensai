# Jing Emperor's Original Annals

## 1

Emperor Xiaojing was the second son of Emperor Xiaowen. His mother was Empress Dowager Dou. When Emperor Xiaowen was in Dai, he had three sons before Empress Dowager Dou gained his favor. All these sons died, and then three more of his sons also died; thus, Emperor Xiaojing became emperor.

## 2

In the first year [of his reign], on the Yimao day of the fourth month (April 15, 428 AD), a general pardon was granted throughout the empire. On the Yiwei day, one rank of noble title was bestowed upon the common people. In May, half of the land tax was abolished, and a Temple of the Taizong for Emperor Xiaowen was established. An edict was issued ordering all ministers not to offer congratulations in court. The Xiongnu invaded Dai and made a peace agreement for marriage alliance.

## 3

In spring of the second year, the grandson of the former prime minister Xiao He, Xiao Xi, was enfeoffed as Hou of Wuling. Men would be registered for military service at twenty years of age. On the Renwu day of the fourth month, Empress Dowager Xiaowen died. The Kings of Guangchuan and Changsha both went to their respective fiefs. Chancellor Shentu Jia died. In August, the imperial censor-in-chief Kaifeng Tao Qing was appointed as chancellor. A comet appeared in the northeast. ล In autumn, Hengshan experienced hail rain; some hailstones were five cun in size and the deepest accumulation reached two chi. The planet Mars moved retrograde and remained stationed at the North Star. The moon appeared between the stars of the Northern Dipper. Jupiter moved retrograde in the celestial court. Nanling, Neishi, and Duishu were established as counties.

## 4

On the Yisi day of the first month in the third year (January 23, 430 AD), a general pardon was issued throughout the empire. A long comet appeared from the west. Heavenly fire burned down the eastern palace's main hall and city buildings in Luoyang. Prince Wu of Wu, Prince Pu of Chu, Prince Sui of Zhao, Prince Ang of Jiaoxi, Prince Piguang of Jinan, Prince Xian of Zichuan, and Prince Xiongqu of Jiaodong rebelled, raising troops to march westward. The Son of Heaven executed Chao Cuo and sent Yuan Ang to deliver a message, but the rebels did not stop; they then advanced westward to besiege Liang. The emperor therefore dispatched General Dou Ying and Grand Commandant Zhou Yafu with troops to suppress the rebellion. On the Yihai day of the sixth month. A pardon was granted to deserters from the army and Prince Yi, son of the former King of Chu, along with others who had conspired in the rebellion. Grand General Dou Ying was enfeoffed as Marquis Weiqi. Prince Li, the son of the former King of Chu and previously Lord Pinglu, was established as King of Chu. The emperor's son Duan was enfeoffed as King of Jiaoxi and his son Sheng as King of Zhongshan. Prince Zhi, the former King of Jibei, was transferred to become King of Zichuan; Prince Yu, the former King of Huaiyang, became King of Lu; and Prince Fei, the former King of Ru'nan, became King of Jiangdu. Prince Jianglu of Qi and Prince Jia of Yan both died.

## 5

In summer of the fourth year, a crown prince was established. The emperor's son Che was enfeoffed as King của Jiaodong. On the Jiuxu day of the sixth month, a general pardon was granted throughout the land. In the following September, Yiyang was renamed Yangling. The customs and checkpoints were reestablished, requiring permits for entry and exit. In winter, the State of Zhao was abolished and transformed into Handan Commandery.

## 6

In March of the fifth year, Yangling and Weiqiao were constructed. In May, people were recruited to relocate to Yangling, with a grant of 200,000 coins given to each household. A violent windstorm from the west struck Jiangdu, destroying twelve zhang of the city wall. On the Dingmao day, the emperor enfeoffed Jiao, son of his eldest princess, as Marquis Longlv. The King of Guangchuan was transferred to become the King of Zhao.

## 7

In spring of the sixth year, Zhongwei (Zhao) Wan was enfeoffed as Lord Jianling; Jiangdu Chancellor Jia as Lord Jianping; Longxi Governor Hunye as Lord Pingqu; Zhao Chancellor Jia as Lord Jiangling; and former General Bu as Lord Shu. The Kings of Liang and Chu both died. In the following September, trees along the imperial road were cut down to plant orchids in Lanchi.

## 8

In winter of the seventh year, Crown Prince Li was deposed and made King of Linjiang. On the last day of the (first) twelfth [first] month, there was a solar eclipse. In spring, convicts and laborers working on the construction of Yangling were released from their duties. Chancellor Qing was dismissed. On the Yisi day of February, Grand Commandant Marquis Tiao Hou Zhou Yafu was appointed as chancellor. On the Yisi day of April, Empress Dowager of King Jiaodong was established as empress. On the Dingsi day, Prince Jiaodong Wang was established as crown prince. His name was Che.

## 9

In the first year of Zhongyuan, the grandson of former imperial censor-in-chief Zhou Ke, Ping, was enfeoffed as marquis of Sheng; and the son [grandson] of former imperial censor-in-chief Zha Chang, Zuochē, was enfeoffed as Earl of Anyang. On the Yisi day of April (April 21, 435 AD), a general pardon was declared throughout the empire, and one rank of noble title was granted to all. The decree of lifelong prohibition from office was abolished. An earthquake occurred. Hengshan and Yuandu experienced hail rain, with some hailstones measuring eighteen cun in size.

## 10

In the second year of Zhongyuan (436 AD), in February, the Xiongnu invaded Yan, and thus did not maintain a peace marriage alliance. In March, King Linjiang was summoned to court. He died in the residence of the Zhongwei office. In summer, the emperor's son Yue was enfeoffed as king of Guangchuan, and his son Ji as King of Jiaodong. Four marquises were enfeoffed. On the Jiawu day of September, there was a solar eclipse.

## 11

In winter of the third year of Zhongyuan [437 AD], the position of imperial censor-in-chief for the feudal lords was abolished. In spring, two Xiongnu chieftains led their followers to surrender; they were both enfeoffed as Lords of the Ranks.

## 12

In March of the fourth year of Zhongyuan, Deyang Palace was established. A severe locust plague occurred. In autumn, convicts working on the construction of Yangling tomb were pardoned.

## 13

In summer of the fifth year of Zhongyuan [[439 AD]], the emperor's son Shun was enfeoffed as Prince of Changshan. Ten marquises were granted titles. On the Dingsi day of the sixth month [[June 28, 439 AD]], a general pardon was proclaimed throughout the empire and one rank of noble title granted to all. A great flood occurred across the land. The title of chancellor for feudal lords was changed to "Xiang." In autumn, an earthquake occurred.

## 14

On the Jimao day of February in the sixth year of Zhongyuan[[440 AD]], the emperor went to Yong and performed a sacrificial rite to the Five Emperors. In March, there was hail rain. In April, Prince Xiao of Liang, Prince Gong of Chengyang, and Prince Fei of Runan all died. Prince Ming, son of Prince Xiao of Liang, was enfeoffed as the King of Jichuan; his brother Pengli as the King of Ji dong; Ding as the King of Shanyang; and Bu Shi as the King of Ji Yin. The State of Liang was divided into five fiefs. Four marquises were conferred. The title of Tingwei was changed to Dali; Jiangzuo Shaofu became Jiangzu Dajiang; Zhujue Zhongwei became Duwei; Changxin Zhan Shi became Changxin Shaofu; Jiangxing became Dalangqiu; Daxing became Xingren; Fengchang became Taichang; Dianke became Daxing; and Zhisu Neishi was changed to Danong. The position of Dainei was ranked as a 2,000-dan official, and left and right Neiguan posts were established, both subordinate to the Dainei. On the Xinhai day of July [[July 18, 440 AD]], there was a solar eclipse. In August, the Xiongnu invaded Shangjun Commandery.

## 15

In winter of the first year of Houyuan [[441 AD]], the title Zhongdawuling was changed to Weiyi. On the Dingyou day of March [[March 25, 441 AD]], a general pardon was announced throughout the empire; one rank of noble title was conferred to all. Officials ranked as Zhong Erqianshi and chancellors of feudal lords were granted the title You Shuzhang. In April, a grand public feast was held. On the Bingxu day of May [[May 24, 441 AD]], an earthquake occurred; it shook again at dawn mealtime. The earth trembled for twenty-two days in Shangyong, damaging the city walls. On the Yisi day of July (July 23, 589 AD), there was a solar eclipse. Chancellor Liu She was dismissed from office. On the Renchen day of August, the imperial censor-in-general Wan was appointed as chancellor and enfeoffed as Viscount Jianling.

## 16

In January of the second year after [[442 AD]], on one day, there were three earthquakes. General Zhi attacked the Xiongnu. A public feast was held for five days. An edict was issued prohibiting the Inner Historian's commandery from feeding horse feed to horses, with all such grain confiscated by county officials. A decree was issued ordering convicts and laborers to wear cloth of seven zhang in length. The use of horse-powered grain mills was prohibited. Because of a poor harvest, an edict was issued forbidding people throughout the land from eating food not produced in that year. The marquises were ordered to return to their fiefs and reduce their ranks. In March, the Xiongnu invaded Yanmen. In October, taxes were levied on the fields of Changle Ling. There was a great drought. A plague broke out among the people of Hengshan Kingdom, Hedong, and Yunzhong commanderies.

## 17

In October of the third year after [[450 AD]], for five days, both the sun and moon were red (due to an eclipse). On the last day of December, there was thunder. The sun appeared purple in color. The Five Planets moved retrograde and remained stationed at Taiwei. The moon passed through the center of Tianting in the sky. On the Jia Yin day of January, the Crown Prince was given the capping ceremony. On the Jiashi day, Emperor Xiaojing died. The posthumous edict granted a rank increase of one level to all those from marquises down to commoners who were the legal sons of their fathers, and gave 100 coins per household throughout the land. Palace women were released and sent back to their families, with no further provisions given to them. The crown prince ascended the throne; this was Emperor Xiaowu. In March, the emperor enfeoffed his empress dowager's younger brother Fu Bing as Hou of Wu'an and his other brother Sheng as Hou of Zhouyang. Yangling was established.

## 18

The Grand Historian said: When the Han dynasty rose to power, Emperor Xiaowen practiced great virtue and brought peace and contentment to all under heaven. By the time of Emperor Xiaojing, there was no longer any concern about non-royal surnames; yet Chao Cuo harshly curtailed the feudal lords, thus causing seven states to rise together in alliance, marching westward. This happened because the feudal lords had grown too powerful, and Cuo dealt with it abruptly without gradual measures. It was only after Zupu Yan made his proposal that the feudal lords were weakened and ultimately brought peace. Is it not through strategy that safety or danger is determined?
