# Biographies of Huainan and Hengsh 山

## 1

Prince Li of Huainan, whose name was Chang, was the youngest son of Emperor Gaozu; his mother had been a concubine of King Zhao Zhang Ao. In the eighth year of Emperor Gaozu's reign, while passing through Dongyuan on his way east, the King of Zhao presented him with this concubine. Prince Li's mother gained favor and became pregnant. King Zhao Ao dared not house her in the palace, so he built an external residence for her to stay in. When Guan Gao and others plotted rebellion at Bai, the matter was discovered, and King Zhao was arrested along with them. His mother's brothers and concubines were all captured and imprisoned in Hedong. Prince Li's mother was also detained, and she told the officials: "I gained favor with the emperor and became pregnant." The official reported this to the emperor. At that time, the emperor was furious at King Zhao and did not yet deal with Prince Li's mother. Prince Li's maternal uncle, Zhao Jian, therefore asked Peiyang Hou to speak on her behalf to Empress Lu. However, Empress Lu was jealous and refused to report this to the emperor; Peiyang Hou did not strongly argue for it. When Prince Li's mother had already given birth to Prince Li, she was filled with anger and immediately committed suicide. The officials took Prince Li to the emperor, who regretted his actions. He ordered Empress Lu to raise him and had Prince Li's mother buried in Zhen Ding. Zhen Ding was where Prince Li's mother came from; it belonged to the Shixian district of her family.

## 2

In the eleventh year of Emperor Gaozu's reign (tenth) [seventh] month, King Huainan Wu Rangbu rebelled. He appointed his son Chang as King of Huainan and granted him the former territory of Wu Rangbu, consisting of a total of four commanderies. The emperor personally led his troops to defeat and eliminate Wu Rangbu; thus Prince Li ascended the throne as king. Prince Li had lost his mother at an early age and often relied on Empress Lu. During the reigns of Emperor Xiaohui and Empress Lu, because of this he enjoyed favor without suffering any harm; however, he always harbored resentment toward Peiyang Hou and dared not act upon it. When Emperor Xiaowen first ascended the throne, King Huainan considered himself the closest in relation and became arrogant and disrespectful, repeatedly failing to abide by the law. The emperor, because of their close relationship, often pardoned him. In the third year, he came to court for an audience. He was extremely arrogant and overbearing. Accompanying the emperor into the hunting grounds, he rode in the same carriage as him and often addressed the emperor as "elder brother." Prince Li was strong and capable; he had the strength to lift a cauldron. He then went to request an audience with Peiyang Hou. Peiyang Hou came out to see him, and Prince Li immediately pulled a iron mace from his sleeve and struck Peiyang Hou, ordering his follower Wei Jing to kill him. Prince Li then rushed to the palace gates, bare-chested and apologized, saying: "My mother should not have been implicated in Zhao's affairs. At that time Peiyang Hou had the power to obtain her case from Empress Lu but did not contend for it; this is his first crime. King Zhao Youri and his mother were innocent, yet Empress Lu had them killed; Peiyang Hou failed to contend for their case as well—this is his second crime. Empress Lu elevated the various Lü clan members to positions of power and sought to endanger the Liu family; Peiyang Hou again failed to contend against this—this is his third crime. "I have, on behalf of the realm, executed the traitorous minister Peiyang Hou in retribution for my mother's death and to avenge her. I now humbly submit myself at the palace gates to accept punishment." Emperor Xiaowen was moved by his resolve, and because of their familial ties, did not punish him, pardoning Prince Li. At that time, Empress Dowager Bo and the crown prince as well as various ministers were all afraid of Prince Li. Because of this, upon returning to his kingdom he became even more arrogant and indulgent, disregarding Han laws; when entering or leaving, he used imperial-style processions with warnings sounded ahead, claimed authority akin to an emperor, made his own decrees and laws, and behaved as if he were a Son of Heaven.

## 3

In the sixth year, he ordered a man named Dan and seventy others together with Ji Pu Hou Chai Wu's crown prince Qi to plot rebellion. They gathered forty chariots at Gu Kou and sent envoys to Minyue and Xiongnu. The matter was discovered, investigated, and officials were dispatched to summon King Huainan. King Huainan arrived in Chang'an.

## 4

"Minister of the Crown, Minister Zhang Cang; Minister of Guests, Minister Feng Jing; Acting Minister of Justice and Director of the Imperial Clan Office, Minister Yi; Minister of Punishments, Minister He; and Commander-in-Chief for Suppressing Bandits, Minister Fu, respectfully submit at risk of their lives: King Huainan Chang has violated the laws of the late emperor and refused to obey imperial edicts. His manner of living is without restraint; he uses yellow-roofed carriages befitting an emperor, entering and exiting in a fashion resembling that of the Son of Heaven, and 擅自 establishes his own decrees while disregarding Han law. Furthermore, the officials he has appointed include his chamberlain Chun as prime minister. He has gathered and collected Han vassal lords and those who have fled due to crimes, hiding them and providing for their residence; he builds homes for them and bestows wealth, titles, ranks, land, and houses upon them. Some are even granted the rank of Guannei Hou (Inner Pass 侯), with stipends equivalent to that of a two-thousand-dan official—privileges they should not possess—all in preparation for some future action. Minister Dan, scholar Wu Kai Zhang and others among the seventy people, together with Crown Prince Qi of Ji Pu Hou, plotted rebellion, intending to endanger the imperial ancestral temple and state. They sent Wu Kai Zhang secretly to inform Chang, plotting with him to send envoys to Minyue as well as Xiongnu to mobilize their troops. Wu Kai Zhang went to Huainan to meet Chang, who frequently sat with him in conversation and shared meals; he provided for his family, arranged a marriage for him, and gave him a stipend equivalent to that of an official of the two-thousand-dan rank. Wu Kai Zhang sent someone to inform Dan that he had already reported this matter to the king. Chun dispatched an envoy to report back to Dan and others. The officials discovered this, and sent Wei Qi of Chang'an and others to arrest Wu Kai Zhang. Chang concealed him and refused to hand him over; he conspired with the former Commander-in-Chief Jian Ji to kill Wu Kai Zhang in order to prevent him from speaking. They prepared a coffin, casket, clothing, and shroud for Wu Kai Zhang, buried him in Feiling Yi, and lied to the officials, saying "we do not know where he is." They also gathered earth as if for a mound and erected a marker on it, saying, "Wu Kai Zhang died and was buried beneath this." Moreover, Chang himself personally killed an innocent person. He ordered officials to execute six other innocent people; he falsely arrested those who had committed capital crimes in order to cover up his own offenses; He arbitrarily sentenced criminals, and without any official accusation or investigation, detained and punished fourteen people with sentences of labor in city walls or pounding grain; he pardoned criminals: eighteen who had been sentenced to death, and fifty-eight others whose punishments were less than labor in city walls or grain pounding; and he bestowed ranks upon ninety-four people, some as high as Guannei Hou. On a previous occasion when Chang was ill, the emperor worried and toiled over it, sending envoys with letters, dates, and dried meat as gifts. Chang did not wish to accept these gifts and refused to meet or bow before the imperial envoy. The people of Nanhai living within Lujiang territory rebelled, and Huainan officials and soldiers attacked them. The emperor, moved by the poverty and hardship of the people in Huainan, sent envoys to bestow five thousand bolts of silk upon Chang as a gift for his officials and soldiers who had labored hard. Chang did not want to accept the gifts, falsely claiming that "there are no laboring or weary officials and soldiers." A man named Wang Zhi from Nanhai submitted a letter to the emperor offering jade, but Jian Ji burned it without reporting it. The officials requested that Jian Ji be summoned for punishment, but Chang did not send him, falsely claiming "Jian is ill." Chun also requested an audience with Chang, expressing a wish to enter the court for inspection. Chang became angry and said, "You want to separate me from my loyalty and attach yourself to Han." King Huainan should be executed in public as a criminal; we humbly request that he be judged according to the law."

## 5

The imperial decree stated: "I cannot bear to subject King Huainan to legal punishment; let him be discussed by the marquises and officials of the two-thousand-dan ranks."

## 6

"Minister Cang, Minister Jing, Minister Yi, Minister Fu, and Minister He respectfully submit at risk of our lives: We have carefully discussed this matter with Marquis Yin and forty-three other officials of the two-thousand-dank rank. All said that 'Chang has not abided by laws or decrees, refused to obey imperial edicts, and secretly gathered followers as well as those plotting rebellion; he lavishly supported fugitives in order to prepare for some action.'" We have deliberated on this matter according to the law."

## 7

The imperial decree stated: "I cannot bear to apply legal punishment upon King Huainan; let Chang be pardoned for his capital crime and deposed from the kingship."

## 8

"Minister Cang et al. respectfully submit at risk of our own lives: Chang has committed a grave capital crime, yet the emperor cannot bear to apply the law and has mercifully pardoned him, deposing him from kingship. We humbly request that Chang be exiled to Qiongyou, Yandao, Shu Jun. Let his son and mother accompany him in residence; the county should build a house for them, providing grain, firewood, vegetables, salt, fermented soybeans, cooking utensils, mats, and bedding. We submit this request at risk of our lives; we respectfully ask that the decree be proclaimed to all under heaven."

## 9

The imperial decree stated: "Provide Chang with five catties of meat and two dou of wine per day. Order ten former concubines or attendants who once gained favor to accompany him in residence. All other matters may proceed as planned."

## 10

All those who had conspired with him were executed. Thus, King Huainan was sent away, transported in a covered wagon, and each county along the route was ordered to provide for his passage. At that time Yuan Ang advised the emperor, saying: "The emperor has always been indulgent toward King Huainan and did not appoint a strict tutor or prime minister for him; this is why he reached such a state. Moreover, King Huainan's nature is strong-willed; now suddenly crushing and humiliating him. I fear he may suddenly encounter mist or dew and fall ill or die. The emperor will then bear the name of having killed his own brother—how could this be!" The emperor said: "I merely wanted to make him suffer a little; now I will restore him." All the counties responsible for escorting King Huainan dared not open the seals on his transport wagon. King Huainan then said to his attendants: "Who would say that your lord is brave?" "How could I be brave!" "I became arrogant and thus did not hear of my faults until it reached this point." "Life is but a fleeting moment; how could I endure such distress!" He then refused to eat and died. When he arrived at Yong, the county official of Yong opened the seal and reported that King Huainan had died. The emperor wept bitterly and said to Yuan Ang: "I did not listen to your advice, and thus lost King Huainan." Yuan Ang replied: "It cannot be undone. I hope Your Majesty will console yourself." The emperor asked, "What should we do about it?" Yuan Ang said: "Only by beheading the prime minister and the Minister of Justice to apologize to all under heaven would suffice." The emperor immediately ordered the prime minister and Minister of Justice to arrest and investigate all county officials responsible for escorting King Huainhan who had failed to open the seal or provide food and attendants, and they were all executed in public. He was then buried at Yong with the rites for a marquis, with thirty households assigned to guard his tomb.

## 11

In the eighth year of Emperor Xiaowen's reign, moved by sympathy for King Huainan, who had four sons all aged seven or eight years old, he granted his son An the title of Fuling Hou, his son Bo the title of Anyang Hou, his son Ci the title of Yangzhou Hou, and his son Liang the title of Dongcheng Hou.

## 12

In the twelfth year of Emperor Xiaowen's reign, a song was composed by the people about Prince Li of Huainan: "One chi of cloth is still enough to sew; one dou of grain is still sufficient for pounding. Yet two brothers cannot live in harmony." The emperor heard of this and sighed, saying: "Yao and Shun exiled their own kin; Duke of Zhou killed Guan and Cai. The world praised them as sage rulers. "Why is that?" "Because they did not allow private feelings to harm the public good." "Do the people of the world really think I am greedy for Huainan Wang's territory?" He then transferred King Chengyang to rule the former territory of Huainan, and posthumously honored Prince Li with the title "King Li," establishing a mausoleum for him in accordance with the rituals befitting a vassal king.

## 13

In the sixteenth year of Emperor Xiaowen's rule, King Huainan Xi was transferred back to his original fief at Chengyang. The emperor felt pity for King Li of Huainan whose disregard for law and rebellious conduct had led to his loss of the kingdom and early death. Thus, he established three of his sons: Fuling Hou An as King of Huainhan, Anyang Hou Bo as King of Hengshan, and Yangzhou Hou Ci as King of Lujiang; they all regained lands that had belonged to King Li, divided among them in thirds. Dongcheng Hou Liang had died earlier and left no descendants.

## 14

In the third year of Emperor Xiaojing's reign, Wu, Chu, and seven other states rebelled; envoys from Wu arrived at Huainan. King Huainan wanted to raise troops in response. His prime minister said: "If Your Majesty is determined to raise an army and respond to Wu, I am willing to serve as the general." The king then entrusted his prime minister with command of the troops. After taking command, the prime minister of Huainan led the army and defended the city, disobeying the king's orders and instead supporting Han. The Han court also sent Qucheng Hou to lead troops in aid of Huainan; thus, Huainan was able to remain intact. When Wu's envoys arrived at Lujiang, King Lujiang refused to respond and instead sent messengers back and forth with Yue. When Wu's envoys reached Hengshan, King Hengshan remained steadfast in his defense and had no second thoughts. In the fourth year of Emperor Xiaojing's reign, after Wu and Chu had been defeated, King Hengshan paid court homage. The emperor regarded him as loyal and trustworthy, and thus comforted him by saying: "The southern regions are low-lying and damp." King Hengshan was transferred to rule Jinbei; this was done as a mark of commendation. Upon his death, he was posthumously granted the title "Zhengwang," meaning King Zhendao. King Lujiang had close relations with Yue, frequently sending and receiving envoys; therefore, he was transferred to become King of Hengshan and ruled the area north of the Yangtze River. King Huainan remained in his original position.

## 15

King Huainan An was a man who loved reading and playing the qin, disliking hunting with hawks and dogs or horseback riding for sport. He also wished to perform secret acts of virtue, pacify the common people, and earn a reputation throughout the land. At times he resented his father King Li's death, often harboring thoughts of rebellion, but lacked an opportunity to act on them. In the second year of Jianyuan, King Huainan paid a court visit. He had always been on good terms with Wu'an Hou, who at that time served as Taiwei. Wu'an Hou met King Huainan at Bashi and said to him: "Now the emperor has no crown prince; Your Majesty is a direct grandson of Emperor Gaozu, practicing benevolence and righteousness—this is known throughout the land." "If one day the emperor passes away without a successor, who else but Your Majesty should be enthroned?"!" King Huainan was greatly pleased and gave Wu'an Hou generous gifts of gold and valuables. He secretly gathered guests, pacified the common people, and made preparations for rebellion. In the sixth year of Jianyuan, a comet appeared; King Huainan was deeply unsettled by it. Someone advised the king: "When the Wu rebellion began, a comet appeared stretching several chi in length, yet blood still flowed for thousands of li." "Now this comet stretches across the entire sky—this indicates that great wars will erupt throughout the land." The king believed that since the emperor had no crown prince, if there were upheaval in the empire, the feudal lords would vie for power. He therefore intensified his preparations of weapons and war equipment, accumulating money to bribe and send gifts to wandering scholars, talented individuals, and influential figures among the commanderies, states, and vassal kings. Eloquent scholars who devised strategies fabricated superstitious prophecies and flattered the king; King Huainan, pleased by their words, gave them generous monetary rewards, and his plans for rebellion grew even more intense.

## 16

King Huainan had a daughter named Ling, who was intelligent and eloquent. The king loved Ling dearly and often gave her money to serve as an informant in Chang'an, where she formed secret connections with those close to the emperor. In the third year of Yuanshuo, the emperor bestowed a reading stand and walking stick upon King Huainan as a sign of respect, allowing him to refrain from attending court. Queen Tu, the queen consort of King Huainan, was favored and loved by the king. The queen gave birth to Crown Prince Qian; Qian married the daughter of Xiu Cheng Jun, who was the emperor's maternal grandmother. The king planned for rebellion and feared that his son-in-law might learn of it and leak the secret, so he conspired with the crown prince to feign indifference toward him, keeping them apart for three months. The king then pretended to be angry with the crown prince and confined him in a room together with his wife for three months, but the crown prince never approached her. When the princess requested to leave, the king wrote an imperial petition and sent her back in disgrace. Queen Tu, Crown Prince Qian, and daughter Ling were all favored by King Huainan; they monopolized state power, encroached upon the farmland and homes of commoners, and arbitrarily detained people.

## 17

In the fifth year of Yuanshuo, Crown Prince Qian studied swordsmanship and believed no one could match his skill. Upon hearing that Langzhong Lei Bei was particularly skilled, he summoned him for a sparring match. Lei Bei repeatedly declined the challenge out of courtesy, but accidentally struck the crown prince during their exchange. The crown prince became angry, and Lei Bei was terrified. At this time, those who wished to join the army would go directly to the capital; Lei Bei immediately expressed his desire to fight against the Xiongnu. Crown Prince Qian repeatedly spoke ill of Lei Bei to the king, and King Huainan ordered Langzhong Ling to expel him in order to prevent further complaints. As a result, Lei Bei fled to Chang'an and submitted a petition to clear his name. The emperor issued an edict referring the matter to Tingwei (the Minister of Justice) and Henan Commandery for investigation. Henan investigated the case, leading to the arrest of Crown Prince Qian. King Huainhan and Queen Tu plotted not to send their son away, so they decided to raise an army in rebellion; however, they hesitated for over ten days without making a final decision. Just then, an imperial decree arrived ordering the immediate interrogation of the crown prince. At that time, the prime minister of Huainhan was angry with Shouchun Cheng for detaining the crown prince and failing to send him off as ordered, and he impeached Cheng for showing disrespect. The king pleaded with his prime minister on behalf of Shouchun Cheng, but the prime minister refused to listen. King Huainhan sent someone to submit a petition accusing his prime minister; the matter was referred to Tingwei for investigation. The investigation revealed evidence linking King Huainhan himself, so he sent men to spy on the Han officials in the imperial court. The high-ranking officials then requested that the king be arrested and investigated. Fearing that the plot would be uncovered, Crown Prince Qian advised: "If Han officials come to arrest Your Majesty, have men dressed as imperial guards hold halberds and stand in the courtyard. If anyone approaches improperly near the king, they should stab and kill them. I will also send people to assassinate the Commander of Huainhan Commandery; then we can raise our troops—it is not too late." At that time, however, the emperor did not approve the request of his officials but instead sent Han Zhongwei Hong to interrogate and investigate King Huainhan. When King Huainhan learned that a Han official had arrived, he immediately followed the plan devised by his son. When the Han Zhongwei arrived, King Huainhan observed that his expression was calm and realized that he had only come to question him about the matter of Lei Bei. Concluding that there was no immediate danger, the king did not take any action. The Zhongwei returned and reported this to the emperor. The high-ranking officials handling the case said: "King Huainhan An obstructed Lei Bei and others who wished to fight against the Xiongnu, defying clear imperial edicts; he should be executed in public." The emperor did not approve this. The high-ranking officials requested that King Huainhan be stripped of his title and no longer allowed to rule, but the emperor again refused their request. The high-ranking officials petitioned for the reduction of five counties from Huainhan's territory; the emperor decreed that two counties be taken away instead. The emperor sent Zhongwei Hong to pardon King Huainhan of his crimes, but punished him by reducing his territory. When the Zhongwei entered the borders of Huainhan, he publicly announced the emperor's pardon for King Huainhan. Initially, when the king heard that Han officials had requested his execution, he did not know about the emperor's decision to reduce his territory. Upon learning of the arrival of a Han envoy, fearing arrest, King Huainhan once again conspired with Crown Prince Qian to assassinate him as previously planned. When the Zhongwei arrived and instead congratulated King Huainhan, the king did not carry out his plan. Later, he lamented to himself: "I practiced benevolence and righteousness yet still lost territory—it is a great shame." Nevertheless, after the reduction of his territory, King Huainhan's plans for rebellion became even more intense. Whenever envoys or messengers came from Chang'an, they spread superstitious lies claiming that the emperor had no male heirs and that Han was in decline; King Huainhan would rejoice at such news. If they reported instead that the imperial court was well governed and had a male heir, he would become angry, dismissing their words as lies.

## 18

Day and night, King Huainhan plotted with Wu Bei, Zuo Wu, and others by studying maps of the empire's geography, arranging where his troops would enter. The king said: "Since the emperor has no crown prince, when he passes away, the court ministers will surely summon King Jiaodong or else King Changshan. With all the feudal lords vying for power, how can I afford to be unprepared!" "And yet I am a grandson of Emperor Gaozu and have personally practiced benevolence and righteousness. The Son of Heaven has treated me kindly, so I can endure this; "Yet after a thousand generations, how could I possibly bow my head and serve as a subject to some upstart!"

## 19

The king sat in the eastern palace and summoned Wu Bei for consultation, saying: "General, please speak." Wu Bei looked dejected and said: "The emperor has shown great leniency toward Your Majesty. How can you still utter such words of national ruin?"! "I have heard that Wu Zixu advised King Goujian of Wu, but when the king refused to listen, he said: 'I foresee a time when deer will be seen roaming freely on the terrace of Gu Su.'" "Now I too see thorny brambles growing in the palace, and my clothes are soaked by dew." The king became angry and imprisoned Wu Bei's parents, holding them captive for three months. He summoned him again and asked: "Will General agree to assist me?" Wu Bei replied: "No, I only came to advise Your Majesty." "I have heard that the wise listen before sound is made and see clearly before things take shape, which is why sages achieve complete success in all their endeavors." "In the past, King Wen took one action and his achievements became renowned for a thousand generations; he was ranked among the Three Sage Dynasties. This is what is called acting in accordance with Heaven's will—thus, people throughout the realm followed him without being summoned." This is a matter clearly evident over a thousand years. The hundred-year-old Qin dynasty and the recent Wu-Chu rebellion are also sufficient to illustrate the rise and fall of a state. "I dare not avoid punishment like Zixu, but I beg Your Majesty not to be deaf as King Goujian was." "In the past, Qin cut off the path of sages, killed scholars, burned poetry and books, abandoned rites and righteousness, valued deceit and force, relied on punishments, and transported grain from across the sea to the western river." At that time, men worked hard in farming yet could barely obtain enough for coarse grain and poor food; women spun and wove but still lacked sufficient cloth to cover their bodies. They sent Meng Tian to build the Great Wall, stretching thousands of li from east to west. Troops were constantly exposed in the open, numbering tens of thousands; the dead could not be counted, corpses littered for a thousand li, and blood flowed across fields. The people's strength was exhausted, and among ten families, five wished to rebel. They also sent Xu Fu into the sea in search of divine beings and rare treasures. He returned with false words, saying: "I saw a great deity in the ocean who asked me, 'Are you an envoy from the Western Emperor?'" "I replied, 'Yes.'" "'What do you seek?'" "I said: 'I wish to request the elixir of longevity.'" "The deity replied, 'Your Qin king's offerings are insufficient; you may see it but not take it.'" He then followed me to the southeast, reaching Penglai Mountain, where I saw a palace of lingzhi mushrooms. There were envoys with copper-colored skin and dragon-like forms, their light shining up into the sky. At that moment, I bowed twice and asked: "What offerings should be presented?" The sea deity said: 'Offer a man of noble name or a woman with outstanding skills, along with the work of a hundred craftsmen, and you will obtain it.'"' The Emperor of Qin was greatly pleased, so he sent 3,000 men and women with exceptional talents, providing them with various types of five grains and the skills of a hundred artisans to accompany Xu Fu on his journey. Xu Fu found a vast plain and fertile land, settled there, and did not return to the king. At that time, the common people grieved and longed for him; out of every ten households, six were inclined toward rebellion. He also sent Wei Tuo to cross the Five Ridges and attack the Baiyue. Wei Tuo, knowing that China was exhausted from labor and hardship, chose to remain king there rather than return. He sent envoys to submit a petition requesting 30,000 women without husbands for the soldiers' clothing and provisions. The Emperor of Qin approved the request for 15,000 of them. At that time, the people's loyalty to the state disintegrated like broken tiles; out of every ten households, seven were inclined toward rebellion. A guest told Emperor Gao: "The time is ripe now." Emperor Gao said, "Wait a while; a sage will rise in the southeast region." No sooner had one year passed than Chen Sheng and Wu Guang rose up. Emperor Gao began his campaign in Feng and Pei, and at the first call, countless people from all over the world responded without prior arrangement. This is what is called taking advantage of a flaw and waiting for an opportunity, acting upon the collapse of Qin. The people desired him as a drought desires rain, so he rose from among the ranks of soldiers and was enthroned as emperor. His achievements surpassed those of the Three Kings, and his virtue is passed down endlessly. Now, Your Majesty sees how easily Emperor Gao gained the empire; yet do you not observe recent events with Wu and Chu? King Wu was granted the title of Liu Shi Jiaoshi, yet he refused to pay homage. He ruled over four commanderies with territory spanning thousands of li. Internally, he cast cinnabar copper into coins; in the east, he boiled seawater for salt; from above, he took wood from Jiangling to build ships, each ship's capacity equaling dozens of Chinese carts, making his state rich and its people numerous. He sent pearls, jade, gold, and silk to bribe the feudal lords, imperial relatives, and high-ranking ministers; only the Dou family refused. With plans finalized and strategies decided, he raised his army and marched westward. He was defeated at Daliang, routed at Hufu, fled eastward, and reached Dan Tu. The Yue people captured him; he died, his ancestral rites ended, becoming a laughingstock to the world. Why was it that with the large forces of Wu and Yue they could not achieve success? It is because they truly defied the will of Heaven and failed to recognize the right time. Now, Your Majesty's forces are not even one-tenth the size of Wu and Chu's at their peak; yet peace throughout the land is ten thousand times greater than it was during Qin. I hope Your Majesty will follow my strategy. If Your Majesty does not heed my advice, then now you see that your endeavor must surely fail and the plot has already been revealed. I have heard that Wei Zi passed by his former capital and felt sorrow, so he composed the song "Mai Xiu," expressing grief over King Zhou's failure to heed Prince Bi Gan's advice. Therefore, Mencius said: "Zhou was as noble as a Son of Heaven, yet in death he was not even comparable to an ordinary man." It is because Zhou had long since cut himself off from the people of the world; it was not on the day he died that Heaven and Earth abandoned him. Now, I too secretly grieve for Your Majesty's abandonment of a thousand chariots' worth of power; you will surely receive the decree of death, be the first to die among your ministers in the East Palace." At this, [the King] was filled with resentment and suppressed anger, tears overflowing his cheeks and streaming sideways. He immediately rose, ascended the steps one by one, and left.

## 20

The king had an illegitimate son named Bu Hai, the eldest of his sons, but the king did not love him. Neither the king, the queen, nor the crown prince regarded him as a brother or son. Bu Hai had a son named Jian, who was talented and spirited, often resentful that the crown prince did not visit his father; He also resented the fact that at this time, all the feudal lords were allowed to enfeoff their sons and brothers as marquises, yet only two sons of Huainan had been granted titles—one was the crown prince, while Jian's father alone had not received a title. Jian secretly formed alliances and plots to expose the crown prince's faults in order to replace him with his own father. The crown prince learned of this, and repeatedly arrested Jian, binding him up and beating him severely. Jian was fully aware of the crown prince's plot to kill the Han Zhongwei, so he instructed his trusted associate Shouchun Zhuang Zhi to submit a memorial to the Son of Heaven in Yuanshuo year six: "Poison is bitter on the tongue but beneficial for illness; loyal words are harsh to the ear but advantageous for conduct. "Now, Prince Jian of Huainan is highly talented and capable; King Huainan's queen dowager Tu and her son, the crown prince Qian, have long harbored resentment toward Jian. lø Jian's father Bu Hai is innocent yet has been repeatedly arrested without authority and they intend to kill him. Now Jian is still alive; he can be summoned for interrogation, as he knows all about the secret affairs of Huainan." The memorial was received by the Son of Heaven, who ordered the Grand Court of Justice to investigate this matter. The Grand Court then passed it down for investigation in Henan Commandery. At that time, Sun Shenqing, the grandson of the late Biyang Hou, was on good terms with Chancellor Gongsun Hong. Resenting Prince Li of Huainan for having killed his great-grandfather, he secretly provided information about Huainan's affairs to Hong. Thus, Hong suspected that Huainan had plans of rebellion and treason, and thoroughly investigated the case. Henan Commandery interrogated Jian, whose testimony implicated Prince Li's crown prince and his associates. King Huainan was troubled by this situation and considered taking action. He asked Wu Bei, "Does the Han court deal with chaos?" Wu Bei replied: "The world is at peace." King Huainan was displeased and said to Wu Bei, "On what basis do you say the world is peaceful?" Bei replied: "I have secretly observed the administration of the imperial court, the righteousness between ruler and subject, the affection between father and son, the distinctions between husband and wife, and the order among the young and old—all are in harmony. The emperor's actions follow ancient principles; customs and institutions remain intact without any deficiencies." Wealthy merchants, heavily laden with goods, travel throughout the world, where no road is impassable; thus, trade flourishes. The Nan Yue submitted in tribute; the Qiang and Bo peoples offered their tributes. The Dong'ou surrendered, extending Han territory to Guangchangyu and opening Shuofang. The Xiongnu had their wings clipped and were left without support or strength. Though it has not yet reached the level of peace in ancient times, it is still a time of order." The king was furious, and Wu Bei apologized for his capital offense. The king then asked Wu Bei: "If there is a military campaign in Shandong, the Han will surely send a great general to lead and control Shandong. What do you think of this great general as a person?" Bei replied: "The person I know well is Huang Yi, who followed the great general in attacking the Xiongnu. When he returned, he told me: 'The great general treats scholars and officials with courtesy, shows kindness to his soldiers, and all are willing to serve him.' "He rides up and down the mountains as if flying; his talent is unparalleled." Bei believed that with such abilities, having led troops repeatedly and gained experience in warfare, he would be hard to oppose. And when the imperial envoy Cao Liang returned from Chang'an, he said that the great general gave clear orders and was brave in facing the enemy, often leading his soldiers into battle. When resting at a campsite, if wells were being dug but not yet completed, he would wait until all the soldiers had water before drinking himself. After the campaign ended, when his troops had all crossed the river, only then did he cross. The gold and silk bestowed by the Empress Dowager were entirely given to the military officers. Even ancient famous generals could not surpass him." The king fell silent.

## 21

King Huainan, seeing that Jian had already been summoned for investigation, feared that the hidden affairs of his state would soon be discovered. He considered taking action but Wu Bei again advised against it. So he asked Wu Bei once more: "Do you think the rebellion by Wu was right or wrong?" Wu Bei said, "I believe it was wrong." King Wu had reached great wealth and honor, yet his actions were inappropriate. He died in Dan Tu, head and feet separated, with no descendants left to carry on his lineage. I have heard that King Wu deeply regretted it. I hope Your Majesty carefully considers this and does not repeat the mistakes for which King Wu later repented." The king said, "For a man, death comes from but one word. Moreover, how could Wu have known to rebel? In a single day, more than forty Han generals passed through Chenggao. Now I have ordered Lou Huan to first block the entrance of Chenggao, Zhou Bei to lead troops from Yingchuan to block the roads at Huanyuan and Yique, and Chen Ding to mobilize forces from Nanyang to guard Wuguan. The governor of Henan has only Luoyang under his control; there is no need for concern. Yet to the north, there are still the Linjin Pass, Hedong, Shangdang, and Hedong Commandery in Zhao State. People say: 'Cut off the entrance to Chenggao, and no one can pass through the world.' If we control the perilous terrain of Sanchuan and raise troops from Shandong, launching a campaign in this way, what do you think, sir?" Wu Bei replied: "I see only disaster, not any benefit." The king said: "Zuo Wu, Zhao Xian, Zhu Jiao Ru and others all believe there will be blessings; ten matters nine are accomplished. You alone think it brings disaster without benefit—why is that?" Wu Bei replied: "Your Majesty's ministers and favored officials who have always been able to command the masses were all previously arrested by imperial decree. There are no others left who can be used." The king said: "Chen Sheng and Wu Guang had no land even to plant a cone, yet with an assembly of only one thousand men, they rose from the marshes. With a single cry, the whole world responded; their forces extended westward as far as Xi, numbering 120,000 troops." "Now, although my state is small, I can still raise more than 100,000 soldiers capable of battle. These are not merely conscripted 戍 troops wielding farm tools and sticks as Chen Sheng did—why then do you say there will be disaster without benefit?" Wu Bei replied: "In the past, Qin was lawless and cruel to all under heaven. It raised a vast imperial entourage, built the Afang Palace, levied heavy taxes taking more than half of people's produce, and conscripted commoners for military service. Fathers were not at peace with their sons, elder brothers did not get along well with younger ones. The government was harsh and punishments severe; the world suffered as if burned to a crisp. People looked up in sorrow, listened eagerly, wept toward heaven, and pounded their chests in resentment of the ruler. Hence, when Chen Sheng cried out, the whole land responded." Now, His Majesty rules over all under heaven, unifying the realm. He universally loves and cares for the people, spreading virtue and bestowing kindness. Though he has not yet spoken, his voice is as swift as thunder; though orders have not been issued, their influence spreads like magic. When the Son of Heaven harbors a thought in mind, his authority stirs across ten thousand li. The people below respond to him as shadow follows form or echo follows sound. And the great general's talent is no less than that of Zhang Han and Yang Xiong. Your Majesty compares yourself to Chen Sheng and Wu Guang; I believe this is a mistake," said Bei. The king asked: "If as you say, then there's no chance of success by luck?" Bei replied: "I have a foolish plan." The king asked, "What is it?" Bei said: "Now the feudal lords have no rebellious intentions and the people harbor no resentment. The Shuofang commandery has vast tracts of fertile land and abundant water and grass, yet the number of migrants is insufficient to populate it. My foolish plan is to forge a document as if issued by the Chancellor and the Imperial Censor, requesting that we relocate powerful local gentry, wandering knights-errant, and those with serious crimes or above. We should pardon them and clear their records of guilt; for those whose property exceeds 500,000 coins, we should also move their families to Shuofang Commandery. Additionally, we can urgently mobilize more soldiers and set strict deadlines for their assembly. We should also forge documents as if issued by the Left and Right Grand Works Commissioners of the Shanglin 苑 Central Officials' Imperial Prison (to arrest) [and] arrest the favored ministers of the feudal lords' crown princes. If we do this, the people will become resentful and the feudal lords fearful. Even if we then send Bianshu to persuade them, perhaps there is a one in ten chance of success by luck?" The king said: "This may work." "Nevertheless," the king added, "I believe it will not come to this extent." Thus, the king ordered his palace slaves to enter the palace and forge an imperial seal, as well as seals for the Chancellor, Imperial Censor, Grand General, military officers, officials of the rank of Zhong Erqianshi, commandery and office magistrates and their deputies, and even the seals of neighboring commandery governors and military commanders. He also prepared Han-style diplomatic staffs and ceremonial hats, intending to follow Wu Bei's plan. He sent men pretending to have committed a crime westward, to report the matter to the Grand General and Chancellor; On one day, we will raise our troops and send men to assassinate the Grand General Qing, while persuading the Chancellor to surrender. It would be as easy as peeling off a blindfold.

## 22

The king wanted to mobilize troops within his state, but he feared that the Chancellor and officials of rank Erqianshi would not obey him. Therefore, the king plotted with Wu Bei to first kill the Chancellor and the Erqianshi officials; They would fake a fire breaking out in the palace, and when the Chancellor and Erqianshi officials rushed to put it out, they could kill them upon their arrival. The plan had not yet been finalized, and the king also wanted to send men dressed in clothes of common thieves, carrying feathered emergency proclamations, coming from the east, shouting, "Nanyue troops have crossed into our territory," so as to use this as a pretext to raise an army. He thus sent men to Lujiang and Kuaiji to act as common thieves, but the plan had not yet been carried out. The king asked Wu Bei: "If I raise my troops and march westward, there must be feudal lords who will respond to me; if none do, what then?" Bei said: "We can move south to capture Hengshan and attack Lujiang, seize the boats of Xunyang, defend the city of Xiachi, establish strongholds at the piers of Jiujiang, block the mouth of Yuzhang Commandery, station powerful crossbowmen along the river for defense, and prevent forces from Nanjun from advancing. Moving eastward to capture Jiangdu and Kuaiji, we can also connect with the powerful Yue people in the south. By dominating the Huaihe and Yangtze regions, we could still prolong our time by several years." The king said: "Good, there is nothing better than this plan. If things become urgent, we can just flee to Yue."

## 23

At this time, the Minister of Justice reported that Wang Sunjian's accusations implicated Crown Prince Qian of the Kingdom of Huainan. The emperor sent a supervisor from the Ministry of Justice, who was appointed as the Inspector of Huainan Commandery, to arrest the crown prince. When they arrived at Huainan, King Huainan learned of this and plotted with his son to summon the Chancellor and officials of Erqianshi rank, intending to kill them and raise an army. They summoned the Chancellor, who arrived; the Chief Administrator of Internal Affairs claimed to be out as an excuse. The Inspector said: "I have received imperial orders and cannot meet the king." Realizing that killing only the Chancellor while the Chief Administrator, Inspector, and others did not come would be of no benefit, King Huainan dismissed the Chancellor. The king hesitated; his plan was still undecided. The crown prince, thinking that the crime he was charged with was plotting to assassinate a Han Inspector of Justice and those who had conspired with him were already dead, believed there would be no further accusations. He then told the king: "All officials capable of being used have been previously detained; now there is no one left worthy of joining us in an uprising." The king thought that launching a rebellion at this time would likely be fruitless, so he said: "I wish to surrender and await arrest." The king also secretly wanted to cease hostilities and thus agreed with the crown prince. The crown prince then cut his own throat, but did not die immediately. Wu Bei turned himself in to the authorities and confessed that he had conspired with King Huainan to rebel, detailing all the evidence of their conspiracy as such.

## 24

The officials then arrested the crown prince and the queen, surrounded the king's palace, and captured all of King Huainan's guests who had conspired in the rebellion within the kingdom. They searched for and seized all evidence of the conspiracy to report it upwards. The emperor and high-ranking officials investigated the case, and thousands of marquises, Erqianshi-level officials, and powerful local figures who were implicated in having conspired with King Huainan in rebellion were all punished to varying degrees according to the severity of their crimes. King Cai of Hengshan, who was the younger brother of King Huainan, should have been implicated and arrested. The officials requested that King Hengshan be taken into custody. The emperor said: "Each feudal lord governs his own state independently; they should not bear collective responsibility for each other's crimes. I will convene a meeting with the feudal kings, marquises, and Chancellor to discuss this matter among the feudal lords." King Pengzu of Zhao, Marquis Rang and forty-two others deliberated, all saying: "Prince An of Huainan committed a most heinous act of treason and was clearly plotting rebellion; he should be executed for his crimes." Chen Duan, a minister of King Jiaoxi, argued: "Prince An of Huainan abandoned the law and pursued evil deeds, harboring deceitful and false intentions to disturb the realm, misleading the people, betraying the ancestral temple, and recklessly spreading superstitious lies. The Spring and Autumn Annals says: 'Ministers must not take up arms; if they do, they should be punished.' An's crime is more serious than that of a general, and his plot to rebel has already been clearly established. Minister Duan personally examined the documents, seals, diagrams, and other evidence of his treasonous and inhumane acts, which were clearly verified. His crime is a most serious act of treachery and inhumanity; he should be subjected to due punishment under the law." Those state officials of rank 200 Shi and above, as well as those comparable to them, who failed in their duty to advise or restrain the king, should all be dismissed from office, stripped of their titles, reduced to commoners, and forbidden from holding any official position. Those who are not officials shall pay a fine of two jin and eight liang in gold as redemption for their death penalty. This will serve to highlight the crime of Prince An and make it clear throughout the realm what is expected of subjects, so that no one dares again entertain wicked or disloyal thoughts." The Chancellor Hong and the Minister of Justice Tang reported this to the emperor, who then dispatched an official from the Office of the Imperial Clan Keeper with a token and imperial decree to deal with King Huainan. Before they arrived, Prince An of Huainan cut his own throat and died. The queen dowager Tu, Crown Prince Qian, and all those who had conspired in rebellion with them were exterminated along with their entire clans. The emperor considered that Wu Bei's formal submission contained many references praising the Han dynasty and wished not to execute him. Minister of Justice Tang said: "Wu Bei was the first to devise a plan for rebellion on behalf of the king; his crime is unforgivable." Thus, Wu Bei was executed. The kingdom was abolished and reorganized as Jiujiang Commandery.

## 25

King Cai of Hengshan had three sons by his queen dowager Chengshu: the eldest son, Shuang, was the crown prince; the second son, Xiao; and the third daughter, Wucai. He also had four sons and daughters by his concubine Xu Lai, and two sons by the beauty Jueji. King Hengshan and King Huainan were brothers who often blamed each other for breaches of etiquette, and they did not get along well. King Hengshan learned that King Huainan had prepared for rebellion, and he also secretly gathered guests to respond in kind, fearing that he might be swallowed up by him.

## 26

In the sixth year of Yuanguang, King Hengshan paid a court visit. His attendant Wei Qing had knowledge of esoteric arts and wished to submit a memorial to the emperor. The king was angry about this, so he falsely accused Qing of capital punishment and forced him to confess under torture. The Chief Administrator of Hengshan believed it unjust and rejected the case. King Hengshan sent men to submit a memorial accusing the Chief Administrator, but when the matter was investigated by the authorities, it was found that King Hengshan himself had acted unjustly. The king also repeatedly encroached upon and seized other people's farmland, destroying tombs to convert them into fields for his own use. The officials requested that King Hengshan Wang be arrested and investigated. The emperor did not approve, but instead appointed officials of rank 200shi or above for the kingdom. King Hengshan was thus enraged and plotted with Xi Ci, Zhang Guangchang, and others to seek out individuals skilled in military strategy and the observation of celestial omens. Day and night, they leisurely engaged in secret discussions about rebellion for the king.

## 27

After Queen Dowager Chengshu died, Xu Lai was installed as the new queen dowager. Jueji also enjoyed his favor. 𝑙 The two women became jealous of each other, and Jueji slandered Queen Dowager Xu Lai to the crown prince, saying: "Xu Lai has sent her maids to use witchcraft to kill your mother." The crown prince harbored resentment toward Xu Lai. Xu Lai's elder brother came to Hengshan, and the crown prince invited him for a drink, then stabbed and injured Queen Dowager Xu Lai's brother with a blade. Queen Dowager Xu Lai became resentful and angry, repeatedly speaking ill of the crown prince to the king. The princess's younger sister, Wucai, after being divorced and returning home, had an affair with a slave and also engaged in relationships with other men. The crown prince repeatedly reprimanded Wucai for this, which angered her, so she refused to have any contact with the crown prince. Queen Dowager Xu Lai heard about it and began treating Wucai kindly. Wucai, along with her elder brother Xiao, had lost their mother at a young age and thus attached themselves to the queen dowager. The queen dowager devised plans to show affection toward them, and together they spread malicious rumors about the crown prince. Because of this, the king repeatedly beat the crown prince with a stick. In the middle of Yuanshuo year four, someone secretly injured the queen dowager's foster mother. The king suspected that the crown prince had sent someone to do it and beat the prince with a stick as punishment. Later, when the king fell ill, the crown prince claimed illness and did not attend him. King Xiao, Queen Dowager Xu Lai, and Wucai slandered the crown prince: "The crown prince is actually not ill; he falsely claimed to be sick with a look of joy on his face." The king was furious and wanted to depose the crown prince, replacing him with his younger brother Xiao. Queen Dowager knew that the king had decided to deposed the crown prince, and she also wanted to have Xiao removed from consideration as heir. The queen dowager had a female attendant who was skilled in dancing, and the king favored her. The queen dowager wanted to have this servant engage in an affair with Xiao to disgrace him, hoping thereby to remove both brothers from consideration for heir and instead install her own son Guang as crown prince. The crown prince Shuang learned of this, and realizing that the queen dowager would not cease in her malicious accusations against him, he decided to engage with her servant in an affair in order to silence her. While the queen dowager was drinking, the crown prince approached to offer a toast and then grabbed her thigh, requesting to sleep with her. The queen dowager became angry and reported this incident to the king. The king summoned him, intending to bind him and beat him with a stick. The crown prince knew that the king often wanted to depose him and instead install his younger brother Xiao, so he told the king: "Xiao has engaged in an affair with your concubine, Wucai with a slave. You are forcing me to eat this injustice; I will submit a memorial." He immediately turned his back on the king and left. The king sent men to stop him, but none could prevent him from leaving; so he personally drove out to pursue and capture the crown prince. The crown prince made outrageous remarks, and as a result, the king put him in shackles and confined him within the palace. Xiao became increasingly favored and trusted by the king. The king was impressed by Xiao's talents and abilities, so he gave him the royal seal to wear, titled him "General," ordered him to reside in an outer residence, provided him with ample money, and instructed him to recruit guests. The guests who came gradually learned of the rebellious plans of Huainan and Hengshan, and day and night they leisurely advised Xiao. The king then ordered Xiao's guest Jiangdu native Jiu He and Chen Xi to make chariots, arrows with sharp tips, and carve imperial seals as well as the seals of generals, ministers, and military officials. The king day and night sought out strong men like Zhou Qiu, often citing the plans of Wu and Chu during their rebellion as a model to bind them together. King Hengshan did not dare to emulate King Huainan by seeking the imperial throne directly, but he feared that if Huainan rose in rebellion, it might swallow his own kingdom. He believed that once Huainan moved westward, it would raise an army and conquer the region between the Jiang and Huai rivers, and he aspired to do the same.

## 28

In autumn of Yuanshuo Year Five, King Hengshan was scheduled to pay a court visit. In the sixth year, when he passed through Huainan on his way, King Huainan spoke with him as brothers and resolved past grievances, making plans for rebellion together. King Hengshan then submitted a memorial claiming illness to excuse himself from attending court, and the emperor sent him a letter granting permission not to attend.

## 29

In the middle of Yuanshou Year Six, King Hengshan sent someone to submit a memorial requesting that Crown Prince Shuang be deposed and his younger brother Xiao be installed as crown prince instead. Shuang, upon hearing this, immediately sent Bai Ying, someone he trusted, to Chang'an to submit a memorial stating that Xiao had made chariots and arrows with sharp tips and committed adultery with the king's attendants, in an attempt to ruin Xiao. Bai Ying arrived at Chang'an but had not yet submitted his memorial when officials arrested him and detained him in connection with the Huainan affair. The king, upon learning that Shuang had sent Bai Ying to submit the memorial, feared that he would reveal secret matters of the kingdom and so submitted a counter-memorial accusing Crown Prince Shuang of committing crimes deserving public execution for his misconduct. The matter was referred to Pei Jun for investigation. In winter of the first year of Yuanshou, officials and ministers sent to Pei Jun in search of those who had conspired with Huainan in rebellion failed to find them, but they captured Chen Xi at the residence of Xiao, son of King Hengshan. The officials accused Xiao of harboring Chen Xi. Xiao believed that Chen Xi had often conspired with the king to rebel and feared that he might expose them. Hearing of a legal provision that allowed one who voluntarily confessed to have their crime pardoned, and suspecting that Crown Prince Shuang had sent Bai Ying's memorial to reveal this matter, Xiao decided to confess first, reporting those who had plotted rebellion with him—Jiu He, Chen Xi, and others. After the Ministry of Justice investigated and verified the case, the officials and ministers requested to arrest King Hengshan for interrogation. The emperor said: "Do not arrest him." He sent Zhongwei An and Daxing Xi to question the king immediately, and the king fully answered with the truth. The officials then surrounded the royal palace and stationed guards there. After Zhongwei Daxing returned and reported this, the officials and ministers requested that Zongzheng and Daxing be sent together with Pei Jun to jointly investigate King Hengshan. Upon hearing of this, the king immediately committed suicide by cutting his throat. Xiao had confessed to rebellion before being discovered and thus was pardoned; he was, however, found guilty of committing adultery with the king's concubine and was executed in public. The queen, Xu Lai, was also condemned for using witchcraft to kill the former queen, Chengshu; and Crown Prince Shuang was condemned by the king on charges of filial impiety. They were both executed in public. All those who had conspired against King Hengshan were exterminated, their entire clans wiped out. The kingdom was abolished and reorganized as Hengshan Commandery.

## 30

Grand Historian said: What the Book of Songs says, "We repel the Rong and Di, we punish Jing and Shu,"—this statement is indeed true. Huainan and Hengshan were closely related by blood, each ruling a territory of thousands of li, enfeoffed as feudal lords. Yet they did not strive to fulfill their duties as vassal ministers in supporting the Son of Heaven, but instead harbored wicked schemes, plotting rebellion. As a result, father and son both lost their kingdoms twice over; neither lived out his life, becoming objects of ridicule throughout the land. This was not merely the fault of the kings themselves, but also due to their shallow customs and the gradual corruption of their ministers and subjects that led to this outcome. The people of Jingchu are strong, brave, and reckless, prone to rebellion—this has been recorded since ancient times.
