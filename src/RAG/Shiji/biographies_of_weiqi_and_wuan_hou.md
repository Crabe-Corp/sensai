# Biographies of Weiqi and Wu'an Hou

## 1

Weiqi Hou Dou Ying was a nephew of Empress Xiaowen's cousin. His father was a native of Shigu Jin. He liked entertaining guests. During the reign of Emperor Xiaowen, Ying served as governor of Wu but was dismissed due to illness. At the beginning of Emperor Xiao Jing's reign, he became Zhanshi.

## 2

Liang Xiaowang was a younger brother of Emperor Xiao Jing; his mother, Empress Dowager Dou, loved him dearly. When Liang Xiaowang came to court, he and his brothers feasted together. At that time, the emperor had not yet designated a crown prince. When they were drunk and in high spirits, he casually said, "After my death, the throne will be passed to Prince Liang." Empress Dowager was delighted. Dou Ying took a cup of wine and presented it to the emperor, saying, "The empire belongs to Emperor Gaozu's empire. It is passed from father to son; this is an agreement of the Han dynasty. How can Your Majesty arbitrarily pass it on to Prince Liang!" From then on, Empress Dowager came to hate Dou Ying. Dou Ying also looked down upon his official position and was dismissed due to illness. lington Empress Dowager removed Dou Ying's gate credentials, forbidding him from entering court or making requests.

## 3

In the third year of Emperor Xiao Jing's reign (154 BC), Wu and Chu rebelled. The emperor observed among his royal relatives that none of the Dous were as virtuous as Dou Ying, so he summoned Ying. Ying entered to see him and firmly declined, citing illness and claiming himself unworthy of the position. Empress Dowager also felt ashamed. The emperor then said, "Now that the empire is in urgent need, how can you, my royal grandson, possibly refuse?" He then appointed Ying as Grand General and bestowed upon him a thousand catties of gold. Ying then recommended several renowned generals and virtuous scholars such as Yuan Ang and Luan Bu who were at home, recommending them for appointment. The gold he was granted, he had displayed under the corridor eaves; whenever military officers passed by, they were allowed to take whatever amount they needed for use. None of the gold entered his home. Dou Ying was stationed at Yingyang, overseeing the armies of Qi and Zhao. After the seven states' forces had been completely defeated, Ying was enfeoffed as Hou Weiqi. All itinerant scholars and guests vied to return to Weiqi Hou. During Emperor Xiao Jing's reign, whenever major matters were discussed in court, neither Marquis Tiao nor Weiqi Hou dared to treat the other as an equal.

## 4

In the fourth year of Emperor Xiao Jing's reign , Crown Prince Li was established, and Weiqi Hou was appointed as his tutor. In the seventh year of Emperor Xiao Jing's reign, Crown Prince Li was deposed, and Weiqi repeatedly argued but could not prevail. Weiqi claimed illness and withdrew to live in seclusion for several months beneath the southern mountains of Lantian. Various guests and eloquent scholars tried to persuade him, but none could bring him back. Gao Sui of Liang then advised Weiqi, saying, "What can make a general rich and noble is the emperor; what can be intimate with General is Empress Dowager. Now, you are the tutor of the crown prince; yet when the crown prince was deposed, you could not fight for him; having failed to prevail in your argument and also failing to die. You voluntarily claimed illness, took a woman from Zhao as a concubine, withdrew into seclusion, and did not attend court. To be compared together like this is to openly expose the emperor's faults. If the two palaces were to take action against you, your wife and children would suffer as well." Weiqi Hou found his words convincing, so he rose again and resumed attending court as before.

## 5

Tao Hou was dismissed from the position of prime minister, and Empress Dowager Dou repeatedly spoke highly of Weiqi Hou. Emperor Xiao Jing said, "Does the empress dowager think that I have any favoritism and thus did not appoint Weiqi as prime minister? Weiqi is just a man who is pleased with himself, and he is too fickle. He cannot serve as prime minister; he lacks steadiness." Thus, he was not appointed. Instead, Jianling Hou Wei Wan was made chancellor.

## 6

Wu'an Hou Tian Fen was the maternal younger brother of Empress Xiaojing, and he was born in Changling. After Weiqi had become Grand General, he was in the height of his power. At that time, Fen served as a junior official and had not yet gained prominence; he would go back and forth to serve wine at Weiqi's gatherings, kneeling and rising like a son or younger relative. By the later years of Emperor Xiao Jing's reign, Fen became increasingly honored and powerful, serving as Taizhong Dafu. Fen was eloquent and articulate; he studied various texts such as the Pan Yu classics, and Empress Dowager Wang regarded him as virtuous. After Emperor Xiao Jing died, the crown prince was immediately enthroned and assumed power. Much of what was pacified and stabilized had been planned by Fen's guests; his younger brother Tian Sheng also, as a brother-in-law to Empress Dowager, in the third year after Emperor Xiao Jing's death, was enfeoffed: Fen as Wu'an Hou and Sheng as Zhouyang Hou.

## 7

Wu'an Hou, newly eager to assume power as prime minister, humbled himself before his guests and elevated renowned scholars who were at home in official positions, hoping thereby to outshine Weiqi and the other generals and ministers. In the first year of Jianyuan , Chancellor Wan fell ill and was dismissed, so the emperor convened a discussion on appointing a new chancellor and Taiwei. Ji Fu advised Wu'an Hou, saying, "Weiqi has long been honored; the scholars of the empire have always looked up to him. Now that you are newly rising, not yet as prominent as Weiqi; if the emperor appoints you as chancellor, you will certainly have to yield to Weiqi. If Weiqi becomes chancellor, General must become Taiwei. The positions of Taiwei and Chancellor are equal in rank; there will also be the reputation of yielding to a virtuous man." Wu'an Hou then subtly advised Empress Dowager to influence the emperor. Thus, Weiqi Hou was made chancellor and Wu'an Hou became Taiwei. Ji Fu congratulated Weiqi Hou and, at the same time, said with a sigh, "Your Lordship's nature is to love good people and hate evil ones. At present, virtuous men praise you, so you have risen to chancellor; yet since you are quick to despise the wicked and there are many evil people, they will also damage your reputation. If you can tolerate both good and bad, then you will be fortunate for a long time; if not, you may now fall from favor due to criticism." Weiqi did not listen.

## 8

Both Weiqi and Wu'an favored Confucianism, promoting Zhao Wan as Yushi Daifu and Wang Zang as Langzhong Ling. They invited Shen Gong of Lu, wishing to establish the Hall of Brightness (Mingtang), ordering marquises and dukes to return to their fiefs, abolishing border checkpoints, instituting ceremonial dress according to rites, in order to promote an era of peace and prosperity. They selected suitable members from the Dou family among the imperial clan who lacked moral integrity and removed them from their official records. At that time, many of the imperial relatives who were marquises or dukes had married princesses and did not wish to return to their fiefdoms. Because of this, daily criticisms reached Empress Dowager Dou. Empress Dowager favored the teachings of Huang-Lao (Daoism), while Weiqi, Wu'an, Zhao Wan, Wang Zang and others were determined to promote Confucian doctrines and belittle Daoist ideas. Therefore, Empress Dowager became increasingly displeased with Weiqi and his associates. And in the second year of Jianyuan, Yushi Daifu Zhao Wan requested that no matters be reported to the Eastern Palace. Empress Dowager Dou was greatly angered, so she dismissed and expelled Zhao Wan, Wang Zang, and others. She also removed the positions of Chancellor and Taiwei, appointing Bai Zhi Hou Xu Chang as chancellor and Wuqiang Hou Zhuang Qingzhi as Yushi Daifu. From then on, Weiqi and Wu'an remained at home in their noble status without official duties.

## 9

Although Wu'an Hou did not hold an official post, due to Empress Dowager Wang's favor, he remained close and favored. He often spoke on matters and achieved much success; thus, officials and scholars who sought power and profit all left Weiqi and flocked to Wu'an. As a result, Wu'an became increasingly arrogant. In the sixth year of Jianyuan (135 BC), Empress Dowager Dou died. Chancellor Chang and Yushi Daifu Qingzhi were dismissed for failing to properly handle funeral arrangements. Wu'an Hou Fen was appointed as chancellor, and Da Siniang Han Anguo became Yushi Daifu. Scholars from all over the empire and marquises of various fiefs increasingly attached themselves to Wu'an.

## 10

Wu'an was not handsome, but he was born into great nobility. Moreover, many of the feudal kings were now grown; since the emperor had just ascended the throne and was still young, Fen, as a close relative serving as chancellor in the capital, believed that unless he strictly adhered to propriety and humbled them forcefully, the empire would not be orderly. At this time, when the chancellor entered court to present matters, he would sit and speak for hours; whatever he said was always accepted. Those recommended by him might rise from commoners directly to the rank of 2,000-dan officials; power had shifted away from the emperor. The emperor then said, "Have you already appointed all your officials? I also want to appoint some officials." Once, he requested more land for the construction of his residence. The emperor was angry and said, "Why don't you just take the Armory!" After this, he withdrew. Once when he hosted a banquet for his guests, he seated his elder brother Gai Hou facing south and himself sat facing east, considering that as the Han chancellor, one could not be privately swayed by familial ties. Wu'an thus became increasingly arrogant, building a residence superior to all others in luxury. His farmland was the most fertile, and his caravans transporting goods purchased from various commanderies and counties were constantly seen on the roads. In front of his hall, he displayed bells and drums, and erected curved banners; hundreds of women were in the inner chambers. The feudal lords presented him with gold, jade, dogs, horses, and all kinds of luxuries, too numerous to count.

## 11

Weiqi had lost the favor of Empress Dowager Dou and became increasingly distant from power, losing influence. His guests gradually withdrew and grew indifferent or arrogant; only General Guan remained loyal to him without change. Weiqi became increasingly silent and frustrated, but he treated General Guan especially kindly.

## 12

General Guan's wife was a native of Yingyin. Her father, Zhang Meng, had once served as a retainer for Marquis Ying of Yingyin and gained favor. Through this connection, he rose to the rank of 2000-dan official; thus, she was given the surname Guan by the Guan family and became known as Guan Meng. During the rebellion of Wu and Chu, Marquis Yingyin Guan He served as a general under Taiwei, who requested that Guan Meng be appointed as an Xiuwei. She went with her father and a thousand men. Guan Meng was old, and Marquis Yingyin strongly requested his services. Feeling dejected and dissatisfied, he often led his troops into the fiercest battles and eventually died in the Wu army's ranks. According to military law, if father and son both served in the army and one was killed in action, they were allowed to return home with the body for burial. Guan Fu refused to return with his father's corpse, and resolutely declared, "I wish to behead King Wu or a general of his army in order to avenge my father." Thus, Guan Fu donned armor and held a halberd, recruiting dozens of brave soldiers from the army who were his friends and willing to follow him. When they left the camp gate, no one dared to advance. Only two men and a dozen or so of his servants rode into the Wu army, reaching beneath the banner of a Wu general, killing and wounding dozens. Unable to advance further, they turned back in full gallop, fleeing into the Han camp; all their servants were lost, and Guan Fu returned alone with just one horse. Guan Fu had suffered more than ten serious wounds; it was only because he happened to have a fine medicinal remedy worth 10,000 gold pieces that he did not die. After his wounds had slightly healed, Guan Fu once again requested from the general, "I now know more about the intricacies of Wu's camp. Please allow me to go back." The general admired his valor and righteousness, but fearing that Guan Fu might be lost, he reported the matter to Taiwei. Taiwei firmly prevented him from going. After Wu was defeated, Guan Fu became famous throughout the empire for this feat.

## 13

Yingyin Hou recommended him to the emperor; the emperor appointed Guan Fu as Zhonglangjiang. After several months, he was dismissed from office for breaking the law. Later, while living at home in Chang'an, all the prominent officials in Chang'an failed to fail to praise him. During the reign of Emperor Xiaojing, he rose to become Chancellor of Dai. After Emperor Xiaojing died and the current emperor ascended the throne, he considered Huaiyang to be a place of strategic importance with strong military forces; therefore, he transferred Guan Fu to serve as governor of Huaiyang. In the first year of Jianyuan, he was appointed Tai Pu. In the second year, Guan Fu and Zhangle Weiwei Dou Fu drank together; they failed to observe proper etiquette in their drinking. Guan Fu became drunk and struck Dou Fu. Dou Fu was a brother of Empress Dowager Dou. The emperor, fearing that the empress dowager would punish Guan Fu, transferred him to serve as Chancellor of Yan. After several years, he was removed from office for breaking the law and returned home to live in Chang'an.

## 14

Guan Fu was a man of firmness and integrity who drank heavily, and he did not like flattery to one's face. If powerful relatives or influential figures ranked higher than himself were unwilling to show him courtesy, he would certainly look down on them; Those scholars who ranked lower than himself, even if they were poorer and of low status, he especially respected as equals. In the presence of many people, he would recommend and honor those of lower rank. Scholars also praised him for this reason.

## 15

Guan Fu did not like literature, but he favored chivalry and kept his word once given. All those with whom he associated were nothing but powerful rascals and notorious villains. His family's wealth accumulated to tens of thousands, and his guests ate at his house daily in dozens or even hundreds. With ponds, fields, and estates, his clan members and guests engaged in power struggles and acted recklessly, causing disorder in Yingchuan. The youths of Yingchuan even sang a song: "When the waters of Ying are clear, the Guan clan is at peace; When the waters of Ying grow murky, the Guan family suffers."

## 16

Although Guan Fu was wealthy while living at home, he had lost power and influence; his retinue of officials, ministers, attendants, and guests gradually dwindled. When Weiqi Hou lost power, he also wanted to rely on Guan Fu to criticize and expose those who had once admired him but later abandoned him. Guan Fu also relied on Weiqi Hou to connect with marquises, members of the imperial clan, and others in order to enhance his reputation. The two supported each other's prestige; their relationship was as close as that between father and son. They became very happy with each other, never tiring of one another, regretting they had come to know each other too late.

## 17

Guan Fu was in mourning and visited the Chancellor. The Chancellor spoke casually, saying, "I wanted to visit Weiqi Hou with Zhongru, but coincidentally, Zhongru is in mourning." Guan Fu said, "General would be so kind as to visit Weiqi Hou, how could I dare use my mourning attire as an excuse!" "Please inform Weiqi Hou's household to prepare the accommodations; General should visit early tomorrow morning." Wu'an agreed. Guan Fu informed Weiqi Hou exactly as he had said about Marquis Wu'anhou. Weiqi and his wife bought more cattle and wine, swept the premises at night, and prepared accommodations from early morning until dawn. At daybreak, he ordered attendants under his gate to wait in anticipation. By midday, the Chancellor had not arrived. Weiqi said to Guan Fu, "Could it be that the Chancellor has forgotten?" Guan Fu was displeased and said, "I requested to come despite my mourning; he should have come." He then mounted his carriage and went out himself to meet the Chancellor. The Chancellor had merely made a casual promise to Guan Fu earlier, with no real intention of going. When Guan Fu arrived at the gate, the Chancellor was still lying in bed. At this point, Guan Fu entered to see him and said, "General, you were kind enough to promise yesterday to visit Weiqi. Weiqi and his wife have been preparing the banquet since dawn until now, and they have not dared to taste a single meal." Wu'an stammered in apology, saying, "I was drunk yesterday and forgot to mention it to Zhongru." He then mounted his carriage to go, but proceeded slowly; Guan Fu became even more angry. When the drinking reached its height and the wine was flowing freely, Guan Fu got up to dance and invited the Chancellor to join him. The Chancellor did not rise, so Guan Fu proceeded from his seat to speak disrespectfully toward him. Weiqi then supported Guan Fu and led him away, apologizing to the Chancellor. The Chancellor continued drinking until nightfall, thoroughly enjoying himself before leaving.

## 18

The Chancellor once sent Ji Fu to request the southern city fields from Weiqi. Weiqi was greatly offended and said, "Even though I am an old servant who has been discarded, can you, General, despite your high rank, really take it by force?" He refused. Guan Fu heard about this and became angry, scolding Ji Fu. Ji Fu, fearing the growing rift between the two men, falsely reassured the Chancellor by saying, "Weiqi is old and near death; it's easy to tolerate him for now. We can wait." Later, Wu'an learned that Weiqi and Guan Fu were indeed angry and refused to give up the land. He became furious and said, "Weiqi's son once killed a man, but I saved his life." "I have never refused anything Weiqi asked of me. Why should I be so stingy about several qing of land?" Moreover, what does Guan Fu have to do with this? "I dare not ask for the land again." Wu'an thus harbored great resentment toward Guan Fu and Weiqi.

## 19

In spring of the fourth year of Yuanguang, the Chancellor reported that Guan Fu's family in Yingchuan was extremely arrogant and oppressive, causing great hardship for the people. He requested an investigation. The emperor said, "This is a matter for the Chancellor to handle; why must you request permission?" Guan Fu also held onto secrets about the Chancellor's private affairs, committing acts of corruption for personal gain and accepting gold from King Huainan along with making secret agreements. With intermediaries among their guests, both sides eventually ceased hostilities and the matter was resolved.

## 20

In summer, the Chancellor married the daughter of King Yan as his wife; with an imperial edict from Empress Dowager, he summoned all marquises and members of the royal clan to attend the celebration. Weiqi Hou visited Guan Fu and wanted to go with him. Guan Fu declined, saying, "I have often caused offense to the Chancellor through drinking-related mistakes; now the Chancellor has another grievance against me." Weiqi said, "The matter is already resolved." He insisted that they go together. When the drinking reached its peak, Wu'an rose to offer a toast; all those seated stood up and knelt in reverence. After Weiqi Hou offered his own toast, only old friends rose from their seats to kneel; the rest merely half-rose on one knee. Guan Fu was displeased. He got up to serve the wine, and when he reached Wu'an, Wu'an half-rose on one knee and said, "I am unable to finish a full cup." Guan Fu became angry and mockingly said, "General is a man of high rank; pour it for him!" At that time, Wu'an refused. When the wine reached Linru Hou, he was whispering with Cheng Bushi and did not rise from his seat. Since Guan Fu could not vent his anger on Wu'an, he turned and scolded Linru Hou, saying, "All my life I have spoken ill of Cheng Bushi, calling him worthless. Today a respected elder is offering a toast, yet you act like a girl whispering secretly!" Wu'an said to Guan Fu, "Cheng Bushi and Li Jian are both Weiwei of the Eastern and Western Palaces. Now you publicly humiliate Cheng General; will you not consider the position of Li General?" Guan Fu replied, "Even if my head is cut off today and I die, how would I know anything about Cheng or Li!" Those present then rose to go to the restroom one by one, gradually leaving. Weiqi Hou left and gestured for Guan Fu to exit as well. Wu'an then became furious, saying, "This is my fault for being arrogant toward Guan Fu." He ordered his cavalry to detain Guan Fu. Guan Fu wanted to leave but could not. Ji Fu got up and apologized, pressing Guan Fu's neck to make him bow in apology. Guan Fu became even angrier and refused to apologize. Wu'an then ordered his cavalrymen to bind Guan Fu and place him in a relay station, summoning the chief clerk and saying, "Today I summoned members of the royal family with an imperial decree." He impeached Guan Fu for insulting those present in a disrespectful manner and had him imprisoned. They then investigated his past offenses, sending officials to arrest various relatives of the Guan family in different districts; all were found guilty and sentenced to be executed by public exposure. Weiqi Hou was deeply ashamed, and used his resources to send guests to plead for Guan Fu's release, but no one could help. Wu'an's officials were all his eyes and ears; the Guan family members had fled or hidden, so while Guan Fu was in custody, he could not reveal Wu'an's secret misdeeds.

## 21

Weiqi took it upon himself to rescue Guan Fu with determination. His wife advised Weiqi, saying, "General Guan has offended the prime minister and clashed with the empress dowager's family. Is it possible to save him?" Weiqi Hou said, "This marquisate was obtained through me; I will give it up through me. There is nothing to regret." "And besides, I will not allow Guan Zhongru to die alone while I live on by myself." So he hid at his home and secretly went out to submit a memorial. He was immediately summoned in, where he fully stated that Guan Fu had merely been drunk and overeating, which was not enough to warrant execution. The emperor agreed with this view, gave Weiqi food as a gift, and said, "We will debate the matter in the eastern court."

## 22

When Weiqi went to the eastern court, he strongly praised Guan Fu's virtues and stated that his offense was merely drunkenness and overeating; it was the prime minister who falsely accused him of other matters. Wu'an, in turn, strongly denounced Guan Fu's reckless and arbitrary actions as crimes against the state and violations of morality. Weiqi realized there was nothing to be done about it, so he proceeded to speak ill of the prime minister's shortcomings. Wu'an said, "The empire is fortunate to be at peace and free from troubles; Fen has been able to serve as a close minister, indulging in his love of music, dogs, horses, farmland, and houses. The entertainers, actors, and skilled craftsmen that Fen loves are not like Weiqi and Guan Fu, who day and night gather heroes and valiant men from all over the world to discuss matters, speaking ill with their words and criticizing in their hearts. They do not look up at heaven or down at earth; they stand between the two palaces, waiting for a change in the empire's fortune so that they may achieve great merit. "I did not know what Weiqi and others were doing." At this, the emperor asked the court ministers, "Which of the two is correct?" The Grand Censor Han Anguo said, "Weiqi mentioned that Guan Fu's father died in service; he himself carried a halberd and galloped into the unpredictable Wu army, sustaining dozens of wounds, his name ranking first among all three armies. This is a brave man from throughout the empire. Unless he has committed some great crime, quarreling over cups of wine is not enough to justify citing other offenses for execution. Weiqi's words are correct." The prime minister also stated that Guan Fu associated with corrupt and cunning people, oppressed commoners, had amassed great wealth, acted recklessly in Yingchuan, trampled upon the royal family, and violated close relatives. This is what is called 'a branch larger than the trunk, a shank thicker than the thigh; if it does not break, it will certainly split.' The prime minister's words are also correct." "Only an enlightened sovereign can decide this matter." Ju An, the Minister of Titles and Ranks, supported Weiqi. Zheng Dangshi, the Director of Internal Affairs, also supported Weiqi but later dared not firmly maintain his position. The rest all dared not speak up. The emperor angrily said to the Director of Internal Affairs, "You have often spoken about Weiqi and Wu'an's strengths and weaknesses throughout your life. Today in court discussion, you cower like a horse under a cart yoke—I will behead all of you." He immediately dismissed the assembly and went to present food to the Empress Dowager. The empress dowager had also already sent people to wait and observe, who fully informed her of what happened. The empress dowager was furious, refused to eat, and said, "While I am still alive now, people trample upon my younger brother. After I die in a hundred years, they will treat him like fish meat." "And besides, how could the emperor possibly be a stone-hearted man!" "He is merely obeying the emperor's will now and acting passively. If the emperor were to die in a hundred years, would there still be anyone among these people who could be trusted?" The emperor apologized and said, "They are both relatives of the imperial family, so I had to debate it in court. "If not for that, this would have been decided by a single prison official alone." At this time, Shijian, the Minister of the Imperial Guards, separately advised the emperor about the affairs concerning these two men.

## 23

Wu'an had already left the court and stopped at the Chumen Gate, where he summoned Han Anguo, the Grand Censor, to his carriage. He angrily said, "You and Zhang Ru are together with one old bald man; why do you hesitate between two sides?" After a long while, Han Anguo said to the prime minister, "Why aren't you happy with yourself? "When Weiqi criticized you, you should have removed your hat and unfastened the seals of office to return them, saying 'Minister, I was fortunate enough by virtue of my close relationship with the emperor to hold this position, but clearly it is not suitable for me. All that Weiqi said about me is true.'" If you had done so, the emperor would have certainly praised your humility and would not have dismissed you. Weiqi would surely feel deep shame inside, shut himself in his house, bite his tongue, and commit suicide. "Now that others criticize you, you also criticize them back; it is like a petty merchant or woman quarreling—it shows how lacking in dignity and grandeur!" Wu'an apologized, saying, "In the heat of the argument, I didn't think to do this."

## 24

At this point, the emperor ordered the Grand Censor's office to investigate and hold Weiqi accountable for his statements about Guan Fu; it was found that many of them were not accurate and contained lies and falsehoods. He was impeached and imprisoned in the Office of Works. During Emperor Xiaojing's reign, Weiqi Hou often received a final imperial decree stating, "If there are matters that are inconvenient, you may submit your judgment based on what is expedient." When he was imprisoned, Guan Fu's crime had escalated to the point of being punishable by clan extermination; as the situation became increasingly dire, none of the officials dared to speak openly to the emperor again. Weiqi then had his nephew's son submit a memorial to the throne in an attempt to be fortunate enough to receive another audience with the emperor. After the memorial was submitted, it was found that there was no record of such a final decree in the Grand Secretariat's archives. The imperial decree had been secretly kept at Weiqi's home, sealed by his household official. They then impeached Weiqi for forging an edict of the late emperor; he was sentenced to execution by public exposure. In the tenth month of the fifth year, Guan Fu and his family were all condemned. Weiqi heard about it after a long time; upon hearing this, he became furious, fell ill with apoplexy, and lost his appetite, wishing to die. Some heard that the emperor had no intention of killing Weiqi; upon hearing this, Weiqi resumed eating, began treatment for his illness, and discussions concluded that he would not die. However, malicious rumors spread to the emperor's ears, so on the last day of December, Weiqi was executed by public exposure in Weicheng.

## 25

That spring, Wu'an Hou fell ill and repeatedly called for someone to serve him as he expressed his apologies. He sent a shaman who exorcised ghosts to examine the situation, and the shaman saw Weiqi and Guan Fu together guarding him, wanting to kill him. In the end, he died. His son Tian succeeded him. In the third year of Yuanshuo, Wu'an Hou was punished for wearing a chanyu robe into the palace, which was considered disrespectful.

## 26

The Prince of Huainan, An, was discovered plotting rebellion and was investigated. Before the previous emperor's reign, Wu'an Hou served as Taiwei. At that time, when he met Prince An at Bashi, he said to him, "The emperor has no crown prince yet; Your Highness is the most virtuous and a grandson of Emperor Gaozu. If the imperial carriage does not return in the evening (i.e., if the emperor dies), who else should be enthroned besides Your Highness!" Prince An of Huainan was greatly pleased and gave him generous gifts of gold and valuables. The emperor had never approved of Wu'an since the time of Weiqi, but did so only for the sake of the empress dowager. When he heard about Prince An's gift-giving to Wu'an, the emperor said, "If Wu'an Hou were still alive, his entire clan would have been exterminated."

## 27

Grand Historian said: Weiqi and Wu'an both gained prominence as relatives of the imperial family; Guan Fu became famous for a single decisive action at one moment. Weiqi's rise to prominence was due to his actions during the Wu and Chu rebellion, while Wu'an's high status came at a critical moment between day and night. Yet Weiqi truly failed to understand the changing times, Guan Fu lacked strategy yet was not humble; these two men supporting each other ultimately caused disaster and chaos. Wu'an held power but craved authority, using a quarrel over cups of wine to hold expectations against others, thus entrapping those two virtuous men. Alas! How sad it is! Transferring anger onto others, his fate did not last long. The common people did not support him, and he was ultimately slandered with evil words. Alas! How sad it is!. This was the source of all misfortune!
