# Hereditary House of Wei Kangshu

## 1

Wei Kangshu was named Feng, a younger brother of King Wu of Zhou and their mother's youngest son. Next there was Ran Ji, who was the youngest among them.

## 2

After King Wu had defeated Yin Zhou, he further granted the remaining people of Yin to Zhou's son Wugeng Lufu, placing him on a level with feudal lords, so that they might continue their ancestral sacrifices without interruption. Since Wugeng had not yet assembled, fearing that he might have treacherous intentions, King Wu therefore ordered his younger brothers Guan Shu and Cai Shu to serve as assistants to Wugeng Lufu in order to pacify the people. After King Wu died, King Cheng was still young. Duke of Zhou Dan governed in place of King Cheng and assumed the administration of state affairs. Guan Shu and Cai Shu doubted Duke of Zhou, so they joined Wugeng Lufu to start a rebellion, intending to attack Chengzhou. Duke of Zhou Dan, acting on King Cheng's orders, raised an army to attack Yin and killed Wugeng Lufu and Guan Shu, exiled Cai Shu, and granted the remaining people of Yin under Wugeng's rule to Kangshu as ruler of Wei, settling them in the former Shang capital between the He and Qi rivers.

## 3

Duke of Zhou Dan was concerned that Kangshu was still young, so he earnestly advised Kangshu, saying: "You must seek out the virtuous and wise elders from Yin, inquire about what caused their former Yin dynasty to rise and fall, and be sure to devote yourself to loving the people." He informed him that Zhou's downfall was due to his indulgence in alcohol, and the mistakes of drinking were used by women; therefore, Zhou's chaos began from this. This is recorded as Zicai, an example for gentlemen to follow as a model. Therefore, it was called "Kanggao," "Jiu Gao," and "Zicai" to instruct him. After the state of Kangshu received these instructions, it was able to harmonize and unite its people, who were greatly pleased.

## 4

When King Cheng grew up and assumed power, he appointed Kangshu as the Zhou Minister of Justice and bestowed precious ritual vessels on Wei to honor his virtue.

## 5

After Kangshu died, his son Kangbo succeeded him. After Kangbo died, his son Kaobo took over. After Kaobo died, his son Sibo succeeded him. After Sibo died, his son Yibai took over. After Yibai died, his son Jingbo succeeded him. After Jingbo died, his son Zhenbo took over. After Zhenbo died, his son Qinghou succeeded him.

## 6

らい Qinghou offered generous bribes to King Yi of Zhou, and King Yi then appointed Wei as a marquis state. Twelve years after Qinghou took the throne, he died, and his son Lihou succeeded him.

## 7

In the thirteenth year of Lihou's reign, King Li of Zhou fled to Zhi, and thus the Regency Administration began. In the twenty-eighth year, King Xuan of Zhou ascended the throne.

## 8

In the forty-second year, Lihou died, and his crown prince Gongbo Yu succeeded him as ruler. Gongbo's younger brother He was favored by Lihou, who gave him many gifts; He used these gifts to bribe officials and launched a surprise attack on Gongbo at his tomb, whereupon Gongbo entered Lihou's burial chamber and committed suicide. The people of Wei therefore buried him beside Lihou, posthumously bestowing on him the title Gongbo, and enthroned He as Marquis of Wei; he was known as Duke Wu.

## 9

After Duke Wu ascended the throne, he restored the policies of Kangshu and thus brought harmony and unity to the people. In the forty-second year, Quanrong killed King You of Zhou. Duke Wu led troops to assist the Zhou in pacifying the Rong tribes and achieved great merit; thus, King Ping of Zhou appointed Duke Wu as a duke. In the fifty-fifth year, he died, and his son Zhuanggong Yang succeeded him.

## 10

In the fifth year of Zhuanggong's reign, he took a daughter of Qi as his wife; she was beautiful but childless. He also married a woman from Chen and had a son, who died at an early age. Tianv's younger sister was also favored by Zhuanggong and gave birth to a son named Wan. After Wan's mother died, Zhuanggong ordered his wife from Qi to raise him as her own and made him crown prince. Zhuanggong had a favored concubine who bore him a son named Zhouyu. In the eighteenth year, Zhouyu grew up and became fond of military affairs; Zhuanggong appointed him as a general. Shi Que advised Zhuanggong, saying: "A concubine's son who likes military affairs and is appointed as a general will bring disorder from this point on." Zhuanggong did not listen. In the twenty-third year, Zhuanggong died and Crown Prince Wan succeeded him; he was known as Marquis Huan.

## 11

In the second year of Marquis Huan's reign, his younger brother Zhouyu became arrogant and extravagant; Marquis Huan demoted him, and Zhouyu fled into exile. In the thirteenth year, Duan, the younger brother of Lord Zhu Bao of Zheng, attacked his elder brother but failed and fled. Zhouyu sought to befriend him. In the sixteenth year, Zhouyu gathered exiled people from Wei and launched a surprise attack to kill Marquis Huan, then declared himself ruler of Wei. Because Duan, the younger brother of Duke Zhu Bao of Zheng, wanted to attack Zheng, he requested assistance from Song, Chen, and Cai together with Zhouyu; all three states agreed to support Zhouyu. Zhouyu had just taken power and was fond of military affairs, having assassinated Marquis Huan; the people of Wei did not love him. Shi Que therefore used his connection to the family of Marquis Huan in Chen and pretended to be friendly with Zhouyu. When they reached the outskirts of Zheng, Shi Que and Duke Chen plotted together and had Youzai Chou serve food to Zhouyu, killing him in Pu. They then welcomed Jinfu, Marquis Huan's younger brother, from Xing and enthroned him; he was known as King Xuan.

## 12

In the seventh year of King Xuan's reign, Lu assassinated its ruler Yin Gong. In the ninth year, Du of Song assassinated his king Shanggong and Kongfu as well. In the tenth year, Zhu Bo of Quwo in Jin assassinated his ruler Aihou.

## 13

In the eighteenth year, initially, King Xuan loved his wife Yijiang; Yijiang bore him a son named Ji, whom he made crown prince and ordered Yougongzi to serve as his tutor. Yougongzi arranged for the crown prince to marry a daughter of Qi, but before she entered his chamber, King Xuan saw that the woman he had chosen as the crown princess was beautiful and took her for himself instead, arranging another wife for the crown prince. King Xuan obtained the daughter of Qi and had two sons, Shou and Shuo; he ordered Zuogongzi to serve as their tutor. After Crown Prince Ji's mother died, King Xuan's principal wife and Shuo conspired together to slander and defame Crown Prince Ji. King Xuan himself, because he had taken the crown prince's wife, felt resentment toward the crown prince and wanted to depose him. When he heard of the accusations against him, King Xuan became furious and sent Crown Prince Ji to Qi while ordering bandits to intercept and kill him at the border. He gave the crown prince a white banner and instructed the border bandits to kill whoever was seen carrying it. Before leaving, Shou, the older brother of Shuo and a half-brother to Crown Prince Ji, learned that Shuo had slandered the crown prince and that the king wanted him killed. He therefore told Crown Prince Ji: "The border bandits will kill you as soon as they see your white banner; you should not go." Crown Prince Ji said, "Disobeying my father's command to save my life is unacceptable." And so he went. Seeing that the crown prince would not stop, Shou stole his white banner and rushed ahead to the border first. The bandits at the frontier saw the evidence and immediately killed him. After Shou had died, Crown Prince Ji arrived as well and told the bandits: "The one who should be killed is me." The bandits then killed Crown Prince Ji together with Shou, reporting back to King Xuan. King Xuan then made his son Shuo the crown prince. In the nineteenth year, King Xuan died and Crown Prince Shuo succeeded him; he was called King Huigong.

## 14

The two factions of Yougongzi and Zuogongzi were dissatisfied with Shuo's ascension. In the fourth year of King Huigong's reign, they resented that King Huigong had slandered and killed the former crown prince Ji to take his place, so they rebelled, attacked King Huigong, enthroned Prince Qianmou, the younger brother of Crown Prince Ji as ruler, and King Huigong fled to Qi.

## 15

Eight years after the enthronement of Wei Jun Qianmou, Duke Xianggong of Qi led feudal lords under royal orders to jointly attack Wei and restored King Huigong to power, executing Yougongzi and Zuogonzi. King Qianmou of Wei fled to Zhou, and King Huigong was once again restored to power. King Huigong spent three years on the throne before fleeing into exile, and after eight years in exile he returned; altogether, this spanned thirteen years.

## 16

In the twenty-fifth year, King Huigong resented Zhou for harboring Qianmou and allied with Yan to attack Zhou. King Hui of Zhou fled to Wen, and Wei and Yan enthroned King Huizhong's younger brother Tui as king. In the twenty-ninth year, Zheng restored King Hui back to power. In the thirty-first year, King Huigong died and his son Yigong Chi succeeded him.

## 17

After Yigong ascended the throne, he loved cranes and indulged in luxurious pleasures. In the ninth year, Di attacked Wei; when King Yigong wanted to raise an army, some soldiers defected. A high-ranking minister said: "The king loves cranes; the cranes can be ordered to attack Di." Di therefore advanced and killed King Yigong.

## 18

When King Yigong took the throne, neither the common people nor the officials were satisfied with him. From the time King Huigong Shuo slandered and killed Crown Prince Ji to usurp power until Yigong, people had always wanted to overthrow him. In the end, they destroyed the descendants of King Huigong and enthroned Shen, the son of Zhao Bo Wan, Qianmou's younger brother; he was known as Daigong.

## 19

In his first year on the throne, King Daigong Shen died. Duke Huan of Qi, frustrated by the frequent turmoil in Wei, led feudal lords to attack Di, built Chuqiu for Wei, and enthroned Huai, King Daigong's younger brother as ruler; he was known as Wengong. Wengong had fled to Qi due to the turmoil and was later brought back by the people of Qi.

## 20

Initially, when Di killed King Yigong, the people of Wei pitied him and wished to restore the descendants of Crown Prince Ji from before King Xuan's time. However, Ji's son had already died, and Shou, who had taken his place in death, also left no heir. Crown Prince Ji had two full younger brothers: one was Qianmou, who once replaced King Huigong as ruler and left the throne after eight years; The other was Zhao Bo. Both Zhao Bo and Qianmou had already died earlier, so Shen, the son of Zhao Bobai, was enthroned as Daigong. After Daigong died, his younger brother Huai was again enthroned as Wengong.

## 21

When Wengong first took the throne, he reduced taxes and ensured fair punishment, personally toiling alongside the people and sharing their hardships in order to win back the hearts of Wei's populace.

## 22

In the sixteenth year, Gongzi Chonger of Jin passed through but was treated with no courtesy. In the seventeenth year, Duke Huan of Qi died. In the twenty-fifth year, Wengong died and his son Chenggong Zheng succeeded him.

## 23

In the third year of Chenggong's reign, Jin wanted to borrow a route through Wei to rescue Song, but Chenggong refused. Jin then crossed from the southern bank of the He River instead to save Song. Jin requested troops from Wei, and a Wei official wanted to agree, but Chenggong would not allow it. The official Yuan Xuan attacked King Chenggong, who fled into exile. Duke Wen of Jin, Chonger, attacked Wei and gave part of its territory to Song as punishment for the previous disrespect shown when he passed through and for not aiding Song in its time of need. King Chenggong of Wei then fled into exile to Chen. Two years later, he went to Zhou seeking reinstatement and met with Duke Wen of Jin. Jin sent someone to poison King Chenggong; however, Chenggong secretly informed the Zhou official in charge of the poisoning and diluted it, thus managing to survive. Later, Zhou petitioned on his behalf with Duke Wen of Jin; Chenggong was finally allowed to return to Wei, and Yuan Xuan was executed. King Jun Xia of Wei fled into exile. In the seventh year, Duke Wen of Jin died. In the twelfth year, King Chenggong paid a court visit to Duke Xiang of Jin. In the fourteenth year, Duke Mu of Qin died. In the twenty-sixth year, Bingchu of Qi assassinated his ruler Yigong. In the thirty-fifth year, King Chenggong died and his son Duke Mu Shu succeeded him.

## 24

In the second year of Duke Mu's reign, King Zhuang of Chu attacked Chen and killed Xia Zhengshu. In the third year, King Zhuang of Chu besieged Zheng; Zheng surrendered, but was later released by the Chu forces. In the eleventh year, Sun Liangfu rescued Lu by attacking Qi and regained the lost territory that had been invaded. Duke Mu died and his son Dinggong Zang succeeded him. In the twelfth year of King Dinggong's reign, he died and his son Xiangong Kan succeeded him.

## 25

In the thirteenth year of King Xianggong's reign, the king ordered Shicao to teach a concubine in his palace how to play the qin. The concubine was not skilled, so Cao beat her. The concubine, favored by the king, complained about Cao to him, and as a result, the king also flogged Cao three hundred times. In the eighteenth year, King Xianggong invited Sun Wensi and Ning Huizi to a meal; both attended. They were not summoned until late in the day and instead went hunting for cranes in the royal park. The two officials followed him, but the king did not take off his hunting clothes to speak with them. The two men were angry and went to Su. Sun Wensi's son often served the king during drinking sessions, and he had Shicao sing the final stanza of "Qiaoyan." Shicao was also angry about having once been flogged three hundred times by the king, so he sang it, intending to anger Sun Wensi and take revenge on King Xianggong of Wei. Wensi told Qu Boyu, who said, "I do not know about it." They then attacked and expelled King Xianggong. King Xianggong fled to Qi, where the state of Qi placed him in Juyi. Sun Wensi and Ning Huizi jointly installed You, a younger brother of Dinggong, as ruler of Wei; he became known as Shanggong.

## 26

Shanggong You was enthroned and granted Sun Wensi Linfu the fiefdom of Su. In the twelfth year, Ning Xi and Sun Linfu competed for favor and became hostile toward each other. Shanggong sent Ning Xi to attack Sun Linfu. Linfu fled to Jin, where he requested the return of former King Xianggong from Wei. King Xianggong was in Qi; Duke Jing of Qi heard about it and accompanied King Xianggong to Jin to request his reinstatement. Jin attacked Wei on their behalf, luring them into an alliance. King Shanggong of Wei met with Duke Ping of Jin, who arrested King Shanggong and Ning Xi before restoring King Xianggong back to the throne in Wei. King Xianggong had been away for twelve years outside the state before returning.

## 27

In the first year of King Xianggong's Later Reign, Ning Xi was executed.

## 28

In the third year, Wu Yanling Jizi, on a diplomatic mission, passed through Wei and met Qu Boyu and Shi. He said, "Wei has many virtuous men; its state will remain stable." Passing through Su, Sun Linfu played the qin for him. He said, "It is not joyful; the tone is too sorrowful. This is how Wei will fall into chaos." In this year, King Xianggong died and his son King Xianggong Wu succeeded him.

## 29

In the sixth year of King Xiang's reign, King Ling of Chu convened a meeting with feudal lords, but King Xiang claimed illness and did not attend.

## 30

In the ninth year, King Xiang died. Originally, King Xiang had a low-ranking concubine whom he favored. She became pregnant and dreamed that someone said to her: "I am Kangshu; I have decreed that your descendants will surely rule Wei. Name your child 'Yuan'." The concubine found this strange and asked Kong Chengzi about it. Chengzi said, "Kangshu is the ancestor of Wei." When the child was born and turned out to be a boy, she informed King Xiang. King Xiang said, "This is ordained by Heaven." He was named Yuan. King Xiang's wife had no sons, so they then made Yuan the heir; he became known as King Linggong.

## 31

In spring of the forty-second year, King Linggong went on an outing to the outskirts and ordered his son Ying to be his charioteer. Ying was the youngest son of King Linggong, with the courtesy name Zinan. King Linggong resented the crown prince for fleeing and said to Ying: "I will make you my successor." Ying replied, "Ying is not worthy enough to disgrace the state; please choose someone else, Your Majesty." In summer, King Linggong died. His wife ordered her son Ying to become the crown prince, saying: "This is what King Linggong decreed." Ying said, "The exiled crown prince Kuaike's son Che is still alive; I dare not accept the position." Thus, Wei installed Che as ruler; he became known as Chugong.

## 32

On the Yiyou day of the sixth month, Zhao Jianzi wanted to bring back Kuaike and therefore ordered Yang Hu to falsely issue orders to more than ten men from Wei in mourning attire to return with him. Jianzi escorted Kuaike. The people of Wei heard about this and sent troops to attack Kuaike. Kuaike could not enter, so he went to Su for refuge; the people of Wei also withdrew their troops.

## 33

In the fourth year of Chugong Che's reign, Tian Qi of Qi assassinated his king Ruzi. In the eighth year, Baozi of Qi assassinated their ruler Dao Gong.

## 34

Confucius came from Chen to Wei. In the ninth year, Kong Wensi asked Zhongni about military matters; Zhongni did not respond. Later, Lu invited Zhongni back and he returned to Lu.

## 35

In the twelfth year: originally, Kong Yu Wensi married the elder sister of Crown Prince Kuaike and gave birth to Kui. A servant named Hun Liangfu in the Kong family was handsome and well-favored. After Kong Wensi died, Liangfu had an affair with Kui's mother. The crown prince was in Su, so Kui's mother sent Liangfu to the crown prince. The crown prince said to Liangfu, "If you can help me return to my country, I will grant you a carriage with four horses, exempt you from three capital punishments, and give you whatever you want." They swore an oath, and the crown prince promised to take Kui's mother as his wife. In the leap month, Liangfu and the crown prince entered [the state] and stayed in an outer garden of the Kong family. At dusk, the two men wore cloaks and rode together; a eunuch named Luo drove for them as they went to the Kong family residence. An elder of the Kong clan, Luan Ning, asked about it, and they claimed it was a relative's concubine and informed him accordingly. They then entered and went directly to Bojishi. After the meal, Kui's mother took up a halberd and led the way; the crown prince and five others donned armor, followed by a cart carrying a boar. Boji ambushed Kui in the latrine, forced him to swear an oath, and then seized him up onto a platform by force. Luan Ning was about to drink when the meat was not yet roasted; he heard the commotion and sent someone to inform Zhongyou. They summoned guards, boarded a carriage, distributed wine and roasted meat, and escorted Chugong Che in his flight to Lu.

## 36

Zhongyou was about to enter when he met Zigao who was coming out, and said, "The gate is already closed." Zilu said, "I will go in anyway for a while." Zigao said, "It is too late; do not involve yourself in the danger." Zilu replied, "Having accepted their food, I cannot avoid their troubles." Zigao then left. Zilu entered and reached the gate, where Gongsun Gan closed it and said, "Do not enter!" Zilu asked, "Is this you, Gongsun?" You seek profit but flee from danger. I am not like that; if I benefit from my salary, I must also help in times of hardship." When a messenger came out, Zilu was finally able to enter. He said, "Why does the crown prince need Kong Kui?" "Even if you kill him, someone else will surely take his place." He also said, "The crown prince lacks courage. "If you burn down the platform, they will surely release Kong Shu." The crown prince heard this and became frightened. He sent Shi Qi and Yu Yan to fight Zilu, who struck back with a halberd, cutting off his ribbon. Zilu said: "A gentleman dies with his hat intact." He fastened his ribbon and died. Confucius heard about the chaos in Wei and said, "Alas! Chai Ye should be coming here, I think?" "You Ye must have died." Kong Kui eventually installed the crown prince Kuaike, who became Duke Zhuang.

## 37

Duke Zhuang, Kuaike, was the father of Chugong; he lived in exile and resented that no officials had come to welcome him as ruler. In the first year of his reign, he ascended the throne and wanted to execute all the senior ministers. He said, "I have been in exile for a long time; have you also heard about this?" The officials were about to rebel, so he stopped.

## 38

In the second year, Kong Qiu of Lu died.

## 39

In the third year, Duke Zhuang went to the city wall and saw Rong Zhou. He said, "What are these Rong captives doing here?" Rong Zhou was offended by this. In the tenth month, Rong Zhou reported to Zhao Jianzi, who then besieged Wei. In the eleventh month, Duke Zhuang fled; the people of Wei installed Gongzi Banshi as ruler of Wei. Qi attacked Wei and captured Banshi, replacing him with Gongzi Qi as ruler of Wei.

## 40

In the first year of Duke Qi's reign, Shi Manfu of Wei expelled his sovereign Qi, who fled to Qi. Chugong Zhe returned from Qi and was reinstated as ruler of Wei. Initially, Chugong had reigned for twelve years before fleeing; after spending four years in exile, he returned to power. In the first year of the Later period of Chugong's reign, rewards were given to those who had followed him into exile. After reigning for twenty-one years, he died; Chugong's uncle Qian attacked the crown prince of Chugong and declared himself ruler. He became Duke Dao.

## 41

Duke Dao died in the fifth year of his reign; his son Jinggong Fuli succeeded him. Jinggong died after reigning for nineteen years; his son Zhao Gong Jiu succeeded him. At this time, the three Jin states were powerful, and Wei was like a minor vassal lord, subject to them.

## 42

In the sixth year of Zhao Gong's reign, Gongzi Men assassinated him and took his place as ruler; he became Huai Gong. In the eleventh year of Huai Gong's reign, Gongzi Tui assassinated Huai Gong and took his place as ruler. He became Shen Gong. Shen Gong's father was Gongzi Shi; Shi's father was Jinggong. After reigning for forty-two years, Shen Gong died; his son Shenggong Xun succeeded him. Shenggong died in the eleventh year of his reign; his son Chenghou Su succeeded him.

## 43

In the eleventh year of Chenghou's reign, Gongsun Yang went to Qin. In the sixteenth year, Wei was further demoted and its title changed to "Hou."

## 44

In the twenty-ninth year, Chenghou died; his son Pinghou succeeded him. Pinghou died in the eighth year of his reign; his son Sijun succeeded him.

lineno In the fifth year of Sijun's reign, Wei was further demoted to the title "Jun," and possessed only Puyang alone.

## 46

Sijun died in the forty-second year of his reign; his son Huaijun succeeded him. In the thirty-first year of Huaijun's reign, he paid a courtesy visit to Wei (the state), and the State of Wei imprisoned and killed him. The State of Wei then installed Sijun's younger brother as ruler, who became Yuantongjun. Yuantongjun was the son-in-law of the State of Wei, so Wei installed him. In the fourteenth year of Yuantongjun's reign, Qin captured the eastern territories of Wei. For the first time, Qin established Dong Jun (the Eastern Commandery), and moved Weiyewang Xian to it, incorporating Puyang into Dong Jun as well. In the twenty-fifth year, Yuantongjun died; his son Junjiao succeeded him. In the ninth year of Junjiao's reign, Qin unified all under heaven and established himself as the First Emperor. In the twenty-first year, the Second Emperor deposed Junjiao and reduced him to a commoner; thus, the ancestral sacrifices of Wei ceased.
