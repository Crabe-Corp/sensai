# Liezhuan of Liu Jing and Shusun Tong

## 1

Liu Jing was a native of Qi. In the fifth year of the Han dynasty, Liu Jing was stationed in Longxi and passed through Luoyang, where Emperor Gaozu was residing. Lou Jing unfastened his cart and, wearing a sheepskin coat, met with General Yu of Qi and said, "I wish to see the emperor and offer advice on matters that would be beneficial." General Yu wished to give him fresh clothes, but Lou Jing said, "If I wear silk, I will present myself in silk; if I wear coarse cloth, I will appear in coarse cloth: I would never dare to change my attire." Thus, General Yu entered the palace and reported this to the emperor. The emperor summoned him in for an audience and granted him food.

## 2

Afterward, the emperor asked Lou Jing, who replied, "Your Majesty has established your capital at Luoyang—does this mean you wish to rival the Zhou dynasty in prosperity?" The emperor said, "Indeed." Lou Jing said, "Your Majesty's way of seizing the empire differs from that of the Zhou dynasty. The ancestors of the Zhou began with Hou Ji, whom Yao enfeoffed at Tai; for more than ten generations, they accumulated virtue and good deeds. Gong Liu fled from Jie and settled in Bin. Taiwang, because of attacks by the Di people, left Bin, carried a horsewhip and settled at Qi; the people of the state vied to follow him. When King Wen became the Xibo, he adjudicated a dispute between Yu and Rui; only then did he receive his mandate. Lü Wang and Bo Yi came from the seacoast to submit to him. King Wu attacked Zhou, and without prior appointment, eight hundred feudal lords gathered at Mengjin; all said that Zhou could be overthrown, so they proceeded to destroy the Yin dynasty. When King Cheng ascended the throne, his uncles of the Zhou Gong clan served as regents and advisors; they then established the capital at Luoyi in the central region of the empire. From all directions, feudal lords paid tribute and fulfilled their duties, with equal distances along the roads. With virtue, it was easy to rule as a king; without virtue, it was equally easy to perish. Those who resided here wished for the Zhou dynasty to govern by virtue and attract people, not to rely on natural barriers or difficult terrain, so as to prevent future generations from becoming arrogant, luxurious, and tyrannical toward the people. When the Zhou dynasty was at its height, all under heaven were harmonious and united; the four Yi peoples looked up to their customs, admired righteousness and virtue, and submitted themselves to serve the Son of Heaven. Not a single soldier was stationed, not a single warrior fought, yet the people from the eight great foreign states all willingly paid homage and fulfilled their tribute duties. When the Zhou dynasty declined, it split into two, and no one in the world came to court; the Zhou could not control them anymore. It was not because their virtue had become thin, but because their strategic position had weakened. Now, Your Majesty rose from Feng and Pei, gathered three thousand soldiers, led them directly to conquer Shu and Han, pacified the Three Qins, fought against Xiang Yu at Yingyang, contended for the pass of Chenggao, engaged in seventy major battles and forty minor ones. This caused the people throughout the empire to have their livers and brains smeared on the ground, fathers and sons left with exposed bones in the fields—too numerous to count. The wails of mourning have not yet ceased, and the wounded still lie unhealed. Yet you wish to rival the prosperity of the reigns of Kings Cheng and Kang? I secretly believe this is unequal. Moreover, the land of Qin is surrounded by mountains and rivers, with four natural barriers that serve as strong defenses. In case of sudden emergency, a force of one million men can be mobilized. By inheriting the former Qin territory and drawing upon its fertile and rich land, this is what is called a "Paradise on Earth." If Your Majesty enters the Passes and establishes your capital there, even if Shandong falls into chaos, the former territory of Qin can be fully preserved. In a fight with another person, if you do not grasp their throat and strike their back, you cannot ensure total victory. Now, Your Majesty entering the Passes to establish your capital and holding onto Qin's former territory—this is also grasping the throat of the empire and striking its back."

## 3

Emperor Gaozu asked his ministers, but since they were all from Shandong, they all contended that the Zhou kings had ruled for several centuries while the Qin dynasty perished after only two generations; therefore, it was better to establish the capital in Zhou. The emperor hesitated and could not make a decision. When Liu Hou clearly stated the advantages of entering the Passes, that very day the imperial carriages moved westward to establish the capital in Guanzhong.

## 4

At this, the emperor said, "The one who originally suggested establishing the capital in Qin territory was Lou Jing. 'Lou' is merely a phonetic variant of 'Liu.'" He was granted the surname Liu, appointed as an Attendant-in-Ordinary (Langzhong), and honored with the title Fengchun Jun.

## 5

In the seventh year of the Han dynasty, King Wan of Yan rebelled, so Emperor Gaozu personally went to attack him. Upon arriving at Jinyang, he learned that Wan had allied with the Xiongnu and intended to jointly attack Han. The emperor became furious and sent envoys to the Xiongnu. The Xiongnu concealed their strong warriors, fat cattle, and horses; only old and weak people along with sickly livestock were shown. Ten successive envoys returned, all reporting that the Xiongnu could be attacked. The emperor sent Liu Jing to visit the Xiongnu again, and upon his return, he reported, "When two states are about to fight, they should display their strengths with pride. Now when I went there, I only saw the old and weak along with emaciated livestock; this must be a deliberate attempt to show weakness while concealing hidden troops in order to gain advantage." I believe that the Xiongnu should not be attacked." At this time, Han troops had already crossed Juzhu Pass, and more than twenty thousand soldiers were already on the march. The emperor became angry and scolded Liu Jing, saying, "You wretched man from Qi! you obtained an official post through mere words; now you dare to recklessly speak and discourage my army." Liu Jing was shackled and imprisoned in Guangwu. The emperor proceeded forward, reaching Pingcheng, where the Xiongnu indeed launched a surprise attack and surrounded Emperor Gaozu at Baoting. It took seven days before he was able to escape. When Emperor Gaozu arrived at Guangwu, he pardoned Liu Jing and said, "I did not follow your advice, which is why I was trapped in Pingcheng. I have already executed the ten previous envoys who claimed it was possible to attack." He then bestowed Liu Jing with a fief of two thousand households, made him an Inner Pass Marquis (Guannei Hou), and granted him the title Jianxin Hou.

## 6

After Emperor Gaozu withdrew from Pingcheng and returned, King Wan of Yan fled into the territory of the Hu people. At that time, Modu was the Chanyu; his forces were strong, with thirty thousand archers, and he frequently troubled the northern frontier. The emperor was concerned about this and consulted Liu Jing. Liu Jing said, "Now that the empire has just been pacified, soldiers are weary from warfare and cannot yet be used to subdue them by force. Modu killed his father to seize the chieftainship, married his stepmothers, and established authority through strength; he cannot be persuaded by benevolence or righteousness. Only a long-term strategy that secures the submission of his descendants can make him your vassal; yet I fear Your Majesty is unable to do so." The emperor said, "If it truly works, why say that I cannot?" What must be done, then?" Liu Jing replied, "If Your Majesty can truly offer your most suitable eldest princess as his wife and provide generous gifts to him, he will know that the Han is sending a proper daughter with great wealth. The barbarian tribes will surely admire her and regard her as Yuezhi (a Xiongnu title for empress). Any son born from this union would naturally become the crown prince. This way, he can succeed Modu as Chanyu. Why is that? Because they are greedy for the Han's valuable gifts. If Your Majesty sends frequent gifts at regular intervals, taking advantage of what is abundant in Han but scarce among them, and then employs eloquent men to subtly instruct them with the principles of propriety, while Modu is still alive, he will naturally be your son-in-law; when he dies, his grandson—your granddaughter's son—will become the Chanyu. Have you ever heard of a grandson-in-law daring to treat his grandfather as an equal in courtesy? Thus, we can gradually bring them under submission without engaging in war. "If Your Majesty cannot send your eldest princess, but instead orders a member of the imperial clan or a concubine to falsely claim to be a princess, he will certainly know this deception and refuse to honor her as a close relative; it would serve no purpose." Emperor Gaozu said, "Well spoken." He wanted to send his eldest princess. Empress Lu wept day and night, saying, "I have only the crown prince and one daughter; how can I abandon her to the Xiongnu!" The emperor ultimately could not send his eldest princess, but instead took a daughter of an ordinary family and named her the "Eldest Princess," marrying her to the Chanyu. He sent Liu Jing to go and conclude a treaty for a marriage alliance.

## 7

Liu Jing, having returned from the Xiongnu, then said, "The White Sheep and Loufan kings of the Xiongnu in the southern region of the Yellow River are as close as seven hundred li to Chang'an; light cavalry could reach Qinzhong within a single day and night. Qinzhong has just been ravaged, with few people, but the land is fertile and abundant; it can be further strengthened by settling more people there. When feudal lords first rose to power, none could succeed except the Tian clan of Qi or the Zhao, Qu, and Jing clans of Chu. Now, although Your Majesty has established your capital in Guanzhong, it is actually sparsely populated. To the north lies proximity to Xiongnu raiders, and to the east are the descendants of the six former states, whose clans remain powerful. Should a sudden upheaval occur in one day, Your Majesty would not be able to sleep soundly with your head high." I wish for Your Majesty to relocate the Tian clan of Qi, the Zhao, Qu, and Jing families of Chu, as well as descendants from Yan, Zhao, Han, Wei, and other prominent noble families with famous names, to Guanzhong. In times of peace, they can serve as a defense against the Xiongnu; if feudal lords rise in rebellion, these people will also be sufficient to lead an eastern campaign. This is the strategy of strengthening the root while weakening the branches." The emperor said, "Well spoken." He then ordered Liu Jing to relocate more than one hundred thousand people from the families he had mentioned into Guanzhong.

## 8

Shusun Tong was a native of Xue. During the Qin dynasty, he was summoned for his literary and scholarly talents and served as an imperial scholar (Daizhao Bohu). After several years, Chen Sheng rose in rebellion in Shandong. An envoy reported this to the throne; Emperor Er Shi summoned the imperial scholars and Confucian scholars and asked them, "The Chu conscripts have attacked Qi and captured Chen—what do you think of this?" More than thirty imperial scholars and Confucian students stepped forward and said, "Ministers must not hold military command; if they do, it means rebellion. This is a capital crime with no pardon. We beg Your Majesty to quickly dispatch troops to attack them." Emperor Er Shi became angry and changed his expression. Shusun Tong stepped forward and said, "What the scholars have said is all wrong. Now that the empire has been unified into one family, the walls of commanderies and counties were destroyed, their weapons melted down, to show the people that warfare would never be used again. Moreover, with a wise ruler above and laws in place below, each person fulfills their duties; the empire converges like spokes on a wheel—how could anyone dare to rebel! This is merely a band of petty thieves and common robbers; they are not worth mentioning. The commandery magistrates and military officers will now capture and punish them—there is no need for concern." Emperor Er Shi was pleased and said, "Well spoken." He then asked all the scholars, who gave varying responses—some claimed it was rebellion, others called them thieves. Thus, Emperor Er Shi ordered the imperial censors to investigate and punish those scholars who had claimed it was a rebellion, as they had spoken out of turn. Those who called them thieves were all dismissed. He then granted Shusun Tong twenty bolts of silk, a set of clothing, and appointed him as an imperial scholar (Bohu). After leaving the palace, Shusun Tong returned to his residence. The scholars said to him, "Master, how flatteringly you spoke!" Shusun Tong replied, "You do not understand; I was nearly devoured by the tiger's jaws!" He then fled and went to Xue, but found that Xue had already surrendered to Chu. When Xiang Liang arrived at Xue, Shusun Tong followed him. They were defeated at Dingtao, and he then followed King Huai. When King Huai was made the Righteous Emperor, and relocated to Changsha, Shusun Tong remained in service under Xiang Wang. In the second year of Han, King Wu of Han entered Pengcheng with five feudal lords, and Shusun Tong surrendered to King Han. After King Han was defeated and retreated westward, he ultimately followed the Han forces.

## 9

Shusun Tong wore Confucian robes, which King Han disliked; so he changed his attire, wearing short-sleeved garments in the style of Chu, and King Han was pleased.

## 10

When Shusun Tong surrendered to Han, he brought along more than a hundred Confucian scholars and disciples, yet he recommended none of them; instead, he exclusively advocated for the promotion of former bandit strongmen. The disciples all secretly cursed, saying, "We have served Master for many years and were fortunate to follow him in surrendering to Han. Now he cannot recommend us, but only speaks of recommending great rascals—why is that?" Shusun Tong heard about this and said to them, "King Han is just now braving arrows and stones in the struggle for supremacy. Can you scholars actually fight?" Therefore, I first recommended those who have cut down generals and captured enemy banners. "You scholars just wait for me; I will not forget you." King Han appointed Shusun Tong as an imperial scholar and bestowed upon him the title Jisi Jun.

## 11

In the fifth year of Han, after unifying all under heaven, feudal lords jointly honored King Han as emperor at Dingtao. Shusun Tong then established the ceremonial rites and titles for him. Emperor Gaozu completely abolished Qin's harsh rituals and laws, replacing them with simpler ones. The ministers drank wine and vied for credit; some were so drunk they shouted wildly or drew swords to strike pillars, which troubled Emperor Gaozu. Shusun Tong realized that the emperor was increasingly weary of this and advised him, "Scholars are difficult to rely on for conquests, but they can be trusted to preserve achievements. I wish to summon Confucian scholars from Lu and work with my disciples to establish court rituals." Emperor Gaozu asked, "Will it not be difficult?" Shusun Tong said, "The Five Emperors had different music; the Three Kings followed different rites. Rites are established according to the customs and sentiments of their time as a means of regulation and formality. Therefore, what can be known about the rites of Xia, Yin, and Zhou—what was retained or modified—is that they did not repeat one another. I wish to adopt some elements from ancient rites and combine them with Qin's ceremonial practices." The emperor said, "You may try it. Make it easy to understand and suitable for what I am capable of doing."

## 12

Thus, Shusun Tong was ordered to summon more than thirty Confucian scholars from Lu. Two scholars in Lu refused to go, saying, "The officials you serve have changed more than ten times. You always flatter them face-to-face to gain favor and privilege. Now that the empire has just been pacified, the dead have not yet been buried, the wounded have not recovered, and you already want to establish rites and music. Rites and music arise only after a hundred years of accumulated virtue—only then can they be revived. We cannot bear to act as you do." What you do does not conform to the ancient ways, and we will not go with you. "Go ahead then, but don't soil us!" Shusun Tong laughed and said, "You are truly a vulgar scholar, ignorant of the need to adapt with the times."

## 13

He then went west with the thirty men he had summoned, and together with more than a hundred scholars from among the emperor's attendants and his own disciples, they rehearsed in the fields. After practicing for over a month, Shusun Tong said, "His Majesty may now come to observe." The emperor observed and then performed the rites. He said, "I am capable of this." He then ordered all ministers to practice and rehearse, gathering in the tenth month.

## 14

In the seventh year of Han, when the Changle Palace was completed, feudal lords and ministers all paid homage on the tenth day of the tenth month. The ceremony was as follows: before dawn, the official in charge of rites arranged the procedures and led them into the palace gate in order. In the court, chariots, cavalry, infantry, and guards were stationed to protect the palace; weapons were displayed and banners raised. A herald announced, "Hasten." Under the hall, dozens of palace attendants stood on both sides of the steps, numbering hundreds. Merit officials, marquises, generals, and military officers were arranged in order to the west, facing east; Civil officials from the prime minister downward stood to the east, facing west. The chief official of rites arranged for nine attendants as envoys and announced the proceedings. Then, the emperor emerged from his chamber in a palanquin; officials performed their duties and cleared the way. They led feudal lords down to local magistrates of rank 600 dan in order to present their congratulations. From the feudal lords downward, none failed to be awed and reverent. After the ceremony concluded, a formal banquet was served again. All those seated in attendance on the hall knelt with bowed heads and rose in order of rank to present longevity offerings. After nine rounds of wine, a herald announced, "The banquet is over." Imperial censors enforced the law and immediately removed anyone who failed to follow the proper procedures. Throughout the entire ceremony, no one dared to make a loud noise or behave improperly. At this point, Emperor Gaozu said, "I only now understand the dignity of being an emperor." He then appointed Shusun Tong as Tai Chang (Minister of Ceremonies) and bestowed on him 500 catties of gold.

## 15

Shusun Tong then added, "My disciples and Confucian scholars have followed me for a long time. They worked with me to establish these rites; I pray Your Majesty will grant them official positions." Emperor Gaozu appointed all of them as attendants (Lang). Shusun Tong exited and distributed the 500 catties' worth of gold to the scholars. The scholars were all delighted and said, "Master Shusun is indeed a sage; he understands the essential matters of this age."

## 16

In the ninth year of Han, Emperor Gaozu transferred Shusun Tong to serve as Taifu (Grand Tutor) to the Crown Prince. In the twelfth year of Han, Emperor Gao Zu wanted to replace Crown Prince Liu Ying with Zhao Wang Ru Yi. Shusun Tong advised His Majesty, "Long ago, Jin Xiang Gong, because of Li Ji's influence, deposed his crown prince and installed Xi Qi as heir; this caused chaos in the state of Jin for several decades, becoming a laughingstock across the world. Qin failed to appoint Fu Su early on, allowing Zhao Gao to fraudulently install Hu Hai as emperor, which led to the dynasty's destruction—this is something Your Majesty has personally witnessed. Nowadays, the crown prince is kind and filial; this is known throughout the land; Lady Lu has shared hardships with Your Majesty, enduring bitter food and toil—how could she be betrayed! If Your Majesty insists on deposing the legitimate heir in favor of a younger son, I beg to be executed first and let my blood soil the ground." Emperor Gaozu said, "Enough, Master. I was merely joking." Shusun Tong replied, "The crown prince is the foundation of the empire; if this foundation even slightly trembles, the whole realm will be shaken. How can one treat such a matter as a joke!" Emperor Gaozu said: "I shall heed your words." Later, when the emperor held a banquet and saw that Liu Hou's invited guests followed the crown prince in paying respects, he finally abandoned his intention to replace the heir.

## 17

After Emperor Gaozu died, Xiao Hui ascended the throne and said to Shusun Tong: "The ancestral mausoleums, temples, and shrines of the late emperor are not well understood by any minister." He was transferred to serve as Tai Chang and established rituals and regulations for the ancestral temple. When Han's various ceremonial laws were gradually established, they were all formulated by Shusun Tong during his tenure as Tai Chang.

## 18

Emperor Xiao Hui resided in the Dongchao Changle Palace, and when he occasionally visited elsewhere, his processions were often troublesome for the people. Thus, a covered passage was constructed to the south of the Wu Ku (Armory). Shusun Tong, while presenting a report on official business, took the opportunity to ask: "Why does Your Majesty personally build elevated passageways and visit the high temple in ceremonial robes every month? The High Temple is for Emperor Gaozu, the founding ancestor of Han. How can future descendants ride along the sacred temple roads?" Emperor Xiao Hui was greatly alarmed and said, "Halt its construction immediately." Shusun Tong said: "A sovereign should not make hasty decisions. Now that it has already been built, the people are aware of it. If we now dismantle it, this would indicate a mistake." I pray Your Majesty establish an ancestral temple to the north of Wei River and conduct monthly ceremonial visits there. This would allow for more temples, which is the foundation of great filial piety." The emperor then issued an order to the relevant officials to establish a temple at Yuan. The Yuan Temple was built because of the elevated passage.

## 19

During the spring, Emperor Xiao Hui once went on an outing from his palace. Shusun Tong said: "In ancient times there was a ritual of offering fruits in spring; now cherries are ripe and can be presented. I pray Your Majesty go out and pick some cherries to offer at the ancestral temple." The emperor agreed. From this, the practice of offering various fruits became established.

## 20

Grand Historian said: The saying goes, "A thousand-catty fur coat is not made from the armpit of a single fox; the beams of terraces and pavilions are not formed by the branch of one tree; the achievements of the Three Dynasties were not due to the wisdom of a single man." Indeed! Emperor Gaozu rose from humble origins and pacified the realm; his strategies and military tactics can truly be said to have been fully employed. Yet Liu Jing, with a single suggestion made while pulling the emperor's carriage, established peace for ten thousand generations. Can wisdom truly belong to one person alone! Shusun Tong adapted his conduct and rituals according to the needs of the times, advancing or retreating as circumstances changed, and ultimately became a Confucian patriarch for the Han dynasty. "The most upright person may seem bent; the Way is indeed flexible"—is this not what was meant?
