# Biographies of Han Changru

## 1

Yushi Daifu Han Anguo was a native of Liang Cheng'an, later moving to Suiyang. He once studied the doctrines of Hanzi and the Miscellaneous School at the residence of Zou Tiansheng. He served Liang Xiaowang as Zhongdafa. During the time of Wu and Chu's rebellion, Xiaowang sent An Guo and Zhang Yu as generals to defend against the Wu forces at the eastern border. Zhang Yu fought bravely, while An Guo maintained a steady defense; thus, Wu could not cross into Liang territory. After the defeat of Wu and Chu, the reputations of An Guo and Zhang Yu became prominent as a result.

## 2

Liang Xiaowang, the maternal younger brother of Emperor Jing, was favored by Dou Taihou, who allowed him to personally appoint his chancellor and officials ranked at Erqianshi, as well as to travel freely in a manner that rivaled the Son of Heaven. The Son of Heaven heard about this and was displeased inwardly. Empress Dowager Dou, knowing that the emperor disapproved, became angry with Liang's envoys and refused to see them, investigating and holding the king accountable for his actions. Han An Guo, as an envoy of Liang, wept before Grand Princess Dachang and said: "What filial piety does Prince Liang show as a son, what loyalty as a minister, that the Empress Dowager has never recognized?" At the time when Wu, Chu, Qi, and Zhao led seven states in rebellion previously, all regions east of the Guan Pass joined together to face westward; only Liang was most closely involved and endured great hardships. Prince Liang, thinking of the Empress Dowager and the emperor in the capital while the feudal lords were causing chaos, spoke with such emotion that tears streamed down his face. He knelt to send off six officials including myself, who led troops to repel Wu and Chu; because of this, the forces of Wu and Chu dared not advance westward and eventually perished—this was due to Prince Liang's efforts. Now the Empress Dowager is holding Prince Liang accountable for trivial matters with strict rituals and expectations. Prince Liang's father and elder brothers were all emperors; having seen great things, when he goes out, he proclaims his arrival with a procession, and upon entering, announces it as if in warning. His chariots and banners are all bestowed by the emperor. Even if he wishes to display himself before the petty counties or gallop freely within his state to impress the feudal lords, it is only so that the whole world may know that the Empress Dowager and Emperor love him dearly. Now whenever envoys from Liang arrive, they are immediately investigated and blamed. Prince Liang is frightened and weeps day and night with longing, not knowing what to do. "What filial son and loyal minister Prince Liang has been, yet the Empress Dowager does not show compassion?" Grand Princess Dachang fully informed the Empress Dowager of this. The Empress Dowager was pleased and said, "Tell the emperor about it." When informed, the emperor's mind was eased. He removed his hat and apologized to the Empress Dowager, saying: "My brothers could not be properly instructed; this has caused you worry." He received all of Liang's envoys, bestowing generous gifts upon them. After this, Prince Liang became even closer and more favored. The Empress Dowager and Grand Princess Dachang bestowed further gifts upon An Guo worth over a thousand gold pieces. His reputation thus became prominent, and he established connections with the Han dynasty.

## 3

Later, An Guo was punished for a legal offense, and while in prison, the jailer Tian Jia humiliated him. An Guo said: "Is it not possible that dead ashes might be rekindled?" Tian Jia replied, "Even if they are rekindled, I can drown them." Not long after, the position of Neishi in Liang became vacant. The Han dynasty sent an envoy to appoint An Guo as Neishi of Liang; he was thus promoted from among convicts to a rank of Erqianshi. Tian Jia fled in fear. An Guo said, "If Tian Jia does not report to his post, I will destroy your clan." Tian Jia therefore appeared in person with bare upper body as a sign of apology. An Guo laughed and said, "Now you can be drowned!" "Are you truly capable of governing?" In the end, he treated him kindly.

## 4

When the position of Neishi in Liang was vacant, Prince Xiaowang had recently acquired Gongsun Gui from Qi and favored him, wishing to request his appointment as Neishi. Empress Dowager Dou heard about this and issued an edict ordering the king to appoint An Guo as the Neishi.

## 5

Gongsun Gui and Yang Sheng advised Prince Xiaowang to request the title of Crown Prince for him as well as additional territory. Fearing that Han ministers might refuse, they secretly sent people to assassinate influential Han strategists. When Gongsun Gui and Yang Sheng's plot led to the killing of former Wu chancellor Yuan Ang, Emperor Jing learned about their scheme and sent envoys to capture Gongsun Gui and Yang Shing, determined to apprehend them. Ten successive Han envoys arrived in Liang; the entire state was mobilized for a massive search from the Neishi down, but after more than a month they could not be found. Neishi An Guo learned that Gongsun Gui and Yang Shi were hiding at Prince Xiaowang's residence. An Guo entered to see the prince and wept, saying: "When a ruler is shamed, his ministers die." "Your Highness has no loyal ministers, hence these troubles have come to this pass." "Now that Gongsun Gui and Yang Shen cannot be captured, I beg leave to resign and accept death as a punishment." The prince said: "Why must it come to this?" Tears streamed down An Guo's face as he said: "Your Highness, please consider how close your relationship is with the emperor compared to that of Tai Shang Huang and Emperor Gaozu, or between the emperor and Prince Linjiang?" Prince Xiaowang replied: "Not as close." An Guo said: "Between Tai Shang Huang and Prince Linjiang, there was the intimate relationship of father and son. Yet Emperor Gaozu declared, 'It is I who took the empire with a three-foot sword,' so Tai Shang Huang never managed to control affairs and lived in Linyang." Prince Linjiang was the legitimate eldest crown prince, but because of a single misstatement, he was deposed and made King of Linjiang; due to an incident involving palace walls, he ultimately committed suicide in the office of the Zhongwei. Why was that? Because governing the empire must never allow private interests to disrupt public affairs. The saying goes: "Even if one has a kind father, how can you be sure he will not become a tiger?" "Even if one has an affectionate elder brother, how can you know he will not turn into a wolf?" "Now Your Highness is among the feudal lords; by delighting in the false words of an unprincipled minister, you have violated imperial prohibitions and undermined just laws." The Son of Heaven has refrained from applying the law to Your Highness out of respect for the Empress Dowager. The Empress Dowager weeps day and night, hoping that Your Highness will reform yourself, yet you remain unrepentant. "If the Empress Dowager were to pass away and her funeral procession departs, who will Your Highness still be able to rely on?" Before An Guo could finish speaking, Prince Xiaowang wept with tears streaming down his face and apologized to An Guo, saying: "I will now hand over Gongsun Gui and Yang Shin." Gongsun Gui and Yang Sin committed suicide. The Han envoys returned with a report, and all matters concerning Liang were resolved—this was due to An Guo's efforts. At this time, Emperor Jing and the Empress Dowager valued An Guo even more highly. After Prince Xiaowang died, Gongwang ascended the throne; however, An Guo lost his position due to a legal offense and remained at home.

## 6

During the Jianyuan period, Marquis of Wu'an Tian Fen served as Taiwei of Han and wielded power due to his noble status. An Guo presented him with gifts worth 500 gold pieces. Tian Fen recommended An Guo to the Empress Dowager; the Son of Heaven had also long heard of his virtue, and thus summoned him to serve as Dudi Duwei, later promoting him to Dashinong. When Minyue and Dongyue attacked each other, An Guo and Daxing Wang Hui led the campaign. Before they reached Yue, the Yue people killed their king and surrendered; thus, the Han forces withdrew as well. In the sixth year of Jianyuan, Marquis Tian Fen became Chancellor, and An Guo was appointed Yushi Daifu.

## 7

The Xiongnu came to request a marriage alliance; the Son of Heaven convened a discussion on the matter. Daxing Wang Hui, a native of Yan, had served many times as an official on the frontier and was well-versed in matters concerning the Hu. The discussion concluded: "Whenever Han and Xiongnu form a marriage alliance, it is usually no more than several years before they break the agreement again. It would be better not to agree but instead raise an army to attack them." An Guo said: "Fighting a war at such a distance of a thousand li will bring no military advantage. Now the Xiongnu, with their swift cavalry and feral hearts, move like birds, making them difficult to capture or control. Even if we seize their land, it would not significantly expand our territory; even if we subdue their people, it would not greatly strengthen us. Since ancient times, they have never been considered human in the same way as we are. If Han sends troops thousands of li to compete for advantage, both men and horses will be exhausted; the Xiongnu can then exploit our weakness with their full strength. Moreover, even when a strong crossbow is at its peak power, its arrow cannot pierce Lu gao (a fine silk fabric). At the end of a fierce wind's force, it lacks the strength to carry away a goose feather. It is not that the beginning lacked power; rather, its strength wanes at the end. Therefore, it would be disadvantageous to attack them; we are better off maintaining a marriage alliance." Many of the ministers who participated in the discussion supported An Guo, and thus the Son of Heaven agreed to the marriage alliance.

## 8

The following year, which was the first year of Yuanguang, a local leader from Mayi in Yanmen named Nie Wengyi approached Emperor Wu through Daxing Wang Hui and said: "Now that the Xiongnu have just formed a marriage alliance with us, they trust our border officials. We can lure them with profit." He secretly sent Nie Wengyi as an agent, who fled into Xiongnu territory and told the Chanyu: "I can behead the magistrates and officials of Mayi and surrender the city; all its wealth will then belong to you." The Chanyu trusted him greatly, believing his words to be true, and agreed with Nie Wengyi. Nie Wengyi then returned, faked the beheading of a prisoner condemned to death, displayed his head on Mayi city walls, and showed it to Xiongnu envoys as proof. He said: "The officials of Mayi are already dead; you may come quickly." Thus, the Chanyu led more than 100,000 cavalry through the pass into Wuzhou Sai.

## 9

At that time, Han forces had hidden more than 200,000 troops of cavalry and materials officers in the valleys near Mayi. Weiwei Li Guang was appointed Xiaoqi General, Taifu Gongsun He as Qingche General, Daxing Wang Hui as Jiangtun General, and Tai Zhongdafa Li Xi as Cai Guan General. Yushi Daifu Han An Guo was appointed Hujun General, to whom all the other generals were subordinate. The plan was for the Chanyu to enter Mayi before the Han forces launched their attack. Wang Hui, Li Xi, and Li Guang were ordered separately from Dai to strike at the Xiongnu's supply lines. Thus, the Chanyu entered Han's Wuzhou Sai in the Great Wall. Before reaching Mayi by more than a hundred li, they proceeded to raid and loot but saw only herds grazing in the fields, with no people in sight. The Chanyu became suspicious and attacked a beacon tower, capturing the Wuzhou 尉 Shi (magistrate's officer). He wanted to interrogate the 尉 Shi by torture. The 尉 Shi said: "Tens of thousands of Han troops are lying in ambush beneath Mayi." The Chanyu turned to his attendants and said, "We were almost tricked by the Han!" He then withdrew his troops. As they left the pass, he said: "I captured this 尉 Shi; it was a gift from Heaven." He ordered that the 尉 Shi be called "Tianwang" (King of Heaven). Word spread along the frontier that the Chanyu had already withdrawn. The Han forces pursued them to the pass, but realizing they could not catch up, withdrew as well. Wang Hui's forces of 30,000 troops learned that the Chanyu would not join battle with the Han. Judging that if they went to attack the supply wagons, they would inevitably face the Chanyu's elite soldiers and thus be sure to suffer defeat, they decided on their own initiative to withdraw their forces without orders; as a result, all of them returned without achieving any success.

## 10

The Son of Heaven was angry at Wang Hui for not attacking the Chanyu's supply wagons and instead 擅自 withdrawing his troops without authorization. Wang Hui said: "Initially, the plan was for the enemy to enter Mayi city and engage in battle with the Chanyu. At that time, I would attack his supply wagons and gain an advantage." "Now that the Chanyu has learned of our plan, he did not come as expected and instead withdrew. With my 30,000 men being no match for him, to proceed would only invite humiliation." "I have always known that returning without action would lead to my execution. Yet I chose to do so in order to preserve Your Majesty's 30,000 soldiers." Thus, Wang Hui was handed over to the Tingwei (Office of the Imperial Judge). The Tingwei judged that Wang Hui had delayed and hesitated in battle, a crime punishable by death. Wang Hui secretly gave 1,000 gold pieces to Chancellor Pei. Pei did not dare to speak to the emperor, but instead told the Empress Dowager: "Wang Hui was the first to propose the Mayi plan. Now that it failed and we are going to execute him, this would be taking revenge for the Xiongnu." The emperor paid a court visit to the Empress Dowager, who then relayed Chancellor Pei's words to him. The emperor said: "Wang Hui was the one who first proposed the Mayi plan, so I mobilized tens of thousands of troops from across the empire and followed his suggestion for this reason." "Moreover, even if capturing the Chanyu was not possible, Wang Hui's forces could have still struck his supply wagons and achieved some success to comfort the hearts of our officials." "Now if we do not execute Wang Hui, there will be no way to justify ourselves before the empire." Upon hearing this, Wang Hui committed suicide.

## 11

An Guo was a man of great strategic vision; his wisdom was sufficient to adapt and succeed in the world, yet he always acted with loyalty and sincerity. He was greedy for wealth. The people he recommended were all upright and virtuous men, more capable than himself. In Liang he recommended Hu Sui, Zang Gu, and Zhi Ta—all of them were renowned men of the empire. The literati also admired him for this reason; only the Son of Heaven regarded him as a national treasure. An Guo served as Yushi Daifu for more than four years. After Chancellor Tian Pei died, An Guo assumed the duties of chancellor; however, while leading a procession, he fell from his carriage and became lame. The Son of Heaven discussed the appointment of a new chancellor, intending to appoint An Guo. He sent an envoy to inspect him, but upon seeing how severely he was lame, changed his mind and instead appointed Pingji Hou Xue Ze as chancellor. After An Guo resigned from office for several months due to illness, his lameness improved; the emperor then reappointed him as Zhongwei. More than a year later, he was transferred to serve as Weiyi.

## 12

Chaji Jiangjun Wei Qing attacked the Xiongnu, marching from Shanggu and capturing the Hu Longcheng fortress. General Li Guang was captured by the Xiongnu but later escaped back to Han territory; Gongsun Ao suffered heavy losses of his troops: both were sentenced to death, but they managed to buy their way out and were reduced to commoners. The following year, the Xiongnu launched a large-scale invasion of Han territory, killing the Taihou (governor) of Liaoxi and advancing into Yanmen, where they killed or captured several thousand people. Chaji Jiangjun Wei Qing went out to attack them from Yanmen. Weiyi An Guo was appointed Cai guan jiangjun (general commanding the militia), stationed in Yuyang. An Guo captured a live prisoner, who reported that the Xiongnu had retreated far away. He then submitted a memorial stating that since it was farming season, he requested to temporarily disband the garrison troops. After more than a month of disbanded garrisons, the Xiongnu launched another large-scale invasion into Shanggu and Yuyang. An Guo's garrison had only more than 700 men; they went out to fight but were defeated and retreated back into the fortress. The Xiongnu captured over a thousand people, along with livestock, before withdrawing. Upon hearing of this, the Son of Heaven became furious and sent an envoy to rebuke An Guo. An Guo was transferred further eastward and stationed at Youbeiping. At that time, the Xiongnu claimed they would invade from the eastern region.

## 13

An Guo had initially served as Yushi Daifu and Hujun, but later gradually fell out of favor and was demoted; while the newly favored young general Wei Qing achieved great success and rose in status. After being distanced from power, An Guo became quiet and withdrawn; his garrison was once again deceived by the Xiongnu and suffered heavy losses, which filled him with deep shame. He was fortunate to be relieved of his post and return, but he was instead transferred further eastward for garrison duty; this left him in a daze and unhappy. After several months, he fell ill, vomited blood, and died. An Guo died in the second year of Yuanshuo (127 BCE).

## 14

Grand Historian said: I worked with Hu Sui on the calendar and laws, and observed Han Changru's righteousness as well as Hu Sui's profound inner depth and sincerity. The world often speaks of many virtuous men in Liang; this is indeed true! Hu Sui rose to the position of Zhanshi, and the Son of Heaven was just beginning to rely on him as a chancellor when Hu Sui died. Otherwise, with his inner integrity and upright conduct, he would have been the kind of man who serves with utmost dedication—a true gentleman indeed.
