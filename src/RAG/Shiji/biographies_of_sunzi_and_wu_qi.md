# Biographies of Sunzi and Wu Qi

## 1

Sun Zi Wu was a native of Qi. He presented his theories on military strategy to King Helu of Wu. King Helu said, "I have read all thirteen chapters of your work; may we test them on a small scale with troops?" He replied, "Yes." King Helu said, "Can we test it with women?" He said, "Yes." Thus he was granted permission. The king summoned beautiful women from the palace, and a total of 180 were obtained. Sun Zi divided them into two units, appointing the king's two favorite concubines as captains of each unit, and ordered all to carry halberds. He gave orders saying, "Do you know your hearts and the positions of your left and right hands and backs?" The women replied, "We do." Sun Zi said, "When I say 'Forward,' look at your hearts; 'Left,' look at your left hands; 'Right,' look at your right hands; "Backward, then look at your backs." The women said, "Yes, sir." After the commands were given, he set up axes and hammers for punishment, then issued them three times with five repetitions each. Then, when the drum was beaten to signal "Right," the women burst into laughter. Sun Zi said, "If the commands are not clear and the orders not thoroughly explained, that is the general's fault." He repeated the three commands with five explanations each and then beat the drum for "Left," but the women laughed again. Sun Zi said, "If the commands are not clear, and the orders not thoroughly explained—that is the general's fault; once they have been clearly given but still not followed properly, that is the soldiers' and officers' fault." He then ordered to execute the captains of both units. King Wu was watching from a pavilion and, upon seeing that his beloved concubines were about to be executed, became greatly alarmed. He hurriedly sent an envoy to issue orders saying, "I now know that the general is capable of commanding troops. Without these two concubines, my meals tasteless and I cannot enjoy them; I beg you not to execute them." Sun Zi said, "I have already accepted the command as general. When a general is in the field, he does not always obey the sovereign's orders." He then executed the two captains and displayed their bodies to make an example of them. He appointed the next in line as new captains, and then beat the drum again. The women moved left, right, forward, backward, knelt, and stood in perfect accordance with the rules and standards, not daring to make a sound. Sun Zi then sent an envoy to report to the king, saying, "The troops are now well-disciplined. Your Majesty may come down and observe them personally. They can be used as you wish; even if ordered into water or fire, they will obey." King Wu said, "General, you may now rest and return to your quarters. I do not wish to come down and watch." Sun Zi said, "The king merely likes the words but cannot use their true meaning." Thus King Helu knew that Sun Zi was capable of commanding troops and finally appointed him as general. To the west, he defeated the powerful state of Chu, entering Ying; to the north, he intimidated Qi and Jin, gaining fame among the feudal lords. Sun Zi played a significant role in these achievements.

## 2

After Sun Wu's death, more than one hundred years later there was Sun Bin. Sun Bin was born between A and Juan; he too was a descendant of the later generations of Sun Wu. Sun Bin once studied military strategy together with Pang Juan. After Pang Juan served the state of Wei, he became a general under King Hui. However, believing his own abilities to be inferior to Sun Bin's, he secretly sent for him. When Sun Bin arrived, Pang Juan feared that he was more capable than himself and resented him. He then had him punished by cutting off both his feet and tattooing his face, intending to keep him hidden from view.

## 3

A Qi envoy was sent to Liang, and Sun Bin secretly met with the envoy in his capacity as a penal prisoner, persuading him. The Qi envoy found him remarkable and secretly transported him back to Qi. Tian Ji, a general of Qi, treated Sun Bin kindly as a guest. Tian Ji often raced against the sons of high-ranking officials in Qi with heavy wagers involved. Sun Zi observed that their horses were not greatly different in speed, and noted that the horses could be classified into superior, medium, and inferior ranks. Sun Zi then said to Tian Ji, "You may place a heavy wager; I can ensure your victory." Tian Ji believed him and placed a bet of one thousand gold pieces with the king and other nobles. When the race was about to begin, Sun Zi said, "Now use your inferior horse against their superior one; take your superior horse and match it against their medium one; and take your medium horse and match it against their inferior one." After the three heats, Tian Ji lost once but won twice, ultimately winning a thousand gold pieces from the king. Tian Ji then recommended Sun Zi to King Wei. King Wei asked about military strategy, and thus appointed him as his teacher.

## 4

Later, when Wei attacked Zhao, the situation became dire for Zhao, which then requested aid from Qi. King Wei of Qi wanted to appoint Sun Bin as general, but Sun Bin declined, saying, "A man who has been punished cannot do so." Thus they appointed Tian Ji as general and Sun Zi as military advisor, who remained in a supply wagon, seated to devise strategies. Tian Ji wanted to lead the troops directly to Zhao, but Sun Zi said, "To untangle a complicated knot, one should not grasp it tightly; to rescue those in combat, one need not join the fray. Strike at their vulnerable points and exploit their weaknesses. When the situation is blocked and the enemy's strength restrained, they will resolve themselves naturally." Now that Wei and Zhao are fighting each other, their light troops and elite soldiers must be exhausted in the field, while the elderly and weak remain inside. You would do better to lead your troops quickly toward Daliang, occupy its roads and passages, and strike where they are weakest. They will then have no choice but to abandon Zhao and return to defend themselves. This way, with one move we can relieve the siege of Zhao and gain an advantage over Wei." Tian Ji followed his advice, and as expected, Wei withdrew from Handan. The two sides then engaged in battle at Guiling, where the Qi forces decisively defeated the Wei army.

## 5

Thirteen years later, Wei and Zhao attacked Han, and Han urgently requested aid from Qi. Qi sent Tian Ji to lead the forces there, marching directly toward Daliang. Pang Juan of Wei heard about it and withdrew from Han to return, but the Qi army had already passed by and moved westward. Sun Zi said to Tian Ji, "The armies of the three Jin states have always been fierce and bold in battle, yet they look down on Qi. Qi is known as timid; a skilled general should take advantage of their momentum and guide it to his benefit." According to the art of war, an army marching a hundred li for profit will lose its commanding general; one marching fifty li for profit will have only half its troops arrive. "Order the Qi army to make ten thousand cooking fires when entering Wei territory, five thousand the next day, and three thousand on the following day." After marching for three days, Pang Juan rejoiced and said, "I have always known the Qi army to be timid. After entering my territory for just three days, more than half their soldiers have already fled." He then abandoned his infantry and led his light and elite troops at double speed to pursue them. Sun Zi estimated the distance of their march and predicted that by dusk they would arrive at Maling. The road to Maling was narrow, with many obstacles and choke points on the sides—ideal for an ambush. He then felled a large tree, whitewashed it, and wrote on it: "Pang Juan will die beneath this tree." He then ordered ten thousand archers from the Qi army to lie in ambush on both sides of the road, instructing them: "At dusk, when you see a fire lit, shoot together." As expected, Pang Juan arrived at night beneath the felled tree and saw the white writing. He then lit a fire to illuminate it. Before he had finished reading, the ten thousand Qi archers shot simultaneously, causing great chaos and disarray in the Wei army. Pang Juan realized that his strategy had failed and his troops were defeated, so he took his own life, saying, "Thus I have completed the name of this upstart!" Qi then capitalized on its victory to completely defeat the Wei forces and captured Crown Prince Shen of Wei, bringing him back. Sun Bin thus became famous throughout the land, and his military strategies were passed down through generations.

## 6

Wu Qi was a native of Wei; he loved to study the art of war. He once studied under Zengzi and served King Lu. When the people of Qi attacked Lu, Lu wanted to appoint Wu Qi as general. However, Wu Qi had married a woman from Qi, and this caused suspicion in Lu. To prove his loyalty and gain recognition, Wu Qi then killed his wife to demonstrate that he would not side with Qi. Lu finally appointed him as general. He led the army in an attack on Qi and decisively defeated them.

## 7

Some people of Lu criticized Wu Qi, saying, "Wu Qi's character is suspicious and ruthless. When he was young, his family had amassed a fortune of thousands of gold pieces. After failing to secure an official post while traveling and seeking employment, he squandered the wealth. Mocked by his fellow townspeople, Wu Qi killed more than thirty men who had slandered him before leaving Weishui Gate in Wei heading east." Before parting with his mother, he bit his arm and swore an oath: "If Wu Qi does not become a high-ranking official or minister, I will never return to Wei again." He then went on to study under Zengzi. After a short while, his mother died, but Wu Qi never returned home. Zengzi disapproved of this and severed ties with him. Wu Qi then went to Lu, studied the art of war, and served King Lu. King Lu doubted him, so Wu Qi killed his wife in order to be appointed as general. Lu was a small state; if it gained the reputation of having won military victories, other feudal lords would plot against Lu. Moreover, Lu and Wei were allied states like brothers; if your Majesty employs Wu Qi, it means abandoning Wei." King Lu remained doubtful and dismissed Wu Qi.

## 8

Wu Qi then heard that King Wenhou of Wei was virtuous, and wished to serve him. King Wenhou asked Li Ke, "What sort of man is Wu Qi?" Li Ke replied, "Wu Qi is greedy and fond of women, but in the art of war, even Sima Rangju cannot surpass him." Thus King Wenhou of Wei appointed Wu Qi as general. He attacked Qin and captured five cities.

## 9

As a general, Wu Qi shared the same clothes and food with his lowest-ranking soldiers. He did not sleep on a mat, did not ride a horse when traveling, personally wrapped up provisions for the wounded, and shared hardships with his soldiers. When one of his soldiers developed an abscess, Wu Qi suckled out the pus to treat him. The soldier's mother heard about this and wept. Someone asked, "He is your son, yet the general personally suckled his wound—why are you crying?" The mother replied, "It's not like that. Last year General Wu suckled my husband's wound; my husband fought without turning his back and was killed by the enemy." "Now General Wu is again suckling my son's wound. I do not know where he will die." That is why I am crying."

## 10

King Wenhou, recognizing Wu Qi's skill in military affairs, his integrity and fairness, and his ability to win the hearts of soldiers, appointed him as governor of Xihe to resist Qin and Han.

## 11

After King Wenhou died, Wu Qi served under his son, King Wuhou. King Wuhou traveled downstream along the Xihe River, and in midstream, he looked back and said to Wu Qi, "How magnificent is this natural fortress of mountains and rivers! This is Wei's greatest treasure!" Wu Qi replied, "It lies in virtue, not in strategic terrain. "In the past, the Sanmiao clan had Dongting to its left and Pengli to its right; yet because they neglected virtue and righteousness, Yu destroyed them." "The capital of Xia Jie had the Yellow River and Ji River to its left, Mount Taihua to its right, Yique to the south, and Yangchang Pass to the north; yet because he ruled with cruelty, Tang deposed him." "The state of Yin Zhou had Mengmen to its left and Taihang Mountain to its right, Changshan to the north, and the great Yellow River flowing southward; yet because he governed without virtue, King Wu killed him." From this we can see that it lies in virtue, not strategic terrain. "If Your Majesty does not cultivate virtue, then all the people on these boats will become enemies." King Wuhou said, "Well spoken."

## 12

(Immediately) appointed Wu Qi as governor of Xihe, he gained great fame. Wei established the position of prime minister and appointed Tian Wen to it. Wu Qi was displeased, and said to Tian Wen, "May I discuss our merits with you?" Tian Wen replied, "Yes." Wu Qi said, "Commanding the three armies, making soldiers willing to die for me, and ensuring enemy states dare not plot against Wei—how do you compare with me?" Tian Wen replied, "I am no match for you." Wu Qi said, "Administering the hundred officials, winning the affection of the people, and filling the treasuries—how do you compare with me?" Tian Wen replied, "I am no match for you." Wu Qi said, "Defending Xihe so that the Qin army dares not face eastward, and Han and Zhao submit to Wei—how do you compare with me?" Tian Wen replied, "I am no match for you." Wu Qi said, "In these three aspects, you are all inferior to me, yet your position is higher than mine. Why is that?" Tian Wen replied, "The ruler is young and the state is in doubt; the ministers have not yet pledged loyalty, and the people do not trust. At such a time, would it be entrusted to you? Or to me?" Wu Qi fell silent for a long time, then said, "It would be entrusted to you." Tian Wen said, "This is why I hold the position above yours." Wu Qi thus realized he was not as capable as Tian Wen.

## 13

After Tian Wen died, Gongsu became prime minister, married a princess of Wei, and resented Wu Qi. Gongsu's servant said, "It is easy to get rid of Wu Qi." Gongsu asked, "How?" His servant said, "Wu Qi is a man of integrity and takes pride in his reputation. You should first tell King Wuhou, 'Wu Qi is a virtuous man, yet Your Majesty's state is small and borders the powerful Qin. I secretly fear that Wu Qi may not wish to remain here.'" King Wuhou would then ask, "What should be done?" You can then tell King Wuhou: 'Try offering him a princess in marriage. If Wu Qi wishes to remain, he will certainly accept it. If he has no intention of staying, he will surely refuse." Use this to determine his intentions.'" You then summon Wu Qi and return with him, but immediately have the princess act angry and disrespectful toward you. Wu Qi will see that the princess looks down on you, and he will certainly refuse the marriage." Thus, when Wu Qi saw how the princess looked down upon the prime minister of Wei, he indeed refused King Wuhou's offer. King Wuhou was suspicious but did not believe it completely. Fearing punishment, Wu Qi left Wei and went to the state of Chu immediately.

## 14

King Daowang of Chu had long heard of Wu Qi's virtue; upon his arrival, he was appointed prime minister of Chu. He clarified the laws and carefully issued orders, eliminated unnecessary officials, dismissed distant relatives of the royal family, and used the resources to support soldiers engaged in warfare. His main goal was to strengthen the military, and he refuted the empty talk of those advocating for alliances or divisions between states. Thus, to the south he pacified the Baiyue tribes; to the north he annexed the states of Chen and Cai, repelling the Three Jin states; and to the west he attacked Qin. The feudal lords were troubled by Chu's growing strength. Therefore, the noble relatives of Chu all wished to harm Wu Qi. When King Daowang died, members of the royal family and high-ranking ministers rebelled and attacked Wu Qi. Wu Qi fled to the king's corpse and took refuge beneath it. The attackers who struck at Wu Qi shot arrows at him, which also hit King Daowang's body. After the funeral of King Daowang, his son ascended the throne and ordered the Lingyi to execute all those who had shot at Wu Qi and also struck the king's corpse. Over seventy families were executed for their involvement in shooting Wu Qi, with entire clans wiped out.

## 15

Grand Historian said: The military strategies commonly praised by the world, such as Sunzi's thirteen chapters and Wu Qi's Art of War, are widely available in this age; therefore I will not discuss them, but instead focus on their actions and policies they implemented. It is said: "Those who can act may not necessarily be able to speak, and those who can speak may not necessarily be able to act." Sunzi's strategy clearly outwitted Pang Juan, yet he could not prevent the disaster that led to his own punishment. Wu Qi advised King Wuhou that virtue was more important than strategic terrain, yet when he implemented this in Chu with harsh and ruthless methods lacking compassion, it led to his own death. How tragic!
