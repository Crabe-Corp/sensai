# Biographies of Bian Que and Cang Gong

## 1

Bian Que was a native of Zheng, Bohai Jun. His family name was Qin, and his given name was Yue Ren. When he was young, he served as the head of an inn. A guest named Changsang Jun once passed through the inn; Bian Que alone found him extraordinary and always treated him with great care. Changsang Jun also knew that Bian Que was no ordinary man. After coming and going for more than ten years, he finally called Bian Que to sit privately with him and said in a quiet tone: "I have secret prescriptions. I am old now and wish to pass them on to you; do not reveal them." Bian Que replied, "I respectfully agree." He then took out medicine from his bosom and gave it to Bian Que, saying: "Drink this with the water of Shangchi; after thirty days you will be able to perceive things." He then took all his secret prescriptions and gave them entirely to Bian Que. Suddenly he was gone, probably not a human being at all. lington After thirty days of drinking the medicine as instructed, Bian Que could see people on the other side of walls. Using this ability to examine illnesses, he could clearly see disorders in all five internal organs; he merely used pulse diagnosis as a pretense. When practicing medicine, he was sometimes in Qi and sometimes in Zhao. In Zhao, he was known as Bian Que.

## 2

During the reign of Duke Zhao of Jin, the ministers were powerful while the royal clan was weak; Zhao Jianzi served as a minister and controlled state affairs. Zhao Jianzi fell ill, remaining unconscious for five days, and the ministers were all alarmed; they then summoned Bian Que. Bian Que entered to examine the illness and then came out. Dong Anyu asked Bian Que about it, and Bian Que said: "The blood vessels are functioning properly; there is nothing strange about this! In the past, Duke Mu of Qin once experienced something similar, regaining consciousness after seven days. On the day he regained consciousness, he told Gongsun Zhi and Zi Yu: "I had great pleasure at the imperial palace. The reason I stayed there for so long was that I happened to be studying something." "The emperor told me: 'Jin State is about to fall into great chaos, and five generations will not know peace. "After that, it will become dominant, but the ruler will die before old age." "The son of the one who becomes dominant will rule in such a way that men and women in his state will have no distinctions between them.'" Gongsun Zhi recorded these words and kept them hidden, thus the Qin strategy emerged. The chaos of Duke Xian, Duke Wen's dominance, and Duke Xiang's defeat of the Qin army at Yao but returning to indulge in licentiousness—these are what I have heard about. Now, Minister Zhao's illness is the same as that; within three days he will surely recover, and when he does, he will certainly have something to say."

## 3

After two and a half days, Zhao Jianzi regained consciousness and told the ministers: "I had great pleasure at the emperor's palace. I traveled with hundreds of deities in Jun Tian, where grand music was played nine times and ten thousand dances performed; it did not resemble the music of the Three Dynasties, but its sound stirred my heart. "A bear tried to attack me, and the emperor ordered me to shoot it; I hit the bear, which then died." "A she-bear came next, and I shot it again. I hit the she-bear, which also died." The emperor was very pleased and gave me two bamboo baskets, each containing a set of items. "I saw my son at the emperor's side; the emperor entrusted me with a Hu dog and said, 'When your son grows up, give it to him.'" "The emperor told me: 'The Jin State will gradually decline from generation to generation and will perish after seven generations. "The Ying clan will greatly defeat the Zhou people west of Fan Kui, but they will not be able to hold onto it.'" Dong Anyu received these words, recorded them, and kept them hidden. He told Zhao Jianzi about Bian Que's words, and Zhao Jianzi rewarded Bian Que with 40,000 mu of farmland.

## 4

Later, Bian Que passed through Guo State. The crown prince of Guo had died, and when Bian Que arrived at the gates of the Guo palace, he asked a junior official who was fond of medicine: "What illness did the crown prince suffer from? Why is the mourning ceremony in the state so elaborate compared to other affairs?" The junior official replied: "The crown prince suffered from an imbalance of blood and qi, which became entangled and could not be discharged. Suddenly manifesting externally, it caused internal harm. "His vital energy could not resist the pathogenic influences, which accumulated and remained trapped without release. Therefore, his yang was relaxed while his yin became urgent, leading to a sudden collapse and death." Bian Que asked: "When did he die?" He replied, "From the crowing of the rooster until now." Bian Que asked: "Has he been embalmed?" The junior official replied, "Not yet; it has not even been half a day since his death." "I am Qin Yue-ren of Bohai, Qi. My home is in Zheng; I have never had the honor of standing before you to serve." "I heard that the crown prince passed away unexpectedly; I am able to revive him." The junior official said: "Surely you are not joking, Master? "How can you say the crown prince can be revived!" "I have heard that in ancient times, there was a physician named Yu Fu. He treated illnesses without using decoctions or herbal washes; instead, he used acupuncture with needles and stones, manipulated the body through stretching and pressing, applied pressure to dispel toxins, and heated compresses for treatment. With one examination of symptoms, he could determine the condition by observing the flow from the five internal organs. He would cut open the skin and muscles, untangle knotted tendons, manipulate marrow and brain matter, lift membranes and remove obstructions, cleanse the intestines, wash out the five internal organs, purify essence, and even change a person's form." "If your methods are as effective as those of Master Yu Fu, then the crown prince can indeed be revived; "if they are not this effective and you still claim to revive him, it would be no more believable than telling a coughing infant." After the whole day, Bian Que looked up to the sky and sighed: "Your understanding of medicine is like looking at the sky through a bamboo tube or reading characters through a narrow gap. "My method does not require pulse diagnosis, observing complexion, listening to the voice, or examining the body's form to determine where the illness lies. "I can discern the yin aspect of an illness by merely hearing its yang symptoms; "and I can determine the yang condition from its yin symptoms. "Diseases manifest on a grand scale; even if the patient is thousands of miles away, I can diagnose them accurately. There are many who make decisions based on this, and it cannot be simply dismissed." "If you do not believe my words, please enter to examine the crown prince. You will hear a ringing in his ears and see that his nostrils are flaring; if you follow along both thighs down to the genital area, it should still be warm."

## 5

The junior official heard Bian Que's words and was so astonished that his eyes widened in shock without blinking, and his tongue trembled as if unable to descend. He then took Bian Que's statement into the palace to report it to Duke Guo. Duke Guo, upon hearing this, was greatly surprised. He came out to meet Bian Que at the central gate and said: "For a long time I have secretly admired your noble virtue, but I have never had the opportunity to pay my respects in person. "Now that you have visited our small state and kindly taken an interest in it, this humble official of a remote land feels deeply honored." "With you here, the crown prince may live; without you, he would be abandoned and left to rot in a ditch, dying forever with no chance of recovery." Before he could finish speaking, the duke began to sob and weep uncontrollably. His spirit was overwhelmed with emotion; tears streamed down his face, his eyes were red and swollen, and he was so grief-stricken that he could not stop himself. His appearance changed visibly from sorrow. Bian Que said: "The crown prince's illness is what is called 'shi jue.'" "It is a condition where yang energy enters the yin interior, disturbing the stomach and spreading along the meridians. The disease affects the collateral channels, branching downward to the sanjiao (triple heater) and bladder. Thus, the yang pulse flows downward while the yin pulse rises upward in resistance; as a result, vital qi becomes blocked and stagnant. Yin energy ascends while yang energy descends internally, causing internal pressure without response outwardly, with external functions severed and unable to function properly. Above there is a disruption of yang meridians, below a rupture of yin connections. With broken yin and severed yang, the complexion fades and the pulse becomes chaotic; hence, the body appears still as if dead." "The crown prince is not yet dead." "When yang enters the yin and supports the internal organs, life is preserved; when yin enters yang and disrupts the internal organs, death occurs." All these conditions are sudden manifestations that occur during a moment of distress in the five internal organs. "A skilled physician can treat such cases, but an unskilled one will doubt and hesitate."

## 6

Bian Que then instructed his disciple Zi Yang to sharpen the needles and grind the stones in order to stimulate the three yang meridians at their five convergence points. After some time, the crown prince regained consciousness. He then instructed his disciple Zibao to prepare a hot compress using five portions of medicinal ingredients, mixed in an eight-part decoction and boiled, which was applied alternately on both sides beneath the ribs. The crown prince sat up. After adjusting the balance of yin and yang, he merely took decoctions for twenty days before fully recovering to his former state. Therefore, people throughout the land all regarded Bian Que as one who could bring the dead back to life. Bian Que said: "Qin Yue-ren is not capable of bringing the dead back to life; this person was destined to recover. I merely helped him regain consciousness."

## 7

Bian Que passed through Qi, and Duke Huan of Qi received him as a guest. When he entered the court to pay his respects, he said: "Your Majesty has an illness in the skin and interstices; if left untreated, it will grow more serious." Duke Huan replied: "I am free of illness." After Bian Que left, Duke Huan said to his attendants: "Physicians are greedy; they want to take credit for curing people who have no illness." Five days later, Bian Que saw the duke again and said: "Your Majesty has an illness affecting your blood vessels; if not treated, it will worsen." Duke Huan replied: "I have no illness." After Bian Que left, Duke Hwan was displeased. Five days later, Bian Que again presented himself and said: "Your Majesty has an illness between the intestines and stomach; if left untreated, it is going to become more serious." Duke Huan did not respond. After Bian Que left, Duke Huán was displeased again. Five days later, when Bian Que came to see him again, he looked at Duke Huan and turned back in retreat. Duke Huan sent someone to ask why this was so. Bian Que said: "When the illness is in the skin and interstitial tissues, it can still be treated with decoctions or hot compresses; "when it reaches the blood vessels, acupuncture and stones are effective; "when it is in the intestines and stomach, medicinal wine can still reach it; "but when it reaches the bone marrow, even the god of life cannot do anything about it." "Now that the illness has reached his bone marrow, I have therefore ceased to make requests." Five days later, Duke Huan fell seriously ill and sent someone to summon Bian Que, but Bian Que had already fled. Duke Huan then died.

If a wise ruler had foreseen the subtle signs and allowed skilled physicians to intervene early, the illness could have been cured and his life saved. What people suffer from is often the abundance of diseases; what physicians suffer from, however, is the scarcity of effective methods. Therefore, there are six types of illnesses that cannot be treated: one who is arrogant and will not heed reason, this is the first type that cannot be cured; one who values wealth more than his own life, this is the second type that cannot be treated; one whose clothing and food are inappropriate for their condition, this is the third type that cannot be treated; one in whom yin and yang are imbalanced and internal organ qi is unstable, this is the fourth type that cannot be treated;  one whose body is too weak to tolerate medicine, this is the fifth type that cannot be treated;  one who believes in shamans rather than physicians, this is the sixth type that cannot be treated. If a person has any one of these conditions, it will be extremely difficult to treat them.

## 10

## 33

## 34
