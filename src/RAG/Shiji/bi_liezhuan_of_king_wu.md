# Bi Liezhuan of King Wu

## 1

Wu Wang Bi was a son of Liu Zhong, elder brother of Emperor Gaozu. Seven years after Emperor Gaozu had pacified the empire, Liu Zhong was appointed King of Dai. But when the Xiongnu attacked Dai, Liu Zhong could not hold out firmly and abandoned his state to flee. He secretly traveled to Luoyang and surrendered himself to the Son of Heaven. Because of their blood relation, the Son of Heaven could not bear to apply punishment, so Liu Zhong was deposed and made Marquis of Heyang. In the autumn of the eleventh year of Emperor Gaozu's reign, King Huainan Yingbu rebelled. He moved eastward to annex Jing territory, seized its military forces, and crossed westward over the Huai River to attack Chu. Emperor Gaozu personally led an army to punish him. Liu Zhong's son, Pei Hou Bi, was twenty years old. He had strength and vigor, and as a cavalry general, he followed the imperial forces to defeat Yingbu's army at Qixi, where they met at Chou. Yingbu fled. King Jing Liu Jia was killed by Yingbu and had no descendants. The emperor was concerned about the light and fierce nature of Wu and Kuaiji, which lacked a strong king to pacify them. Since his sons were young, he appointed Bi as King of Wu in Pei, granting him rule over three commanderies and fifty-three walled cities. After the investiture with the seal had been completed, Emperor Gaozu summoned Bi for divination and said to him, "Your appearance has the look of a rebel." He felt regret in his heart, but since the appointment had already been made, he patted Bi's back and told him, "Fifty years after Han, there will be unrest in the southeast. Could it be you?" "However, all those with the same surname under heaven are one family; be careful not to rebel!" Bi kowtowed and said, "I dare not."

## 2

During the reigns of Emperor Xiaohui and Empress Dowager Gao, when the empire had just been pacified, officials in commanderies, states, and feudal kingdoms each focused on governing their people with care. Wu had Tongshan in Yuzhang Commandery. Bi then attracted fugitives from across the empire (bandits) to cast coins and boiled seawater into salt, so that there was no need for taxation; as a result, his state's treasury became rich and prosperous.

## 3

During the reign of Emperor Xiaowen, Wu's crown prince came to court for an audience and was allowed to attend a drinking and board game session with the Crown Prince. The tutors and attendants of Wu's crown prince were all from Chu, known for their lightness and fierceness. The crown prince was also naturally arrogant. During a board game, they quarreled over the rules and showed disrespect; Crown Prince pulled up the board game and struck Wu's crown prince, killing him. At this time, his body was sent back for burial. Wu Wang was furious and said, "Since we share the same lineage under heaven, if he had died in Chang'an, then let him be buried in Chang'an. Why is it necessary to bring his corpse back here for burial?" He sent the remains back to Chang'an for burial once again. From this point on, Wu Wang gradually lost the proper etiquette of a vassal minister and claimed illness as an excuse to refrain from attending court. The imperial court knew that Wu Wang claimed illness not attending court because of his son's death, and investigated to confirm he was actually not ill. Whenever envoys from Wu came, they were immediately detained and interrogated. Wu Wang became frightened and devised more plots as a result. Later, when someone was sent to make a request on Wu's behalf in autumn, the emperor again questioned the envoy from Wu. The envoy replied, "The king is indeed not ill; the Han court detained several of our envoys, so he claimed illness as an excuse." Moreover, it is said that "being able to see fish in a deep pool is an ill omen." Now, when the king first pretended to be ill, and upon being discovered, faced urgent reprimand. He became more defiant, fearing punishment from the emperor, so he had no choice but to plot recklessly. "Only if Your Majesty abandons him and allows him a fresh start will this be resolved." Thus, the Son of Heaven pardoned Wu's envoy and sent him back, while bestowing a reading stand and walking stick upon King Wu, allowing him to retire as an elder and exempting him from attending court. Wu was relieved of his guilt and gradually abandoned his plots. Yet, because of its copper and salt resources in the state, the common people were exempt from taxation. When corvée labor was required, it was always provided at a fair price. At appropriate times of the year, he would inquire after outstanding talents and bestow rewards upon villages and neighborhoods. When officials from other commanderies or states wished to come and arrest fugitives, the people of Wu would litigate together to prevent them and refused to hand over the fugitives. In this way for more than forty years, it was because of this that he could command his people's loyalty.

## 4

Chao Cuo served as a household official for the Crown Prince and was favored by him, often casually mentioning that Wu's offenses could be used to justify reducing its territory. 藟 Numerous memorials were submitted to Emperor Xiaowen by him, but the emperor was lenient and could not bear to punish Wu; thus, Wu became increasingly arrogant. When Emperor Xiaojing ascended the throne, Chao Cuo became Grand Censor and advised the emperor: "In the past, when Emperor Gaozu first pacified the empire, he had few brothers and his sons were still young. Therefore, he greatly enfeoffed members of the imperial clan with the same surname. Hence, King Douhuiwang, a younger brother of the late emperor, was granted more than seventy walled cities in Qi; Uncle Yuanwang, an illegitimate younger brother, was given over forty walled cities in Chu; and Cousin Bi, son of Emperor Gaozu's elder brother Liu Zhong, was made King of Wu with more than fifty walled cities: three illegitimate relatives were enfeoffed, dividing half the empire. Now, King Wu previously had a dispute over his crown prince and falsely claimed illness to avoid attending court. According to ancient law, he should have been executed; however, Emperor Xiaowen could not bear it and instead granted him a reading stand and cane as a sign of leniency. He was shown the utmost kindness and should have repented and reformed himself. Instead, he became even more arrogant and extravagant, casting coins in the mountains, boiling seawater into salt, luring fugitives from across the land, and plotting rebellion. Now, if we reduce his territory, he will rebel; if we do not, he will still rebel. If we reduce it, the rebellion may come sooner, but the calamity would be smaller; if we do not reduce it, the rebellion will come later, but the disaster will be greater." In winter of the third year, King Chu came to court. Chao Cuo then said that in previous years, King Chu Wu had committed private misconduct while serving as a mourner for Empress Dowager Bo; he requested his execution. The emperor issued an edict of pardon but punished him by reducing the Donghai Commandery from his territory. He also reduced Yuzhang and Kuaiji commanderies from Wu's territory. And in the previous two years, King Zhao had committed crimes and lost his Hejian Commandery as punishment. King Jiaoxi Ang was found guilty of illegal activities in the sale of official ranks, so six counties were stripped from his domain.

## 5

Ministers at court were just beginning to discuss reducing Wu's territory. King Wu Bi feared that the reduction of his territory would never end, so he used this as a pretext to launch his plot and prepare for rebellion. Thinking that none of the feudal lords were worthy of being trusted with his plans, he heard that King Jiaoxi was brave, valiant in spirit, and fond of military affairs, and all of Qi feared him. Therefore, Wu Wang sent the central official Ying Gao to visit King Jiaoxi. Without written documents, he orally conveyed the message: "King Wu is unworthy and has long-standing worries. He dares not remain aloof but sends me to express his sincere desire for friendship." The king asked, "What advice do you have for me?" Ying Gao said, "Now the emperor has risen to power through treachery and is deceived by wicked ministers. He favors petty virtues, listens to slanderous villains, arbitrarily alters laws and decrees, seizes territories from feudal lords, imposes ever more levies, and punishes the virtuous with increasing severity." There is a local saying: "Licking bran will reach rice." "Wu and Jiaoxi are well-known feudal lords. If we are discovered at once, we may not be able to act freely anymore." King Wu has an internal illness and has been unable to attend court for more than twenty years. He has long feared being suspected, with no way to clear his name; even now, he walks with bent shoulders and cautious steps, yet still fears not being absolved of suspicion. I secretly heard that Your Majesty was unjustly implicated in a matter concerning official ranks. What I have heard about the reduction of feudal lords' territories, their crimes did not reach such severity; this time it may not even be reduced to losing territory." The king said, "Indeed, that is true. What do you suggest we should do?" Ying Gao said: "Those who share enmity assist each other; those with shared interests remain together. Those of the same sentiment support one another, and those with common desires move in unison. Those bound by mutual benefit are willing to die for each other. "Now, King Wu believes that he shares the same worries as Your Majesty. He wishes to take advantage of this opportunity and follow reason by sacrificing his life to eliminate the troubles and harms for all under heaven. Would it be possible for you to do likewise?" The king was startled and exclaimed, "How dare I act in such a way? Now, although the emperor is strict, it is certain that we may still face death; how could we possibly not remain loyal?" Ying Gao said: "Grand Censor Chao Cuo has misled the Son of Heaven, encroached upon feudal lords' territories, suppressed loyal officials and blocked virtuous men. The court is filled with resentment, and all the feudal lords have thoughts of betrayal; human affairs have reached their limit. A comet has appeared, and locusts have repeatedly emerged—this is a once-in-a-millennium opportunity, the very reason why sages rise to address great troubles. Therefore, King Wu wishes to use Chao Cuo as a pretext internally and follow Your Majesty's chariot externally. Roaming the world at will, wherever we face, cities would surrender; wherever we point, they would submit—there would be no one in the empire who dared not obey. If Your Majesty is kind enough to agree with just one word, then King Wu will lead King Chu in seizing the Hangu Pass, guarding the grain stored at Ao Granary in Yingyang, and resisting Han troops. We shall prepare camps and lodgings, waiting for Your Majesty to arrive. If Your Majesty deigns to come in person, then the empire can be united under our banner, with two rulers dividing its rule—would that not be possible?" The king said, "Well spoken." Ying Gao returned to report to King Wu. Still fearing that Jiaoxi might not agree, King Wu personally served as an envoy and went to Jiaoxi in person to solidify the alliance face-to-face.

## 6

Some of King Jiaoxi's ministers, having heard of his plan, advised him: "Serving one emperor is the greatest joy. Now that Your Majesty has allied with Wu and turned westward, if this plan succeeds, two rulers will vie for power, and troubles will only begin. The territories of the feudal lords are not even a tenth or twentieth part of Han's commanderies, yet to rebel and cause worry for Empress Dowager is certainly not a wise long-term strategy." King Jiaoxi did not listen. He then sent envoys to make agreements with Qi, Zichuan, Jiaodong, Ji'nan, and Jinbei; all agreed and said: "Chengyang King Jingwang was righteous in attacking the Lu clan. We will not involve him—once our affairs are settled, we shall divide things among ourselves."

## 7

The feudal lords, having recently suffered reductions and punishments, were frightened and filled with resentment toward Chao Cuo. When the decree to reduce Wu's Kuaiji and Yuzhang commanderies arrived, King Wu had already raised his troops first. On the Bingwu day of the first month, King Jiaoxi executed Han officials ranked at two-thousand stones or lower; similarly, King Jiaodong, Zichuan, Ji'nan, Chu, and Zhao did likewise, and thus they all mobilized their armies to move westward. King Qi regretted his decision and drank poison to commit suicide, breaking the rebellion pact. lık Jinbei Wang's city walls were damaged and not yet repaired; his Director of Attendants seized control of him, preventing the mobilization of troops. King Jiaoxi served as the leader, and together with King Jiaodong, King Zichuan, and King Ji'nan, they jointly launched an attack on Linzi. King Zhao immediately rebelled as well, secretly sending envoys to the Xiongnu to form a military alliance with them.

## 8

When the seven states launched their rebellion, King Wu mobilized all his soldiers and issued a decree throughout the state: "I am sixty-two years old, yet I shall personally lead the army. My youngest son is only fourteen, but he will also take the lead among the soldiers." "All men of military age—those as old as I am and those as young as my son—are to be conscripted." He mobilized more than 200,000 men. To the south, he sent envoys to Minyue and Dongyue; Dongyue also raised troops to join him.

## 9

On the Jiazi day of the first month in the third year of Emperor Xiaojing's reign, he initially raised his troops at Guangling. He moved westward across the Huai River and thus merged with King Chu's forces. He sent envoys with letters to the feudal lords, saying: "King Wu Liu Bi respectfully greets King Jiaoxi, King Jiaodong, Prince Zichuan, Prince Ji'nan, King Zhao, King Chu, King Huainan, King Hengshan, King Lujiang, and the former son of King Changsha: I hope you will kindly instruct me! "The Han court has traitorous ministers who have no merit to the empire yet encroach upon and seize territories from feudal lords. They send officials to arrest, detain, interrogate, and humiliate us as a matter of routine, failing to treat Liu clan kin with the respect due to fellow sovereigns. They cut off meritorious ministers of previous emperors and promote villains and corrupt men, thus causing chaos in the world and endangering the state." "The emperor is often ill and mentally impaired, unable to discern or investigate properly. "We intend to raise an army to eliminate these wrongdoers and respectfully await your instructions." "Although our state is small, it spans three thousand li in territory; its population may be few, but we can muster fifty thousand elite soldiers. "I have long served the Nan Yue for over thirty years; their kings and lords are all willing to provide soldiers to follow me, adding another thirty thousand or so." "Although I am unworthy, I wish to personally accompany the feudal kings. "The Yue forces heading straight for Changsha will be led by the prince to pacify all north of Changsha, advancing westward toward Shu and Hanzhong." "I inform Yue, King Chu, and the three kings of Huainan to march with me in a western direction; the kings of Qi and King Zhao shall secure Hejian and Henan, some entering through the Linjin Pass or meeting me in Luoyang; the kings of Yan and Zhao have an agreement with the Hu king; King Yan shall pacify Dai and Yunzhong to the north, rally the Hu forces through Xiaoguan Pass, advance toward Chang'an, rectify the emperor's affairs, and stabilize the ancestral temple of Emperor Gaozu." "I hope you will strive to accomplish this task. "The sons of King Yuwang of Chu and the three kings of Huainan have not bathed or washed for over ten years, their resentment seeping into their very bones. They have long desired to take action. I did not understand the intentions of these feudal lords before, so I dared not listen." "Now, if the feudal kings can preserve what remains and revive what has perished, strengthen the weak by punishing tyrants, and thus stabilize the Liu clan, this would be what the state desires." "Although our state is poor, I have long restrained my expenses on clothing and food, accumulated gold and money, strengthened weapons and armor, gathered grain supplies, working day and night for over thirty years." "I have done all this so that the feudal kings may strive to make use of it. Those who behead or capture a general will be rewarded with five thousand jin of gold and granted the title of ten-thousand-household marquis; those who behead or capture an officer, three thousand jin of gold, and a five-thousand-household title; those who behead or capture assistant generals will receive two thousand jin of gold and a two-thousand-household title; those who behead or capture officials of the 2,000-dan rank will receive one thousand jin of gold and be granted a one-thousand-household title; those who behead or capture 1,000-dan officials will receive five hundred jin of gold and a five-hundred-household title: all shall be granted the rank of marquis." Those who surrender with their troops or cities, if they bring ten thousand soldiers and a city with ten thousand households, shall be rewarded as if capturing a general; five thousand people and households, as if capturing an officer; three thousand people and households, like capturing an assistant general; one thousand people and households, equivalent to capturing a 2,000 dan official; Lower-ranking officials shall receive ranks and gold according to their respective merits. All other rewards and grants will be double those stipulated by military law. Those who already hold noble titles or fiefs shall receive additional ones without any reduction of their existing holdings. "I hope the feudal kings will clearly issue these orders to their officials and scholars, for I dare not deceive them." "My gold and money are scattered throughout the empire in many places; they do not necessarily have to be taken from Wu. The feudal kings may use them day and night without ever exhausting their supply." "If there are those who deserve rewards, inform me, and I shall personally send them to you." "I respectfully convey this message."

## 10

When the letter of rebellion from the Seven States reached the Son of Heaven, the emperor dispatched Taiwei Tiao Hou Zhou Yafu to command thirty-six generals and march against Wu and Chu; he also sent Quzhou Hou Li Ji to attack Zhao; General Luan Bu attacked Qi; Grand General Dou Ying was stationed at Yingyang to oversee the troops of Qi and Zhao.

## 11

When news of Wu and Chu's rebellion reached the court, before the army had been mobilized or Dou Ying had set out, he consulted the former prime minister of Wu, Yuan Ang. Yuan Ang was then at home in retirement and was summoned by imperial decree to an audience. The emperor, while discussing troop mobilization and military provisions with Chao Cuo, asked Yuan Ang: "You once served as prime minister of Wu. Do you know the character of Tian Lubo, a minister of Wu?" "Now that Wu and Chu have rebelled, what do you think about this matter?" Yuan Ang replied: "There is no need to worry; they will be crushed now." The emperor said, "King Wu of Wu has been casting coins in the mountains and boiling seawater to make salt. He lures outstanding men from all over the world and has now raised an uprising with white-haired elders." "If so, would he not have ensured his plan was completely foolproof before taking action?" "Why do you say they are incapable of achieving anything?" Yuan Ang replied, "It is true that Wu has the benefits of copper and salt, but how could they possibly attract outstanding men to follow them!" "If Wu had truly gained the support of such outstanding individuals, those people would have assisted King Wu in upholding righteousness and not participated in rebellion." "The people Wu lured are nothing but lawless young men and fugitives engaged in illegal coin casting. That is why they have banded together to rebel." Chao Cuo said, "Yuan Ang's strategy is sound." The emperor asked, "What is your plan?" Yuan Ang replied: "I request that the attendants be dismissed." The emperor had the attendants leave, and only Chao Cuo remained. Yuan Ang said: "What I am about to say is something no ordinary minister should know." The emperor then had Chao Cuo dismissed. Chao Cuo hurriedly retreated to the eastern corridor, deeply resentful. The emperor finally asked Yuan Ang, who replied: "Wu and Chu sent each other letters stating, 'King Gaozu's sons and brothers were granted their own fiefdoms. Now the traitorous minister Chao Cuo has arbitrarily imposed excessive punishments on feudal lords, seizing their territories.'" Therefore, they used rebellion as a pretext to march westward together and eliminate Chao Cuo in order to reclaim their former territories and cease hostilities. "At present, the only solution is to execute Chao Cuo and send envoys to pardon Wu, Chu, and the seven states, restoring their previously confiscated territories. Then all hostilities can be ended without further bloodshed." The emperor fell silent for a long time, then said: "If this is indeed the case, I will not hesitate to sacrifice one man in order to appease the empire." Yuan Ang said: "This is my humble suggestion; I hope Your Majesty will carefully consider it." The emperor then appointed Yuan Ang as Taichang and made the son of King Wu, Duke De Hou, the Zongzheng. Yuan Ang began preparing for his journey. Ten or so days later, the emperor sent the Zhongwei to summon Chao Cuo under false pretenses and led him away toward Dongshi. Chao Cuo was beheaded in the morning attire at Dongshi. The emperor then dispatched Yuan Ang to oversee ancestral rites, with Zongzheng assisting relatives, and sent them to inform Wu as Yuan Ang had advised. When they arrived at Wu, the armies of Wu and Chu had already attacked Liang's defenses. Because of their familial ties, Zongzheng was allowed to enter first for an audience and instructed King Wu to kneel and accept the imperial decree. King Wu, upon hearing that Yuan Ang had arrived, also knew of his intention to persuade him and replied with a laugh: "I have already declared myself the Eastern Emperor. To whom else should I pay homage?" He refused to see Yuan Ang but kept him in the camp, intending to force him into command. Yuan Ang refused and was placed under guard by the rebels, who intended to kill him. Yuan managed to escape at night on foot, fled to Liang's army, and returned to report to the emperor.

## 12

Tiao Hou led six relay carriages and gathered troops in Yingyang. When he arrived at Luoyang, he met Ju Meng and was delighted to say: "The seven states have rebelled. I traveled here by relay carriage, never expecting to arrive safely." He also thought that if the feudal lords had gained Ju Meng's support, yet Ju Meng remained inactive. "As long as I hold Yingyang, there is nothing to worry about east of here." When he arrived at Huaiyang, he asked Deng Duyi, an old retainer of his father Duke Jiaohou, "What is your strategy?" The guest replied: "The Wu army is very sharp and difficult to confront directly. "The Chu forces are light-footed but cannot sustain a prolonged campaign." "At present, the best strategy for you is to lead your troops northeast and take position at Changyi. Leave Liang to face Wu; Wu will certainly deploy all their elite forces to attack it." "You, General, should dig deep trenches and build high fortifications. Send light troops to cut off the Huai Si River crossing and block Wu's supply routes." "When Wu and Liang exhaust each other in battle and their supplies run out, you can then use your full strength to strike at their exhausted forces. This will surely lead to the defeat of Wu." Tiao Hou said: "Good." He followed the strategy, and so entrenched his forces south of Changyi while sending light troops to cut off Wu's supply lines.

## 13

When King Wu first launched his rebellion, his minister Tian Lubo was appointed as the Grand General. Tian Lubo said: "Gathering our troops and marching westward along a single route without any alternative paths will make it difficult to achieve success. "I request five thousand men and wish to take a separate route along the Yangtze and Huai rivers, capturing Huainan and Changsha, entering through Wuguan Pass, and meeting with Your Majesty. This would be an unexpected strategy." King Wu's crown prince advised: "Your Majesty has taken the name of rebellion. It is difficult to trust others with your army; those you rely on may also turn against you. How can we proceed?" "Moreover, if you independently command troops separately, there will be many unforeseen advantages and disadvantages; it is uncertain. It would only harm yourself." King Wu refused Tian Lubo's proposal.

## 14

Wu's junior general Huan Jiangjun advised the king: "Wu has many infantry troops, and infantry is effective in difficult terrain; the Han have more chariots and cavalry, which are advantageous on flat ground. "I hope Your Majesty will not linger in any city or fief that refuses to surrender, but instead abandon them and swiftly march westward to occupy Luoyang's military storehouse, seize the grain from Aocang, and control the strategic mountainous and riverine terrain to command the feudal lords. Even without entering Guan Pass, the empire will already be yours." "If Your Majesty proceeds slowly and leaves behind cities and fiefs, the Han chariots and cavalry will arrive in force, charge into the outskirts of Liang and Chu, and our cause will be lost." King Wu consulted his veteran generals, who said: "This is merely a reckless plan by a young man; how could he possibly understand the broader strategy?" Thus, King Wu did not adopt General Huan's strategy.

## 15

King Wu personally commanded all his troops and, before crossing the Huai River, granted positions as generals, lieutenants, commanders, and military officers to all his guests. Zhou Qiu alone was not appointed. Zhou Qiu was a native of Xiapi who had fled to Wu and became a wine seller with no reputation. King Wu Bi looked down on him and did not entrust him with any position. Zhou Qiu paid a visit to the king and said: "I am unworthy and have not been allowed to serve in your army. "I do not dare to request command of troops, but I only ask for one Han token from Your Majesty; I will surely repay you in some way." The king then gave it to him. Zhou Qiu, having obtained the token, rode swiftly into Xiapi at night. At that time, Xipi had heard of Wu's rebellion and all its cities were on defense. Upon arriving at the relay station, he summoned the city magistrate. He ordered the official to enter his residence and had his attendant behead him on charges of a crime. He then summoned the capable officials who were friends with his brothers and told them: "Wu's rebel army is about to arrive. When they do, they will massacre Xipi in no more time than it takes to finish a meal. "If we surrender first, our families and homes will be safe, and those with ability may even be enfeoffed as marquises." When they went out and spread the message, all of Xipi surrendered. Zhou Qiu raised 30,000 men in one night and sent someone to report to King Wu. He then led his troops northward to raid cities and fiefs. By the time he reached Chengyang, his forces had grown to more than 100,000 and defeated the army of the Chengyang Commander-in-Chief. Upon hearing that King Wu had been defeated and was fleeing, Zhou Qiu assessed that no one would share in his success, so he led his troops back to Xipi. Before reaching the city, he died of a boil on his back.

## 16

In the second month, after King Wu's army was defeated and he fled, the Son of Heaven issued an imperial decree to the generals: "I have heard that those who do good are rewarded by heaven with blessings; those who commit evil deeds are punished by heaven with misfortune. Emperor Gaozu personally recorded meritorious deeds and established feudal lords. When You Wang and Douhui Wang died without heirs, Emperor Wendi felt compassion and extended grace by enfeoffing You Wang's son Sui and Douhui Wang's son Ang, among others, to serve as successors of their ancestors' ancestral temples and as vassal states of Han. His virtue matched the heavens and earth, his wisdom equaled that of the sun and moon. King Wu Bi betrayed virtue and violated righteousness, luring in fugitives and criminals from all under heaven, disrupting the currency of the realm. He claimed illness to avoid court attendance for more than twenty years. Officials repeatedly petitioned for punishment against Bi, but Emperor Wendi showed leniency, hoping he would reform himself and do good. Now he has conspired with King Chu Wu, King Zhao Sui, King Jiaoxi Ang, King Jinan Biguang, King Zichuan Xian, and King Jiaodong Xiongqu to form a coalition in rebellion. They have acted in defiance of the Mandate of Heaven, raising arms to endanger the ancestral temples, assassinating ministers and Han envoys, coercing the people, killing the innocent, burning homes, and desecrating tombs—behaving with extreme cruelty. Now Ang and others have committed yet another grave violation of the Mandate, burning ancestral temples and looting imperial possessions. This deeply pains me. I am wearing plain clothes and avoiding the main hall in mourning; you generals should encourage the scholars and officials to fight against these rebellious enemies. Those who attack the rebels should achieve merit by penetrating deeply and killing many enemies. All those who behead or capture rebels equivalent to officials of 300 dan rank or higher must be executed, without exception. Anyone daring to question the decree or fail to follow its orders shall be decapitated."

## 17

Initially, when King Wu crossed the Huai River and joined with King Zhao Sui to defeat the Han forces at Jibi in the west, they advanced on a winning streak, their momentum very strong. King Liang Xiao Wang was alarmed and sent six generals to attack Wu, but the Han forces were defeated again. The two Liang generals and their troops all retreated back to Liang. Liang repeatedly sent envoys to Tao Hou seeking rescue, but Tao Hou refused. They also sent envoys to defame Tao Hou before the emperor, who then ordered someone to inform Tao Hou to rescue Liang. Yet again, Tao Hou refused on grounds of military discretion. Liang appointed Han Anguo and Zhang Yu, the younger brother of a deceased official from Chu, as generals, and thus managed to inflict some defeats on Wu's forces. Wu's army wanted to advance westward, but Liang's city defenses were strong and they dared not proceed. Instead, they retreated toward Tao Hou's forces and met at Xiayi. When Wu wanted to fight, General Tao Hou entrenched his position but refused battle. With their supplies cut off and troops starving, the Wu forces repeatedly challenged for battle. At night they fled to Tao Hou's camp, causing alarm in the southeast. General Tao Hou had ordered defenses prepared in the northwest, and indeed the rebels attacked from that direction. Wu suffered a crushing defeat; many of his troops died of starvation or deserted. Thus, King Wu fled with several thousand of his elite soldiers at night, crossed the Yangtze River to Dantu, and took refuge in Dongyue. Dongyue had about 10,000 troops and sent men to gather up the fugitive soldiers. The Han sent envoys with generous rewards to bribe Dongyue, who then deceived King Wu. When King Wu left the city to greet his troops, Dongyue's men immediately ambushed and killed him, placed his head in a lacquered container, and rushed it by relay post to report the news. King Wu's sons Zihua and Ziju fled to Minyue. After King Wu abandoned his army and fled, the forces quickly disintegrated, with many units gradually surrendering to the Grand Marshal or Liang's troops. King Chu Wu's army was defeated, and he committed suicide.

## 18

When the three kings besieged Qi's Linzi, they could not capture it for three months. When Han forces arrived, Kings Jiaoxi, Jiaodong, and Zichuan each withdrew their troops. King Jiaoxi then removed his clothes and shoes, sat on dry grass, drank water, and apologized to the Empress Dowager. The king's crown prince, De, said: "The Han forces are far away. I have observed that they are already exhausted; we can attack them. I request to gather the remnants of Your Majesty's army and strike them. If we fail to defeat them, we may still flee into the sea—it is not too late." The king said: "My troops have already been shattered and cannot be mobilized." He did not listen. The Han general Gonggao Hou Tuidang sent a letter to the king saying: "I have been ordered by imperial decree to punish those who act unjustly. Those who surrender will be pardoned and restored to their former status; those who do not surrender will be annihilated." "Where are you, King? Decide quickly what course of action to take." The king then prostrated himself bare-chested at the Han army's camp and pleaded: "Minister Ang failed to observe the law properly, causing alarm among the people. I have troubled General so far from home to reach this desolate land; I dare to request punishment by being minced like meat for sauce." Gonggao Hou held up the gong and drum in his presence and said: "Your Majesty has endured military hardships; I would like to hear about how you raised your troops." The king kowtowed and crawled forward on his knees to reply: "Recently, Chao Cuo, a minister favored by the Son of Heaven, altered Emperor Gaozu's laws and decrees, encroaching upon the fiefs of the feudal lords. Ang and others considered this unjust and feared that it would bring chaos to the realm, so seven states raised their armies in order to punish Chao Cuo. Now we have heard that Chao Cuo has been executed; Ang and others respectfully withdraw our troops and return." The general said: "If Your Majesty found Chao Cuo to be unworthy, why did you not report it?" (And) there had been no imperial decree or tiger token; you raised troops and attacked the legitimate state without authorization. Judging from this, your intent was not truly to punish Chao Cuo." He then produced the imperial decree and read it aloud for the king. After finishing reading, he said: "Your Majesty should decide your own course of action." King said: "Ang and I have committed sufficient crimes to warrant death." He then committed suicide. The Empress Dowager and the crown prince both died as well. Kings Jiaodong, Zichuan, and Jinan all perished; their states were abolished and incorporated into Han. General Li surrounded Zhao for ten months before capturing it, at which point King Zhao committed suicide. King Jibei was spared punishment due to coercion and was instead relocated as king of Zichuan.

## 19

Initially, King Wu led the rebellion first, uniting his forces with those of Chu and linking up with Qi and Zhao. He raised troops in the first month, but by the third month all were defeated; only Zhao surrendered later. The Han court then reappointed Yuan Wang's youngest son Pinglu Hou Li as King of Chu, continuing the descendants of Yuan Wang. King Ru'nan Fei was relocated from his former fief in Wu's old territory and enfeoffed as King of Jiangdu.

## 20

Grand Historian said: The enfeoffment of King Wu was due to his father Sheng. He imposed light taxes, mobilized the people, and monopolized the profits from mountains and seas. The seeds of rebellion were sown by his son's actions. Striving for martial prowess and instigating trouble, he ultimately lost the very foundation of his power; betraying his own kin by plotting against the royal family, he was eventually destroyed. Chao Cuo acted with long-term concern for the state, yet misfortune struck him personally. Yuan Ang's manipulative persuasion brought initial favor but later disgrace. Therefore, in ancient times, the fiefs of feudal lords did not exceed a hundred li, and mountains and seas were never granted as hereditary domains. "The saying 'Do not be close to the Rong and Di barbarians, for this will alienate your subjects'—was it referring to Wu?" "The saying 'Do not take the lead in seeking power, lest you suffer its consequences'—could this be about Yuan Ang and Chao Cuo?"
