# Calendar Record

## 1

In ancient times, the calendar was established and the first month of spring was set as the beginning. At that time, ice melted and hibernating creatures awoke from their slumber; all kinds of grasses sprang to life, and the zimu bird was the first to migrate north. All things were thus prepared for the year; they originated in the east, followed sequentially through the four seasons, and concluded at the winter solstice. At that time, the rooster crowed three times, and it became bright. olocation Following the twelve lunar months, it concluded on the Chou day. The sun and moon were completed; therefore, there was brightness. Brightness is the beginning (Meng), obscurity is the end (You), and the combination of brightness and obscurity represents yin and yang. Yin and yang alternately flourish, thus following the principle of harmony and order. The sun returns to the west, yet rises in the east; the moon returns to the east, yet shines brightly from the west. If correctness does not follow the heavens and is also not determined by human will, then all matters are easily ruined and difficult to accomplish.

## 2

When a sovereign changes his surname and receives the mandate, he must be cautious at the beginning. He should reform the calendar, change the ceremonial colors, trace back to the fundamental principle of Heaven's origin, and follow in accordance with its will.

## 3

Grand Historian said: The time before Shen Nong is already ancient history. It was the Yellow Emperor who examined and determined the stars and calendar, established the Five Elements, initiated the cycles of waxing and waning, regulated intercalary months, and thus there were officials in charge of celestial deities, terrestrial spirits, and all categories of things; these were called the Five Officials. Each official was responsible for their respective order, without mutual disorder. Therefore, the people could have trust, and deities could thus possess virtuous qualities. The people and the deities had distinct duties; they revered each other without desecration. Therefore, the deities bestowed bountiful harvests upon them, the people offered sacrifices with their possessions, calamities did not arise, and whatever was sought would never be lacking.

## 4

When the Xiaohao clan declined, the Jiuli people disrupted virtue; the people and deities became mixed in disorder, unable to distinguish things properly. Calamities occurred frequently, and no one could complete their life span. Zhuanxu inherited the throne and thus appointed Nanzheng Zhong to oversee Heaven in charge of deities, and Huozheng Li to oversee Earth in charge of the people. He ordered them to restore the old order so that there would be no mutual encroachment or desecration.

## 5

Later, the Sanmiao people submitted to the virtue of Jiuli, so both officials abandoned their duties. As a result, intercalary months became disordered, the first month was destroyed, the Sheti calendar lost its records, and the chronological sequence collapsed. Yao continued the descendants of Zhong and Li, not forgetting those who preserved the old traditions; he appointed them to resume their duties and established the office of Xihe. When time was clearly defined and measurements were accurate, yin and yang would be in harmony, winds and rains would follow their proper seasons, vital energies would flourish, and the people would suffer no premature deaths or epidemics. When Emperor Yao grew old, he abdicated in favor of Shun and solemnly warned the ancestral shrine, saying, "The mandate of Heaven's calendar lies upon your person." Shun also passed this same command to Yu. From this we can see what the sovereigns valued most.

## 6

The Xia dynasty used the first month as the beginning of the year, the Yin (Shang) dynasty used the twelfth month, and the Zhou dynasty used the eleventh month. The calendar systems of these three dynasties followed a cyclical pattern; when reaching an end, they would return to their original starting point. When the world is governed properly, there will be no loss of order and sequence; when it is not so, then the calendar system cannot be implemented among the feudal lords.

## 7

After the reigns of King You and King Li, the Zhou royal house declined; subordinate ministers held power. Historians did not record time accurately, and rulers no longer announced the new month. Therefore, descendants of the chouren (calendar officials) dispersed, some settling among the Chinese states and others among the barbarian tribes. As a result, their knowledge of omens and celestial phenomena was abandoned and no longer unified. In the 26th year of King Xiang of Zhou, an intercalary third month was added, but this was criticized in the Spring and Autumn Annals. The calendar system of former kings established order at the beginning, maintained correctness during the middle, and corrected deviations at the end. Establishing order at the beginning ensures that sequences do not deviate; maintaining correctness during the middle prevents confusion among the people; correcting deviations at the end ensures that affairs do not go against reason.

## 8

Afterward, during the Warring States period, all states competed for dominance; their focus was on conquering enemies and resolving urgent crises. How could they have time to consider such matters! At that time, only Zou Yan was clear about the transmission of the Five Virtues and could distinguish the cycles of waxing and wanning to demonstrate his theories among the feudal lords. And also, because Qin destroyed the six states and military affairs were extremely busy, in addition to its brief time as supreme ruler, there was no opportunity or leisure for such matters. They also somewhat promoted the Five Victories, claiming that they had received the auspicious sign of the Water Virtue. They renamed the Yellow River as "De Shui" (Virtuous Water) and set October as the first month, favoring black in ceremonial colors. However, regarding calendar calculations and intercalary months, they were still unable to grasp their true principles.

## 9

When the Han dynasty rose to power, Emperor Gaozu said, "The Northern Altar awaited my arrival," and also believed that he had received the auspicious sign of Water Virtue. Although those well-versed in calendar calculations, such as Zhang Cang, all agreed with this view. At that time, the empire had just been pacified and was in the early stages of establishing its fundamental order. Empress Dowager Gao, as a female ruler, also had no leisure for such matters; therefore, they continued to follow the Qin calendar system and ceremonial colors.

## 10

It was not until the reign of Emperor Wen that Gongsun Chen, a native of Lu, submitted a memorial on the Five Virtues' cycle, stating, "The Han dynasty has acquired the Earth Virtue; it is appropriate to begin anew, reform the calendar system, and change ceremonial colors. There should be an omen: a yellow dragon appeared." The matter was referred to Chancellor Zhang Cang, who had also studied the laws and calendar system. He considered it incorrect and dismissed the proposal. Later, a yellow dragon was sighted in Chengji; Zhang Cang himself recanted his previous stance, and thus the matter he wished to discuss could not be established. Meanwhile, Xin Yuangping gained an audience by observing atmospheric signs and made many claims about the calendar system and ceremonial colors; he was favored and held in high esteem. However, later he instigated chaos, so Emperor Wen dismissed him and no longer asked about such matters.

## 11

When the current emperor ascended the throne, he summoned the Daoist scholar Tang Du to divide up the celestial divisions; and Ba Luo Xiahou calculated and adjusted the calendar system, after which the measurement of days and months aligned with that of the Xia dynasty. Thus, he changed the era name, altered official titles, and performed a sacrificial ceremony on Mount Tai. He then issued an edict to the Grand Historian, saying: "Previously, officials stated that the stellar measurements were not yet determined. We have widely invited and consulted experts to clarify these measurements, but still could not fully resolve them. I have heard that in ancient times the Yellow Emperor united all things and did not die; he clearly identified measurements and verified them, established distinctions between clarity and turbidity, initiated the Five Officials, and set up divisions for qi (vital energy) and material phenomena. But this was already ancient history. The records are incomplete and the music system has declined; I deeply lament this situation. I consider myself still unable to fully understand these matters, but by carefully calculating the divisions of days, they generally correspond with the superiority of Water Virtue. Today, following the summer solstice, Huangzhong is used as Gong (palace tone), Linzhong as Zhi (sharp tone), Taicu as Shang (merchant tone), Nanlv as Yu (feather tone), and Guxian as Jue (angle tone). From this point onward, the qi returned to its proper state, the Yu tone became clear again, and the names of tones resumed their correct variations. When the day of Zi coincided with the winter solstice, then the principle of yin-yang separation and union was properly observed. On the Jiazi day of the first lunar month, at dawn on the winter solstice (which has now been clearly determined), it was decreed that this year should be changed to the first year of Taichu. The year was named "Yanfeng Sheti Ge," the month "Biju," and on this day, it was Jiazi. At midnight, at dawn of the new moon coinciding with the winter solstice."

## 12
