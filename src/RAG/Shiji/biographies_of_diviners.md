# Biographies of Diviners

## 1

Since ancient times, those who received the Mandate of Heaven and became kings—has the rise of a ruler ever not been decided by divination to determine heavenly will? This was especially true in the case of the Zhou dynasty, as could also be seen with the Qin. The enthronement of Daiwang was entrusted to diviners. The office of Taibu originated with the rise of the Han dynasty.

## 2

Sima Jizhu was a native of Chu. He practiced divination in the eastern market of Chang'an.

## 3

Song Zhong was a zhongdafa, and Jia Yi was a bo shi. On the same day they both went out for their regular bath and cleansing ritual, accompanying each other in discussion, reciting the Yijing and the teachings of past sage kings, thoroughly examining human conditions, and gazing at each other with sighs. Jia Yi said: "I have heard that in ancient times, sages did not reside in the imperial court; they were certainly among diviners and physicians. Now I have seen the Three Dukes, the Nine Ministers, and officials of the court; all are known to me. Let us test them among diviners to observe their character." The two men then traveled together in a carriage to the market, strolling through the shops of fortune-tellers. It had just rained, and the road was nearly deserted. Sima Jizhu sat idly, with three or four disciples in attendance, discussing the principles of heaven and earth, the movements of sun and moon, and the fundamentals of yin-yang auspiciousness and inauspiciousness. The two officials bowed twice to pay their respects. Sima Jizhu observed their appearance and demeanor, which seemed like those of knowledgeable men; he then treated them with courtesy, instructing his disciples to invite them to sit. After they were seated, Sima Jizhu resumed the previous discussion, distinguishing between the beginning and end of heaven and earth, explaining the cycles of sun, moon, and stars, differentiating the junctures of benevolence and righteousness, listing omens of auspiciousness and inauspiousness. He spoke for thousands of words, all of which were logical and well-reasoned.

## 4

Song Zhong and Jia Yi were suddenly enlightened, tightening their sashes and sitting upright with great seriousness. They said: "Observing your appearance, hearing your words, I have secretly observed the world for a long time, but have never seen anyone like you. Why now do you occupy such a humble position and why is your conduct so obscure?"

## 5

Sima Jizhu laughed heartily, clapping his hands and said: "Observing the two of you, I thought you were men of learning. Now your words are so shallow, and your expressions so crude! Now, what is it that my master considers virtuous? Who does he hold in high esteem? Why now do you belittle and defile an elder?"

## 6

The two gentlemen said: "High official rank and generous salary are what the world holds in high esteem, occupied by virtuous and talented individuals. Now you occupy a position that is not appropriate for your status; therefore, it is called humble. Your words are untrustworthy, your actions ineffective, and what you obtain is unwarranted—hence, it is called obscure." Diviners are those whom the world commonly despises and neglects. Everyone says: "Diviners often speak boastfully and sternly to gain favor, falsely elevate people's fortune and destiny to please their desires, recklessly predict calamities to wound hearts, fabricate talk of ghosts and gods to exhaust one's wealth, and demand excessive bows and thanks for personal gain." This is what I am ashamed of; therefore, it is considered humble and obscure."

## 7

Sima Jizhu said: "Please sit calmly. Have you seen a young boy with disheveled hair? When the sun and moon shine upon him, he moves; when they do not, he stops. If you ask him about blemishes, flaws, auspiciousness or inauspiciousness of the sun and moon, he cannot explain it. From this we can see that those who are able to distinguish between the virtuous and the unworthy are few indeed.

## 8

"The conduct of a virtuous person is to offer upright advice in accordance with righteousness; if they advise three times and are not heeded, then they withdraw. They praise others without expecting reward, and criticize others without fearing resentment; their concern is solely the benefit of the state and the people. Therefore, they do not accept an office unless it matches their responsibilities, nor do they receive a salary unless it corresponds to their merit; When they see someone who is not upright, even if that person is noble, they will not show respect. If they see a person with moral stains, even if the person holds high rank, they will not lower themselves to him; They do not rejoice at gaining something nor grieve over losing it. If the blame is not their own, even if they suffer repeated disgrace, they feel no shame.

## 9

"Now what you call virtuous people are all a source of shame. They humble themselves with flaws and move forward, fawning in their speech; They draw each other in through power, guide one another by profit; They form cliques and exclude the upright, seeking respect and reputation to receive official stipends; They pursue private interests, pervert the law of their ruler, and prey upon peasants; they wield official authority as a weapon and manipulate the law for opportunity, seeking profit through violent and oppressive means: this is no different from holding a sharp blade to rob people. When they first took office, they exerted double effort in deceit and trickery, embellishing false achievements with hollow documents to deceive the sovereign, using their position of power to rise above others; When tested for office, they did not yield to the virtuous or present their achievements honestly; instead, they exaggerated falsehoods as truths, turned nothing into something, and made little seem much, in order to gain advantage, power, and high rank; They indulge in food and drink, ride about with concubines and singing girls, neglect their parents, break laws to harm the people, and drain public resources: these are thieves who do not wield spears or bows, aggressors who need no bowstrings or blades, deceivers of parents without yet committing a crime, and assassins of rulers whose deeds have not yet been punished. How can such people be considered virtuous and talented?

## 10

"Burglars and thieves cannot be prevented, the Yi and Mo peoples cannot be pacified, corruption and evil cannot be suppressed, official affairs are wasted and disorderly and cannot be governed, the four seasons do not harmonize and cannot be regulated, annual harvests fail and cannot be adjusted. If a talented and virtuous person does nothing about these matters, that is disloyalty; if one lacks talent or virtue yet holds an official position, profits from the ruler's stipend, and obstructs the place of a virtuous person, that is usurping office; To promote those who have connections and to honor those with wealth is falsehood. Have you never seen the difference between a chixiao bird and a phoenix in flight? Orchid, zhi, xiongqing are abandoned in the vast fields, while wormwood and xiao grow into forests; this causes virtuous men to retreat and remain obscure among the masses—this is what you all have become.

## 11

"To transmit without creating is the righteousness of a gentleman. Now, diviners must follow the principles of heaven and earth and emulate the four seasons; they act in accordance with benevolence and righteousness. They divide yarrow stalks to determine hexagrams, rotate the 栻 board and align the chess pieces properly, then speak about the benefits or harms of heaven and earth, and the success or failure of affairs. In ancient times, when past sage kings established a state, they first consulted tortoise shells and yarrow stalks along with the sun and moon; only then would they dare to act. They determined the proper time and day, and only after that did they enter a household. When giving birth to a child, one must first consult for auspiciousness or inauspiousness; only then is the child considered legitimate. Since Fu Xi created the Eight Trigrams, and King Wen of Zhou expanded them into 384 yao lines, the world was governed in peace. King Goujian of Yue followed King Wen's Eight Trigrams to defeat enemy states and became dominant over all under heaven. From this, how can divination be considered harmful!

## 12

"Moreover, a diviner sweeps the floor and prepares a seat, adjusts his cap and sash properly, and then speaks about matters—this is an act of propriety. Their words may be heard by ghosts and gods, who then offer sacrifices; loyal ministers serve their rulers, filial sons care for their parents, kind fathers raise their children—these are acts of virtue. And for a few dozen or hundred coins, the sick may be healed, those near death may live again, troubles may be avoided, affairs may succeed, and marriages arranged so that sons and daughters can raise families: this is virtue; how could it merely be worth dozens or hundreds of coins! This is what Laozi meant by "the highest virtue does not dwell on virtue, and thus possesses true virtue." Now, diviners bring great benefit yet receive little reward—how different is this from what Laozi said?

## 13

"Zhuangzi said: 'A gentleman is free from the worry of hunger and cold within, and from the fear of robbery or plunder without; he respects his superiors when in a high position, and does no harm to others when in a lowly one—this is the way of a gentleman.'" Now, the occupation of a diviner is such that it does not require accumulation or storage in granaries; it needs no carts to transport it. The burden one carries is light, and when used, its resources are inexhaustible. Holding something that cannot be exhausted, traveling through an endless world—this conduct is even beyond what Zhuangzi practiced. Why then do you say divination should not be done? The heavens are insufficient in the northwest; stars and constellations shift toward that direction; the earth is lacking in the southeast, where the sea serves as a pool; at noon the sun must move on, and when full the moon must wane; the way of past sage kings is sometimes preserved, sometimes lost. To hold diviners responsible for their words always being true—is this not confusing!

## 14

"Have you seen eloquent scholars and debaters? When they deliberate on affairs and make plans, their reasoning is always sound; yet still, they cannot sway the ruler with a single sentence. Therefore, in speech they must cite past sage kings, and when speaking, they must refer to ancient times; When deliberating on affairs and making plans, they embellish the achievements of past sage kings while mentioning their failures and harms, using these to instill fear or joy in the ruler's mind, seeking thereby to fulfill their own desires. Exaggerated and boastful speech could not be more extreme than this. Yet, to strengthen the state, achieve success, and remain loyal to one's ruler, nothing else can accomplish these ends. Now, a diviner leads the confused and teaches the foolish. Can those who are foolish or confused truly understand with just one sentence! Their words can never be too numerous.

## 15

"Therefore, a qiji cannot be yoked with worn-out donkeys in the same team, and a phoenix will not associate with swallows or sparrows; likewise, virtuous men do not share ranks with the unworthy. Therefore, a gentleman occupies a humble and secluded position to avoid the crowd, conceals himself to escape worldly comparisons. He subtly displays virtue and righteousness to eliminate collective harm, clarifies innate nature, assists superiors in nurturing subordinates, achieves much benefit without seeking honor or reputation. "You are merely babbling fools—how could you understand the way of an elder!"

## 16

Song Zhong and Jia Yi suddenly lost their composure, pale with embarrassment, staring blankly in silence without a word to say. At this point, they gathered up their robes and rose, bowing twice before taking leave. They walked with a dejected gait; upon leaving the gate, they could barely climb into their carriage, bowing over the crossbar of the vehicle and lowering their heads, unable to utter a word.

## 17

Three days later, Song Zhong met Jia Yi outside the palace gate; they led each other to a secluded place and spoke in private, sighing as they said: "The higher one's virtue, the greater the peace; the higher one's power, the greater the danger. To occupy a prominent position is to risk losing oneself every day. If a diviner makes an inaccurate prediction, he does not lose his stipend; but if one advises the ruler poorly and makes mistakes, there is no place for him to stand. The difference between these two is vast—like heaven wearing a hat while earth wears shoes." This is what Laozi referred to as "the formless and nameless, the beginning of all things." Heaven and earth are vast; myriad beings bustle about—some in peace, some in peril, none knowing where they will settle. You and I, how could we possibly foresee those things! They have endured for a long time yet remain ever more at peace; even the righteousness of Zengzi cannot differ from this."

## 18

After some time, Song Zhong was sent as an envoy to the Xiongnu but did not reach his destination and returned early; he was punished for this. Meanwhile, Jia Yi served as the tutor to Prince Liang Huaiwang; when the prince fell from his horse and died, Jia Yi refused to eat out of grief and eventually died from sorrow. This is an example of pursuing superficial splendor while cutting off its roots.

## 19

Grand Historian said: In ancient times, diviners were not recorded because there are many who did not appear in the texts. But by the time of Sima Jizhu, I have taken note and recorded him.

## 20

Chu Xiansheng said: When I was a Lang officer, while touring Chang'an, I saw an outstanding scholar who practiced divination. Observing his daily conduct and manner of walking, how he sat or stood with natural grace, always ensuring his clothes and hat were properly arranged when facing the townspeople—there was indeed the demeanor of a gentleman. When a woman came to consult him about love and marriage, he maintained a solemn expression, never once smiling with his teeth. Since ancient times, the virtuous have avoided worldly affairs; some dwell in secluded places like Wuzhe, others live among people but remain silent, and there are those who retreat into divination to preserve their lives. Sima Jizhu was a virtuous scholar of the State of Chu. He traveled to Chang'an for study, mastered the Yi Jing (Book of Changes), and studied the teachings of Huangdi and Laozi; he had broad knowledge and far-reaching insight. Observing his conversations with two high-ranking officials, he cited the words and deeds of ancient sage kings and sages; clearly, this was not something a man of shallow knowledge or minor skills could achieve. And as for those who gained fame in divination across thousands of miles, they were found everywhere. The Classic says: "Wealth is the highest, followed by rank; once one attains rank, each person learns a single skill to establish their position." Huang Zhi was an official; Chen Junfu, a woman: she became famous throughout the world for her skill in judging horses. Zhang Zhong of Qi and Duke Qucheng gained fame across the land through their mastery of swordsmanship and martial skills. Liu Zhangru became famous for his skill in judging pigs. The Chu family of Yingyang gained fame through their ability to judge cattle. There were many who became famous for their skills, all possessing extraordinary and unmatched qualities—how could they possibly be fully described? Hence it is said: "If the land is not suitable, planting will not bring growth; if one lacks the right mindset, teaching will not succeed." When a family educates its descendants, it should observe what they are naturally inclined toward. If their inclination is to live with integrity and simplicity, one should nurture that tendency accordingly. Hence it is said: "By observing how a man builds his home and names his son, one can judge the character of a scholar; if a son has a proper place in society, he may be called a virtuous person." When I was a Lang officer, I shared an office with the Tai Bu Daizhao who also held the rank of Lang. He said: "During the reign of Emperor Wu of Han, when people gathered to consult diviners about auspicious days, they would ask on which day it would be suitable to take a wife? The Five Elements scholars said it was suitable, the Feng Shui experts said it was not, the Jianchu diviners said it would be inauspicious, the Congchen diviners declared it a great ill omen, the calendar specialists called it slightly ominous, the Heaven and Man scholars said it was slightly auspicious, while the Taiyi school proclaimed it to be highly auspicious. Unable to resolve their dispute, they submitted a report for imperial decision. The emperor's decree stated: 'Avoid all ominous taboos and take the Five Elements as the main guide.' " People should follow the guidance of the Five Elements.
