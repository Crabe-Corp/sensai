# Hereditary House of Wu Taibo

## 1

Wu Taibo and Taibo's younger brother Zhongyong were both sons of Zhou Taiwang, and the elder brothers of Wang Ji Li. Ji Li was virtuous and had a sage son named Chang. Taiwang wanted to establish Ji Li and thus also Chang, so the two brothers Taibo and Zhongyong fled to Jingman, tattooed their bodies, and cut off their hair as a sign that they were no longer fit for use, in order to avoid succession by Ji Li. Ji Li was indeed established as ruler; he became King Ji, and Chang became King Wen. Taibo's flight to Jingman led him to call himself Gouwu. The people of Jingman honored him, and more than a thousand families followed and submitted to him; he was established as Wu Taibo.

## 2

After Taibo died without an heir, his younger brother Zhongyong succeeded him and became known as Wu Zhongyong. Zhongyong died, and his son Ji Jian was established as ruler. Ji Jian died, and his son Shu Da was established as ruler. Shu Da died, and his son Zhou Zhang was established as ruler. At this time, King Wu of Zhou had conquered the Yin dynasty and sought descendants of Taibo and Zhongyong; he found Zhou Zhang. Zhou Zhang was already ruler of Wu, so they therefore enfeoffed him. They then enfeoffed Zhou Zhang's younger brother Yu Zhong at the former Xia site to the north of Zhou; he became known as Yu Zhong and was listed among the feudal lords.

## 3

Zhou Zhang died, and his son Xiong Sui succeeded him; Xiong Sui died, and his son Ke Xiang was established as ruler. Ke Xiang died, and his son Qiangjiuyi was established as ruler. Qiangjiuyi died, and his son Yuqiao Yiwu was established as ruler. Yuqiao Yiwu died, and his son Kelu was established as ruler. Kelu died, and his son Zhourao was established as ruler. Zhourao died, and his son Quyu was established as ruler. Quyu died, and his son Yiwu was established as ruler.  Yiwu died, and his son Qin Chu was established as ruler. Qin Chu died, and his son Zhuan was established as ruler. Zhuan died, and his son Po Gao was established as ruler. Po Gao died, and his son Goubi was established as ruler.  At this time, Duke Xian of Jin destroyed the northern Zhou state of Yu, in order to launch a campaign against Guo. Goubi died, and his son Qu Qi was established as ruler.  Qu Qi died, and his son Shou Meng was established as ruler.  When Shoumeng became ruler, Wu began to grow stronger and declared himself king.

## 4

From the time Taibo founded Wu, five generations passed before King Wu conquered Yin and enfeoffed his descendants in two branches: one was Yu, located within China; the other was Wu, located among the Yi man. Twelve generations later, Jin destroyed Yu in China. Two generations after the fall of Yu in China, Wu among the Yi man rose to prominence. In total, from Taibo to Shoumeng there were nineteen generations.

## 5

In the second year of King Shoumeng's reign, Shen Gong Wuchen, a disgraced official who had fled from Chu, resented General Zi Fan and defected to Jin. From Jin, he was sent to Wu, where he taught them military tactics and chariot warfare, and arranged for his son to serve as an envoy of Wu; thus, Wu first established relations with the Central States. Wu attacked Chu. In the sixteenth year, King Gong of Chu led a campaign against Wu and reached Hengshan.

## 6

In the twenty-fifth year, King Shoumeng died. Shoumeng had four sons: the eldest was Zhufan, followed by Yuzhai, then Yumei, and finally Jizha. Jizha was virtuous, and Shoumeng wanted to establish him as ruler, but Jizha declined the offer. Therefore, they established the eldest son Zhufan as ruler, who assumed authority and managed state affairs.

## 7

In the first year of King Zhufan's reign, after completing his mourning period, he offered the throne to Jizha. Jizha declined, saying: "When Duke Xuan of Cao died, the feudal lords and the people of Cao considered it unjust to have Duke Cao as ruler and intended to establish Zi Zang. However, Zi Zang left the country in order to allow Duke Cao to remain in power; the gentlemen said, 'He was able to uphold his principles.'" "You are rightful in your succession. Who dares to challenge you!" To possess a state is not in accordance with my principles." "Although I may be unworthy, I wish to follow the righteousness of Zi Zang." The people of Wu insisted on establishing Jizha as ruler, but he abandoned his house and took up farming; thus, they gave it up. In autumn, Wu attacked Chu, and the forces of Chu defeated our army. In the fourth year, Duke Ping of Jin was newly established as ruler.

## 8

In the thirteenth year, King Zhufan died. He had given orders to pass the throne to his younger brother Yuzhai, intending to continue in succession until finally transferring the state to Jizha and stopping there, so as to honor the wishes of their late father King Shoumeng, and also to commend Jizha's righteousness. All brothers wished to transfer the state, allowing it to gradually reach Jizha. Jizha was enfeoffed at Yanling; thus he was called Ji Ziyu of Yanling.

## 9

In the third year of King Yuzhai's reign, Qing Feng, the prime minister of Qi, committed a crime and fled from Qi to Wu. Wu granted Qing Feng the county of Zhufang as his fief, married him a daughter, and he became wealthier than when he was in Qi.

## 10

In the fourth year, Wu sent Jizha on an official visit to Lu, where he requested to observe the Zhou musical performances. They sang the poems of Zhou Nan and Zhao Nan. He said: "How beautiful! It marks a good beginning, but it is not yet complete." "Yet there is diligence without resentment." They sang the poems of Bei, Yong, and Wei. He said: "How beautiful! How profound! It expresses sorrow without being overwhelmed by it." "I have heard that the virtue of Duke Kangshu and King Wu was like this. Could these be the poems from Wei?" They sang the poem "Wang." He said: "How beautiful! It expresses thoughtfulness without fear. Could this be from the eastern regions of Zhou?" They sang the poems of Zheng. He said: "It is overly refined; the people can hardly bear it. Could this be why it perished first?" They sang the poems of Qi. He said: "How beautiful! How grand and expansive is this great style!" "The one who demonstrates the vastness of the East Sea—could it be Tai Gong?" "The state's potential cannot yet be measured." They sang the poems of Bin. He said: "How beautiful! How expansive and joyful without excess—could this be from the east under the rule of Duke Zhou?" They sang the poems of Qin. He said: "This is what is called the music of Xia." "To possess the grandeur of Xia is to be great, and this ultimate greatness—could it originate from the ancient traditions of Zhou?" They sang the poems of Wei. He said: "How beautiful! How flowing and harmonious, grand yet broad, modest yet easy to follow. Conducted with virtue as its support—this is the way of a hegemon." They sang the poems of Tang. He said: "How profound in thought! Does it carry the lingering style of the Tao Tang clan?" "If not, how could such sorrow reach so far back?" "Unless it is the descendants of noble virtue, who else could achieve this!" They sang the poems of Chen. He said: "A state without a true ruler—how can it endure long?" From Zhuang onwards, there was no criticism. They sang the Minor Odes (Xiaoya). He said: "How beautiful! Thoughtful yet unwavering, resentful yet silent—does this mark the decline of Zhou virtue?" "There are still remnants of the people from the time of the former kings." They sang the Major Odes (Daya). He said: "How vast and flourishing! Though intricate, it maintains a straightforward structure—could this be the virtue of King Wen?" They sang the Hymns (Song). He said: "How perfect it is! Upright yet not arrogant, intricate yet not convoluted, close yet not oppressive, distant yet not detached. It moves without excess, repeats without tedium, mourns without sorrow, rejoices without indulgence, uses resources without depletion, expands without extravagance, gives freely without waste, takes what it needs without greed, resides in stillness without stagnation, and acts with purpose without drifting." "The five tones are harmonious; the eight winds are balanced. The rhythms have measure, and the order is maintained—this is what great virtue has in common." When he saw the dances of Xiangshao and Nanyue, he said: "How beautiful! They still evoke emotion." When he saw the dance Da Wu, he said: "How beautiful! Could this be how Zhou was at its height?" When he saw the dance Shaohu, he said: "Even a sage's greatness still carries some sense of shame; how difficult it is to be a sage!" When he saw the dance Daya, he said: "How beautiful! Diligent yet without ostentation of virtue!" "If not Yu, who else could match this?" When he saw the dance Zhao Shao, he said: "How complete is this virtue! How great it is—like Heaven's all-encompassing radiance and Earth's boundless capacity. Even with the most exalted virtue, nothing could surpass it." "This is the pinnacle of what I have seen. If there are any other musical performances, I dare not witness them."

## 11

After leaving Lu, he proceeded on his mission to Qi. He advised Yan Pingzhong: "You should quickly return your fief and political power." Without a fief or political authority, you will be free from calamity. The administration of Qi's state affairs is about to shift; until it finds a new direction, the troubles will not cease." Therefore, Yanzi handed over his political power and fief through Chen Huanzi, which is why he escaped the calamity of Luan Gao.

## 12

After leaving Qi, he went on a mission to Zheng. He met Zican and treated him as an old friend. He said to Zican: "The current rulers of Zheng are extravagant; trouble is about to come, and the political affairs will inevitably fall upon you." "When you assume governance, be cautious and follow propriety." Otherwise, the state of Zheng will fall into ruin." After leaving Zheng, he went to Wei. He advised Quyuan, Shigou, Shi, Gongzi Jing, Gongsu Fa, and Gongzi Chao: "Wei has many virtuous men; there is no cause for concern."

"The master has incurred the lord's displeasure and thus remains here. His fear is still insufficient, yet can he now turn to rebellion?" "The master's presence here is like a swallow nesting on a curtain." "How can music be played while the lord is in mourning?" He then left. Wenzi heard of this and never again listened to the qin or se for the rest of his life.

## 14

He went to Jin and advised Zhao Wenzzi, Han Xuanzi, and Wei Xianzi: "The state of Jin will likely be divided among the three families!" As he was about to leave, he said to Shuxiang: "You must strive hard!" "The lord is extravagant yet has many capable men; the high officials are all wealthy. The power of governance will rest with these three families." "You are upright, so you must think carefully about how to free yourself from calamity."

## 15

At the beginning of Ji Zha's first diplomatic mission, he passed northward through Duke Xu. Duke Xu admired Ji Zha's sword but did not dare to speak of it aloud. Ji Zha understood this in his heart, but since he was on a mission to the upper states, he had not yet presented it as a gift. When he returned and arrived at Xu, Duke Xu had already died. Ji Zha then unfastened his precious sword, tied it to a tree by the duke's tomb, and left. His attendant said: "Duke Xu is already dead; who else would you give this to?" Ji Zi replied: "Not so." "At that time, my heart had already promised it to him. How could I betray my own sincerity because he is dead!"

## 16

In the seventh year, the Marquis of Chu Gongzi Wei assassinated his king Jiaao and took the throne in his place; this was King Lingwang. In the tenth year, King Lingwang of Chu convened feudal lords and led a campaign to attack Zhufang in Wu, in order to punish Qi's Qingfeng. Wu also attacked Chu, capturing three fiefs before withdrawing. In the eleventh year, Chu launched an attack on Wu and reached Yulu. In the twelfth year, Chu attacked again, encamping at Qianxi, but the Chu army was defeated and fled.

## 17

In the seventeenth year, King Yuji died, and his younger brother Yumei ascended the throne. In the second year of King Yumei's reign, Gongzi Qiji of Chu assassinated his ruler Lingwang and took the throne.

## 18

In the fourth year, King Yumei died and wished to pass the throne to his younger brother Ji Zha. Ji Zha declined and fled away. At this, the people of Wu said: "The late king had a decree that when an elder brother dies, his younger brother should succeed him and must pass it on to Ji Zha." "Now that Ji Zha has fled from the throne, then King Yumei will be succeeded by his own younger brother." Now he is dead; his son should succeed him." Thus, they enthroned King Yumei's son Liao as king.

## 19

In the second year of Wang Liao's reign, Gongzi Guang launched an attack on Chu but was defeated and lost the royal warship. Gong Guang feared this outcome, so he attacked Chu again, recovered the royal ship, and returned.

## 20

In the fifth year, Wu Zixu, a defected minister of Chu, fled to Wu and was received as a guest by Gongzi Guang. Gongzi Guang was the son of King Zhufan. He often believed that since his father had four brothers, the throne should have passed down to Ji Zha. If Ji Zha did not accept the state, then Guang's father was next in line for succession. "If it does not pass to Ji Zha, I should be enthroned." He secretly recruited virtuous men and planned to assassinate King Liao.

## 21

In the eighth year, Wu sent Gongzi Guang to attack Chu; he defeated the Chu army and brought back from Jucha the mother of Chu's former crown prince Jian. Continuing northward, he defeated the armies of Chen and Cai. In the ninth year, Gongzi Guang attacked Chu and captured Jucha and Zhongli. Initially, a maiden from the border fief of Biliang in Chu quarreled with a woman from Wu's border fief over mulberry trees. The two families became enraged and killed each other. The officials in charge of both states' border fiefs heard about this and grew angry, leading to conflict between them; ultimately, they destroyed the border fief of Wu. King Wu was furious, so he launched an attack on Chu and captured two capitals before withdrawing.

## 22

When Wu Zixu first fled to Wu, he advised King Liao of the benefits of attacking Chu. Gongzi Guang said: "Xu's father and elder brother were humiliated by Chu; he merely wishes to avenge his own grudge." I do not see any benefit in this." At this, Wu Yuan realized that Gong Guang had other intentions and thus sought out the brave warrior Zhuanzhu to present him to Gong Guang. Gong Guang was pleased and thus received Wu Zixu as a guest. Wu Zixu withdrew to till the fields, waiting for Zhuanzhu's mission to unfold.

## 23

In winter of the twelfth year, King Pingwang of Chu died. In spring of the thirteenth year, Wu wished to take advantage of Chu's mourning period and launched an attack. They sent Gongzi Gaiyu and Zhu Yong with troops to besiege Liu and Lu in Chu. They also sent Ji Zha on a mission to Jin to observe the shifting dynamics among the feudal lords. Chu dispatched troops to cut off Wu's rear, and thus the Wu forces could not retreat. At this time, Gongzi Guang of Wu said: "This is an opportunity that cannot be missed." He told Zhuanzhu: "Without seeking, how can one obtain!" "I am the true heir to the throne and should be enthroned. I wish to claim it." "Even if Ji Zha arrives, he will not depose me." Zhuanzhu said: "King Liao can be killed." His mother is old and his son weak, while two of his nobles are leading troops to attack Chu. Chu has cut off their route. At present, Wu is externally trapped by Chu and internally lacks loyal ministers; there's no way they can resist me." Gong Guang said: "My life is your life." On the Bingzi day of the fourth month, Gong Guang concealed armed men in a hidden chamber and paid homage to King Liao for a banquet. King Liao ordered troops to be stationed along the route, from his palace to Gong Guang's residence. At every gate, step, and chamber, these were King Liao's relatives, each holding a dagger at either side. Gongzi Guang pretended to have an injured foot and entered the hidden chamber, instructing Zhuanzhu to place a dagger inside a roasted fish for serving. Zhuanzhu drew the dagger and stabbed King Liao, while the guards clashed their weapons at his chest; thus, King Liao was assassinated. Gongzi Guang finally usurped the throne and became king, known as Wu Wang Helu. Helu then appointed Zhuanzhu's son as an official of high rank.

## 24

When Ji Zha arrived, he said: "If the ancestral rites of our late king are not abandoned, if the people have no ruler who has been deposed, and if the state's sacrifices continue to be honored, then this is my sovereign. How could I dare to resent anyone?" I will mourn the dead and serve the living, awaiting Heaven's decree. lodash It was not I who caused disorder; those who are enthroned must be followed—this is the way of our ancestors." He returned to his post, wept at King Liao's tomb, and resumed his position in waiting. The two Wu nobles Zhu Yong and Gaiyu, whose troops had been besieged by Chu, upon hearing that Gongzi Guang had assassinated King Liao and declared himself king, surrendered their forces to Chu. Chu granted them fiefs in Shu.

## 25

In the first year of King Helu's reign, Wu Zixu was appointed as an envoy and involved in state affairs for counsel. Chu executed Bo Zhouli; his grandson Bo Pi fled to Wu, who appointed him as a high official.

## 26

In the third year, King Helu of Wu, together with Wu Zixuser and Bo Pi, led troops to attack Chu, captured Shu, and killed two Wu defectors who were noble sons. Gong Guang plotted to march into Ying, but General Sun Wu said: "The people are weary; it is not yet the time. Let us wait." In the fourth year, they attacked Chu and captured Liu and Lu. In the fifth year, they attacked Yue and defeated them. In the sixth year, Chu sent Zichang Nangwa to attack Wu. They met and attacked them, defeating the Chu army greatly at Yuzhang, captured Ju Chao of Chu, and then returned.

## 27

In the ninth year, King Helu of Wu asked Wu Zixu and Sun Wu: "Initially you said Ying was not yet ready to be entered; now what is it like?" The two men replied, "Chu's general Zichang is greedy, and Tang and Cai both resent him. If the king wishes to launch a major attack, he must secure the support of Tang and Cai first." Helu followed their advice, mobilized all his troops, allied with Tang and Cai, and marched west to attack Chu as far as the Hanshui River. Chu also sent out troops to resist Wu, forming an army on both sides of the river. King Helu's younger brother Fu wished to fight, but Helu did not agree. Fu said: "The king has already entrusted me with troops; the priority for soldiers is to take advantage of favorable conditions, so what are we waiting for?" So he led his 5,000 men to launch a surprise attack on Chu. The Chu army was heavily defeated and fled. Thus King Wu Helu unleashed his troops in pursuit. By the time they reached Ying, there were five battles, and Chu was defeated five times. Chu King Zhao Wang fled from Ying and ran to Yun. Yun Gong's younger brother wanted to assassinate King Zhao, so King Zhao and Yun Gong fled to Sui. Thus Wu troops entered Ying. Wu Zixu and Bo Pi whipped the corpse of King Ping to avenge their father's grudge.

## 28

In spring of the tenth year, Yue heard that King Wu was in Ying and the state was defenseless, so they attacked Wu. Wu sent a separate force to attack Yue. Chu urgently appealed for help from Qin, who dispatched troops to rescue Chu and attacked Wu; the Wu army was defeated. Fu, King Helu's younger brother, seeing that Qin and Yue had jointly defeated Wu, found the king remaining in Chu without returning. Fu fled back to Wu and declared himself King of Wu. Upon hearing this, Helu led his troops back and attacked Fu. Fu was defeated and fled to Chu. King Zhao of Chu thus managed to re-enter Ying in the ninth month, and granted Fu Gai a fief at Tangxi, establishing the Tangxi clan. In the eleventh year, King Wu sent his crown prince Fuchai to attack Chu and captured Fan. Chu was afraid and left Ying, relocating to Ruo.

## 29

In the fifteenth year, Confucius served as prime minister of Lu.

## 30

In summer of the nineteenth year, Wu attacked Yue; King Goujian of Yue met and fought them at Zuli. Yue sent brave warriors to challenge Wu's forces, advancing in three rows toward the Wu army, shouting loudly before cutting their own throats as a display of defiance. The Wu army watched in awe, and taking advantage of this moment, Yue attacked the Wu forces, defeating them at Gusu. In the battle, King Helu was wounded on his finger, and his troops retreated seven li. King Wu died from his injuries. Before his death, King Helu had the crown prince Fuchai installed as ruler and said to him: "Will you forget that Goujian killed your father?" Fuchai replied: "I dare not!" In the third year, he took revenge on Yue.

## 31

In the first year of King Fuchai's reign, he appointed the official Bo Pi as Taizai (Grand Administrator). He trained in warfare and archery, always determined to avenge Yue. In the second year, King Wu Fuchai led his elite troops to attack Yue and defeated them at Fu Jiao, avenging Gusu. King Goujian of Yue then retreated with 5,000 armed men to Kuaiji and sent the official Zhong through Wu's Taizai Bo Pi to negotiate peace, offering to surrender his state and serve as a subject or concubine. King Fuchai was about to agree, but Wu Zixu advised: "In the past, the Guo clan killed Zhen Guan and attacked Zhen Xun, annihilating Emperor Xiang of Xia. Emperor Xiang's wife Hou Min was pregnant at the time and fled to You Reng, where she gave birth to Shaokang. Shaokang later became the chief herdsman of You Reng. The Guo clan then sought to kill Shaokang, who fled to You Yu. You Yu admired the virtue of Xia and thus married him to two daughters, granting him a fief in Lun with one square li of farmland and a thousand men under his command. Later he gathered the remnants of Xia's people and restored their official positions. He sent men to entice them, eventually destroying the Guo clan, restoring Yu's achievements, and honoring Xia in sacrifices to Heaven, preserving their former legacy. Now Wu is not as strong as the Guo clan was, yet Goujian surpasses Shaokang in strength. If we do not take this opportunity to destroy him now, but instead let him grow stronger, will it not be difficult later?" Moreover, Goujian is a man who can endure hardship. If we do not destroy him now, we will surely regret it later." King Fuchai did not listen to this advice but followed Taizai Bo Pi instead, and finally agreed to peace with Yue. He concluded an alliance and withdrew his troops.

## 32

In the seventh year, King Fuchai of Wu heard that Duke Jing of Qi had died and his ministers were vying for favor while the new ruler was weak. Thus he mobilized troops to launch a northern campaign against Qi. Wu Zixu advised: "King Goujian of Yue eats without double flavors, wears clothes without multiple colors, honors the dead and visits the sick, and is seeking to use his people for a purpose. This man will not die; he must become a future threat to Wu. Now Yue is like an illness in our very heart, yet Your Majesty does not deal with it first and instead focuses on Qi—does this not seem mistaken!" King Fuchai did not heed the advice, so he proceeded to launch a northern attack against Qi and defeated their army at Ailing. Upon reaching Zeng, Wu summoned Duke Ai of Lu and demanded 100 sacrificial offerings. Ji Kangzi sent Zi Gong to persuade Taizai Bo Pi with Zhou dynasty rituals, and thus the demand was stopped. Wu then remained in the southern regions of Qi and Lu, seizing territory. In the ninth year, Wu attacked Lu on behalf of Zou, and only withdrew after concluding an alliance with them. In the tenth year, they returned from a campaign against Qi. In the eleventh year, Wu again launched a northern attack on Qi.

## 33

King Goujian of Yue led his people to pay homage to Wu, offering rich gifts and tributes, which pleased King Fuchai. Only Wu Zixu feared, saying: "This is the abandonment of Wu." He advised: "Yue is like a stone in our heart. Now that they have achieved their ambitions against Qi, it is as useless to us as barren land. Moreover, the edicts of Pangeng warned 'Do not neglect a falling state,' and that was how Shang revived itself." King Fuchai did not hearken to this advice and sent Wu Zixu to Qi. Wu Zixu entrusted his son with the family of Bao in Qi, then returned to report to King Fuchai. King Fuchai heard about this and was greatly enraged; he bestowed on Wu Zixu the sword Shulou, ordering him to commit suicide. As he was about to die, he said: "Plant a zhi tree on my grave so that it may one day be made into utensils. Pluck out my eyes and place them at the eastern gate of Wu, so I can witness Yue's destruction of Wu."

## 34

The Bao clan in Qi assassinated Duke Dao of Qi. King Fuchai heard this and wept outside the military gate for three days, then launched an attack on Qi from the sea. The people of Qi defeated Wu, so King Fuchai withdrew his troops and returned.

## 35

In the thirteenth year, Wu summoned the rulers of Lu and Wei to a conference at Tuogao.

## 36

In spring of the fourteenth year, King Wu convened feudal lords in Huangchi to the north, seeking hegemony over Central China and to preserve the Zhou dynasty. On the Bingzi day of the sixth month (June 24, 482 BC), King Goujian of Yue attacked Wu. On the Yiyou day, 5,000 Yue soldiers fought against Wu. On the Bingxu day, they captured Fuchai's crown prince You. On the Dinghai day, Yue entered Wu. The people of Wu reported their defeat to King Fuchai, who was ashamed that it had become known. Some had leaked the news; in anger, King Fuchai beheaded seven men at his tent. On the Xinchou day of July, King Wu and Duke Ding of Jin vied for precedence. King Fuchai said: "In terms of status in the Zhou court, I am senior." Duke Ding of Jin replied: "Among the Ji clan, I hold a higher rank as an elder lord." Zhao Mang was angry and intended to attack Wu, so he yielded precedence to Duke Ding of Jin. After concluding the alliance with Jin, King Fuchai parted ways from them but wished to attack Song. Taizai Bo Pi said: "We can defeat it, but we cannot hold it." Thus he led his troops back to the country. With their crown prince captured, the state weakened internally; King Fuchai had been away for a long time and his soldiers were exhausted. Therefore, he sent rich gifts to make peace with Yue.

## 37

In the fifteenth year, Tian Chang of Qi killed Duke Jian.

## 38

In the eighteenth year, Yue became even stronger. King Goujian of Yue, leading his troops, again defeated Wu's army at Lize. Chu annihilated Chen.

## 39

In the twentieth year, King Goujian of Yue again attacked Wu. In the twenty-first year, he then besieged Wu. On Dingmao day of the eleventh month in the twenty-third year, Yue defeated Wu. King Goujian of Yue wanted to relocate King Fuchai of Wu to Yongdong and gave him a hundred households to live with. King Fuchai said: "I am old now, and I can no longer serve the King." "I regret not heeding Zixu's advice, which has led me to this predicament myself." He then committed suicide by cutting his throat. King Goujian of Yue destroyed Wu and executed Taizai Bo Pi, considering him disloyal, before returning home.

## 40

Grand Historian said: Confucius remarked, "Taibo can be called the most virtuous. Three times he offered up the world; the people could find no words to praise him." After reading the ancient texts of Spring and Autumn Annals, I came to know that the Chinese Yu was a brother of Jingman Gouwu. The benevolent heart of Ji Zi of Yanling, with his endless admiration for righteousness, could discern clarity and muddiness from the subtlest signs. Alas! How vast was his knowledge and how erudite a gentleman he was!
