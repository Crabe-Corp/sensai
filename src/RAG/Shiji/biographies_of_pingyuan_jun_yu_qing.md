# Biographies of Pingyuan Jun Yu Qing

## 1

Pingyuan Jun Zhao Sheng was a son of the royal family of the State of Zhao. Among his sons, Zhao Sheng was the most virtuous. He enjoyed entertaining guests, and as many as several thousand guests came to visit him. Pingyuan Jun served as prime minister under King Huawen of Zhao and King Xiaocheng of Zhao, leaving the post three times and resuming it three times. He was granted a fief in Dongwucheng.

## 2

The tower of Pingyuan Jun's residence overlooked the homes of common people. Among the commoners' houses was a man with a limp, who walked awkwardly while drawing water. A concubine of Pingyuan Jun lived in the tower and looked down to see him; she laughed at him loudly. The next day, the man with a limp came to Pingyuan Jun's gate and requested an audience, saying: "I have heard that you value scholars. Scholars come from as far away as a thousand li because you honor men of talent while holding your concubines in low esteem. "I, unfortunately, suffer from a crippling illness, and the ladies of your harem looked down on me and laughed at me. I wish to obtain the head of the one who laughed at me." Pingyuan Jun smiled in response and said, "Agreed." After the man with a limp left, Pingyuan Jun laughed and said, "Look at this little fool. He wants to kill my concubine merely because she laughed at him once. Isn't that going too far!" In the end, he did not have her killed. After more than a year had passed, many of his guests and retainers gradually left, more than half of them gone. Pingyuan Jun was puzzled by this and said, "The way I have treated all of you has never been less than courteous. Why then are so many of you leaving?" One retainer from his household stepped forward and replied, "Because you did not kill the concubine who laughed at the man with a limp, they think you value beauty over scholars. That is why the scholars have left." Thus Pingyuan Jun beheaded the concubine who had laughed at the man with a limp. He personally carried her head to present it to the man, and apologized to him. After that, his retainers gradually began returning once more. At this time, Qi had Mengchang Jun, Wei had Xinling Jun, and Chu had Chunshen Jun; therefore, they vied with one another to attract scholars.

## 3

When the Qin forces besieged Handan, Zhao sent Pingyuan Jun to seek help and form an alliance with Chu. He promised to take along twenty retainers who were both brave and capable in both martial and literary skills. Pingyuan Jun said, "If persuasion by words alone can achieve success, then that would be excellent. łożył If words cannot prevail, we will take an oath of blood beneath the eaves of a grand hall and must secure the alliance before returning. We need not look beyond our own retainers; selecting twenty from among them will be sufficient." Nineteen were found, but there was no one else suitable enough to make up the full number of twenty. Among the retainers was a man named Mao Sui, who stepped forward and addressed Pingyuan Jun, saying: "I have heard that you are to form an alliance with Chu and promised to take twenty retainers from among your guests without seeking others outside. Now there is one missing, I hope you will allow me to serve as the twentieth and accompany you on this mission." Pingyuan Jun asked, "For how many years have you been in my service?" Mao Sui replied, "Three years now." Pingyuan Jun said: "A man of talent in the world is like a pointed awl placed inside a pouch; its tip would immediately protrude. "Now, you have been in my service for three years, yet no one has spoken highly of you and I myself have heard nothing about you. This means that you are not remarkable at all." "You lack ability; therefore, you should remain here." Mao Sui said: "I ask only to be placed in the pouch today. If I had been given a chance earlier, I would have already pierced through and shown my ability; it would not merely be the tip that protrudes." Pingyuan Jun finally agreed to take Mao Sui along with him. The nineteen others exchanged glances and smiled at him, but did not openly oppose it.

## 4

When Mao Sui arrived in Chu alongside the other nineteen men, he debated with them, and all nineteen were convinced by his arguments. Pingyuan Jun formed an alliance with Chu, discussing the advantages and disadvantages of the situation. He spoke from dawn until noon without reaching a decision. The nineteen men said to Mao Sui: "Sir, please step forward." Mao Sui gripped his sword and ascended the steps boldly. He said to Pingyuan Jun: "The advantages and disadvantages of forming this alliance can be decided in just two sentences. "You have spoken from dawn until noon, yet still no decision has been made. Why is that?" King of Chu asked Pingyuan Jun: "Who is this guest?" Pingyuan Jun replied, "He is one of my retainers." King of Chu scolded, saying: "Why don't you step down! I am speaking with your lord. Who are you to be here!" Mao Sui pressed his sword forward and said: "Your Majesty's scolding of me is because you rely on the might of your numerous people in Chu. But within ten paces, Your Majesty cannot depend on the multitude of Chu's people; your life is in my hands. My lord stands before you—why do you dare to scold?" Moreover, I have heard that King Tang ruled the world with a territory of only seventy li, and King Wen subdued the feudal lords with land spanning merely one hundred li. Was it because they had numerous soldiers? No—it was because they truly knew how to seize the strategic advantage and unleash their authority. "Now, your kingdom of Chu spans five thousand li in territory and commands a million men armed with halberds—this is the foundation for becoming a hegemon or overlord. With such strength, no other state can match Chu. Bai Qi is but a petty man, yet he led tens of thousands to attack your state. In one battle, he captured Yanyang and Jing; in the second, he burned Yiling; in the third, he shamed Your Majesty's ancestors. This is a grudge that will last for generations and a disgrace to Zhao, yet Your Majesty does not know how to resent it. The purpose of forming this alliance is for the benefit of Chu, not for Zhao. "My lord stands before you—how dare you scold him?" King of Chu said, "Yes, yes. As the wise gentleman has said, I will indeed honor this alliance and offer my state's destiny to it." Mao Sui asked: "Is the alliance settled then?" King of Chu replied, "It is settled." Mao Sui told the attendants of King of Chu: "Bring me the blood of a chicken, a dog, and a horse." Mao Sui held up a bronze tray and knelt before King of Chu, saying: "Your Majesty should first taste the blood to confirm the alliance. Next will be my lord, followed by myself." Thus, the alliance was finalized in the palace hall. Mao Sui took the tray of blood with his left hand and raised his right to call out to the nineteen men, saying: "You all should join me in tasting this blood below the hall. "You are all mediocre indeed—just as I said before, you are people who accomplish things only by relying on others."

## 5

After the alliance was secured and Pingyuan Jun returned to Zhao, upon his arrival he said: "I dare no longer judge men of talent. I have selected as many as a thousand retainers and at least hundreds, believing I had not missed any man of talent in the world. Now it turns out that I failed to recognize Mao Sui among them. With just one visit to Chu, Master Mao made Zhao as esteemed as the Nine Tripods and Da Lü bell. Master Mao's eloquence with his three-inch tongue was stronger than an army of a million soldiers. I dare no longer claim to be able to judge men of talent." Thus, he regarded Mao Sui as his most honored guest.

## 6

After Pingyuan Jun returned to Zhao and the State of Chu sent Chunshen Jun with an army to rescue Zhao, Wei's Xinling Jun also seized command of General Jin Bi's forces by forged orders to go aid Zhao. However, neither force had yet arrived. The Qin forces were pressing the siege of Handan hard; the city was in dire straits and on the verge of surrender. Pingyuan Jun was greatly troubled by this situation. Li Tong, a son of an official in charge of the guesthouse at Handan, advised Pingyuan Jun: "Are you not worried about Zhao's destruction?" Pingyuan Jun replied: "If Zhao falls, I will become a captive. How could I not be worried?" Li Tong said: "The people of Handan are so desperate that they cook bones and exchange children to eat. The situation is dire indeed. Yet your harem numbers in the hundreds, with concubines wearing fine silk and eating rich food, while the common people wear tattered clothes and subsist on scraps and coarse grain. The people are exhausted and their weapons have run out, some even sharpening sticks to serve as spears and arrows, yet your possessions—your musical instruments and ritual bells remain untouched. If Qin conquers Zhao, how can you retain these things?" But if Zhao survives intact, will you not have everything you need?" Now, if you truly order your ladies and all below them to mix among the soldiers, share in their labor, and distribute all household possessions to reward the troops, then when men are at their most desperate and suffering, they will be more than willing to show loyalty." Thus Pingyuan Jun followed his advice and gathered three thousand soldiers who were ready to die for the cause. Li Tong then led the three thousand men toward the Qin army, and the Qin forces withdrew thirty li as a result. At this time, reinforcements from Chu and Wei also arrived, so the Qin troops withdrew, and Handan was saved. Li Tong died in battle; his father was posthumously enfeoffed as Li Hou.

## 7

Yu Qing wanted to recommend Pingyuan Jun for a fief, citing Xinling Jun's role in saving Handan. Gongsun Long heard about this and rode to see Pingyuan Jun at night, saying: "I have heard that Yu Qing wants to recommend you for a fief based on Xinling Jun's rescue of Handan. Is this true?" Pingyuan Jun replied: "Yes." Gongsun Long said, "This is most inappropriate. The king appointed you as prime minister of Zhao not because he believed there were no men of talent or intelligence in the state. The king granted you Dongwucheng as a fief not because he considered you to have earned it through merit, but because there were no other men of distinction in the country. It was given to you simply because you are his relative. You accepted the seal of prime minister without protesting your lack of ability, and took a fief without mentioning that you had no merit—this was because you believed it was due to being related by blood. Now, if Xinling Jun is recommended for a fief because he saved Handan, it would mean that your relative receives a city while the common people are expected to be rewarded based on their merits. This is most unacceptable. Moreover, Yu Qing holds both sides of the matter in his hands. If it succeeds, he will hold the stronger claim and demand repayment; if it fails, he will use a hollow reputation to gain your favor. You must not listen to him." Pingyuan Jun thus did not heed Yu Qing's recommendation.

## 8

In the fifteenth year of King Xiaocheng's reign in Zhao, Pingyuan Jun died. His descendants continued through generations, but eventually perished along with the State of Zhao.

## 9

Pingyuan Jun treated Gongsun Long very well. Gongsun Long was skilled in the argument of "Jian Bai" (the distinction between hardness and whiteness). When Zou Yan passed through Zhao and spoke about ultimate truths, Gongsun Long's influence waned.

## 10

Yu Qing was a traveling orator and statesman. He traveled on foot, carrying his staff and bamboo basket, to persuade King Xiaocheng of Zhao. After one audience, he was granted a hundred ying of gold and a pair of white jade discs; after the second meeting, he became an upper minister of Zhao, hence his title as Yu Qing.

## 11

The Qin and Zhao forces fought at Changping; Zhao did not win and lost one commandant. King Zhao summoned Lou Chang and Yu Qing, saying: "The army has not won the battle, and another commandant has died. I am about to order the soldiers to gather their armor and rush into battle. What do you think?" Lou Chang said, "It will be of no help; it is better to send a distinguished envoy to negotiate peace." Yu Qing said: "Chang's suggestion for negotiation assumes that without negotiation, the army will surely be crushed. But the initiative in negotiating peace lies with Qin." Moreover, Your Majesty's assessment of Qin—do you wish to destroy Zhao's army or not?" The king said: "Qin has spared no effort, and it must certainly intend to crush Zhao's army." Yu Qing said: "If Your Majesty listens to me, you should send envoys with valuable treasures to form alliances with the States of Chu and Wei. If Chu and Wei desire your precious treasures, they will certainly receive our envoys. Once Zhao's envoys enter Chu and Wei, Qin must suspect that the states are uniting in a coalition, and will certainly be alarmed. If this is done, then negotiation can indeed take place." King Zhao did not listen and instead made peace with the Pingyang Jun, sending Zheng Zhu into Qin. Qin received him. King Zhao summoned Yu Qing and said: "I have sent Pingyang Jun to negotiate with Qin, and Qin has already received Zheng Zhu. What do you think of this?" Yu Qing replied: "Your Majesty will not achieve peace; the army is bound to be crushed. All those in the world who celebrate war are on Qin's side. Zheng Zhu is a man of high status; if he enters Qin, King Qin and Lord Ying will certainly treat him with great honor to show the world. Chu and Wei, believing that Zhao has already made peace, will certainly not come to Your Majesty's aid. Once Qin realizes that the world will not come to your rescue, then peace cannot be achieved." As expected, Lord Ying publicly honored Zheng Zhu before those in the world who celebrated Qin's victory and ultimately refused to make peace. After the great defeat at Changping, Qin forces surrounded Handan, becoming a laughingstock of the world.

## 12

After lifting the siege of Handan, Qin allowed King Zhao to pay homage and sent Zhao Hao to make a treaty with Qin, ceding six counties in exchange for peace. Yu Qing said to King Zhao: "When Qin attacked Your Majesty, did they grow weary and return? Or do you think that their strength is still sufficient for further advances, yet they love you and thus refrain from attacking?" The king said: "When Qin attacked me, they spared no effort. It must be that they grew weary and returned." Yu Qing said: "Qin used all their strength to attack a place they could not take, and grew weary before returning. Yet Your Majesty is now giving them what they could not have taken through their own efforts; this is helping Qin destroy itself. Next year, when Qin attacks again, there will be no one to rescue you." The king passed Yu Qing's words on to Zhao Hao. Zhao Hao said: "Can Yu Qing really know the full extent of Qin's strength? If he truly knows where Qin's strength cannot advance, then we should not give them even this small piece of land. If we do so and let Qin attack again next year, will Your Majesty be able to avoid ceding more territory in order to make peace?" The king said: "Then I shall listen and allow you to cede the land. Can you guarantee that Qin will not attack me again next year?" Zhao Hao replied: "This is something I dare not take responsibility for. In the past, the relations between the three Jin states and Qin were friendly. Now that Qin is friendly with Han and Wei yet attacks Your Majesty, it must be because your service to Qin cannot compare to that of Han and Wei. Now, I am for your sake relieving the burden of this attack by opening the gates and exchanging gifts to establish friendly relations with Han and Wei. By next year, Your Majesty will be able to gain benefits from Qin alone; this is why your service to Qin must inevitably come after that of Han and Wei." This is something I cannot take responsibility for."

## 13

The king informed Yu Qing of this. Yu Qing replied: "Zhao Hao said, 'If we do not make peace now, Qin will attack again next year. Will Your Majesty then be able to avoid ceding internal territory in order to negotiate peace?' Now that we are negotiating peace, Hao is still unable to guarantee that Qin will not launch another attack. Even if we cede six cities now, what benefit would it bring! If Qin attacks again next year and we are forced to cede more territory that they could not have captured through their own strength in order to make peace, this is a strategy of self-destruction. It would be better not to negotiate at all. Although Qin is good at attacking, it cannot take six counties; although Zhao may not be able to defend them well, we will ultimately not lose these six cities. Qin grew weary and withdrew; their troops must now be exhausted. If I use these six cities to rally the world's forces against Qin's weary army, then what I lose to the world will be compensated by gains from Qin. Is it not better for our state to gain advantages this way, rather than sit idly and cede territory, thereby weakening ourselves while strengthening Qin?" Now, Hao says 'Qin is friendly with Han and Wei but attacks Zhao because it must believe that Han and Wei will not come to Zhao's aid, thus Your Majesty's army will be isolated. This is because your service to Qin does not compare to that of Han and Hui.' This would mean that Your Majesty has to cede six cities every year to serve Qin, which means sitting idly until all the cities are lost. Next year when Qin again demands territory, will Your Majesty give it to them? If you refuse, this would mean abandoning previous efforts and provoking disaster from Qin; If you give it to them, there will be no more land left to give. The saying goes: 'A strong state is good at attacking; a weak one cannot defend.' Now, if we sit idly and allow Qin to take what it wants, their troops will not be exhausted while they gain more territory; this would strengthen Qin and weaken Zhao. To use a growingly powerful Qin to carve up an increasingly weak Zhao, such a strategy will not stop on its own. Moreover, Your Majesty's territory is limited while Qin's demands are endless; to supply endless demands with limited land will inevitably lead to the complete loss of Zhao."

## 14

King Zhao had not yet decided. Lou Huan arrived from Qin, and King Zhao discussed the matter with him, asking: "Which is more favorable—giving land to Qin or refusing to give it?" Lou Huan evasively replied: "This is not something I can decide." The king said, "Although that may be the case, please state your personal opinion." Lou Huan replied: "Have Your Majesty heard of the mother of Gongfu Wenbo?" Gongfu Wenbo served in Lu, and when he died from illness, two women committed suicide inside the house. His mother heard of this but did not weep. Her house steward said: "How could there be a mother who does not cry when her son dies?" His mother said: "Confucius is a virtuous man, yet he was driven out of Lu, but this person did not follow him. Now that two women have committed suicide over his death, such a person must be disrespectful to elders but overly kind to women." Therefore, from the mother's words, she is a virtuous mother; from the wife's perspective, he must inevitably be seen as an envious wife. Thus, although the words are the same, when spoken by different people, human hearts change accordingly. Now that I have just come from Qin and say not to give land, this would be unwise; to suggest giving it away might make Your Majesty think that I am acting in Qin's interest: therefore, I dare not respond." "If I were allowed to advise His Majesty, it would be better to give the land away." The king said: "Agreed."

## 15

Yu Qing heard of this and went in to see the king, saying: "This is a deceptive argument. Your Majesty must be cautious and do not give it away!" Lou Huan heard about this and went to see the king. The king repeated Yu Qing's words to Lou Huan. Lou Huan replied: "That is not the case." Yu Qing understands one aspect, but not the second. Why is it that when Qin and Zhao are in conflict, all under heaven rejoice? They say: 'We shall take advantage of the strong by preying on the weak.' Now that Zhao's army is trapped by Qin, those in the world who are celebrating a victory must all be on Qin's side. Therefore, it would be better to quickly cede land and make peace, in order to mislead the world and appease Qin's intentions. Otherwise, the states of the world will take advantage of Qin's anger and Zhao's exhaustion to carve up Zhao like a melon. Zhao is about to fall—what can it do against Qin?" Therefore, I say Yu Qing understands one aspect but not the second. I hope Your Majesty will decide based on this and no longer deliberate further."

## 16

Yu Qing heard about it, went to see the king and said: "How dangerous is Lou Zi's advice for Qin! This will only deepen the suspicions of all under heaven, how can it possibly appease Qin's mind? Does he not consider that this would show weakness to the world?" Moreover, when I said 'do not give it,' I did not merely mean to refuse giving it. Qin demands six cities from Your Majesty, and instead of giving them to Qin, the king should offer these six cities as a bribe to Qi. Qi is Qin's deep enemy; if they receive Your Majesty's six cities, they will unite their forces and strike westward against Qin. Qi would certainly listen to the king without waiting for all the words to be spoken. In this way, Your Majesty loses territory to Qi but gains compensation from Qin. Moreover, the deep grudge between Qi and Zhao can thus be avenged, and this would demonstrate to all under heaven that we are capable of action. If Your Majesty acts on this initiative, before the enemy army even approaches our borders, I foresee that Qin will send heavy bribes to Zhao and instead conclude a peace with Your Majesty. If we make peace with Qin, the states of Han and Wei hearing about it will certainly regard Your Majesty as highly respected; Respecting the king, they must present valuable treasures to take precedence over the king. Thus, with one move Your Majesty would secure the alliance of three states and change the course of relations with Qin." King Zhao said: "Well spoken." Therefore, he sent Yu Qing east to see King Qi and plotted against Qin with him. Before Yu Qing returned, the envoys of Qin had already arrived in Zhao. Lóu Huǎn heard about it and fled away. Therefore, Zhao granted Yu Qing a city as a fief.

## 17

After a short while, Wei requested to join the alliance. King Xiaocheng of Zhao summoned Yu Qing for consultation. Passing by Pingyuan Jun, Pingyuan Jun said: "I hope you will discuss the alliance." Yu Qing entered to see the king. The king said: "Wei has requested to join our alliance." Yu Qing replied, "Wei is mistaken." The king said: "I have not yet agreed to it." Yu Qing replied, "Your Majesty is mistaken." The king said: "Wei has requested to join the alliance, and you say Wei is mistaken. I have not yet agreed, but you also say that I am mistaken. Then does this mean the alliance can never be formed?" Yu Qing replied: "I have heard that when a small state engages in an alliance with a great power, if there is benefit, the great power reaps its rewards; if there is failure, it is the small state that suffers the consequences. Now Wei, a small state, offers to bear its misfortune, yet Your Majesty, as the great power, refuses to accept its benefits. Therefore, I said that Your Majesty is mistaken and so is Wei." I secretly believe it would be advantageous to form an alliance." The king said: "Well spoken." Thus, he formed an alliance with Wei.

## 18

Because of the matter involving Wei Qi, Yu Qing did not value the seals of high-ranking officials and marquises worth ten thousand households; instead, he traveled incognito with Wei Qi, eventually leaving Zhao and finding himself in hardship in Liang. After Wei Qi's death, Yu Qing, feeling discontented, composed a book. Drawing from the Spring and Autumn Annals above and observing recent times below, he wrote eight chapters titled "Jieyi" (Moral Integrity), "Chenghao" (Titles and Honors), "Chuaimo" (Estimation and Deliberation), and "Zhengmou" (Political Strategies). He used it to criticize the gains and losses of state affairs, and this work was passed down through generations as the "Yu Shi Chun Qiu" (Spring and Autumn Annals of Yu).

## 19

Grand Historian said: Pingyuan Jun was a graceful and refined nobleman in an impure age, yet he failed to grasp the broader picture. A vulgar saying goes, "Profit makes the wise man foolish." Pingyuan Jun was seduced by Feng Ting's fallacious arguments and caused Zhao to suffer a disastrous defeat at Changping, where over 400,000 troops were lost, nearly leading to the fall of Handan. Yu Qing was able to foresee events and accurately assess situations; his strategies for Zhao were truly masterful! Yet, because he could not bear to abandon Wei Qi, he eventually found himself trapped in Daliang. Even an ordinary man would know this was unwise—how much more so for a wise person? Nevertheless, it was not until Yu Qing faced hardship and distress that he could compose his writings to reveal himself to future generations.
