# Biographies of the Marquis of Huaiyin

## 1

The Marquis of Huaiyin, Han Xin, was a native of Huaiyin. When he was first an ordinary commoner, Han Xin was poor and had no reputation; thus, he could not be recommended or selected for a government post. He also lacked the ability to manage his livelihood through commerce or trade. He often relied on others for food and drink, which many people found annoying. He frequently stayed with Nanchang Tingzhang, the head of the village in his hometown, who provided him meals. After several months, however, Tingzhang's wife became troubled by this situation and began cooking early in the morning so that she could eat before Han Xin arrived for his meal. When it was time to eat, Han Xin came as usual, but no food was prepared for him. Han Xin realized her intention and became angry, so he left in anger forever.

## 2

While Han Xin was fishing beneath the city walls, some elderly women were washing cloth nearby. One of them saw that Han Xin was hungry and gave him food to eat; she continued feeding him for several dozen days while doing her washing. Han Xin was pleased and said to the washerwoman, "I will surely find a way to greatly repay you, Mother." The woman became angry and said, "A grown man is unable to support himself. I felt pity for you, young master, and gave you food; how could I possibly expect repayment?"

## 3

There were some youths in the butcher's quarter of Huaiyin who insulted Han Xin, saying, "Although you are tall and strong and like to carry a sword and dagger, your heart is actually timid." They publicly humiliated him by saying, "If Han Xin dares to die, he would stab me; if he does not dare to die, then crawl under my crotch." Thus Han Xin looked at him carefully, bent down and crawled beneath the youth's crotch, lying prostrate on his stomach. All the people in the market laughed at Han Xin, considering him timid.

## 4

When Xiang Liang crossed the Huai River, Han Xin took up his sword and followed him. He served under Xiang's command but remained unknown and unremarkable. After Xiang Liang was defeated, Han Xin then joined Xiang Yu, who appointed him as a Langzhong (attendant-official). Han Xin repeatedly submitted strategies to Xiang Yu, but Xiang Yu did not employ them. When King Han entered Shu, Han Xin defected from the Chu state to join the Han forces but was still unknown and served as a Lianao (a low-ranking official). He was sentenced to death for a legal offense. Thirteen of his fellow prisoners had already been executed, and it was now Han Xin's turn. Looking up, he happened to see Teng Gong and said, "Does His Majesty not wish to attain the empire?" "Why is a man of valor being executed!" Teng Gong was surprised by his words and impressed by his appearance, so he released him without executing him. After conversing with him, Teng Gong greatly admired him. Teng Gong recommended him to the King, who then appointed Han Xin as Zhisu Duyi (an official in charge of grain supplies). However, the King did not yet regard him as extraordinary.

## 5

Han Xin often conversed with Xiao He, who found him remarkable. When they arrived at Nanzheng, dozens of generals had already fled along the way. Han Xin estimated that Xiao He and others had repeatedly recommended him to the King but were ignored; thus, he also fled. Xiao He heard of Han Xin's escape before it could be reported to the King and immediately set out in pursuit without delay. Someone informed the King, saying, "The Chancellor Xiao He has fled." The King was furious and felt as if he had lost his right and left hands. After one or two days, Xiao He came to pay homage to the King. The King was both angry and delighted, scolding Xiao He by saying, "Why did you run away? What were you thinking?" Xiao He replied, "I would not dare to flee. I was chasing after someone who had escaped." The King asked, "Who exactly are you pursuing?" He replied, "Han Xin." The King scolded again, saying, "Numerous generals have fled, dozens of them; yet you did not pursue any of them. You are chasing Han Xin—this is a deception." Xiao He said, "Common generals are easy to find. As for someone like Han Xin, he is a national treasure without equal. If Your Majesty wishes merely to remain the King of Hanzhong forever, then there is no need for Han Xin; but if you wish to contend for the empire, there will be none with whom you can plan and discuss affairs except Han Xin. It all depends on Your Majesty's decision." The King said, "I also wish to move eastward. How could I remain confined here for long?" Xiao He said, "If Your Majesty is determined to go east and can employ Han Xin, then he will stay; if you cannot use him, Han Xin will eventually flee forever." The King said, "I shall appoint him as a general for your sake." _l Xiao He replied, "Even if he is made a general, Han Xin will certainly not stay." The King said, "Then appoint him as the supreme general." Xiao He replied, "That would be most fortunate." Thus the King wished to summon Han Xin and bestow the title upon him. Xiao He said, "Your Majesty has always been arrogant and disrespectful. Now you are bestowing the title of supreme general as if calling a child—it is precisely for this reason that Han Xin left." "If Your Majesty truly wishes to appoint him, you must choose a favorable day, observe purification rites, set up an altar and ceremony, and prepare proper rituals; only then will it be acceptable." The King agreed. All the generals were delighted, each thinking that he himself would be appointed as supreme general. When the time came to bestow the title of supreme general, it was Han Xin who received it; the entire army was astonished.

## 6

After completing his formal bow in acceptance, Han Xin took his seat above the King. The King said, "The Chancellor has spoken highly of you many times, General. What strategies do you have to advise me?" Han Xin bowed in thanks and then asked the King, "Now that we are moving eastward to contend for supreme power, is it not against Xiang Wang?" King Han replied, "Indeed." Han Xin continued, "Does Your Majesty consider himself braver and stronger in benevolence than Xiang Wang?" King Han remained silent for a long time before replying, "I am not as good." Han Xin bowed twice in congratulation and said, "Even I believe that Your Majesty is not as capable as Xiang Wang. However, I have served under him before; please allow me to speak of his character." Xiang Wang is fierce and loud, capable of terrifying a thousand men into submission. Yet he cannot entrust or delegate responsibilities to able generals; this is merely the courage of an ordinary man. Xiang Wang appears respectful and kind to others, speaks gently and kindly. When someone is ill, he sheds tears and shares food and drink with them; yet when it comes to rewarding those who have rendered service and deserve enfeoffment, he holds onto the seal so tightly that its edges become worn out, unable to bear parting with it—this is what is called the mercy of a woman. Although Xiang Wang has dominated the empire and made the feudal lords his subjects, he does not reside in Guanzhong but instead establishes his capital at Pengcheng. He has violated the agreement made with Emperor Yi and enfeoffed those close to him as kings, which has caused dissatisfaction among the feudal lords. The feudal lords have seen Xiang Wang expel and drive Emperor Yi to the south of the Yangtze River; thus, they too have driven out their own overlords and declared themselves kings in fertile lands. Wherever Xiang Wang goes, he leaves nothing but destruction and devastation; thus the people of the world harbor resentment toward him. The common folk do not truly support or follow him—they are merely coerced by his power. Though he is nominally a hegemon, in reality, he has lost the hearts of the people across the land. Therefore it can be said that his strength is easily turned into weakness. Now, if Your Majesty sincerely follows the opposite approach—entrusting the brave and martial men of the world—what enemy cannot be vanquished! Rewarding meritorious subjects with fiefs from cities across the empire—what force will not submit! Marching righteous troops to follow those who long for returning eastward—what resistance cannot be dispersed! Moreover, the Three Qin kings have served as generals for the state of Qin and led Qin's sons and brothers in warfare for several years. The number of people they killed or caused to die is beyond count. They also deceived their own troops into surrendering to the feudal lords. When these surrendered soldiers arrived at Xin'an, Xiang Wang deceitfully buried more than 200,000 Qin captives alive. Only Han Shang, Xin Heng, and Yi managed to escape. The fathers and elder brothers of the Qin people harbor deep resentment toward these three men, with their hatred piercing to the bone marrow. Now that Chu has used its power to enfeoff these three as kings, the people of Qin love none of them. When Your Majesty entered Wu Pass, not a single hair on the people was harmed. You abolished Qin's harsh laws and made an agreement with the people of Qin, establishing only three legal provisions. The people of Qin have no one who does not wish for you to rule over them as their king. According to the agreement among the feudal lords, Your Majesty should be made King of Guanzhong, and this is well known by all the people there. Yet when Your Majesty lost your rightful position and entered Hanzhong instead, not a single Qin person did not feel resentment toward this. Now if Your Majesty raises an army to move eastward, the Three Qins can be pacified merely by sending proclamations." At this, King Han was greatly pleased and considered himself fortunate for having obtained Han Xin later than he should have. He then followed Han Xin's strategy and assigned the various generals their targets to attack.

## 7

In August, King Han raised his army and marched eastward from Chen Cang, proceeding to pacify the Three Qins. In the second year of King Han's reign, he marched out of Guanzhong and captured Wei and Henan; the kings of Yan and Yin all surrendered. He allied with Qi and Zhao to jointly attack Chu. In April, he reached Pengcheng, but the Han forces were defeated and scattered, retreating in disarray. Han Xin then regrouped his forces and rejoined King Han at Yingyang, where they once again defeated the Chu army between Jing and Suo. Because of this, the Chu troops were ultimately unable to advance westward.

## 8

After King Han's defeat and retreat from Pengcheng, the King of Sai, Xin Heng, and the King of Zhai, Yi, defected to Chu. Qi and Zhao also turned against Han and made peace with Chu. In June, the King of Wei, Pao, paid a visit to his homeland under the pretense of seeing to his family's illness. Upon reaching his state, he immediately closed the river passes and rebelled against Han, making an agreement with Chu for peace. King Han sent Li Sheng to persuade King Pao, but failed to bring him over. In the eighth month, he appointed Han Xin as Left Chancellor to attack Wei. The King of Wei massed his troops at Puzhan and blocked Linjin. Han Xin therefore created additional decoy forces, arranging boats as if he intended to cross at Linjin, while secretly sending hidden troops from Xiayang in wooden jars and pots across the river to launch a surprise attack on Anyi. The King of Wei, Pao was startled and led his troops out to meet Han Xin, who then captured him. Thus Wei was pacified and established as Hedong Commandery. King Han dispatched Zhang Er to accompany Han Xin, leading troops eastward and northward to attack Zhao and Dai. In the following September, they defeated the forces of Dai and captured Xia Yueyu. Whenever Han Xin subdued Wei or broke Dai, King Han immediately sent envoys to recruit their elite troops and dispatched them to Yingyang to resist Chu.

## 9

Han Xin and Zhang Er led several ten thousand soldiers, intending to move eastward through Jingxing Pass to attack Zhao. King Zhao and Cheng'an Jun Chen Yu heard that Han was about to launch a surprise attack, so they gathered their troops at the mouth of Jingxing Pass, claiming a force of 200,00 soldiers. Guangwu Jun Li Zuocar advised Cheng'an Jun, saying, "I have heard that the Han general Han Xin crossed the Xihe River, captured the King of Wei, and took prisoner Xia Yueyu. Recently he has just seen bloodshed at Yuyu. Now he is supported by Zhang Er, and they are planning to attack Zhao. This is a case of riding on victory while fighting far from their homeland; such momentum cannot be withstood. I have heard that when provisions are transported over a thousand li, the soldiers inevitably show signs of hunger; and when firewood is gathered after cooking, the army cannot eat until late. Now, along the road through Jingxing Pass, wagons cannot travel side by side and cavalry cannot form ranks. Marching hundreds of li, their supplies must be trailing behind them. I respectfully request that you entrust me with 30,000 elite troops to take a secret route and cut off their supply lines; you should dig deep trenches and build high fortifications, maintaining a strong camp without engaging in battle. They will be unable to fight ahead and cannot retreat. My hidden forces will cut off their rear, leaving them nothing to plunder in the fields. Within ten days at most, we can bring the heads of these two generals before our camp. I hope you will give careful consideration to my strategy. No, they are certainly going to be captured by these two men." Cheng'an Jun was a Confucian scholar, often claiming that righteous armies did not employ deception or unconventional strategies. He said, "I have heard in the art of war that if you outnumber your enemy ten to one, you surround them; and if you double their numbers, you fight." Now Han Xin's forces are claimed to number in the tens of thousands, but in reality they do not exceed several thousand. They have marched hundreds of li to attack us and must already be exhausted. If we now avoid them without fighting, how can we face a greater threat in the future! Then the feudal lords will think us timid and will lightly come to attack us." He did not heed Guangwu Jun's strategy, so Guangwu Jun's plan was not put into action.

## 10

Han Xin sent someone to secretly observe and learned that the strategy had not been adopted. When he returned with this report, Han Xin was greatly pleased and then dared to lead his troops down accordingly. Before reaching thirty li from the mouth of Jingxing Pass, they stopped for the night. At midnight, they secretly set out and selected 2,000 light cavalrymen. Each soldier carried a red banner and took a secret route through the mountain to observe Zhao's army. They were warned: "When Zhao sees us retreating, he will certainly empty his camp to pursue us. If you quickly enter Zhao's camp, remove their banners, and raise our Han red banners." He ordered his subordinate generals to prepare food, saying: "We will break the Zhao army today and have a meal together!" The generals all did not believe it but pretended to respond with, "Yes, as you say." He told the military officers, "Zhao has already occupied a favorable position and built fortifications. Moreover, since they have not yet seen our general's banners and drums, they will not dare to attack us head-on. I fear that when we reach an obstructed or dangerous area, they may retreat." Han Xin then sent 10,000 men ahead to deploy in formation with their backs to the river. Zhao's army saw this and laughed heartily. At dawn, Han Xin raised the banners and drums of a general and marched out through Jingxing Pass. Zhao opened his camp to attack him, and they engaged in fierce battle for a long time. At this point, Han Xin and Zhang Er pretended to abandon their banners and drums and fled toward the army by the river. The troops at the riverside opened ranks to let them in, and then they engaged in battle once more with great intensity. As expected, Zhao's forces emptied their camp to chase after Han Xin and Zhang Er for the Han banners and drums. Han Xin and Zhang Er had already entered the riverside camp, where all the soldiers fought with a determination to die or win; they could not be defeated. The 2,000 cavalrymen Han Xin had sent out as hidden forces waited for Zhao's camp to be empty and its troops chasing profit. Then they galloped into Zhao's camp and removed all of Zhao's banners, replacing them with 2,000 red Han banners. Zhao's army, now unable to defeat Han Xin and having failed to capture him or Zhang Er, wanted to return to their camp. However, they found all the banners in the camp had been replaced with red Han flags, which greatly alarmed them. They believed that the Han forces had already captured King Zhao and his generals. The troops then fell into chaos and fled; even though Zhao's commanders tried to kill some of them, they could not stop the rout. Thus, Han forces launched a pincer attack and completely defeated Zhao's army, capturing it in full. They beheaded Cheng'an Jun at the Shui River and captured King Zhao Xie.

## 11

Han Xin then ordered his troops not to kill Guangwu Jun, offering a reward of 1,000 gold pieces for anyone who could capture him alive. Soon, someone brought Guangwu Jun bound and delivered to Han Xin's camp. Han Xin then untied him, seated him facing east while he himself sat facing west, treating him as a teacher. The generals, having beheaded prisoners of war and completed their celebrations, asked Han Xin: "According to the art of war, one should have mountains or hills on the right rear and water or marshes on the left front. Now you ordered us to deploy with our backs to the river, saying we would break Zhao's army and eat together. We were 不服 (unconvinced)." Yet in the end we won—what strategy was this?" Han Xin said, "This is actually stated in the art of war; it's just that you gentlemen did not notice." "Doesn't the art of war say, 'Place them in a deadly situation and they will survive; put them in a place of certain death and they will find life'?" "Moreover, I do not have the advantage of long-term training and discipline over my soldiers. This is what is called 'driving common people into battle.' The only way to ensure victory in such a situation is to place them in a desperate position so that each man fights for his own survival; if I had given them an escape route, they would have all fled. Could such men still be used in battle?" The generals were all convinced and said, "Well said." "It is beyond our capability," they added.

## 12

Han Xin then asked Guangwu Jun, "I wish to attack Yan in the north and strike Qi in the east. What strategy should I adopt to achieve success?" Guangwu Jun politely declined, saying, "I have heard that a defeated general is not qualified to speak of bravery, and an official from a fallen state cannot plan for survival. Now I am but a captured prisoner who has been defeated; how can I possibly be entrusted with plans of great importance!" Han Xin said, "I have heard that Bai Li Xi was in Yu when Yu fell, and later served Qin when it became dominant. It is not because he was foolish in Yu but wise in Qin; rather, it depends on whether his advice was used or ignored." If Cheng'an Jun had truly listened to your strategy, a man like me would have already been captured. It is precisely because he did not listen to you that I am now able to serve." He then insisted and asked, "I sincerely entrust my heart to your counsel. I hope you will not refuse." Guangwu Jun said, "I have heard that even the wisest person may make a mistake in a thousand considerations; a foolish man who considers something a thousand times will surely gain one insight. Therefore, it is said: 'The words of a madman may contain wisdom for the sage to choose from.' I only fear that my strategy might not be useful. I will, however, offer you this humble loyalty." Cheng'an Jun had a strategy of victory in every battle, yet he lost it all at once. His army was defeated under Hao, and his body perished on the Shidi River. Now, General, you crossed the Xihe River and captured King Wei. You took prisoner Xia Yue at Yuyu, swiftly advanced through Jingxing Pass in one campaign, defeated Zhao's 200,000 troops before morning, and executed Cheng'an Jun. Your name is known throughout the land, your might shakes the world; even farmers have stopped plowing and laid down their hoes, wearing fine clothes and enjoying good food, all listening eagerly for your command. Such is what you are best at, General. However, your soldiers are exhausted and worn out; in reality, they are difficult to use effectively. Now, if you wish to deploy tired and worn-out troops against the strong walls of Yan, a prolonged battle may drain your strength without success. Your situation will become exposed and vulnerable; as days pass and supplies run out, even weak Yan might refuse to submit, while Qi will surely resist at its borders to strengthen itself. If Yan and Qi remain in a stalemate without resolution, then the balance of power between Liu Bang and Xiang Yu will not yet be decided. This is what you lack, General. In my humble opinion, I secretly believe this strategy has gone too far. Therefore, a skilled general does not use his weaknesses to attack an enemy's strengths, but rather uses his own strengths to strike the enemy's weaknesses." Han Xin asked, "Then what is the way forward?" Guangwu Jun replied, "Now that I am considering the best course for you, General, there is no better option than to halt your troops and rest your soldiers. Stabilize Zhao by showing care for its people and their orphans. Within a hundred li, daily offerings of cattle and wine should be made to entertain the officers and soldiers, lifting their spirits. Then, with your army facing north toward Yan's territory, send eloquent envoys carrying short letters to Yan, highlighting your strengths. Yan will surely dare not refuse to obey. Once Yan has submitted, have persuasive envoys spread the news eastward to Qi. Qi will then follow suit and submit without resistance; even if there are wise men, they will be at a loss as to how to advise Qi. If this is done, the affairs of all under heaven can then be planned and achieved. "Indeed, there are cases in warfare where one first establishes a reputation before engaging in action—this is precisely what I mean." Han Xin said, "Well said." He followed his strategy and sent envoys to Yan. Yan submitted as if the wind had blown it down. Han Xin then dispatched an envoy to report back to Han, and at the same time requested that Zhang Er be appointed King of Zhao in order to stabilize and pacify the state. The King of Han agreed, and so Zhang Er was enfeoffed as the King of Zhao.

## 13

Chu repeatedly sent elite forces to cross the river and attack Zhao. King Zhao Er and Han Xin moved back and forth to rescue Zhao, using these campaigns to pacify Zhao's cities and towns, and raised troops to send to Han. At this time, Chu was urgently besieging King Han at Xingyang. King Han broke out to the south and moved toward Wan and Ye, where he met Liu Poxi (Qingbu), then fled into Chenggao. Once again, Chu launched a fierce siege against him. In the sixth month, King Han left Chenggao and crossed eastward over the Yellow River, traveling alone with Peng Gong, and joined Zhang Er's forces at Xiuwu. Upon arriving, he stayed in a relay inn for the night. The next morning, he falsely claimed to be a Han envoy and galloped into Zhao's encampment. Zhang Er and Han Xin had not yet risen, so he seized their seals and insignias from within their sleeping quarters. He then summoned the generals under his command, replacing them with his own appointees. When Zhang Er and Han Xin awoke, they learned that King Han had arrived and were greatly startled. King Han seized the command of their armies and immediately ordered Zhang Er to prepare for the defense of Zhao's territory. He appointed Han Xin as Chancellor, mobilizing the remaining Sui forces not yet dispatched to attack Qi.

## 14

Han Xin led his troops eastward, but before crossing the Pingyuan region, he heard that King Han had sent Li Shiqi to persuade Qi into submission. Han Xin wanted to halt his advance. Fan Yang's eloquent man Kuaitong advised Han Xin, saying: "General, you were ordered to attack Qi, yet the Han court sent a secret envoy alone to subdue Qi. Could there possibly be an imperial decree ordering you to stop?" How can you afford not to proceed! Moreover, Li Sheng is but a single scholar who, by merely leaning on the carriage and moving his three-inch tongue, has subdued more than seventy cities of Qi. You, General, with tens of thousands of troops, took over a year to conquer only fifty or so cities in Zhao. Having served as general for several years, are you now less effective than this single upright Confucian scholar?" Thus convinced by these words, Han Xin followed his advice and crossed the river. Qi had already accepted Li Sheng's terms and was now staying behind to indulge in drinking, relaxing its defenses against Han forces. Han Xin then launched a surprise attack on the Qi army at Lixia and advanced all the way to Linzi. King Tian Guang of Qi, believing that Li Sheng had betrayed him, boiled him alive and fled to Gaomi, sending envoys to Chu for rescue. After pacifying Linzi, Han Xin advanced eastward in pursuit of Tian Guang and reached the western side of Gaomi. Chu also sent Long Ju as general, claiming a force of 20,000 soldiers, to rescue Qi.

## 15

King Tian Guang and General Long Ju combined their forces to fight Han Xin, but the battle had not yet begun. Someone advised Long Ju, saying: "The Han army is fighting far from home and in desperation; their momentum cannot be resisted. The armies of Qi and Chu are defending their own territory, where it is easier for them to retreat or scatter. It would be better to dig in deeply and have King Qi send trusted envoys to reclaim the lost cities. When these lost cities hear that their king is still alive and that Chu has come to rescue them, they will surely turn back from Han. "The Han army is stationed 2000 li away as an invading force, and all the cities of Qi will turn against them. In such a situation, they will find no food to sustain themselves and can be made to surrender without even fighting." Long Ju said, "I have always known Han Xin's character; he is easy to deal with. Moreover, if we rescue Qi without fighting and merely make them surrender, what merit would I have?" "Now, if we fight and win, we can capture half of Qi. Why should we stop!" So they engaged in battle, with Han Xin and Long Ju arraying their forces on opposite sides of the Wei River. Han Xin then ordered men at night to prepare more than 10,000 sandbags filled with sand, which were used to block the upper reaches of the river. He led part of his army across the river and attacked Long Ju, pretending to be defeated and retreating. Long Ju was delighted and said, "I knew all along that Han Xin is a coward." He then pursued Han Xin across the river. Han Xin sent men to release the sandbags, and a great surge of water followed. Long Ju's army was unable to cross in large numbers when suddenly attacked, and Long Ju was killed. The forces of Long Ju on the eastern side of the river scattered and fled, and King Tian Guang escaped as well. Han Xin then pursued the retreating enemy to Chengyang, capturing all the Chu soldiers he encountered.

## 16

In the fourth year of Han (204 BC), he finally pacified and brought peace to Qi. He sent a messenger to King Han, saying: "Qi is a land of deceit and frequent changes; it is an unpredictable state. It borders Chu in the south. Without appointing a provisional king to pacify it, the situation will remain unstable. I request that I be appointed as the provisional King of Qi for convenience." At that time, Chu was urgently besieging King Han at Yingyang. When the envoy from Han Xin arrived and delivered his letter, King Han became furious upon reading it and scolded, saying: "I am trapped here, waiting day and night for you to come assist me, yet now you wish to proclaim yourself king!" Zhang Liang and Chen Ping stepped on King Han's foot and whispered in his ear, saying: "With the current unfavorable situation for Han, how can we possibly prevent Han Xin from becoming king? It would be better to take advantage of this and appoint him as such, treating him well so that he will defend the territory for us." Otherwise, trouble may arise." King Han also realized this and, in a fit of anger, said again: "A great man who pacifies the feudal lords should be made an actual king, not just a provisional one!" He then sent Zhang Liang to formally appoint Han Xin as King of Qi and summoned his troops to fight against Chu.

## 17

After losing Long Ju, King Xiang of Chu became fearful and sent Xu Yi native Wu She to persuade King Qi Han Xin, saying: "The people throughout the land have long suffered under Qin's rule; we all joined forces to attack it. Now that Qin has been defeated, we calculated contributions and divided the land, granting territories so that each could be a king in order to give rest to our soldiers. Now, King Han has raised his army again and marched eastward, encroaching on others' territories and seizing their lands. After defeating the Three Qins, he led his troops out of the Guan Pass, gathering the forces of feudal lords to strike east against Chu. His ambition is not to stop until he swallows up the entire world; how insatiable he is! Moreover, King Han cannot be trusted. He has been captured by King Xiang on multiple occasions and was in his power; out of mercy, King Xiang spared his life. Yet as soon as he escaped, he betrayed the agreement and attacked King Xiang again. How untrustworthy is he! Now, although you may consider yourself to have a close relationship with King Han and are exerting your efforts in fighting for him, ultimately you will still be captured by him. The reason why you have survived until now is because King Xiang still lives. At present, the matter between these two kings rests in your hands. If you support King Han, he will win; if you support King Xiang, he will prevail. Once King Xiang is defeated today, the next target will be you. You have an old relationship with King Xiang; why not rebel against Han and form an alliance with Chu, dividing the empire into three parts to rule together?" If you let this opportunity pass and bind yourself to Han in order to fight against Chu, can a wise man really be so inflexible!" Han Xin replied respectfully, "I served King Xiang before; my position was no higher than that of a Langzhong, and I held the rank of merely a spear-bearer. My words were not heeded, and my strategies were never used. That is why I defected from Chu to Han. King Han gave me the seal of a supreme general, assigned me tens of thousands of troops, shared his clothes with me when I had none and provided food for me. He listened to my words and adopted my strategies; thus, I have been able to reach this position. A ruler who treats one with such deep trust and affection—how could it be auspicious for me to betray him? Even if I were to die, I would not change my loyalty. Please convey my apologies to King Xiang!"

## 18

After Wu She had left, a native of Qi named Kuai Tong realized that the fate of the entire world rested in Han Xin's hands. He wished to devise an extraordinary strategy to move him and thus advised Han Xin by claiming to be skilled in physiognomy: "I have once studied the art of reading people's faces." Han Xin asked, "What is your skill at judging people like?" He replied: "Whether a person is noble or lowly depends on the structure of their bones; whether they are worried or joyful can be seen in their facial expressions; and whether they will succeed or fail lies in their decisiveness. Judging by these three factors, I am never wrong once in ten thousand cases." Han Xin said, "Well said. What do you see in me?" He replied: "Please allow a moment of privacy." Han Xin said, "Let the attendants step away." Kuai Tong said: "Judging by your face alone, you will be no more than a marquis, yet even that position would be precarious and unstable. But judging from the back, your destiny is so noble it cannot even be described in words." Han Xin asked: "What do you mean by that?" Kuai Tong said: "When the turmoil first erupted across the land, outstanding heroes and valiant men raised their banners with a single call. Scholars from all over gathered like clouds and fog, assembling in great numbers, coming together like fish scales or flames rising on the wind. At that time, their only concern was to destroy Qin. Now, with the struggle between Chu and Han, innocent people across the land are shedding their blood on the ground; fathers and sons lie exposed in the fields, their bones uncountable. The Chu forces rose from Pengcheng, chasing defeated enemies and advancing as far as Yingyang, sweeping forward with momentum and shaking the entire world with their power. Yet now they are trapped between Jing and Suo, pressed against the western mountains and unable to advance—this has been going on for three years. King Han commands an army of hundreds of thousands, yet he is held at a distance from Gong and Luo, blocked by the natural dangers of mountains and rivers. He fights several battles in a single day but achieves no gains; his defeats at Yingyang and injuries at Chenggao have forced him to flee between Wan and Ye. This is what one might call a situation where both wisdom and courage are trapped. The sharp momentum of the army has been broken by difficult terrain, and their supplies have run out from within the treasury. The people are exhausted and filled with resentment, uncertain and without support. In my estimation, only a truly wise and virtuous ruler can put an end to the calamities of the world; otherwise, this situation cannot be resolved. Now, the fates of these two rulers hang in your hands. If you support Han, then Han will prevail; if you side with Chu, then Chu will triumph. I wish to lay bare my heart and offer my sincere advice, but I fear you may not be willing to use it. If you truly listen to my plan, there is no better course of action than for both sides to benefit and coexist. Divide the empire into three parts, each standing as a tripod leg—then none will dare move first. With your virtue and wisdom, commanding a large army, holding the powerful state of Qi, allied with Yan and Zhao, you could strike from an unguarded direction to control their rear. Following the people's desires, turning westward to plead for the lives of the common folk, then the entire world would follow like the wind and echo your call—who would dare refuse! You could weaken the strong and assist the weak, establishing feudal lords. Once these lords are established, all under heaven will submit to your authority and return their loyalty to Qi. Restoring the former glory of Qi, holding Jiao and Si regions, winning over feudal lords with virtue, and maintaining a dignified posture of courtesy—then kings from all under heaven would come to pay homage at your court in Qi. I have heard that when Heaven offers an opportunity and one does not take it, misfortune will instead befall him; When the time comes but you do nothing, disaster will follow. I hope you carefully consider this."

## 19

Han Xin said: "King Han has treated me with great kindness, providing me with his own carriage to ride in, his clothes to wear, and his food to eat. I have heard it said that one who rides in another's carriage must bear their burdens, one who wears another's clothes must carry their worries, and one who eats another's food must be willing to die for them. How could I possibly turn my back on righteousness for the sake of personal gain!" Kuai Tong said: "You believe yourself to be loyal to King Han and wish to establish a legacy for ten thousand generations, but in my secret opinion, you are mistaken. Originally, King Changshan and General Cheng'an Jun were commoners who became 刎颈 (sworn) friends. Later, they quarreled over the matters of Zhang Yan and Chen Ze, and thus came to resent each other. King Changshan betrayed King Xiang, presented the head of Xiang Ying in surrender, and fled back to King Han. King Han borrowed troops and marched eastward; he killed General Cheng'an Jun south of the Zhi River, beheading him so that his head and feet were separated. In the end, he became a laughingstock to all under heaven. These two men once had an extremely close relationship. Yet in the end they captured each other—why was that? Misfortune arises from excessive desires, and human hearts are hard to fathom. Now, if you wish to practice loyalty and trustworthiness in your dealings with King Han, it will surely not be as firm as the bond between those two men. Moreover, the matters at hand are far greater than Zhang Yan or Chen Ze. Therefore, I believe that your assumption that King Han will not endanger you is also a mistake. The ministers Zhong and Fan Li saved the state of Yue from destruction, helped King Goujian achieve dominance, established great achievements and fame—but in the end met their deaths. When all wild beasts are gone, the hunting dogs are boiled. In terms of friendship, it is not as close as Zhang Er and General Cheng'an Jun; In terms of loyalty and trustworthiness, it does not surpass that of Zhong and Fan Li toward King Goujian. These two examples are sufficient for you to observe. I hope you will deeply consider this matter. Moreover, I have heard that those whose bravery and strategy shake the ruler's authority endanger themselves, and those whose achievements overshadow all under heaven receive no reward. Let me speak of your great achievements, Your Majesty: You crossed the Xi River, captured King Wei, took General Xia Yue prisoner, led troops down Jingxing Pass, killed General Cheng'an Jun, subdued Zhao, intimidated Yan, pacified Qi, crushed a Chu army of 200,000 to the south, and personally slew Long Ju in the east. Turning westward for vengeance—this is what can be called unparalleled achievements under heaven and strategies that appear only once in an age. Now, you carry the 威名 (renowned authority) that shakes your ruler and possess achievements so great they cannot be rewarded. If you return to Chu, the people of Chu will not trust you; If you return to Han, the people of Han will fear and be terrified: where do you intend to go with such a position?" To hold the position of a subject yet possess authority that shakes your ruler, and to have a reputation so high across the land—I secretly fear for your safety." Han Xin replied respectfully: "Please rest now, Master. I will think about it carefully."

## 20

A few days later, Kuai Tong advised again: "Listening is the sign of a matter; planning is its key. If one listens too much and plans poorly yet still hopes for long-term safety, such cases are rare indeed. If one does not make even one or two mistakes in listening, words cannot confuse them; And if one's planning remains consistent with the fundamentals and ends, no amount of rhetoric can distract them. One who follows the duties of a servant loses the authority of a great empire; One who clings to meager rations misses out on the position of high minister. Therefore, wisdom is the decisive judgment; doubt is a harm to affairs. To scrutinize trivial details while missing the grand strategy of the world—though one may truly understand it, if one hesitates and dares not act—it becomes the calamity for all endeavors. Hence it is said: "A fierce tiger's hesitation is no match for a wasp or scorpion stinging decisively; "A qiji horse hesitating in place is not as good as an inferior horse walking steadily at ease; "The hesitation of Meng Ben is no match for the certainty of a common man; Even if one possesses the wisdom of Shun and Yu, to remain silent in contemplation is no better than a mute or deaf person giving orders with gestures." This saying emphasizes that what matters most is the ability to act. Achieving great accomplishments is difficult yet losing them is easy; seizing an opportunity is hard, but letting it slip away is simple. Opportunity, once gone, will not return. I hope you will carefully consider this." Han Xin hesitated and could not bear to betray Han, and also believed that with his many achievements, Han would never take away his fief of Qi from him. Thus he dismissed Kuai Tong. Kuai Tong's advice went unheeded; thereafter, he feigned madness and pretended to be a witch.

## 21

When the King of Han was trapped at Guling, he employed Zhang Liang's strategy and summoned the King of Qi, Xin. Thus, Han Xin led his troops to join forces at Gaixia. After Xiang Yu was defeated, Emperor Gaozu seized the King of Qi's army by surprise. In the first month of Han Year Five, the King of Qi was transferred to be the King of Chu, with his capital at Xupu.

## 22

Upon arriving in his fief, Xin summoned the old woman who had once fed him while he was destitute and gave her a thousand gold pieces as a reward. He also visited the Xiangxiang Nanchang Tingzhang, gave him one hundred coins, and said: "You are a petty man; your kindness did not last to the end." He summoned the young man who had once humiliated him by making him crawl beneath his legs and appointed him as a Wei officer in Chu. He told the generals and ministers: "This is a valiant man. When he humiliated me, could I not have killed him? To kill him would have been without justification, so I endured and allowed things to reach this point."

## 23

Zhong Limi, a former general of King Xiang who had fled, lived in Yilu and had long been on good terms with Xin. After the death of King Xiang, Zhong Limi fled to take refuge with Xin. Emperor Gaozu bore a grudge against Zhong Limi and, upon learning that he was in Chu, issued an order to the state of Chu to capture him. When Xin first went to his fief, he toured the counties and towns with troops marching in and out. In Han Year Six, someone submitted a petition accusing the King of Chu, Xin, of rebellion. Emperor Gaozu adopted Chen Ping's strategy, and as the Son of Heaven toured the land to convene feudal lords, he declared that there was a Yunmeng marsh in the south. He sent envoys to inform the feudal lords to gather at Chen: "I will tour Yunmeng." In reality, he intended to ambush Xin, who was unaware of this. As Emperor Gaozu approached Chu, Xin wanted to raise troops and rebel, but upon considering that he was innocent of any crime, he wished to pay homage to the emperor. Yet he feared being captured. Some advised Xin: "Slay Zhong Limi and present his head to the Son of Heaven; he will surely be pleased, and you will have no worries." Xin met with Zhong Limi to discuss matters. Zhong Limi said: "The reason the Han forces have not attacked and captured Chu is because I am in your hands. If you wish to capture me to ingratiate yourself with Han, then I will die today and you too shall perish shortly after." He then scolded Xin: "You are not a man of integrity!" And so he committed suicide. Xin took Zhong Limi's head and went to Chen to pay homage to Emperor Gaozu. The emperor ordered the warriors to bind Xin and place him in a cart behind his own carriage. Xin said: "Indeed, as people say, 'When the cunning rabbit is dead, the good hound is boiled; "When the high-flying bird is gone, the fine bow is stored away; "When a rival state falls, its strategists perish." "Now that the empire is at peace, I am indeed doomed to be boiled!" The emperor said: "People have accused you of rebellion." He then had Xin shackled and imprisoned. When they arrived at Luoyang, the emperor pardoned Xin's crimes and reappointed him as the Marquis of Huaiyin.

## 24

Xin knew that King Han feared and resented his abilities, so he often claimed illness to avoid attending court or accompanying the king on expeditions. Because of this, Xin harbored resentment day and night; he was always discontented and ashamed to be ranked equally with Marquises Jiang and Guan. Xin once visited General Fan Kuai, who knelt to greet and see him off, addressing himself as a subject and saying: "Your Majesty has deigned to visit me!" After leaving the gate, Xin laughed and said: "I am reduced to being ranked with men like Fan Kuai!" The emperor often casually asked Xin about whether various generals were capable, and their abilities varied. The emperor asked: "With my ability, how many troops can I command?" Xin replied: "Your Majesty is capable of commanding no more than 100,000 men." The emperor asked: "And what about you?" Xin said: "For me, the more troops, the better." The emperor laughed and said: "If you are so capable with large numbers of men, then why have you been captured by me?" Xin replied: "Your Majesty may not be able to command troops directly, but you are skilled at commanding generals. That is why I have been captured by Your Majesty. And what your Majesty possesses is a gift from Heaven, not something achieved through human effort."

## 25

Chen Xi was appointed as the governor of Julu and bid farewell to the Marquis of Huaiyinh. The Marquis of Huai Yin took his hand, dismissed the attendants, and walked with him in the courtyard. Looking up at the sky, he sighed and said: "Can I speak to you about something?" "I have something important to say to you." Xi replied: "I am at your command, General." The Marquis of Huaiyiny said: "The region you govern is where the finest troops in the land are stationed; and you, my lord, are a trusted and favored minister of the emperor. If people say that you have rebelled, the emperor will certainly not believe it; if such rumors come a second time, he may begin to suspect you; and if they reach him for the third time, he will surely become angry and lead his own troops in person. "I shall rise up from within to support you; together we can plan to take control of the empire." Chen Xi had long known of his ability and trusted him, saying: "I will follow your advice carefully!" In Han Year Ten, Chen Xi indeed rebelled. The emperor personally led the campaign against him, while Xin was ill and did not accompany him. He secretly sent men to Chen Xi's forces with a message: "Just raise your troops, and I will support you from here." Xin then conspired with his household retainers to issue a forged imperial decree at night, pardoning all officials' convicts and slaves, intending to raise them up to attack Empress Lu and the Crown Prince. The plans were already in place, awaiting word from Chen Xi. One of his attendants had offended Xin, who then imprisoned him and intended to kill him. The attendant's younger brother reported the situation to Empress Dowager Lu, revealing Xin's plot of rebellion. Empress Lu wanted to summon Xin but feared that his followers would not comply, so she conspired with Xiao He the Prime Minister and had someone falsely sent from the emperor's camp to announce that Chen Xi had been killed, prompting all marquises and ministers to offer congratulations. The Prime Minister deceived Xin by saying: "Even though you are ill, please force yourself to come in and offer your congratulations." When Xin entered, Empress Lu ordered the warriors to seize him and behead him inside the Zhongshi Hall of Changle Palace. As Xin was about to be executed, he said: "I regret not following Kuai Tong's advice. To be deceived by a woman and child—could this not have been the will of Heaven!" Thus, they exterminated Xin's three clans.

## 26

Emperor Gaozu had returned from the campaign against Chen Xi's forces and, upon arriving, saw that Xin was dead. He felt both joy and pity, and asked: "What did Xin say before his death?" Empress Lu said: "Xin expressed regret for not following Kuai Tong's plan." Emperor Gaozu said: "He was a talented orator from Qi." The emperor then issued an order for the state of Qi to arrest Kuai Tong. When Kuai Tong arrived, the emperor asked: "Did you advise the Marquis of Huaiyinyin to rebel?" Kuai Tong replied: "Indeed I did. It was my intention all along. The fool failed to follow my strategy, so it is only fitting that he met his end in this way." If that fool had followed my plan, how could Your Majesty have exterminated him!" The emperor became furious and said: "Boil him alive." Kuai Tong said: "Alas, how unjust this boiling is!" The emperor asked: "You advised Han Xin to rebel. What injustice is there in that?" Kuai Tong replied: "When the Qin dynasty's framework collapsed and its restraints loosened, great turmoil erupted in Shandong. People of different surnames rose up together, and heroes gathered like crows." "When the Qin lost its deer, all under heaven rushed to seize it. Thus, those with superior talent and swift feet were the first to obtain it." "The dog of Zhidi barked at Yao. Yao was not lacking in benevolence, but the dog simply barked because it did not recognize its true master." "At that time, I knew only Han Xin; I did not know Your Majesty." "Moreover, there were many people throughout the empire who sharpened their weapons and wished to do what Your Majesty did; they simply lacked the strength to succeed." "How could one possibly boil them all alive?" Emperor Gao said: "Let him go." Thus, Kuai Tong's crime was pardoned.

## 27

Grand Historian said: When I visited Huaiyin, the people of Huaiyin told me that even when Han Xin was still a commoner, his aspirations were different from those of ordinary men. When his mother died, he was poor and could not afford a proper burial; yet he still went to select an elevated and spacious site for her grave, ensuring that the surrounding area would be suitable for accommodating ten thousand households. I examined his mother's tomb, and indeed it was so. Had Han Xin studied the Way and practiced humility, had he not boasted of his achievements or shown off his abilities, then perhaps he could have been compared to Zhoushou and Tai Gong in terms of merit for the Han dynasty, and his descendants would have enjoyed sacrifices through generations. Instead of striving to achieve this, when the empire had already been unified he plotted rebellion and was thus wiped out along with his entire clan—was that not entirely deserved!
