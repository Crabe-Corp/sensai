# Biographies of Wan Shi and Zhang Shu

## 1

Wanshi Jun's name was Fen; his father was a native of Zhao, and their surname was Shi. After the fall of Zhao, they moved to Wen. When Emperor Gaozu marched eastward against Xiang Ji, he passed through Hedong. At that time, Fen was fifteen years old and served as a minor official, attending upon Emperor Gaozu. Emperor Gaozu spoke with him and admired his respectful demeanor. He asked, "What do you have?" He replied, "Fen has only his mother, who unfortunately lost her sight. Our family is poor." We have an elder sister who can play the qin." Emperor Gaozu said, "Will you be able to follow me?" He replied, "I am willing to do my utmost." Thus Emperor Gaozu summoned his elder sister to be a Meiren (a concubine title), appointed Fen as Zhongjuan, in charge of receiving written petitions and official documents, and relocated their family to the Zhongqi Li district in Chang'an because of his sister's status as a Meiren. By the time of Emperor Xiaowen, he accumulated enough merit to attain the rank of Dazhongdafa (Grand Court Counselor). He had no literary talent, but was respectful and cautious beyond comparison.

## 2

During the reign of Emperor Wendi, Duke Dongyang Zhang Xiangru served as Taifu (Grand Tutor) to the crown prince and was later dismissed from office. When selecting someone suitable for the position of Taifu, all recommended Fen, so he became Taifu to the crown prince. After Emperor Xiaojing ascended the throne, he was appointed as one of the Nine Ministers; lington Because he was close to power and feared him, they transferred Fen to serve as a chancellor for the feudal lords. Fen's eldest son was Jian, followed by Jia, Yi, and Qing. All of them were known for their well-mannered conduct, filial piety, and carefulness; they all attained official ranks equivalent to the rank of Erqianshi (a high-ranking position). At this, Emperor Jing said, "Master Shi and his four sons all attained the rank of Erqianshi; such honor and favor for a subject have thus gathered at their family's door." Fen was given the title Wanshi Jun.

## 3

In the later years of Emperor Xiaojing's reign, Wanshi Jun retired to his home with the salary of a Shangdafa (Senior Court Counselor), but still served as an official attending court ceremonies on seasonal occasions. Whenever passing by the palace gates and towers, Wanshi Jun would always get out of his carriage and quicken his pace; upon seeing a horse used for ceremonial processions on the road, he would certainly bow in respect. When his descendants who served as minor officials returned home to pay respects, Wanshi Jun would certainly meet them wearing formal court attire and never call them by their names. If his descendants committed any mistakes, he did not scold or reprimand them; instead, he would sit in a side chamber and face the table without eating. Only after that would his sons reprimand each other, with the eldest among them prostrating himself bare-chested and earnestly pleading for forgiveness; only when they corrected their mistakes was he willing to forgive. Whenever his descendants who had reached the age of wearing a formal cap were present, even during leisure time at home, he would always wear a cap himself, maintaining a dignified and composed demeanor. His servants and attendants also behaved joyfully yet diligently, being cautious in all matters. When the emperor occasionally bestowed food upon him at home, he would bow deeply and prostrate himself before eating it, as if the emperor were present. In observing mourning rites, his sorrow was profound and heartfelt. His descendants followed his teachings and behaved in the same manner. The family of Wanshi Jun was renowned throughout the commanderies and states for their filial piety and cautious conduct; even the upright scholars from Qi and Lu considered themselves inferior to them.

## 4

In Jianyuan year 2, Wang Zang, the Langzhongling (Minister of the Imperial Guards), was punished for his literary misconduct. The Empress Dowager believed that Confucian scholars were often verbose but lacked substance, while the family of Wanshi Jun practiced virtue without boasting about it. Thus, she appointed his eldest son Jian as Langzhongling and his youngest son Qing as Neishi (Minister in Charge of Internal Affairs).

## 5

Jian grew old with white hair, yet Wanshi Jun remained healthy. When Jian served as Langzhongling, he returned to visit his parents every five days during his official leave. Entering the room of his father's residence, he would secretly ask a servant for his father's undergarments and toilet brush, personally wash them, then return them to the servant without letting Wanshi Jun know—this became his regular practice. When Jian served as Langzhongting, whenever there were matters worth discussing, he would summon no one else and speak freely with great frankness; But when appearing in court, he seemed unable to utter a word. This is why the emperor personally honored and respected him.

## 6

Wanshi Jun moved to reside in Ling Li. Neishi Qing, after returning home drunk, did not get out of his carriage upon entering the outer gate. Upon hearing this, Wanshi Jun refused to eat. Qing was frightened and prostrated himself bare-chested to plead for forgiveness, but his father did not accept it. The entire family, along with his elder brother Jian, prostrated themselves bare-chested. Wanshi Jun rebuked them and said, "Neishi is a man of high rank; when he enters the neighborhood, all the elders and residents flee in fear, yet Neishi sits in his carriage as if nothing were wrong—was this not entirely appropriate?" Only then did Wanshi Jun accept their apologies and dismiss them. From that point on, Qing and all his younger brothers would quicken their pace as they entered the neighborhood gate, hurrying home with utmost respect.

## 7

Wanshi Jun died in the middle of Yuanshuo year 5. His eldest son, Langzhongling Jian, wept with deep sorrow and grief, needing to support himself on a cane in order to walk. After more than a year, Jian also died. All of his descendants were filial, but Jian was the most so, even surpassing Wanshi Jun in devotion.

## 8

When Jian served as Langzhongning, he submitted written reports on official matters. After the emperor reviewed them and passed them down for action, Jian read through them and said, "This is a mistake! The character 'ma' (horse) should have five strokes in the tail section, but now it has only four—this is one stroke short. I will surely be punished and put to death!" He was extremely frightened and anxious. Such was his cautious nature; he treated all other matters in the same way.

## 9

Wanshi Jun's youngest son, Qing, served as Taifu. When he was driving the emperor out, the emperor asked how many horses were in the carriage. Qing counted all the horses with his whip and raised his hand to say, "Six horses." Among Wanshi Jun's sons, Qing was the most laid-back in manner; yet he still behaved like this. When he served as chancellor of Qi, the entire state of Qi admired his family's conduct; without issuing many orders, the state was well governed. A temple honoring Shi Xiang (Stone Chancellor) was even established in his honor.

## 10

In Yuanshou year 1, the emperor established a crown prince and selected officials suitable to serve as his tutor. Qing was chosen for this role, moving from his post as governor of Pei to become Taifu (Grand Tutor) of the Crown Prince. After seven years, he was promoted to Yushi Daifu (Minister of Justice).

## 11

In autumn of Yuanding year 5, the prime minister committed a crime and was dismissed from office. An imperial decree was issued to the Yushi Daifu: "Master Shi was highly respected by the late emperor, and his descendants are filial. Therefore, appoint Yushi Daifu Qing as prime minister and bestow upon him the title of Muqiu Hou (Marquis of Muqiu)." At that time, the Han dynasty was waging war in the south against the two Yue states, attacking Goguryeo in the east, driving out the Xiongnu to the north, and launching a campaign against Dayuan in the west; many affairs occupied the empire. The Son of Heaven toured within the realm, restored ancient shrines to deities, performed fengshan sacrifices (sacrifices on Mount Tai), and revived rites and music. The imperial household had limited resources, so Sang Hongyang and others were responsible for generating profit. Wang Wenshu and his associates enforced strict laws, while Er Kuan and others promoted Confucian scholarship to the rank of Nine Ministers. These officials took turns being appointed and held power; matters did not require final decision by the prime minister, who was merely expected to be upright and cautious. He remained in office for nine years but had no ability to offer any meaningful advice or correction. He once wished to request the punishment of Shuo Zhong, a close minister of the emperor, and Jiuchen Xuan for their crimes, but failed to convince anyone. Instead, he himself was blamed and had to pay a fine as atonement.

## 12

In the middle of Yuanfeng year 4, there were two million displaced people in Guandong, forty thousand of whom had no official records. The high ministers and nobles discussed proposing to relocate these migrants to the frontier regions to accommodate them. The emperor considered the prime minister old and cautious, unable to participate in such discussions. Thus he granted him permission to retire from office, while ordering an investigation into those Yushi Daifu and below who had proposed the relocation plan. The prime minister felt ashamed of being unfit for his post and submitted a memorial stating, "I am fortunate to have been allowed to serve as prime minister. My incompetence is like that of a weak horse; I have no ability to assist in governance. The city walls and granaries are empty, and many people have become displaced or fled. I deserve to be executed on the chopping block, yet His Majesty has shown mercy by not punishing me according to law. I wish to return my prime ministerial seal and title of marquis, request permission to retire from the world, and step aside for more capable men." The Son of Heaven said, "The granaries are already empty, the people poor and displaced. Yet you proposed relocating them, causing unrest and instability, endangering the situation, and now you resign your post—where would you go to escape responsibility for these difficulties?" The emperor rebuked Qing in writing. Deeply ashamed, Qing resumed his duties.

## 13

Qing was known for his thorough and cautious writings, yet he had no grand strategies; he merely spoke on behalf of the common people. Three years later, in the middle of Taichu year 2, Chancellor Qing died and was posthumously honored with the title Tiann Hou. Qing's second son was named De; Qing loved and trusted him, so the emperor appointed De as his successor to inherit the marquis title. Later, he served as Taichang (Minister of Rites), but was sentenced to death for breaking the law and commuted his punishment by paying a fine, thus becoming an ordinary commoner. While Qing was prime minister, thirteen of his descendants who held official posts attained the rank equivalent to Erqianshi. After Qing's death, they gradually lost their positions due to crimes, and the family's reputation for filial piety and caution declined further.

## 14

Jianling Hou Wei Wan was a native of Dailing. Wan became an imperial attendant through his skill in chariotry, served under Emperor Wendi, and gradually advanced to the position of Zhonglangjiang (Commander of Attendants) based on his service record. He was upright and cautious with no notable achievements or controversies. When Emperor Xiaojing was crown prince, he summoned attendants to a banquet; Wan feigned illness and did not attend. At the time Emperor Wendi was about to die, he entrusted Emperor Xiaojing with these words: "Wan is a man of virtue; treat him kindly." After Emperor Wendi's death and the ascension of Emperor Xiaojing, for more than a year no reprimands were issued to Wan. As a result, Wan became increasingly cautious in his conduct.

## 15

Emperor Xiaojing once visited Shanglin Garden and summoned Zhonglangjiang Wan to accompany him as a co-rider. Upon returning, he asked, "Do you know why you were allowed to ride with me?" Wan replied, "I was fortunate enough to be promoted to Zhonglangjiang based on my service record; I do not know the reason myself." The emperor asked, "When I was crown prince and summoned you, you refused to come. Why?" Wan replied, "I have committed a capital offense; it is true that I was ill!" The emperor then bestowed upon him a sword. Wan said, "The late emperor had already granted me six swords; I dare not accept another." The emperor said, "Swords are items that people often give and receive as gifts—why should this be any different now?" Wan replied, "They are all still here." The emperor ordered the six swords to be fetched. They were still in their sheaths and had never been worn. Whenever imperial attendants committed offenses, Wan often took the blame upon himself without competing with other generals; When there were achievements or merits to be credited, he always gave them to other commanders. The emperor regarded him as upright and sincere without ulterior motives, so he appointed Wan as Taifu (Grand Tutor) for the Prince of Hejian. During the rebellion of Wu and Chu, an imperial decree appointed Wan as a general to lead troops from Hejian in attacking Wu and Chu. For his achievements, he was later appointed Zhongwei (Minister of War). After three years, for his military service, in the sixth year before Emperor Xiaojing's reign, Wan was enfeoffed as Jianling Hou.

## 16

The following year, the emperor deposed the crown prince and executed Lixi and his associates. The emperor considered Wan a man of virtue and could not bear to punish him, so he granted Wan permission to retire. He then ordered Zhi Du to investigate and arrest the Lixi clan. After this was done, the emperor appointed Prince of Jiaodong as crown prince and summoned Wan back, appointing him Taifu (Grand Tutor) once again. After some time, he was promoted to Yushi Dafu (Minister of Justice). Five years later, he replaced Tao Hou She as prime minister and performed his duties by submitting reports to court in accordance with standard procedures. However, from the time of his initial appointment until he became prime minister, there was nothing remarkable or notable about him. The emperor regarded him as sincere and steady, suitable to serve a young ruler, and thus held him in high esteem and bestowed many rewards upon him.

## 17

After serving as prime minister for three years, Emperor Xiaojing died and Emperor Wu ascended the throne. In the Jianyuan period, the prime minister was dismissed from office because during Emperor Xiaojing's illness many officials and prisoners were wrongfully punished, and he had failed in his duties. After that, Wan died, and his son Xin succeeded him. He lost his title of marquis due to the crime related to the offering of tribute wine (zhuo jin).

## 18

Zhibuyi, Marquis of Saihou, was a native of Nanyang. He served as a Lang (attendant) and worked under Emperor Wendi. One of his fellow attendants who had returned home mistakenly took the gold belonging to another Lang. Later, when the owner discovered this and wrongly suspected Zhibuyi, he apologized for having it and bought new gold to repay him. When the fellow who had returned home came back with the gold, the previous Lang who had lost it was greatly ashamed. Because of this incident, Zhibuyi was praised as a man of virtue. Emperor Wendi commended him and gradually promoted him to Tai Zhong Da Fu (Grand Court Counselor). When he appeared at court, some people criticized him by saying: "Zhibuyi has an excellent appearance, but how can one overlook the fact that he is good at stealing his sister-in-law's belongings!" Upon hearing this, Zhibuyi said: "I actually have no brother." Yet he never tried to clear his name.

## 19

During the rebellion of Wu and Chu states, Zhibuyi led troops as an official holding a rank equivalent to Erqianshi (a high-ranking local governor). In the first year of Emperor Xiaojing's Houyuan era, he was appointed as Yushi Dafu. The emperor honored his contributions during the Wu and Chu rebellion and thus enfeoffed Zhibuyi as Marquis of Saihou. During Emperor Wu's Jianyuan period, he was dismissed from office along with the prime minister Wan due to their respective mistakes.

## 20

Zhibuyi studied the teachings of Laozi (Daoism). Wherever he was stationed, he performed his official duties as usual but always feared that people would discover the traces of his administrative work. He did not like to seek fame or recognition and was thus regarded as a man of virtue (a "longzhe"). After Zhibuyi died, his son Xiangru succeeded him. His grandson Wang lost the title of marquis due to a crime related to tribute wine (zhuojin).

## 21

Zhou Wen, who served as Langzhongling, had the personal name Ren. His ancestors were originally natives of Rencheng. He was recommended for office due to his medical skills. When Emperor Xiaojing was still crown prince, he appointed Zhou Wen as She Ren (attendant). Accumulating achievements over time, during the reign of Emperor Xiaowen, he rose to the position of Tai Zhong Da Fu. At the beginning of his reign as emperor, Xiaojing appointed Ren as Langzhongling (Minister of the Imperial Guards).

## 22

Ren was a man who was secretive and reserved, never revealing anything. He often wore patched-up clothes and trousers stained with urine, deliberately appearing unclean in order to gain favor through humility. When Emperor Xiaojing entered the inner chambers for secret amusements with concubines, Ren was always present by his side. Until Emperor Xiaojing's death, Ren remained Langzhongling and never spoke of any of these matters. When the emperor once asked about people, Ren replied: "The Son of Heaven should observe them himself." Yet he never spoke ill of anyone. Because of this, Emperor Xiaojing visited his home twice in person. His family later moved to Yangling. The emperor bestowed many gifts upon him, but he often declined them and dared not accept them. Gifts from feudal lords and court officials were also repeatedly offered to him, yet he never accepted any of them.

## 23

When Emperor Wu ascended the throne, he regarded Ren as a loyal subject of his late father and held him in high regard. Ren then resigned due to illness, retired on the salary equivalent to an Erqianshi official, and his descendants all attained high-ranking positions.

## 24

Zhang Shu, who served as Yushi Dafu, had the personal name Ou and was a concubine-born son of Hou Yue, Marquis of Anqiu. During Emperor Xiaowen's reign, he advised the crown prince on matters related to legal and administrative principles (xingming). However, although Ou studied the School of Legalism, he was a man of virtue. During Emperor Xiaojing's reign, he was held in high esteem and often served as one of the Nine Ministers. In the fourth year of Emperor Wu's Yuanshuo era, when Han Anguo was dismissed from office, an imperial decree appointed Ou as Yushi Dafu (Grand Censor). Since Ou began his career in government service, he never once accused others of wrongdoing; instead, he always handled official matters with sincerity and as a man of virtue. His subordinates regarded him as a virtuous leader and dared not commit serious misconduct against him. When the emperor submitted cases for judgment, if there were matters that could be dismissed or overturned, he would do so; If a case was unavoidable and had to proceed, he would weep before the emperor in person and then seal it for execution. Such was his compassion toward others.

## 25

When he became old and ill, he requested to be relieved of office. The emperor then issued an imperial decree granting him retirement, allowing him to return home on the salary of a Shangdaifu (Senior Court Official). He lived in Yangling. His descendants all attained high-ranking official positions.

## 26

Grand Historian said: Confucius once said, "A gentleman wishes to be slow in speech but quick in action." Could this not have been referring to Wan Shi, Jianling, and Zhang Shu? Therefore, their methods of instruction did not rely on severity yet achieved results, and without strictness they still brought about order. Saihou was subtle and skillful in his dealings, while Zhou Wen's behavior of enduring flattery drew criticism from gentlemen for being close to sycophancy. Yet these could be called men of steadfast virtue!
