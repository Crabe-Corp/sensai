# Biographies of Flatterers and Favorites

## 1

The proverb says, "Working hard on the fields is not as good as meeting a good year; being a capable official is not as beneficial as encountering favorable circumstances," which is certainly no empty talk. It is not only women who flatter with their beauty, but officials also do so.

## 2

In the past, there were many people who gained favor through their appearance. When the Han dynasty rose to power, Emperor Gaozu was extremely fierce and defiant; yet Jiru gained favor through flattery. During the reign of Emperor Xiaohui, there was Hongru. These two men had no real talent or ability; they merely gained high status and favor through their fawning and flattery, sharing the emperor's bedchamber and rising with him. All the ministers and officials relied on them to intercede and plead cases. Therefore, during Emperor Xiaohui's reign, the attendants and Attendants-in-Ordinary all wore phoenix-shaped headdresses, shell-patterned sashes, applied facial powder, imitating Hongru and Jiru and their kind. The two men moved their families to Anling.

## 3

During the reign of Emperor Xiaowen, among the emperor's favored ministers, the scholar-official was Dengtong, and the eunuchs were Zhao Tong and Beigong Bozi. Beigong Bozi was loved for his kind and elderly demeanor; while Zhao Tong gained favor through his knowledge of astrology, often serving as Emperor Wen's charioteer. Dengtong had no special skills or talents. Deng Tong was a native of Nan'an, Shu Jun; he served as a Huangtou Lang officer by washing and cleaning boats. Emperor Xiaowen once dreamed that he wanted to ascend to heaven but could not. A yellow-haired young man pushed him upward into the heavens from behind, and as he looked back, he saw a tear in the back of this man's sash. After waking, he went to Jian Tai and secretly searched for the young officer who had pushed him in his dream. He immediately spotted Deng Tong, whose sash was torn at the back—just as it had been in the dream. He summoned him and asked for his name and surname, learning that he was surnamed Deng and named Tong. Emperor Wen was pleased and favored him increasingly each day. Tong was also a man of good will and caution, not fond of socializing outside the palace; even when granted leave for washing and bathing, he did not wish to go out. Thus, Emperor Wen bestowed on Tong tens of thousands in rewards, and promoted him all the way up to the rank of Shang Da Fu (Senior Grand Officer). Emperor Wen often went to Deng Tong's home for leisure and play. Yet Deng Tong had no other abilities; he could not recommend any officials, only carefully guarding himself to please the emperor. lø The emperor sent a skilled physiognomist to examine Deng Tong, who said, "He will die of poverty and starvation." Emperor Wen said, "It is up to me whether Tong becomes rich." "How can he be poor?" Thus, Emperor Wen granted Deng Tong the Tongsan copper mines in Yandao, Shu, allowing him to mint his own coins. "Dengshi Qian" (Deng's money) circulated throughout the land. He became this wealthy.

## 4

Emperor Wen once suffered from an abscess, and Deng Tong often sucked the wound for him. Emperor Wen was unhappy about this and casually asked Tong, "Who in the world loves me most?" Tong replied, "It should be no one more than the Crown Prince." The crown prince entered to inquire about his father's illness. Emperor Wen made him suck the abscess, and he did so with a reluctant expression. Later, upon learning that Deng Tong had often sucked his wound for the emperor, the crown prince felt ashamed and thus resented Tong. After Emperor Wen died and Emperor Jing took the throne, Deng Tong was dismissed from office and returned to his home life. Not long after, someone reported that Deng Tong had secretly cast coins outside the designated areas in violation of regulations. The case was investigated by officials, and indeed some evidence was found. The matter was finally settled, and all of Deng Tong's family assets were confiscated, leaving him still in debt for tens of thousands. Whenever the Crown Princess gave gifts to Deng Tong, officials would immediately confiscate them; not even a single hairpin was allowed on his person. Thus, the Crown Princess had to secretly provide him with clothes and food. In the end, he could not even afford a single coin, dying in someone else's home as a pauper.

## 5

During the reign of Emperor Xiao Jing, there were no favored ministers at court; yet among them was only Zhou Wenren, the Director of the Office of Attendants. Although he received great favor and his treatment exceeded that of ordinary officials, it was not particularly close or deep.

## 6

Nowadays, the emperor's favored ministers among scholar-officials are Hanwang Sunyan, and among eunuchs is Li Yannian. Sunyan was a collateral descendant of Hou Gonggao. When the current emperor was Prince Jiaodong, Sunyan and he became fond of each other while studying calligraphy together. And when the emperor became crown prince, his affection for Sunyan grew even closer. Sunyan was good at horseback riding and archery, and also skilled in flattery. After the emperor ascended the throne, he wished to launch a campaign against the Xiongnu. Since Sunyan had previously studied Hu military tactics, he was thus granted even greater honor and status, rising to the rank of Shangdaifu (Senior Grand Officer), with rewards comparable to those given to Deng Tong. At that time, Sunyan often shared the emperor's bedchamber, rising and resting together. When Prince Jiangdu came to court, an imperial decree allowed him to accompany the emperor on a hunting expedition in Shanglin Park. Before the imperial procession had even set out along its route, the emperor first sent Sunyan to ride in a secondary carriage, followed by dozens or hundreds of horsemen, galloping ahead to scout for game. Prince Jiangdu saw this from afar and mistook Sunyan for the emperor; he ordered his attendants to disperse and knelt in homage on the roadside. Sunyan rode past without stopping or acknowledging him. After passing by, Prince Jiangdu was furious and wept before the Empress Dowager, saying, "I beg to be allowed to return to my fiefdom's garrison so that I may serve as a palace guard, just like Han Yan." The empress dowager thus harbored resentment toward Sunyan. Sunyan served the emperor and entered or left Yongxiang without restriction, but his immoral conduct was reported to the Empress Dowager. The empress dowager became furious and sent an envoy to bestow a death sentence on Sunyan. The emperor tried to plead for mercy, but in the end could not prevent it; thus, Sunyan died. And Han Yue, Marquis Andao, who was his younger brother, was also favored through flattery and favoritism.

## 7

Li Yannian was a native of Zhongshan. His parents, himself, his brothers and sisters were all former entertainers or performers. Yannian was castrated for a legal offense and became an eunuch serving in the palace. The Princess Pingyang mentioned that Yannian's younger sister was good at dancing. When the emperor saw her, he liked her and, after she entered Yongxiang, summoned and honored Yannian highly. Yannian was good at singing and created new musical styles; the emperor, who was then promoting rituals to honor Heaven and Earth, wished to compose hymns and songs with music for them. Yannian skillfully interpreted the emperor's intentions and arranged the musical sequences of the initial poems. His younger sister was also favored by the emperor and bore a male child. Yannian wore the seal of an official ranked at 2,000 dan (a high-ranking position) and was titled Xieshenglv ("Harmonizer of Musical Laws"). He shared the emperor's bedchamber as well, enjoying great honor and favor comparable to that of Han Yan. After some time, he gradually became involved in immoral relationships with palace attendants, entering and leaving the palace with arrogance and recklessness. After his younger sister Lady Li died, the emperor's affection waned, and he arrested and executed Yannian and his brothers.

## 8

From then on, most of the favored favorites within the palace were from among the emperor's in-laws; however, they are not worth mentioning. Wei Qing and Huo Qubing also rose to prominence as relatives of the empress, but they mainly advanced themselves through their own abilities and talents.

## 9

Grand Historian Sima Qian said: How great is the power of love and hatred in its time! The conduct of Mi Zixia alone is sufficient to observe how later people attained favor through flattery. Even a hundred generations can be understood from it.
