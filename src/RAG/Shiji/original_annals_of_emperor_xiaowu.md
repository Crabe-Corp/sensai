# Original Annals of Emperor Xiaowu

## 1

Emperor Xiaowu was the second son of Emperor Xiaojing. His mother was Empress Dowager Wang. In the fourth year of Emperor Xiaojing's reign, he was made King of Jiaodong as a son of the emperor. In the seventh year of Emperor Xiaojing's reign, Crown Prince Li was deposed and made King of Linjiang, and Prince Jiaodong was appointed crown prince. In the sixteenth year of Emperor Xiaojing, he died, and the crown prince ascended the throne as Emperor Xiaowu. At the beginning of his reign, Emperor Xiaowu showed particular reverence for rituals to gods and spirits.

## 2

In the first year, more than sixty years had passed since the founding of the Han dynasty. The realm was at peace, and members of the gentry all looked to the Son of Heaven to perform the Fengshan sacrifices and institute calendar reforms. The emperor favored Confucian learning and recruited virtuous and capable men. Zhao Wan and Wang Zang, among others, were appointed as high ministers due to their literary attainments, and they wished to discuss the establishment of an ancient-style Mingtang (Hall of Illumination) in the southern part of the capital city for receiving feudal lords. They had not yet completed their proposals on matters such as touring the realm, performing the Fengshan sacrifices, and reforming the calendar and color symbolism. At this time, Empress Dowager Dou, who favored the Huanglao (Yellow Emperor and Laozi) philosophy, disliked Confucianism. She had people secretly uncover corrupt dealings involving Zhao Wan et al., summoned them for investigation, and Zhao Wan and Zang committed suicide; all their proposed initiatives were abolished.

## 3

Six years later, Empress Dowager Dou died. The following year, the emperor summoned literary men such as Gongsun Hong.

## 4

The next year, on his first visit to Yong, the emperor performed sacrificial rites at the five altars for suburban offerings. Thereafter, he conducted these sacrifices every three years. At this time, the emperor sought a divine master and lodged him at Tishi Guan in Shanglin Park. The Divine Master was a woman from Changling, who had become so grief-stricken over her son's death that she claimed to have encountered spirits through the mediumship of Xianhou Wanruo. Wanruo conducted rituals in her home, and many people went there to worship. Pingyuan Jun went to worship there; afterward, his descendants became honored and prominent. When Emperor Wu ascended the throne, he treated her with great courtesy and housed her in an inner shrine. He heard what she said but never saw her person, they say.

## 5

łoż At this time, Li Shaojun also presented methods for altar worship, grain cultivation, and longevity to the emperor, who honored him highly. Shaojun was a former physician of Lord Shenze Hou, who had introduced him for his expertise in alchemy and formulas. He concealed his age and place of birth, often claiming to be seventy years old, and said he could manipulate objects and ward off aging. He traveled widely among the feudal lords, promoting his formulas and methods. He had no wife or children. People heard that he could manipulate objects and achieve immortality, so they gave him more gifts; he often had an abundance of money, silk, clothing, and food. Everyone thought that without managing any property he was still well-provided for; since no one knew where he came from, they believed in him even more and competed to serve him. Shaojun had a natural talent for formulas and was good at making clever predictions that often proved accurate. Once, while drinking with Marquis Wu'an, there was an elderly man over ninety years old at the gathering. Shaojun then said he had once played and hunted with the elder's grandfather. The old man, as a child, had followed his grandfather on that outing and remembered the place; everyone at the table was astonished. When Shaojun presented himself to the emperor, there were some old bronze vessels in the palace, and the emperor asked him about them. Shaojun said, "These vessels were placed in Baiqin during the tenth year of Duke Huan of Qi's reign." Later, when they examined their inscriptions, it turned out that they were indeed vessels from Duke Huan of Qi. The entire palace was astounded and regarded Shaojun as a deity, believing him to be hundreds of years old.

## 6

Shaojun told the emperor, "By worshiping the stove altar, one can summon objects. Summoning objects allows cinnabar to be transformed into gold; when golden vessels are used for food and drink, longevity increases. With increased longevity, one may see the immortals of Penglai in the sea. Seeing them enables one to perform the Fengshan sacrifice and attain immortality—this was how Emperor Huangdi did it." I once traveled by the sea, where I met Anqi Sheng, who gave me jujubes to eat as big as melons. Anqi Sheng was an immortal who resided in the midst of Penglai; he appeared to people when conditions were right, and disappeared when they were not." Thus, the Son of Heaven began personally performing sacrifices to the stove altar and sent alchemists to sea in search of Anqi Sheng and others on Penglai. At this time, people started transforming cinnabar and other medicinal ingredients into gold.

## 7

After some time, Li Shaojun fell ill and died. The Son of Heaven believed he had transformed himself into an immortal and did not die, so he ordered Huang Chui and Shi Kuanshu to receive his formulas. The search for Anqi Sheng on Penglai yielded no results, and many eccentric alchemists from Yan and Qi in the coastal regions imitated each other, fabricating more tales of divine matters.

## 8

Bo Youji from Bo submitted a proposal for the worship of Taiyi, saying: "The most exalted among heavenly deities is Taiyi. The assistants to Taiyi are the Five Emperors." "In ancient times, the Son of Heaven sacrificed to Taiyi in the southeast suburbs during spring and autumn. He used a full set of three sacrificial animals (tai lao), for seven days, building an altar with eight passageways for spirits." Thus, the Son of Heaven ordered the Grand Priest to establish a shrine for Taiyi in the southeastern suburbs of Chang'an and to conduct regular sacrifices according to Bo Youji's method. Later, someone submitted a memorial stating: "In ancient times, the Son of Heaven used a full set of sacrificial animals to worship the Three Ones—Tianyi, Diyi, and Taiyi—once every three years." The Son of Heaven approved this and ordered the Grand Priest to oversee the worship at Bo Youji's Taiyi altar according to these methods. Later, another person submitted a memorial saying: "In ancient times, the Son of heaven regularly performed sacrificial rites in spring and autumn; when sacrificing to Huangdi, one broken mirror and one 枭 were used; for Mingyang, a sheep was used; when sacrificing to Mahang, one green male horse was used; for Taiyi, Gaoshan Shanjun, and Dizhang, a bull was used; for Wuyi Jun, dried fish were used; and for the Yin-Yang Shizhe, one bull was used." The emperor ordered the ritual officials to oversee these rites according to this method and conduct them at the side of Bo Youji's Taiyi Altar.

## 9

Later, there were white deer in the Son of Heaven's hunting park; their hides were used as ritual offerings to manifest auspicious omens and thus white gold was produced.

## 10

The following year, during a suburban sacrifice at Yong, they captured a one-horned beast that resembled a boar. The officials said: "Your Majesty has been solemn and reverent in performing the suburban sacrifices; Heaven, pleased with your offerings, has bestowed a one-horned beast upon you. This is probably a qilin." Therefore, they offered it at the five altars of Zhi, adding an additional bull for each sacrificial fire offering. The Son of Heaven bestowed white gold upon the feudal lords to harmonize the auspicious signs with heaven and earth.

## 11

At this time, King Jibei, believing that the Son of Heaven was about to perform the Fengshan sacrifices, submitted a memorial offering Mount Tai and its surrounding towns. The emperor accepted it but compensated him with other counties in return. King Changshan had committed a crime and was exiled; the Son of Heaven enfeoffed his younger brother at Zhen Ding to continue the ancestral rites, and converted Changshan into a commandery. After this, all five sacred mountains came under the jurisdiction of the Son of Heaven's commanderies.

## 12

The following year, Shao Weng from Qi presented methods for contacting spirits and gods to the emperor. The emperor had a favored concubine, Lady Wang, who died. Shao Weng claimed that by night he could summon the image of Lady Wang and the stove spirit through his methods; the Son of Heaven saw them from behind a curtain. Thus, he appointed Shao Weng as General Wencheng and bestowed many rewards upon him, treating him with the courtesy due to a guest. Wencheng said: "If Your Majesty wishes to communicate with spirits, your palaces and attire do not resemble those of the divine; otherwise, divine beings will not come." Therefore, they made chariots decorated with painted clouds and used them on appropriate days to drive away evil spirits. They also built the Ganzhuan Palace, in which a pavilion was constructed and painted with images of Heaven, Earth, Taiyi, and other deities; ritual implements were placed there to summon heavenly gods. After more than a year, his methods became increasingly ineffective, and the divine beings did not appear. Then he wrote messages on silk cloth and fed them to a cow, pretending he was unaware of it, claiming there were wonders in the cow's belly. After slaughtering the cow and examining its entrails, they found the silk cloth; the writing on it mentioned strange things, which made the Son of Heaven suspicious. Someone recognized the handwriting and inquired about it from others; indeed, it was a forged document. Thus, General Wencheng was executed, but this was kept secret.

## 13

Afterward, they built structures such as Baoliang, Tongzhu, and Chenglu Xianren zhang.

## 14

The year after Wencheng's death, the Son of Heaven fell seriously ill at Dinghu Palace; he summoned every kind of shaman and physician available, but his illness did not improve. You Shui Fa Gen then said: "There is a witch in Shangjun who became ill and was possessed by a spirit." The emperor summoned her and placed her at Ganzhuan for worship. When he became ill, he sent people to consult the Divine Master. The Divine Master said: "The Son of Heaven need not worry about his illness. His condition will soon improve; he should make an effort to meet with me at Ganzhuan." Thus, when his illness improved, the emperor went in person to Ganzhuan, and indeed, he was cured. He issued a general pardon throughout the realm and established a shrine for the Divine Master at Shou Gong. The most honored among the deities was Taiyi; his assistants were figures such as Dajin and Siming, who all accompanied him. He could not be seen directly, but one could hear his voice, which was like that of an ordinary person speaking. He would come and go at irregular times; when he arrived, the wind would suddenly become still. He resided within a curtained chamber. Sometimes he spoke during the day, but usually at night. The Son of Heaven would purify himself before entering. Through the witch, he served as host and managed food and drink offerings. Whatever was desired was conveyed through spoken words from below. They also established Shou Gong and Bei Gong, raised feathered banners, set up sacrificial offerings, to honor the Divine Master with ritual propriety. What the Divine Master said, the emperor had people record in writing; these records were called "Hua Fa." What he spoke of was known to common people and nothing extraordinary, yet the Son of Heaven alone found it pleasing. The matter remained secret; no one in later generations knew about it.

## 15

Three years later, the officials said that the beginning of an era should be named after an auspicious omen from Heaven and not numbered simply as one or two. The first era was named Jianyuan; the second, Yuan Guang, because of a long comet; and the third, Yuan Shou, because during a suburban sacrifice a one-horned beast was captured.

## 16

The following winter, the Son of Heaven performed a suburban sacrifice at Yong and proposed: "Now that I have personally sacrificed to Heaven, but not yet offered sacrifices to Hou Tu (the Earth Deity), then the rites are incomplete." The officials and Tai Shigong, as well as ritual officer Kuanshu, discussed: "Sacrificial animals for Heaven and Earth should have horns like silkworm cocoons or chestnuts. Now that Your Majesty is personally offering sacrifices to Hou Tu, it would be appropriate for the worship of Hou Tu to take place at a round altar in the marshland with five altars. At each altar, one yellow bull and full sacrificial offerings should be used. After the sacrifice, everything should be buried completely, and those accompanying the rites should wear yellow clothing." Thus, the Son of Heaven proceeded eastward and established the first Hou Tu shrine on Shui Hill in Fenyin as proposed by Kuanshu et al. The emperor personally gazed toward the altar and paid homage, following the same rites used for Heaven. After completing the rituals, the Son of Heaven traveled to Yingyang before returning. Passing through Luoyang, he issued an edict: "The Three Dynasties are long gone; they are distant and difficult to preserve. Therefore, I shall bestow thirty li of land upon the descendants of Zhou as the Lord Zhounanjun, to offer sacrifices in honor of their ancestors." In this year, the Son of Heaven began touring the commanderies and counties, gradually approaching Mount Tai.

## 17

That spring, Marquis Yuecheng submitted a memorial concerning Luan Da. Luan Da was a native of Jiaodong Gong; he had previously studied under the same teacher as General Wencheng, and later became the Shangfang officer for King Jiaodong. Marquis Yuecheng's sister was Queen of Wangwang, but she had no sons. After King Kang died, a son of one of his concubines was installed as king. Queen Kang had immoral conduct and did not get along with the king, endangering each other through legal means. Queen Kang, having heard of General Wencheng's death and wishing to ingratiate herself with the Son of Heaven, sent Luan Da through Marquis Yuecheng to request an audience in order to present his methods. After the Son of Heaven had executed General Wencheng, he regretted his early death and lamented that his methods were not fully revealed. Upon meeting Luan Da, he was greatly pleased. Luan Da was tall and handsome, spoke eloquently with many strategies, and dared to make grandiose claims without hesitation. He boldly claimed: "I have often traveled in the sea and met figures such as Anqi and Xianmen. But they considered me lowly and did not believe me." They also thought King Kang was merely a vassal lord, unworthy of receiving my methods. I repeatedly advised King Kang, but he did not heed me either." My teacher said: 'Gold can be transmuted, river breaches can be sealed, the elixir of immortality can be obtained, and immortal beings can be summoned.' I fear that if I suffer the same fate as General Wencheng, all alchemists will keep their mouths shut and dare not speak of methods!" The Son of Heaven said: "General Wencheng merely died from eating horse liver. If you can truly perfect your methods, what would I not spare for you!" Luan Da said: "My teacher did not seek anyone; it was people who sought him out. If Your Majesty truly wishes to summon him, then you must honor the envoy, ensure he has relatives with him, and treat them as honored guests. Do not show disrespect; let each be given a seal of authority. Only then can they serve as intermediaries between mortals and immortals." Whether the immortal will agree or not remains to be seen. Only by showing great respect to his envoy can we hope to summon him." Thus, the Son of Heaven ordered that small methods be tested first. They raised banners, and the flags struck each other on their own.

## 18

At this time, the Son of Heaven was greatly concerned about the river breach and the failure to produce gold, so he appointed Luan Da as General Wuli. After more than a month, he received four gold seals and wore the seals of General Tianshi, General Ditu, General Datong, and General Tiandao. An imperial decree was issued to the Grand Historian: "In ancient times, Yu dredged the Nine Jiang rivers and regulated the Four Dus. Recently, the river overflowed onto dry land, and embankments have not ceased to be reinforced. For twenty-eight years that I have ruled over all under Heaven, it seems as if Heaven has sent me a man of talent who can achieve great success. The Book of Changes says, "A flying dragon" and "a goose gradually arriving at the riverbank"—this likely refers to him. Therefore, I hereby bestow a fief of 2000 households upon General Dishi Da as Lord Letong." He was granted the first rank mansion befitting a marquis and given one thousand attendants. Imperial carriages, horses, canopies, tents, and other valuables were bestowed upon his household. The emperor also married him to Princess Weichang, giving her ten thousand catties of gold and renaming the fief as Gongzhu Dangli. The Son of Heaven personally visited General Wuli's mansion. Envoys sent to inquire about the provisions given to him were constantly traveling along the roads, one after another. From the principal princess down to generals and ministers, all set up banquets at his home, offering gifts and tributes. At this time, the Son of Heaven also carved a jade seal inscribed "General Tiandao," and sent an envoy dressed in feathered robes to stand on white reeds at night. General Wuli similarly wore feathered robes and stood on white reeds to receive the seal, thereby showing that he was not considered a subject. The one wearing the "Tiandao" seal would serve as an intermediary between the Son of Heaven and the gods of Heaven. Thus, General Wuli often conducted night sacrifices at his home in hopes of summoning divine beings from above. The gods had not yet arrived, but many spirits and ghosts gathered instead; however, he was somewhat able to control them. Later, he prepared his belongings and set out eastward into the sea in search of his teacher. After several months, Luan Da had been awarded six seals and his prestige shook the entire realm. In the regions of Yan and Qi along the coast, no one failed to grasp their wrists and claim they possessed secret methods capable of achieving immortality.

## 19

In the middle of June that summer, a local shaman named Jin from Fenyin, while performing rituals at a Hou Tu shrine near Weishui in Huiyuan, saw an earth formation shaped like a hook. When he dug it up, he found a tripod. This tripod was greatly different from ordinary tripods; its inscriptions were engraved but without maker's marks or identification, which seemed strange. He reported it to the officials. The official informed Tai Shou Sheng of Hedong, who then reported it upward. The Son of Heaven sent envoys to investigate and confirm whether the shaman Jin had obtained the tripod through deceit. Finding no fraud, they honored it with rituals, brought the tripod to Ganzhuang, followed along on the journey, and presented it at court. When they arrived in Zhongshan, during a warm afternoon, there was a yellow cloud covering the area above them. A boar passed by; the Son of Heaven shot it himself and used it as an offering to the clouds. Upon arriving in Chang'an, all the high officials, ministers, and scholars discussed and proposed that the Son of Heaven should honor and revere the sacred tripod. The Son of Heaven said: "Recently, the river has overflowed and harvests have repeatedly failed; therefore, I toured to perform sacrifices to Hou Tu in hopes that he would help the people grow grain. This year's bountiful harvest has not yet been acknowledged; why then did this tripod appear?" The officials all said: "We have heard that in ancient times, the great emperor raised a single sacred tripod; one signifies unity, and it is to which both Heaven and Earth and all things are ultimately connected. Huangdi made three precious tripods, symbolizing Heaven, Earth, and Humanity. Yu collected the metal from the nine regions, cast nine tripods, all of which were used to offer sacrifices and cook food for Heaven, gods, and spirits. When encountering a sage ruler, they would emerge; the tripods passed from Xia to Shang dynasties. As Zhou virtue declined and the sacred soil of Song was lost, the tripod disappeared and remained hidden. The Ode says: "From hall to foundation, from sheep to ox; Great tripods and small cauldrons, without misfortune or disaster—this is the virtue of longevity." Now that the tripod has arrived at Ganzhuang, it shines with a lustrous transformation like a dragon, bearing boundless auspiciousness. In harmony with the sacred site of Zhongshan, yellow and white clouds descended as a canopy; beasts appeared as omens. Bows were drawn and arrows shot, gathering at the altar below to offer great sacrifices in celebration. Only those who are divinely chosen to become emperors truly understand its meaning and harmonize with virtue. The tripod should be presented before the ancestral shrine, enshrined in the imperial court, to align with this clear divine response." An edict was issued: "Approved."

## 20

Those who went to the sea in search of Penglai said that Penglai was not far away, but they could not reach it—perhaps because its aura could not be seen. The Son of Heaven then dispatched an official skilled in observing atmospheric signs to locate the aura and clouds associated with it.

## 21

That autumn, the Son of Heaven visited Yong for a planned suburban sacrifice. Some said: "The Five Emperors were assistants to Taiyi. Therefore, we should establish a shrine for Taiyi and have the Son of Heaven personally perform sacrifices there." The Son of Heaven was uncertain and had not yet decided. Gongsun Qing, a native of Qi, said: "This year we obtained the sacred tripod; on the first day of winter solstice in the winter, it was Xin Si. This is identical to the time of Huangdi." Qing had a written record stating: "Huangdi obtained the sacred tripod at Wan (Qiu), and consulted Guiyu Qu. Qu replied: "The Yellow Emperor obtained the sacred tripod and a divine oracle. In that year, on the first day of Dongzhi it was Ji You; this marked the beginning of Heaven's cycle, ending and then repeating." Thus, Huangdi welcomed the sun and calculated with oracle sticks. Afterwards, every twenty years he would determine the first day of Dongzhi, a total of twenty calculations over 380 years. Finally, Huangdi attained immortality and ascended to Heaven." Qing then, through Suozhong, wished to present this information. Suozhong examined the document and found it unorthodox, suspecting it to be fabricated. He replied: "The matter of the sacred tripod has already been decided; what else is there for us to do!" Qing then submitted his proposal through a favored court official. The Son of Heaven was very pleased and summoned Qing for questioning. Qing replied: "I received this book from Shen Gong, but Shen Gong has already died." The Son of Heaven asked: "Who was Shen Gong?" Qing said: "Shen Gong was a native of Qi. He associated with Anqi Sheng, received teachings from Huangdi, left no written records, and possessed only this tripod-related text. It says: 'The Han dynasty's rise corresponds to the time of the Yellow Emperor. The sage ruler of the Han will be among the grandchildren or great-grandchildren of Emperor Gaozu. When the sacred tripod appears and connects with divine beings, it is time for the Son of Heaven to perform the Fengshan sacrifices." Among the seventy-two kings who performed Fengshan rites, only Huangdi was able to ascend Mount Tai for the ceremony." Shen Gong said: "The Han ruler should also perform the Fengshan sacrifice; by doing so, he will attain immortality and ascend to Heaven. During Huangdi's time, there were ten thousand feudal lords, among whom seven thousand received divine blessings and spiritual honors. There are eight famous mountains in the world; three of them lie among the southern and eastern barbarians, while five are located within China proper. Within China, these five mountains—Huashan, Shoushan, Taishi, Taishan, and Donglai—are the ones where Huangdi often traveled and convened with divine beings. Huangdi engaged in warfare while also learning the ways of immortality. Concerned that the common people were not following the right path, he executed those who denied ghosts and gods. After more than a hundred years, he was finally able to communicate with divine beings. Emperor Huangdi made an outlying sacrifice to the Supreme Deity in Yong and stayed there for three months. Guiyu Qu, who was also known as Dahong, died and was buried in Yong; this is the so-called Hongzhong. After that, he met with ten thousand spirits at Huangdi's Bright Court. The Bright Court refers to Ganshan. What is referred to as Huanmen is actually Gu Kou. Emperor Huangdi extracted copper from Shoushan and cast a tripod at the foot of Jingshan. After the tripod was completed, a dragon descended with its beard hanging down to welcome Emperor Huangdi. Huangdi mounted the dragon, and more than seventy of his ministers and concubines followed him up; thus he ascended to Heaven. The lesser officials could not ascend, so they all grasped the dragon's beard; as a result, the beard was pulled out and Huangdi's bow fell. The common people looked up to see Emperor Huangdi ascend to Heaven; they then embraced his bow and the dragon's beard, wailing in grief. Hence, later generations named that place Dinghu, and called his bow Wu Hao." At this, the Son of Heaven said: "Alas! If I could truly become like Huangdi, I would regard leaving my wife and children behind as easily as discarding a worn-out shoe." He then appointed Qing as an Attendant Officer and sent him eastward to await the divine at Taishi.

## 22

The Son of Heaven thus performed a sacrificial rite in Yong, reaching Longxi, and ascended Kongtong to the west before proceeding to Ganshan. He ordered ritual officials Kuanshu and others to prepare the altar for Taiyi, modeled after the altar of Baoji's Taiyi, with three concentric circles. The altars for the Five Emperors were arranged around it in accordance with their respective directions, with Huangdi's altar located to the southwest; eight paths leading to the realm of spirits were cleared. The offerings used for Taiyi followed those at Yong's Yizhi, with the addition of sacrificial items such as sweet wine, dates, and dried meat. A single yak was sacrificed to prepare the ritual vessels and food. The Five Emperors received only the sacrificial dishes, ritual vessels, and sweet wine as offerings. The four directions of the ground beneath were prepared with sacrificial food for the attendants of the deities and for the Northern Dipper. After the sacrifice, all remaining offerings were burned in a ritual fire. The sacrificial ox was white; a deer stood among it, and a pig was placed within the deer's enclosure, with water poured over them all. Sacrifices to the sun were made with oxen, while sacrifices to the moon used a ram and a pig or an entire animal. The priests and slaughterers for Taiyi wore purple robes and embroidered garments. Each of the Five Emperors was associated with a color corresponding to their attributes; the sun was red, and the moon white.

## 23

On the first day of the eleventh month (Jinshi Shixu, 104 BC), when it was also the winter solstice, at dawn before full daylight, the Son of Heaven began his sacrificial rites to Taiyi. When paying homage to the sun in the morning and to the moon in the evening, he performed a bowing gesture; and when venerating Taiyi, he followed the rites practiced at Yong. The ritual master offered a eulogy, saying: "Heaven first bestowed the sacred tripod and divine oracle on the Emperor; from new moon to new moon, ending yet beginning anew. The Emperor respectfully bows in homage." And he wore yellow on top. The altar was filled with rows of torches, and cooking utensils were placed around the altar. The officials in charge reported: "There is a light above the altar." The ministers and high-ranking officials said, "The Son of Heaven first performed a sacrificial rite to Taiyi at Yunyang; the officials in charge presented the xuanyu jade and choice sacrificial animals for the offering. That night, there was a beautiful light, and by daybreak, yellow vapor rose to connect with Heaven." The Grand Historian, ritual official Kuanshu, and others said: "This is a sign of divine favor and auspicious blessings; we should therefore establish an altar for Taiyi in this place where the light appeared to demonstrate its response. Let the Grand Priest oversee it, and conduct sacrifices during the autumn and winter festivals. The Son of Heaven should perform this sacrificial rite once every three years."

## 24

That autumn, in preparation for a campaign against the Nanyue kingdom, they made offerings and prayers to Taiyi. They crafted banners decorated with male holly, painted depictions of the sun, moon, Northern Dipper, ascending dragon, as well as representations of Tianyi and the Three Stars; these were used as the vanguard for Taiyi and named "Lingqi." When praying for military success, the Grand Historian would hold up the banner to point toward the enemy state. Wuli General was sent but dared not go to sea and instead went to worship at Mount Tai. The Son of Heaven had someone secretly follow and investigate, but in reality, nothing was seen. Wuli falsely claimed to have seen his teacher; the methods he presented were all exhausted, and most of them did not work as promised. The Son of Heaven then executed Wuli.

## 25

That winter, Gongsun Qing awaited the divine in Henan and saw traces of an immortal on Guoshi Cheng. There was a creature resembling a pheasant moving back and forth atop the city wall. The Son of Heaven personally visited Guoshi Cheng to inspect the tracks. He asked Gongsun Qing: "Are you trying to emulate Wencheng or Wuli?" Qing replied: "Immortals do not seek the favor of sovereigns; it is the sovereign who seeks them. If the path is not given sufficient time and space, the divine will not come. Matters concerning the divine may seem roundabout or absurd; they require many years of accumulation before results can be achieved." Thus, each commandery and kingdom cleared roads and renovated palaces, pavilions, famous mountains, and shrines for deities in anticipation of the Son of Heaven's visit.

## 26

That year, after the Nanyue kingdom was conquered, there was a favored minister named Li Yannian who gained favor through his musical talents. The Son of Heaven appreciated it and summoned the ministers and high officials to deliberate, saying: "Among the common people, even private sacrifices still include music with dancing and drumming. Now that we perform sacrifices at the outlying altars without any music, is this appropriate?" The ministers and high officials replied: "In ancient times, sacrifices to Heaven and Earth all included music, and thus the deities could be properly honored." Some said: "The Emperor Tai ordered Su Nu to play a fifty-stringed zither, but the music was so sorrowful that he could not bear it and forbade her from continuing. Hence, the zither was broken into one with twenty-five strings." Thus, after pacifying the southern Yue region, sacrifices and prayers to Taiyi and Hou Tu began to include music and dance. More singers were recruited, and from this time onward, the twenty-five-stringed zither and the konghou (a type of harp) came into use.

## 27

The following winter, the Son of Heaven proposed: "In ancient times, one first reviewed troops and dismissed soldiers from campaigns before performing the Fengshan sacrifices." He then proceeded north on an inspection tour of Shufang, mustering more than 100,000 troops. On his return journey, he paid homage to Emperor Huangdi's tomb at Qiaoshan and dismissed the soldiers at Xuru. The Son of Heaven said: "I have heard that Huangdi did not die; now there is a tomb, why is this so?" Someone replied: "Huangdi had already attained immortality and ascended to Heaven; his ministers buried his clothing and hat instead." When he arrived at Ganshan, in preparation for the upcoming Fengshan ceremony on Mount Tai, he first performed a classification sacrifice to Taiyi.

## 28

Since obtaining the sacred tripod, the Son of Heaven and his ministers as well as scholars have discussed performing the Fengshan sacrifices. The Fengshan ceremony was a rare and grand event, its rituals and ceremonies unknown to most; thus the Confucian scholars collected references from the Book of Documents, Rites of Zhou, and other texts concerning the veneration of deities through sacrificial bull offerings. Ding Gong from Qi, over ninety years old, said: "The Fengshan sacrifice is the union of names associated with immortality. Emperor Qin Shi Huang was unable to perform it. If Your Majesty is determined to ascend, you should do so gradually; if done properly without encountering wind or rain, then the Fengshan sacrifice can be completed." The Son of Heaven therefore ordered the Confucian scholars to practice the ritual bull offerings and draft the procedures for the Fengshan ceremony. After several years, preparations were nearly complete. Having heard the words of Gongsun Qing and the alchemists, who claimed that Huangdi and those before him had performed the Fengshan sacrifices by summoning strange creatures and communicating with divine beings, the Son of Heaven wished to emulate Huangdi in seeking contact with immortals on Penglai. He aspired to surpass the moral achievements of the Nine Emperors and incorporated Confucian teachings to provide a scholarly basis for these endeavors. The Confucian scholars, unable to clearly explain the Fengshan rituals and constrained by their adherence to classical texts like the Book of Songs and ancient writings, dared not offer bold suggestions. The Son of Heaven displayed the ritual objects for the Fengshan sacrifice to the Confucian scholars. Some among them said, "These do not match ancient practices," while Xu Yan added that "the students from Tai Chang performed the rites less skillfully than those in Lu." Zhou Ba submitted a plan with accompanying illustrations on how to conduct the Fengshan ceremony; as a result, Emperor dismissed Xu Yan and Zhou Ba, and completely abandoned all Confucian scholars without employing them.

## 29

In March, the Son of Heaven proceeded east to Guoshi and performed a ritual at Zhongyue Taishi Mountain. The attendants following him in the valley below heard as if someone were saying, "Ten thousand years!" They asked the Son of Heaven about it, but he said nothing; they inquired among those below, and they also remained silent. Therefore, he bestowed a fief of three hundred households to maintain the shrine at Taishi, naming it Chonggao Yi. He then traveled eastward up Mount Tai; when he found the grass and trees on the mountain had not yet sprouted, he ordered people to place stones at the summit of Taishan.

## 30

The Son of Heaven then proceeded eastward for an inspection tour along the coast and performed rituals to worship the Eight Deities. Thousands of people from Qi submitted memorials speaking about divine beings, strange phenomena, or miraculous methods, yet none proved effective. 𝑙 He then dispatched more ships and ordered thousands of people who claimed to know about the divine mountains in the sea to search for immortals on Penglai Island. Gongsun Qing, holding an imperial token of authority, often traveled ahead to await divine mountains. When he arrived at Donglai, he claimed that one night he saw a man several zhang tall; when he approached, the figure vanished, but its footprints were very large and resembled those of some beast or bird. Some ministers claimed to have seen an old man leading a dog, who said, "I wish to see the Great Official," but then suddenly disappeared. The Son of Heaven had already seen the large footprints, yet did not believe them; but when he heard from his ministers about an old man, he greatly believed it to be a sign of an immortal. He stayed by the sea for some time, sending thousands of alchemists and private envoirs in carriages to seek immortals.

## 31

In April, he returned to Fenggao. The Son of Heaven considered that the various Confucian scholars and alchemists who spoke about the ceremonies for offering sacrifices on Mount Tai each had different views, which were not in accordance with established rites and difficult to implement. The Son of Heaven arrived at Liangfu and performed a ritual sacrifice to the deity of the land. On the Yimao day, he ordered Confucian scholars serving as attendants to wear pibian caps and girdles of rank, and perform the ritual by shooting a bull. He sealed the eastern side at the foot of Mount Tai in accordance with the rites used for the suburban sacrifice to Taiyi. The stone was two chi wide and nine chi high, and beneath it there were jade tablets inscribed with writing, the contents of which remained secret. After the ceremony was completed, the Son of Heaven ascended Mount Tai alone with Attendant-in-Attendance Fengche Zihou, who also performed a sealing ritual. All these matters were kept secret. The next day, he descended via the hidden path. On the Bingchen day, he performed the sacrifice at Suzhan Shan, northeast of the base of Mount Tai, following the rites used to worship Houtu. The Son of Heaven personally paid homage in yellow robes and fully employed music for all rituals. Between the Yangtze and Huai rivers, a single reed with three ridges was used as an offering mat for the deity. The five-colored soils were further mixed into the sealing mound. Exotic beasts, flying birds, and white pheasants from distant regions were released, many of which were added to the sacrificial offerings. Beasts such as sì (a mythical beast), máoniú (wild ox), xī (rhinoceros), and xiàng (elephant) were not used. All of them arrived at Mount Tai before being released to go away. During the ceremonies for sealing and sacrificing on Mount Tai, there was light visible at night, and during the day white clouds arose from above the sealed mound.

## 32

After returning from the ceremonies of sealing and sacrificing on Mount Tai, the Son of Heaven sat in the Mingtang Hall, where ministers took turns presenting their wishes for his long life. At this time, he issued an edict to the Grand Historian: "I, with my humble self, have inherited the highest authority; I am filled with anxiety and fear that I may not be worthy. My virtue is shallow, and I do not fully understand rites and music. I performed sacrifices to Taiyi, and there were signs of auspicious light; the omens seemed promising. I was deeply moved by strange phenomena but dared not stop, so I proceeded to ascend Mount Tai for the sealing ceremony, reaching Liangfu, and then conducted the sacrifice at Suzhan. I have now renewed myself; I wish to celebrate this new beginning with the officials and commoners. Therefore, I grant each hundred households one ox and ten dan of wine, and give two bolts of cloth and silk to those who are orphans, widows, or elderly over eighty years old. Bo, Fenggao, Sheqiu, and Licheng shall be exempt from paying this year's taxes and levies. I now issue a general pardon for the entire realm, following the decree of amnesty issued on the Yimao day. No further construction or labor projects shall be carried out along the route I have traveled through. Matters occurring more than two years ago shall not be investigated." He also issued another decree: "In ancient times, the Son of Heaven conducted an inspection tour once every five years and performed rites at Mount Tai. The feudal lords had designated places for lodging and paying homage. Therefore, I order each feudal lord to establish a residence beneath Mount Tai."

## 33

After the Son of Heaven had completed the ceremonies for sealing and sacrifice on Mount Tai, there were no storms or disasters; yet alchemists continued to claim that the divine mountains such as Penglai might be attainable. Thus, he was delighted at the possibility of encountering them and once again traveled eastward to the seashore in hopes of meeting Penglai. Fengche Zihou suddenly fell ill and died within a single day. The Son of Heaven thus proceeded northward along the coast, reaching Jieshi in the north, and conducted an inspection tour from Liaoxi through the northern frontier to Jiuyuan. In May, he returned to Ganquan. The officials reported that the appearance of a precious tripod marked the beginning of Yuandong; thus, this year was designated as the first year of Yuanfeng.

## 34

That autumn, there appeared an unusual star in the constellation Dongjing. More than ten days later, another unusual star appeared in Saneng. Wang Shuo, an expert in observing atmospheric omens, said: "I observed the star alone emerging like a gourd; after a meal's time it re-entered." The officials stated, "Your Majesty has established the Han dynasty's ceremonies of sealing and sacrifice; this must be how Heaven is rewarding virtue with a stellar omen."

## 35

The following winter, he performed the suburban sacrifices to the Five Emperors at Yong and upon his return paid homage with sacrificial rites to Taiyi. The eulogy read: "The star of virtue shines brightly, its radiance a sign of auspiciousness." "The longevity star continues to appear, its brilliance deep and luminous. The trustworthy star is clearly visible; the Son of Heaven respectfully bows in homage at the sacrificial offering by the Grand Priest."

## 36

That spring, Gongsun Qing claimed to have seen a divine being on Donglai Mountain who said, "I wish to see the Son of Heaven." The Son of Heaven therefore visited Guoshi City and appointed Gongsun Qing as Zhongdafa. He then proceeded to Donglai, where he stayed for several days without seeing anything except the footprints of a giant. Once again, thousands of alchemists were sent out to seek divine beings and strange phenomena, as well as to gather herbs and medicinal plants. This year was marked by a drought. Therefore, since the Son of Heaven had not yet received an imperial name, he offered prayers at Wanli Sha and passed by to worship at Mount Tai. He returned to Huzi, personally inspected the damming of the Yellow River's breach, stayed for two days, and then performed a sacrificial rite by casting offerings into the river before departing. He dispatched two high-ranking officials to lead troops in blocking the breach; as a result, the Yellow River shifted into two channels and returned to its ancient course established by Yu.

## 37

At this time, after the southern Yue had been subdued, a native named Yong Zhi said: "The Yue people traditionally believe in ghosts, and their shrines are all visited by spirits, often with effective results. "In the past, King Dongou revered ghosts and lived to be one hundred and sixty years old. Later generations became arrogant and neglectful, hence their decline." Therefore, he ordered Yue shamans to establish shrines for the worship of Yue deities. These altars had no platforms or sacrificial tables; they worshipped heavenly gods, Supreme Deity, and a hundred ghosts, using chicken bones for divination. The Son of Heaven believed in this, and the Yue rituals involving chicken bone divination began to be used.

## 38

Gongsun Qing said: "Immortals can be seen, but the Son of Heaven travels too hastily; that is why they are not encountered. Now Your Majesty could build a pavilion, as was done at Guoshi City, and place meat and dates there; divine beings should then be able to appear. Moreover, immortals prefer to dwell in high towers." Therefore, the Son of Heaven ordered that in Chang'an a pavilion named Feilian Gui Guan be built, and at Ganquan an additional pavilion called Yiyanshou Guan. He instructed Gongsun Qing to hold a token of authority and prepare offerings while waiting for divine beings; he also constructed the Tongtian Tai (Through Heaven Pavilion), placing sacrificial items beneath it in hopes of summoning immortals and deities. At this time, a new front hall was built at Ganquan, marking the beginning of the expansion of various palace buildings. In summer, a zhi (a type of fungus regarded as an auspicious omen) grew inside the palace walls. The Son of Heaven, having dammed the river and built the Tongtian Tai, saw what appeared to be luminous clouds. He then issued a decree: "Nine stems of zhi have grown within the walls at Ganquan; therefore, I pardon all under heaven and order that no further labor projects be carried out."

## 39

The following year, he launched a campaign against Chaoxian. In summer, there was a drought. Gongsun Qing said: "During the time of Huangdi, Heaven became dry when he performed the sealing ceremony; it remained so for three years." The Son of Heaven then issued a decree: "There is a drought in Heaven—could this be due to the sealing ceremony?" Therefore, I command that throughout the realm, the deity Lingxing shall be honored and worshipped with reverence."

## 40

The following year, the Son of Heaven performed a suburban sacrifice at Yong, opened up the Huizhong Road, and conducted an inspection tour. In spring, he arrived at Mingze and returned via Xhe.

## 41

The following winter, the Son of Heaven toured Nanjun, reaching Jiangling before proceeding eastward. He ascended and paid homage to Tianzhushan in Qian, naming it the Southern Sacred Mountain. Sailing along the Yangtze River, he departed from Xunyang through Zongyang, passed Poyang Lake, and paid homage to its famous mountains and rivers. He traveled northward to Langya, proceeding along the seashore. In the fourth month, he arrived at Fenggao to perform repairs on the sealing mound.

## 42

Initially, when the Son of Heaven sealed Mount Tai, there had been an ancient Mingtang Hall at the northeastern base of the mountain; however, its location was perilous and not open or spacious. The Son of Heaven wished to construct a Mingtang Hall near Fenggao but had yet to understand the proper design and specifications for it. Gong Sui, a native of Jinan, presented to the Son of Heaven a diagram of an ancient Mingtang from the time of Huangdi. The diagram of the Mingtang showed one hall with no walls on any four sides, covered with reeds. Water flowed through it; a circular palace wall formed an elevated corridor. There was a tower above, and access to the structure came from the southwest, which was named Kunlun. The Son of Heaven would enter this way in order to pay homage and offer sacrifices to the Supreme Deity. Therefore, the Son of Heaven instructed that a Mingtang be built at Wenshang in Fenggao according to Gong Sui's diagram. When the repairs and sealing were completed five years later, he conducted sacrifices to Taiyi and the Five Emperors in the main hall of the Mingtang, arranging the ancestral shrine for Emperor Gaozu opposite them. The deity Houtu was worshipped in a lower chamber with twenty grand sacrificial offerings of cattle, sheep, and pigs. The Son of Heaven entered via the Kunlun passage and for the first time paid homage in the Mingtang according to the rites used at a suburban sacrifice. After the ceremony, offerings were burned beneath the hall. The Son of Heaven also ascended Mount Tai again and secretly performed a sacrifice at its summit. At the foot of Mount Tai, sacrifices were offered to the Five Emperors according to their respective directions; Huangdi was worshipped together with Chidi, and officials attended the ceremonies. A fire was lit on Mount Tai's summit, and all below responded in kind.

## 43

Two years later, on the first day of the eleventh month, Jiazi, which coincided with the winter solstice, calendar scholars calculated it as the beginning of a new cycle. The Son of Heaven personally arrived at Mount Tai and on the first day, Jiazi of the eleventh month—coinciding with the winter solstice—worshipped the Supreme Deity in the Mingtang during each sealing and sacrificial ceremony. The eulogy read: "Heaven has bestowed upon the Son of Heaven the divine tablet of Taiyuan, which cycles and begins anew. "The Son of Heaven reverently bows to Taiyi." He traveled eastward to the sea, examining those who had entered the ocean and alchemists seeking immortals; none proved effective, yet he sent out even more people in hopes of encountering them.

## 44

On the Yiyou day of the eleventh month (December 15, 83 BC), a fire broke out at Boliang. On the first day, Jiawu of the twelfth month (January 14, 82 BC), the Son of Heaven personally performed a sacrificial rite at Gaoli for Houtu. Standing by the Bohai Sea, he intended to perform distant sacrifices to places like Penglai in hopes of reaching a miraculous realm.

## 45

The Son of Heaven returned; because the Boliang fire had occurred, he received court officials and submitted reports at Ganquan. Gongsun Qing said: "Huangdi built the Qinglei Terrace, and after twelve days of burning offerings there, Huangdi then established the Mingting. "Mingting is Ganquan." Many alchemists claimed that ancient emperors had once established capitals at Ganquan. Later, the Son of Heaven again received feudal lords at Ganquan, and guest houses for the feudal lords were built there. Yong Zhi then said: "Among Yue customs, when a fire occurs, the new house must be rebuilt larger in order to overcome and subdue it." Thus they built the Jianzhang Palace, planned with ten thousand gates and a hundred thousand households. The front hall was designed to be as high as Weiyang; to its east stood the Fengque, over twenty zhang in height. To its west lay Tangzhong, where tigers were kept in enclosures stretching for dozens of li. To the north, a large pool was constructed. Jiantai, over twenty zhang high, was named Taiye Chi. Within it were Penglai, Fangzhang, Yingzhou, and Huliang, symbolizing divine mountains, turtles, and fish in the sea. To its south were Yuting, Bimen, and other structures resembling great birds. They then built the Shenming Tai and Jinggan Lou, each over fifty zhang in height, connected by a covered carriage road.

## 46

In summer, the Han dynasty changed its calendar system, taking the first month as the beginning of the year. The color yellow was favored, and official titles were altered with five-character seals. This marked the beginning of the Taichu Yuannian era. In this year, the western campaign against Dayuan was launched. A great locust plague arose. Lady Ding, Luoyang's Yu Chu and others performed ritual curses against the Xiongnu and Dayuan through their respective rites.

## 47

The following year, officials reported that at Yong's Five Altars there were no prepared sacrificial vessels or fully matured offerings; the fragrant items were incomplete. The Son of Heaven then ordered ritual officials to provide sacrificial bulls, vessels, and offerings at the altars. The five-colored foods were prepared according to the principle of overcoming elements, while wooden dummy horses replaced young foals for sacrifice. Only sacrifices to the Five Emperors and those personally performed at the suburban rites used young foals. For all other named mountains, rivers, and sites where young foals were traditionally used in sacrifices, wooden dummy horses were substituted instead. Only when the Son of Heaven passed by a site was it permissible to use a young foal for sacrifice. All other rituals remained as before.

## 48

The following year, the Son of Heaven toured eastward along the sea to examine immortals and divine beings, but none proved effective. Some alchemists said: "During the time of Huangdi, five cities and twelve towers were built to await divine beings at fixed times; this was called Yingnian." The Son of Heaven approved their construction according to the plan, naming it "Mingnian." The Son of Heaven personally observed rituals and worshipped the Supreme Deity, wearing yellow robes.

## 49

Gong Su Dai said: "Although Huangdi once sealed Mount Tai, it was actually Fenghou, Fengju, and Qibo who instructed Huangdi to perform the sealing ceremony at Dongtai Shan and the sacrificial rite on Fanshan with matching symbols; only then did he achieve immortality." The Son of Heaven had already ordered the preparation of sacrificial items and traveled to Dongtai Shan. However, since Dongtai Shan was low and small, not befitting its reputation, he merely commanded ritual officials to perform rites there without conducting a sealing or sacrificial ceremony. Later, the Son of Heaven ordered Dai to conduct sacrifices and await divine beings. In summer, he returned to Mount Tai and performed the five-year ritual as before, adding a sacrificial ceremony at Shilu. Shilu is located in the southern part of the base of Mount Tai. Many alchemists claimed it to be a gate where immortals resided, so the Son of Heaven personally conducted the sacrificial rite there.

## 50

Five years later, he returned to Mount Tai for another sealing ceremony and on his return journey passed by Changshan Mountain to perform a sacrifice there.

## 51

The temples established by the present Son of Heaven are for Taiyi and Houtu, with a personal suburban sacrifice held every three years. The Han dynasty's system of sealing and sacrificial rites was thus established, with a major sealing ceremony conducted once every five years. The temples of Baoji Taiyi, San Yi, Mingyang, Maxing, and Chixing—five in total—and the ritual officials of Kuanshu offered seasonal sacrifices at appropriate times. In total there were six temples, all overseen by the Grand Priest. As for the Eight Gods and other deities, as well as temples such as Mingnian, Fanshan, and others with various names, sacrifices were performed only when the Son of Heaven happened to pass by; once he left, they ceased. Temples established by alchemists were independently managed by their founders. When these individuals died, the temples were abandoned and no longer maintained by official ritual officials. All other temples remained as they had been before. The present Son of Heaven's sealing and sacrificial ceremonies, twelve years after their initiation, were extended to all Five Great Mountains and Four Major Rivers. However, the alchemists' efforts to await divine beings or seek Penglai by entering the sea ultimately proved ineffective and yielded no results. Gongsun Qing, who had been sent to await divine beings, still used the footprints of a great person as an excuse, but these efforts also proved ineffective. The Son of Heaven became increasingly weary and disinterested in the strange and impractical words of alchemists, yet still maintained a loose connection with them without completely cutting ties, hoping to encounter one who might be genuine. From this time onward, the number of alchemists speaking about temples and divine beings increased even more; yet their effectiveness was clearly evident.

## 52

Grand Historian said: I followed the Son of Heaven on his tour to perform sacrifices at temples for heaven, earth, and various famous mountains and rivers, including the sealing and sacrificial ceremonies. I entered the Shou Gong to attend sacrifices and listen to discussions about divine matters, thoroughly examining what the alchemists and ritual officials had said. Afterward, I withdrew and compiled a detailed account of all those who had dealt with spirits and deities since ancient times, clearly presenting their inner motives and outward actions. Later generations of virtuous men may thus have the opportunity to review them. As for detailed matters such as ritual vessels, jade offerings, and the etiquette of presenting and responding with wine, these were left to the officials in charge.
