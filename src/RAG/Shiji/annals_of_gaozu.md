# Annals of Gaozu

## 1

Gaozu was a native of Zhongyang Li, Fengyi, Pei, surnamed Liu, courtesy name Ji. His father was called Taigong, and his mother was called Liu Ao. Liu Ao once rested by the banks of a large marsh, when she dreamed that she met a deity. At that time thunder and lightning darkened the sky; Taigong went to look, and saw a dragon coiled above her. Soon after she became pregnant, and thus gave birth to Gaozu.

## 2

Gaozu was a man of great stature, with a prominent nose and dragon-like features, handsome beard and mustache, and seventy-two black moles on his left thigh. He was kind and loved people, enjoyed giving gifts, and had an open-minded nature. He often displayed a grand demeanor and did not concern himself with the family's daily affairs or productive work. When he grew up, he was once tested for a government post and became the chief of Sishui Pavilion. He treated all officials in the court with contempt, and loved alcohol and women. He often bought wine from Wang Ao and Wu Fu, got drunk and lay down to sleep. Wu Fu and Wang Ao saw a dragon frequently above him, and found it strange. Whenever Gaozu bought wine and stayed to drink, the amount of wine he consumed was several times more than usual. After witnessing these strange occurrences, by the end of each year, these two families often canceled their debt records and forgave his debts.

## 3

Whenever Gaozu was conscripted to serve in Xianyang, he would take the opportunity to sightsee and observe Emperor Qin Shi Huang. He sighed deeply and said, "Alas! A true man should be like this!"

## 4

Lü Gong of Shanfu was friendly with the magistrate of Pei. To avoid an enmity, he followed him as a guest and thus settled in Pei. The powerful and influential officials in Pei heard that the magistrate had an important guest, so they all went to offer congratulations. Xiao He was in charge of the reception and presided over the order, instructing all officials: "Those who do not contribute at least one thousand coins should sit downstairs." Gaozu, as the chief of a pavilion, had always looked down on other officials. So he falsely submitted an invitation card stating "a gift of ten thousand coins," when in fact he did not bring even one coin. When the invitation was presented, Lü Gong was greatly surprised and rose to meet him at the door. Lü Gong was a man who loved reading faces; upon seeing Gaozu's appearance, he greatly respected him and led him in to sit. Xiao He said, "Liu Ji always makes grandiose statements but rarely accomplishes anything." Gaozu thus treated the guests with contempt and took the seat of honor, without showing any deference. When the wine was nearly finished, Lü Gong looked at Gaozu intently and deliberately kept him behind. Gaozu stayed until the banquet ended, last to leave. Lü Gong said, "I have long been fond of reading faces and have examined many people, but none compare to Ji's features. I hope Ji will take good care of himself." "I have a daughter; I wish for her to become your humble servant as a broom-wielding concubine." After the banquet, Lü Ao became angry with Lü Gong and said, "You always wanted to give this daughter an extraordinary match, to a noble person. The magistrate of Pei is your good friend; when he asked for her in marriage you refused him. Why did you rashly promise her to Liu Ji?" Lü Gong said, "This is not something a woman can understand." In the end, he married his daughter to Liu Ji. Lü Gong's daughter was none other than Empress Lu; she bore Emperor Xiaohui and Princess Luyuan.

## 5

When Gaozu served as the chief of a pavilion (tingchang), he often returned home to his fields on leave. Empress Lu and her two sons were working in the field when an old man passed by asking for a drink; Empress Lu gave him food and water. The old man read Empress Lu's fortune, saying, "Madam is destined to be the most honored woman in the world." He was asked to read the fortunes of her two sons. Upon seeing Emperor Xiaohui, he said, "The reason madam will achieve such honor is because of this boy." When reading Princess Luyuan's fortune, it was also foretold that she would be honored. After the old man had left, Gaozu happened to arrive from a nearby house. Empress Lu told him in detail that there was a visitor who had passed by and foretold great honor for her children and herself. Gaozu asked, "Has he gone far?" He then went after him and caught up to ask the old man. The old man said, "Earlier, your wife and children all resembled you; your fortune is so great it cannot be described." Gaozu then thanked him, saying, "If indeed as the old man says, I will never forget this kindness." When Gaozu became powerful and honored, no one knew where the old man had gone.

## 6

When Gaozu was the chief of a pavilion, he had a hat made from bamboo bark and ordered someone to go to Xue for its production. He often wore it; even after becoming powerful, he continued wearing it. This is what was known as the "Liu family hat."

## 7

Gaozu, serving as a pavilion chief, was responsible for escorting convicts to Lishan on behalf of the county. Many of these convicts escaped along the way. Realizing that if they continued, all would escape anyway, Gaozu stopped at a marsh west of Feng for the night to rest and drink. That night he released his convicts from their shackles and let them go free. He said, "All of you may leave; I shall also depart from here!" More than ten strong men among the convicts volunteered to follow him. Gaozu, drunk on wine, took a shortcut through the marsh at night and ordered one man to go ahead as a guide. The scout returning reported, "There is a large snake blocking our path; we should turn back." Gaozu, still drunk, said, "A true man dares to go forward—what have we to fear!" He then advanced and drew his sword to strike down the snake. The snake was thus split in two, clearing the path ahead. After walking several li, Gaozu became drunk again and lay down to rest. Later, when others arrived at the place where the snake had been, they found an old woman crying in the night. They asked her why she was weeping, and the old woman said, "A man killed my son; that is why I am crying." Someone asked, "Why did this old woman's son get killed?" The old woman said, "I am the son of the White Emperor. I transformed into a snake and blocked the road, but now it has been slain by the son of the Red Emperor; that is why I weep." The people thought the old woman was lying and wanted to report her, but she suddenly vanished. When others arrived later, Gaozu awoke. The people told Gaozu what had happened, and he alone was secretly pleased and felt proud of himself. His followers gradually became more fearful of him.

## 8

Emperor Qin Shi Huang often said, "There is the aura of an emperor in the southeast," and thus traveled eastward to suppress it. Gaozu began to suspect this, fled into hiding, and concealed himself among the rocks and marshes of Mang and Dang mountains. Empress Lu searched for him along with others and often found him. Gaozu was puzzled and asked her about it. Empress Lu said, "There is often a cloud above where you stay; that's why I can always find you when I go looking." Gaozu was secretly pleased. Some of the young men from Pei heard about this and many wished to join him.

## 9

In autumn of the first year of the Second Emperor of Qin (Qin Ershi), Chen Sheng and others rose up in Qi, reached Chen, declared themselves king, and proclaimed a state called "Zhangchu." Many commanderies and counties killed their local officials to respond to Chen She. The magistrate of Pei was afraid, and wanted the people of Pei to join Chen She's rebellion. The clerks Xiao He and Cao Can said, "You are an official of the Qin dynasty. Now you wish to betray it; if you lead the people of Pei in rebellion, they may not listen." "We suggest that you summon those who have fled and are outside the city; you can gather hundreds of people, then use force to command the crowd. The people will not dare refuse." Thus they ordered Fan Kuai to summon Liu Ji. Liu Ji had already gathered dozens, even hundreds of followers.

## 10

Thus Fan Kuai followed Liu Ji and came back. Later, the magistrate of Pei regretted his decision and feared that things might change; he thus closed the city gates for defense and planned to execute Xiao He and Cao Can. Xiao He and Cao Can, fearing for their lives, fled over the city walls to take refuge with Liu Ji. Liu Ji then wrote on a piece of silk cloth and shot it onto the city wall, addressing the elders of Pei: "The people have long suffered under the Qin dynasty. Now although you are defending for the magistrate of Pei, with all the feudal lords rising together, they will now massacre Pei." "If you all kill the magistrate together and choose a capable young man to lead, then support the feudal lords in rebellion, your families will be safe." "Otherwise, fathers and sons alike will be slaughtered; there is no point in doing nothing." The elders then led the young men to kill the magistrate of Pei and opened the city gates to welcome Liu Ji, wishing for him to become their new magistrate. Liu Ji said, "The world is in chaos and the feudal lords are rising together; if we now appoint an unqualified leader, one defeat will lead to total ruin. "I do not dare to love myself, but I fear my abilities are too weak to protect you all." "This is a major matter; let us instead jointly select someone more suitable." Xiao He, Cao Can and others were all civil officials who loved themselves; they feared that if the matter failed, the Qin would exterminate their families. Thus, they gave everything to Liu Ji. All the elders said, "We have heard many strange and auspicious things about Liu Ji throughout our lives; he is destined for greatness. Moreover, when we consulted diviners, none were as favorable as Liu Ji." Thus Liu Ji repeatedly declined the offer. The crowd dared not act otherwise, so they established Liu Ji as Lord of Pei. They honored the Yellow Emperor and sacrificed to Chiyou in the courtyard of Pei, sprinkled blood on drums and banners, which were all red. This was because the white snake killed had been the son of the White Emperor; its killer was thus the son of the Red Emperor, hence the red color. At this time, young and powerful officials such as Xiao He, Cao Can, Fan Kuai, and others gathered two or three thousand youths from Pei to attack Huling and Fangyu before returning to defend Feng.

## 11

In the second year of the reign of Qin Ershi, Zhou Zhang, a general under Chen She, led his army westward to Xi but then returned. Yan, Zhao, Qi, and Wei all declared themselves kings independently. The Xiang clan rose in Wu. Qin's commander Jian Ping led troops to besiege Feng; after two days, Liu Ji went out to fight and defeated them. He ordered Yang Chi to defend Feng and personally led the army toward Xue. Jian Zhuang, the governor of Sizhou, was defeated at Xue and fled to Qi. Peigong's left military commander captured Jian Zhuang and killed him. Peigong returned his army to Kangfu, then arrived at Fangyu (Zhou Shi came to attack Fangyu), but no battle had yet taken place. Chen Wang sent the Wei man Zhou Shi to seize territory. Zhou Shi sent someone to tell Yang Chi, "Feng was originally a Liang stronghold that was relocated. Now the Wei forces have already secured dozens of cities in the region." "If you now submit to Wei, Wei will appoint you as a marquis and commander of Feng." "If you refuse, we will massacre the people of Feng." Yang Chi had long been unwilling to submit to Peigong. When Wei invited him, he immediately defected and began defending Feng for Wei. Peigong led his troops to attack Feng but could not capture it. Peigong fell ill and returned to Pei. Peigong resented Yang Chi and the youths of Feng for rebelling against him. Upon hearing that Dongyang Ningjun and Qin Jia had declared Jingju as a provisional king in Liu, he went to join them, hoping to request troops to attack Feng. At that time, Qin's general Zhang Han was at Chen, while the deputy commander Sima Yi led troops northward to pacify the former Chu territories, sacking Xiang and advancing as far as Dang. Dongyang Ningjun and Peigong led their troops westward to fight against them at the western side of Xiao, but they were defeated. They retreated, gathered their forces in Liu, and then led an attack on Dang, capturing it after three days. By recruiting troops from Dang, they gained about five or six thousand soldiers. They attacked Xiayi and captured it. They returned their army to Feng. Hearing that Xiang Liang was in Xue, Peigong rode with more than a hundred mounted men to visit him. Xiang Liang added 5,000 soldiers and ten commanders of the rank of Wudafu to Peigong's forces. Peigong returned, led his troops, and attacked Feng again.

## 12

After following Xiang Liang for more than a month, Xiang Yu had already captured Xiangcheng and returned. Xiang Liang summoned all the other commanders to gather at Xue. Hearing of Chen Wang's certain death, they then enthroned King Huaiwang Sun Xin as the ruler of Chu, establishing his capital at Xuyi. Xiang Liang was given the title Wuxin Jun. After staying for several months, they attacked Kangfu to the north, rescued Donga, and defeated the Qin army. The Qi forces withdrew, so Chu pursued the retreating enemy alone, sending Peigong and Xiang Yu to attack Chengyang separately, which they then sacked. They stationed their army east of Puyang and fought against the Qin forces, defeating them.

## 13

The Qin army rallied again, defending Puyang with a ring of water as protection. The Chu forces withdrew and attacked Dingtao, but failed to capture it. Peigong and Xiang Yu moved westward to attack territory as far as the area below Yongqiu, where they fought against the Qin army, achieving a great victory and killing Li You. They then returned to attack Waihuang, but failed to take it.

## 14

Xiang Liang defeated the Qin army twice and began showing signs of arrogance. Song Yi advised against it, but he did not listen. Qin reinforced Zhang Han's forces, who struck Xiang Liang at night with silent weapons and decisively defeated him at Dingtao. Xiang Liang died in the battle. Peigong and Xiang Yu were attacking Chenliu at that time; upon hearing of Xiang Liang's death, they led their troops eastward together with General Lü. General Lü stationed his army to the east of Pengcheng, Xiang Yu's forces were stationed to the west of Pengcheng, and Peigong's troops were at Dang.

## 15

After Zhang Han had defeated Xiang Liang's army, he considered the Chu forces no longer a threat and thus crossed the Yellow River to attack Zhao in the north, achieving a decisive victory. At that time, Zhao Xie was king; Qin general Wang Li besieged him in Julu City. This is what was known as the army of Hebei.

## 16

In the third year of Qin Ershi's reign, King Huaiwang of Chu, upon learning that Xiang Liang's forces had been defeated, became fearful. He moved his capital from Xuyi to Pengcheng and personally took command of both General Lü Chen's and Xiang Yu's armies. He appointed Peigong as the governor of Dang Jun, bestowed upon him the title of Wu'an Hou, and placed him in command of the troops from Dang Jun. Xiang Yu was granted the title Chang'an Hou and given the designation Lu Gong. Lü Chen was appointed as Siku, while his father Lü Qing became Lingyi.

## 17

Zhao repeatedly requested aid, so King Huaiwang appointed Song Yi as supreme general, Xiang Yu as deputy general, and Fan Zeng as the lowest-ranking general to lead an army northward to rescue Zhao. He ordered Peigong to move westward to seize territory and enter Guan Pass. He made an agreement with the generals: whoever first entered and secured Guanzhong would be declared its king.

## 18

At that time, the Qin forces were strong and often pursued their enemies in victory; none of the generals found it advantageous to enter Guan Pass first. Only Xiang Yu, resentful that Qin had destroyed his father's army, was determined and eager to join Peigong in moving westward into Guan Pass. King Huaiwang's veteran generals all said, "Xiang Yu is a strong and ruthless bandit by nature. "Xiang Yu once attacked Xiangcheng, leaving no survivors there; he buried all the defenders alive. Every place his forces passed through was left in ruins and destroyed." "Moreover, although Chu has advanced several times before, both Chen Wang and Xiang Liang were defeated." "It would be better to send a more experienced and virtuous leader westward with the banner of righteousness, to inform and persuade the elders of Qin." "The people of Qin have long suffered under their ruler. If a virtuous leader were to go there now without committing violence or oppression, the region should submit willingly." "Now Xiang Yu is fierce and ruthless; he cannot be sent." "Only Peigong, who has always been broad-minded and a man of virtue, can be sent." They finally refused to allow Xiang Yu to go and instead dispatched Peigong westward to seize territory, gathering the scattered troops of Chen Wang and Xiang Liang. He took the route from Dang to Chengyang, where he confronted Qin forces at Gangli and defeated two Qin armies. The Chu army launched an attack against Wang Li and decisively crushed his forces.

## 19

Peigong led his troops westward, where he encountered Peng Yue at Changyi and joined him in attacking the Qin forces. However, they suffered an unfavorable battle outcome. They retreated to Li, where they encountered Gangwu Hou and seized his troops—about 4,000 men—and incorporated them into their own forces. Together with Wei general Huang Xin and Wei Shentu Wupu's troops, they attacked Changyi, but failed to capture the city. They moved westward past Gaoyang. Li Shiqi, who served as a gatekeeper, said: "Many generals have passed through here, but I see that Peigong is a tall and noble man of virtue." He then requested an audience to advise Peigong. At that moment, Peigong was sitting cross-legged on a mat, having two women wash his feet. Li Shiqi did not bow but instead performed a long courtesy, saying: "If you truly wish to punish the tyrannical Qin, you should not sit in such an arrogant manner before an elder." At this, Peigong rose, straightened his robe to apologize, and invited him to take a seat of honor. Li Shiqi advised Peigong to attack Chenliu by surprise, where they could seize the accumulated grain stores of Qin. Peigong then appointed Li Shiqi as Guangye Jun, and his relative Li Shang as a general, to command the forces of Chenliu. Together they attacked Kaifeng, but failed to capture it.  They moved westward and fought Qin general Yang Xiong at Baoma, then again east of Quyu, achieving a major victory. Yang Xiong fled to Yingyang, and the Second Emperor sent an envoy to execute him as a warning to others. They then launched a southern attack on Yinyang, which they proceeded to sack. Through Zhang Liang, they gradually seized the territory of Han at Huanyuan.

## 20

At that time, Zhao's deputy general Sima Ang was preparing to cross the Yellow River into Guan Pass; in response, Peigong attacked Pingyin to the north and cut off the river crossings. They then moved south, fighting at the east of Luoyang, where their forces suffered an unfavorable outcome. They retreated to Yangcheng, gathered mounted troops from within the army, and fought against Nanyang governor Yi at Chou Dong, defeating him. They proceeded to seize Nanyang Jun. Governor Yi of Nanyang fled and took refuge in the city of Wan, defending it. Peigong led his forces past Wan and moved westward. Zhang Liang advised, "Although Peigong wishes to enter Guan Pass quickly, the Qin forces are still numerous and occupy strong defensive positions. "If we do not capture Wan now, its defenders will attack us from behind while the powerful Qin forces remain in front of us; this is a dangerous course." Therefore, Peigong led his troops at night through an alternate route, changed their banners, and by dawn had encircled Wan City three times. Governor Yi of Nanyang wanted to commit suicide. His retainer Chen Hui said, "It is not too late to avoid death." He then scaled the city wall to meet Peigong and said, "I have heard that you made an agreement: whoever first enters Xianyang will be declared its king. "Now you are holding Wan in reserve. "Wan is the capital of a major commandery, connected to dozens of walled cities with many people and abundant supplies. The officials believe that surrendering will lead only to death, so they are determinedly defending the city." "If you continue attacking for days on end, many soldiers will be killed or wounded; "if you withdraw your troops from Wan, the city will surely follow you. If you proceed forward, you will lose the agreement to enter Xianyang first; if you turn back, you will face the threat of a strong and determined Wan." "For your sake, there is no better plan than to negotiate their surrender, grant the governor a title, and have him remain in charge. Then you can take his armed forces with you as you move westward." "The other cities that have not yet surrendered will hear of this and rush to open their gates in anticipation. You will then pass through unimpeded, without any obstacles." Peigong said, "Good idea." He then appointed the governor of Wan as Yin Hou and bestowed upon Chen Hui a fief of one thousand households. Leading his troops westward, not a single city refused to surrender. They reached Danshui, where Gao Wu Hou Sai and Xiang Hou Wang Ling surrendered at Xiling. They then returned to attack Huyang, where they encountered Mei Yan, a deputy general of Fanjun; together with him, they captured Xi and Li, which both surrendered. He sent Ning Chang from Wei as an envoy to Qin, but the messenger had not yet returned. At this time, Zhang Han had already surrendered his army to Xiang Yu in Zhao.

## 21

Initially, Xiang Yu and Song Yi had marched north to rescue Zhao. When Xiang Yu killed Song Yi and took his place as supreme general, all the other generals, including Qing Bu, came under his command. He defeated Wang Li's Qin forces and made Zhang Han surrender; all the feudal lords submitted to him. When Zhao Gao had already killed Second Emperor, he sent envoys to come forward and propose a truce, offering to divide the Guanzhong region among them as kings. Peigong suspected the offer was a trick, so he adopted Zhang Liang's plan and sent Lü Sheng and Lu Jia to persuade the Qin generals with promises of profit. Taking advantage of this, they launched a surprise attack on Wuguan Pass and captured it. They then fought the Qin army again to the south of Lantian, further displaying additional deceptive banners and flags. They ordered that no looting or plundering be allowed in all areas they passed through; the Qin people were pleased, and the Qin forces became dispirited, so they decisively crushed them. They fought again to its north and thoroughly defeated the enemy. Leveraging their momentum, they proceeded to crush them.

## 22

In the tenth month of the first year of Han, Peigong's forces arrived at Bashi ahead of all the other feudal lords. Qin Wang Ziying rode a plain cart pulled by white horses, bound his neck with a ribbon, sealed the imperial seal and symbols of authority in boxes, and surrendered on the roadside at Zhi. Some of the generals suggested executing the former Qin king. Peigong said, "Initially, King Huaiwang sent me here precisely because I was known for my tolerance; and now that the man has already surrendered and submitted, to kill him would be an ill omen." He therefore handed the former Qin king over to the officials for custody and proceeded westward into Xianyang. He wanted to stay in the palace for rest and lodging, but Fan Kuai and Zhang Liang advised against it. As a result, he sealed up the treasuries of valuable treasures and returned his troops to Bashi. He summoned the elders, influential local figures, and prominent people from all the counties and said to them, "For a long time now, you elders have suffered under Qin's harsh laws. Those who criticized the government were executed along with their entire clans, and those who spoke in pairs against it were put to death in public." "I have made an agreement with the feudal lords that whoever enters Guan first will be made king of this region, and I should therefore become the king of Guanzhong." He made an agreement with the elders: there would be only three legal provisions: those who kill shall die, those who injure others or commit theft shall receive corresponding punishment. All other laws and regulations of Qin were completely abolished. All officials and common people remained in their positions and homes as before, without disturbance. "I have come here for the purpose of ridding you elders of harm; I do not intend to invade or oppress anyone, so there is no need to be afraid!" "And the reason why I have returned my troops to Bashi is simply to await the arrival of the feudal lords and establish a formal agreement." He then sent people along with Qin officials through the counties, towns, and villages to inform and reassure them. The people of Qin were greatly pleased and competed in bringing cattle, sheep, wine, and food to offer as hospitality for the soldiers. Peigong once again declined to accept these offerings, saying, "There is plenty of grain in the granaries; we are not lacking anything. I do not wish to burden you." The people were even more delighted and feared only that Peigong would not become the king of Qin.

## 23

Some advised Peigong, saying, "Qin is ten times wealthier than all other regions combined and has a strong geographical position. Now we hear that Zhang Han has surrendered to Xiang Yu, who has declared himself King Yong and is set to rule Guanzhong. If he arrives now, Peigong may not be able to retain this region. "We should quickly send troops to defend Hangu Pass, keeping the feudal lords' armies out, and gradually conscript forces from Guanzhong to strengthen ourselves in order to resist him." Peigong approved this plan and followed it. In the middle of the eleventh month, Xiang Yu indeed led the feudal lords' forces westward and intended to enter Guan, but the pass gates were closed. Upon learning that Peigong had already pacified Guanzhong, Xiang Yu was furious and sent Qing Bu to attack and capture Hangu Pass. In the middle of the twelfth month, he arrived at Xi. Peigong's left 司马 Cao Wushang, hearing of King Xiang's anger and his intention to attack Peigong, sent a message to Xiang Yu saying, "Peigong intends to become the king of Guanzhōng, has appointed Ziying as prime minister, and has taken all the treasures for himself." He wanted this information to be used in order to secure a fief. Yafu advised Xiang Yu to attack Peigong. At that time, Peigong was entertaining his soldiers and planned to engage in battle the next day. At this time, Xiang Yu had an army of forty thousand troops, claiming a hundred thousand. Peigong's forces numbered ten thousand soldiers, claiming twenty thousand; they were not strong enough to match the enemy. Coincidentally, Xiang Bo wanted to save Zhang Liang and went to see him at night. He then used diplomatic words to persuade Xiang Yu, and thus Xiang Yu stopped his attack. Peigong led more than a hundred mounted attendants, drove them to Hongmen, and met with Xiang Yu to apologize. Xiang Yu said, "This was what Peigong's left 司马 Cao Wushang told me. Otherwise, why would I have taken such action!" Because of Fan Kuai and Zhang Liang, Peigong was able to be released and return. After returning, he immediately executed Cao Wushang.

## 24

Xiang Yu then proceeded westward, burning and looting the Qin palaces in Xianyang; wherever he passed, there was nothing left but destruction. The people of Qin were deeply disappointed, yet they feared him and dared not resist.

## 25

Xiang Yu sent envoys back to report to King Huaiwang. King Huaiwang said, "As agreed." Xiang Yu resented King Huaiwang for not allowing him to march westward with Peigong into Guan, but instead ordering him north to rescue Zhao and thus arriving later than the agreed time. He said, "King Huaiwang is merely a ruler installed by my relative Xiang Liang; he has no military achievements or merit. How can he claim to be the one who set the agreement! The decision on who rules the world was made by the generals and myself, Xiang Ji." He then pretended to honor King Huaiwang as Yidi, but in reality did not obey his commands.

## 26

In the first month, Xiang Yu declared himself the Hegemon-King of Western Chu and ruled over nine commanderies in Liang and Chu regions, with Pengcheng as his capital. He violated the agreement and instead reappointed Peigong as King of Han, ruling over Ba, Shu, and Hanzhong with Nanzheng as his capital. He divided Guanzhong into three parts and established three former Qin generals: Zhang Han was made King of Yong, with Feiqiu as his capital; Sim Ma Xin was made King of Sai, with Linyang as his capital; Dong Yi was made King of Zhai, with Gaonu as his capital. Chu general Xiaqiu Shenyang was made King of Henan, with Luoyang as his capital. Zhao general Sima Ang was made King of Yin, with Chaoguo as his capital. King Zhao Xie was relocated and made king of Dai. Zhao prime minister Zhang Er was made King of Changshan, with Xianguo as his capital. Dangyang Jun Qing Bu was made King of Jiujiang, with Liu as his capital. King Huaiwang's Zhugu Gu Ao was made King of Linjiang, with Jiangling as his capital. Fanjun Wu Rui was made King of Hengshan, with Zhu as his capital. Yan general Zang Tu was made King of Yan, with Ji as his capital. The former King of Yan Han Guang was relocated and made king of Liaodong. Han Guang refused to comply, so Zang Tu attacked and killed him at Wuzhong. Cheng'an Jun Chen Yu was granted three counties in Hejian, with his residence at Nanpi. Mei Yan was granted a fief of 100,000 households.

## 27

In the fourth month, after the troops dispersed from Xixia, the feudal lords each returned to their respective states. When King of Han departed for his state, Xiang Yu sent 30,000 soldiers to accompany him. In addition, tens of thousands of people from Chu and the feudal lords who admired and followed him also went along, entering Shizhong through the south of Du. They burned and destroyed the 栈道 (ladder-like roads) behind them as they went, to prevent feudal lords' troops from attacking from behind. This also served to show Xiang Yu that they had no intention of returning eastward. When they arrived at Nanzheng, many generals and soldiers along the way deserted and returned home; the troops all sang songs expressing their longing to return east. Han Xin advised King of Han, saying, "Xiang Yu has granted the title of king to those among his generals who have achieved merit, yet you alone remain in Nanzheng. This is akin to exile. The officers and soldiers are all from the east of the mountain (Shandong), and day and night they long to return home. If we take advantage of their eagerness, we can achieve great success. Once the world is pacified, people will all seek peace and stability; they cannot be used again for war. "Therefore, it would be better to make a decisive plan to face eastward and contend for power over the entire world."

## 28

Xiang Yu left Guan and sent people to relocate Yidi. He said, "In ancient times, emperors ruled a thousand li of territory and always resided upstream." He then sent envoys to move Yidi to Chenzhou, Changsha, urging him to leave quickly. As his ministers gradually turned against and betrayed him, Xiang Yu secretly ordered King of Hengshan and King of Linjiang to attack him, killing Yidi in the southern regions of the Yangtze River. Xiang Yu harbored resentment against Tian Rong and appointed the Qi general Tian Du as King of Qi. Tian Rong was furious, so he declared himself King of Qi, killed Tian Du, and rebelled against Chu; He gave Peng Yue a general's seal and ordered him to rebel in the Liang region. Chu sent Xiao Gong Jiao to attack Peng Yue, but Peng Yue thoroughly defeated him. Chen Yu resented Xiang Yu for not making him a king and sent Xia Shuo to persuade Tian Rong, requesting troops to attack Zhang Er. Qi provided Chen Yu with an army, which defeated King Changshan Zhang Er; Zhang Er fled back to Han. They retrieved King Zhao Xie from Dai and reinstalled him as the King of Zhao. The King of Zhao then appointed Chen Yu as the King of Dai. Xiang Yu was greatly enraged and launched a northern campaign against Qi.

## 29

In the eighth month, King of Han used General Han Xin's strategy to return via the old road and launched a surprise attack on Wang Zhanghan, King of Yong. Zhanghan met them at Chen Cang and attacked Han forces, but the army of Yong was defeated and retreated; They halted for battle in Zhaochi, were again defeated, and fled to Feiqiu. King of Han then pacified the territory of Yong. He advanced east to Xianyang, led his troops to besiege Feiqiu where King of Yong was located, and dispatched various generals to pacify Longxi, Beidi, and Shangjun. He ordered the generals Xue Ou and Wang Xi to exit through Wuguan, join forces with Wang Ling's troops in Nanyang, and go to Pei to welcome his father-in-law Taigong and Empress Lu Hou. Chu heard of this and sent troops to resist them at Yangxia, preventing their advance. He appointed Zheng Chang, former magistrate of Wu, as King of Han, to resist the Han forces.

## 30

In the second year, King of Han advanced eastward to secure territory; Sai Wang Xin, Zhai Wang Yi, and Henan Wang Shen Yang all surrendered. King of Han Chang did not comply, so Han Xin was sent to attack and defeated him. At this time, the commanderies of Longxi, Beidi, Shangjun, Weinan, Heshang, and Zhongdi were established; A Henan Commandery was also set up outside the passes. He reappointed Han Taivwei Xin as King of Han. Generals who surrendered with ten thousand men or a single commandery were granted the title of Wanhulu (Ten-thousand-householder). They repaired and reinforced the defenses at Heshang. All former Qin parks, gardens, and ponds were opened for people to cultivate. In the first month, Zhang Ping, younger brother of King of Yong, was captured. A general pardon of criminals was declared.

## 31

When King of Han left the pass and arrived at Shan, he comforted the elders outside the pass. Upon his return, Zhang Er came to pay homage, and King of Han treated him with great courtesy.

## 32

In February, an order was issued to abolish the Qin state altars and replace them with those of Han.

## 33

In March, King of Han crossed from Linjin, and Wang Bao, King of Wei, led troops to accompany him. They captured Heinei, captured Yin Wang, and established the Heinei Commandery. They crossed south at Pingyinjin ford and arrived at Luoyang. Sanlao Dong Gong of Xincheng intercepted King of Han to advise him about the death of Yidi, citing righteousness. King of Han heard this and wept aloud while uncovering his upper body in grief. He then mourned for Yidi, holding a three-day mourning period. He dispatched envoys to inform the feudal lords: "The whole world jointly established Yidi as emperor, and all faced north to serve him. Now Xiang Yu has wantonly killed Yidi in the south of the Yangtze, committing great treason and acting without morality." "I personally mourn for him; all feudal lords should wear plain white mourning attire. I will mobilize all troops within the passes, recruit soldiers from the Three Rivers region, float down the Yangtze and Han rivers to the south, and wish to join feudal lords in attacking Chu for killing Yidi."

## 34

At this time Xiang Wang was campaigning north against Qi, where Tian Rong fought him at Chengyang. Tian Rong suffered a defeat and fled to Pingyuan, where the local people killed him. All of Qi surrendered to Chu. Chu then burned their cities and towns, capturing and enslaving their men and women. The people of Qi rebelled against them. Tian Rong's younger brother Heng installed Rong's son Guang as King of Qi, and the new King of Qi rebelled against Chu at Chengyang. Although Xiang Yu had heard that Han was advancing eastward, since he was already engaged with the Qi forces, he wished to first crush them before attacking Han. King of Han thus managed to rally the armies of five feudal lords and entered Pengcheng. Xiang Yu, upon hearing this, withdrew his troops from Qi and marched out of Lu through Huling to Xiao. There he engaged in a major battle with Han forces at the east bank of Sui River near Lingbi, Pengcheng, thoroughly defeating the Han army, killing so many soldiers that the river was blocked by their bodies and could not flow for some time. He captured King of Han's parents, wife, and children from Pei and held them in his army as hostages. At this time, when the feudal lords saw that Chu was strong and Han had suffered a defeat, they all withdrew from Han and returned to supporting Chu. Sai Wang Xin fled into Chu.

## 35

Zhou Lühou, the elder brother of Empress Lu Hou and a Han general commanding troops, was stationed in Xiayi. King of Han followed him, gradually recruiting soldiers, and established his army at Dang. King of Han then passed westward through the territory of Liang and arrived at Yu. He sent a court official, Sui He, to King of Jiangyou Wang Bu and said: "If you can persuade Wang Bu to raise his troops in rebellion against Chu, Xiang Yu will have to remain behind to attack him. Then I shall surely be able to secure the whole world within a few months." Sui He went to persuade King of Jiangyou Bu, and indeed, Bu turned against Chu. 龙头 Chu sent Long Ju to attack him.

## 36

After King of Han was defeated at Pengcheng and fled westward, he sent people along the way to search for his family, but they had also perished or gone missing, so they could not be found. After the defeat, he was able to find Xiao Hui alone. In June, he was established as Crown Prince, and a general pardon of criminals was issued. He ordered the crown prince to defend Linyang, and all sons of feudal lords in Guanzhong were gathered at Linyang for protection. They diverted water to flood Feiqiu; Feiqiu surrendered, and Zhang Han committed suicide. The name of Feiqiu was changed to Huaili. At this time, he ordered the ritual officials to worship Heaven and Earth, the Four Directions' Supreme Deities, mountains, and rivers, offering sacrifices at appropriate times. He mobilized conscripts within the passes to reinforce the frontier defenses.

## 37

At this time, King of Jiangyou Bu fought against Long Ju and was defeated; he fled with Sui He back to Han in secret. King of Han gradually recruited soldiers and, together with his generals and troops from Guanzhong, launched more offensives. As a result, the Han forces were greatly revitalized at Xingyang and defeated Chu between Jing and Suo.

## 38

In the third year, King of Wei Bao paid a visit to see his ailing parents but upon arriving immediately cut off river crossings and rebelled in support of Chu. King of Han sent Li Sheng to persuade Bao, who refused to listen. King of Han dispatched General Han Xin to attack and thoroughly defeated him, capturing Bao. He then pacified the territory of Wei and established three commanderies: Hedong, Taiyuan, and Shangdang. King of Han then ordered Zhang Er to join Han Xin in advancing eastward through Jingxing to attack Zhao, where they killed Chen Yu and King Zhao Xie. The following year, Zhang Er was enfeoffed as the King of Zhao.

## 39

King of Han's army stationed south of Xingyang, built a causeway connecting to the Yellow River, in order to seize the Ao granary. They confronted each other for over a year. Xiang Yu repeatedly raided and seized the Han causeway, causing the Han army to suffer from food shortages, so he besieged King of Han. King of Han requested peace, offering to cede all territory west of Xingyang to Han. Xiang Wang did not agree. King of Han was worried and thus adopted Chen Ping's strategy, giving him 40,000 jin of gold to sow discord between the Chu ruler and his ministers. As a result, Xiang Yu began to suspect Yafu (Fan Zeng). At this time, Yafu had advised Xiang Yu to capture Xingyang immediately. When he saw that his advice was not trusted and suspicion arose, he became furious, claimed old age, requested permission to retire from service, and return to the ranks of common soldiers. He died before reaching Pengcheng.

## 40

With their food supplies cut off, the Han army sent out more than two thousand women at night from the eastern gate wearing armor; Chu forces attacked them on all sides. General Ji Xin then took the king's chariot, impersonating King of Han to deceive Chu. The Chu forces all shouted "Ten thousand years!" and gathered at the eastern city gate to watch, allowing King of Han to escape with dozens of horsemen through the western gate. He ordered Zhou Ke, the Minister of Justice, Wei Bao, and Zong Gong to defend Xingyang. The soldiers and generals who could not follow him were all left behind in the city. Zhou Ke and Zong Gong said to each other, "A king who has rebelled against his country is hard to rely on for defending the city." They then killed Wei Bao.

## 41

When King of Han escaped from Xingyang and entered the passes, he gathered troops with the intention to advance eastward again. Yuan Sheng advised King of Han: "For several years, Han and Chu have been confronting each other at Xingyang, but Han has often been in a difficult situation. I wish Your Majesty would exit through Wuguan; Xiang Yu will surely lead his troops southward. You can then take up a strong defensive position, allowing the forces between Xingyang and Chenggao to rest for a while. Send Han Xin and others to pacify the territory of Zhao in Hebei, link up with Yan and Qi, then Your Majesty may return to Xingyang; it will not be too late. In this way, Chu's defenses would be stretched thin and their forces divided; Han could rest and then fight again. With such an advantage, defeating Chu is certain." King of Han followed his strategy, moving his army between Wan and Ye to gather forces with Qing Bu.

## 42

Xiang Yu heard that King of Han was in Wan and indeed led his troops southward. King of Han held a strong defensive position and did not engage in battle. At this time, Peng Yue crossed the Sui River and fought Xiang Sheng and Xue Gong at Xiaopi; Peng Yue thoroughly defeated the Chu army. Xiang Yu then led his troops eastward to attack Peng Yue. King of Han also led his army north and stationed it at Chenggao. After defeating and driving away Peng Yue, Xiang Yu heard that King of Han had again stationed his army at Chenggao. He then led his troops westward once more, captured Xingyang, executed Zhou Ke and Zong Gong, took the King of Han, Wang Xin, prisoner, and proceeded to besiege Chenggao.

## 43

King of Han fled, escaping alone with Teng Gong in a cart through the Yumen Gate of Chenggao, crossed northward over the Yellow River, and hurried to spend the night at Xiuwu. He claimed to be a messenger and rode in early morning into the camp of Zhang Er and Han Xin, seizing their army. He then sent Zhang Er north to raise more troops from the territory of Zhao, and dispatched Han Xin eastward to attack Qi. With the forces of Han Xin at his command, King of Han regained strength. King of Han led his troops to the Yellow River and held a banquet for his army south of Xiaoxiuwu, intending to fight again. The Attendant-in-Ordinary Zheng Zhong then advised King of Han not to engage in battle, suggesting instead that he build high ramparts and deep trenches. King of Han heeded his advice and dispatched Lu Wan and Liu Jia with twenty thousand infantry and hundreds of cavalry to cross at Baoma Jin, enter Chu territory, and join Peng Yue in defeating the Chu army west of Yange. They then recaptured more than ten cities in Liang territory.

## 44

After receiving his orders to move eastward, Huaiyin had not yet crossed the Pingyuan region. King of Han sent Li Sheng to persuade King Tian Guang of Qi, who then defected from Chu and made peace with Han, agreeing to jointly attack Xiang Yu. Han Xin followed Kuaitong's strategy and launched a surprise attack that broke through Qi's defenses. King of Qi boiled Li Sheng alive, then fled east to Gaomi. Xiang Yu learned that Han Xin had already raised the forces of Hebei and defeated Qi and Zhao, and was about to attack Chu. Thus, he sent Long Ju and Zhou Lan to counter him. Han Xin engaged in battle with them, while the cavalry general Guan Ying attacked, thoroughly defeating the Chu army and killing Long Ju. King Tian Guang of Qi fled to Peng Yue. At this time, Peng Yue was commanding his troops in the territory of Liang, constantly harassing the Chu forces and cutting off their supply lines.

## 45

In the fourth year, Xiang Yu said to Cao Jiu, the Taishi Ma of Haichun Hou: "Guard Chenggao carefully. If Han provokes a battle, be cautious and do not engage in combat; just prevent them from advancing eastward." I will definitely secure the territory of Liang within fifteen days, then return to join you again." He then marched to attack Chenliu, Waihuang, and Suyang, capturing them. Han indeed repeatedly provoked the Chu army, but the Chu forces did not respond. After several days of being insulted by Han's envoys, the Taishi Ma became angry and led his troops across the Si River. When half of the soldiers had crossed, Han attacked them, thoroughly defeating the Chu forces and seizing all the gold, jade, and valuables from the Chu state. The Taishi Ma Cao Jiu and the Chief Clerk Zhang Xin both committed suicide in the Si River. When Xiang Yu arrived at Suyang and heard that Lord Haichun had been defeated, he withdrew his troops. The Han army was just encircling Zhongli Mi east of Xingyang when Xiang Yu arrived, and the Han forces retreated to dangerous and difficult terrain.

## 46

After Han Xin had defeated Qi, he sent a messenger who said: "Qi borders Chu and is of great strategic importance. If I am not granted the title of King, I fear it will be difficult to pacify Qi." King of Han wanted to attack him. Liu Hou said: "It would be better to take advantage of the situation and appoint him as such, allowing him to govern Qi on his own." He then sent Zhang Liang with the seal and sash to bestow upon Han Xin the title of King of Qi.

## 47

When Xiang Yu heard that Long Ju's army had been defeated, he became fearful and sent Wu She, a native of Xutai, to persuade Han Xin. Han Xin did not listen.

## 48

The prolonged stalemate between Chu and Han had yet to be resolved; the strong and vigorous were exhausted from military service, while the elderly and weak were worn out by transporting provisions. King of Han and Xiang Yu met between Guangwu to speak with each other in person. Xiang Yu wanted to challenge King of Han personally in single combat. King of Han listed Xiang Yu's offenses, saying: "Initially, both I and Xiang Yu received orders from King Huaiwang, who declared that whoever first entered and secured Guanzhong would be made its king. Xiang Yu violated the agreement by making me king over Shu and Han instead; this is his first crime. "Xiang Yu forged an order to kill Qingzi, the Duke of Guanjun, and elevated himself; this is his second crime. "After Xiang Yu had rescued Zhao, he should have returned to report back, but instead seized the forces of the feudal lords and entered Guan without authorization; this is his third crime. "King Huaiwang had decreed that upon entering Qin, there should be no looting or violence. Xiang Yu burned the palaces of Qin, dug up the tomb of Emperor Shi Huangdi, and secretly seized its treasures; this is his fourth crime. "He also brutally killed Ziying, the surrendered prince of Qin; this is his fifth crime. "He deceitfully buried alive twenty thousand young men and sons from Qin at Xin'an, while making their generals kings; this is his sixth crime. "Xiang Yu granted the best territories to his favored generals, displacing and driving out their original lords, causing his subordinates to rebel; this is his seventh crime. "Xiang Yu expelled King Yi from Pengcheng and made it his own capital, seizing the territory of King Han and unifying Liang and Chu under himself, giving much to himself; this is his eighth crime. "Xiang Yu sent men secretly to assassinate King Yi in Jiangnan; this is his ninth crime. "As a subject, he assassinated his lord and killed surrendered soldiers; as a ruler, he governed unjustly and violated agreements. Such actions are unacceptable to the world—utterly rebellious and inhumane; this is his tenth crime." "I am leading a righteous army with the feudal lords to punish cruel tyrants. I will send convicts and criminals to kill Xiang Yu—why should I personally trouble you, General, by challenging him in single combat!" Xiang Yu became furious and shot an arrow from a hidden crossbow that struck King of Han. King of Han was wounded in the chest, but he touched his foot instead and said: "The 虏 has only hit my toe!" King of Han lay ill from his wound, but Zhang Liang strongly urged him to rise and visit the troops in person, so as to reassure the soldiers and prevent Chu from taking advantage of Han's weakness. King of Han went out to inspect his troops but fell seriously ill; he therefore hurried into Chenggao for rest.

## 49

After recovering from his illness, King of Han moved west into Guan and arrived at Linyang. He visited the elders to show concern, held a banquet, and displayed the head of former Sai Wang Xin on a pole in the market square of Linyang. He stayed for four days before returning to his army at Guangwu. More troops from Guanzhong were dispatched.

## 50

At this time, Peng Yue commanded his forces in the territory of Liang and constantly harassed the Chu army, cutting off its supply lines. Tian Heng went to join him. Xiang Yu repeatedly attacked Peng Yue and others, while King Tian Xin of Qi also advanced against Chu. Xiang Yu, fearing the combined pressure, made a truce with King of Han and agreed to divide the empire: all territory west of Honggou would belong to Han, while that east of it would be under Chu. Xiang Wang returned King of Han's parents and wife and children to him, and the entire Han army cheered "Long live!" before parting ways.

## 51

Xiang Yu withdrew his forces and retreated eastward. King of Han initially wanted to withdraw westward, but following the strategy of Liu Hou and Chen Ping, he advanced his troops in pursuit of Xiang Yu. His army halted at the southern part of Yangxia, where he arranged a meeting with King Tian Xin of Qi and Lord Chengjian Hou Peng Yue to jointly attack the Chu forces. When they arrived at Guling, however, the appointed allies did not show up for the rendezvous. The Chu forces attacked the Han army and thoroughly defeated them. King of Han retreated back into his fortress, dug deep trenches, and took up a strong defensive position. Following Zhang Liang's strategy, King of Han summoned both Han Xin and Peng Yue, who then arrived as planned. Meanwhile, Liu Jia entered Chu territory and besieged Shouchun. After King of Han defeated the forces at Bilin, he sent envoys to summon Zhou Yin, the Taishi Ma, to raise troops from Jiangyou and meet him as King Wuwang. They proceeded to massacre Chengfu along the way. Liu Jia, together with the feudal lords of Qi and Liang, all gathered in Gaixia for a decisive confrontation. King Wuwang Bu was appointed as King of Huainan.

## 52

In the fifth year, Gaozu and his allied feudal lords' forces jointly attacked the Chu army to decide the final victory against Xiang Yu at Gaixia. The Marquis of Huaiyin, Han Xin, led thirty thousand troops to face the enemy directly. General Kong was stationed on the left, and General Fei on the right, with the Emperor in reserve behind them, followed by Lord Jianghou and General Chai. Xiang Yu's forces numbered about ten thousand. Han Xin first engaged the enemy, but suffered initial setbacks and withdrew slightly. Generals Kong and Fei then launched a flanking attack, which put the Chu forces at a disadvantage. Taking advantage of this, the Marquis of Huaiyin counterattacked and decisively defeated them at Gaixia. When Xiang Yu's soldiers heard the Han army singing Chu songs, they believed that Han had already captured all of Chu territory. As a result, Xiang Yu retreated in disorder, leading to his complete defeat. He dispatched the cavalry general Guan Ying to pursue and kill Xiang Yu at Dongcheng, beheading eight thousand of his soldiers. Thus, Han gradually pacified and secured the territory of Chu. Lu remained staunchly defended by the remnants of Chu forces and could not be captured easily. King of Han led his allied feudal lords' troops northward, showed the people of Lu Xiang Yu's head, and thus Lu surrendered. He was then buried in Gucheng with the title "Lu Gong," a respectful honor for Xiang Yu. Returning to Dingtao, he rushed into King Tian Xin's camp and seized his army.

## 53

In the first month, the feudal lords along with generals and ministers jointly petitioned to bestow the title of Emperor upon King of Han. King of Han said: "I have heard that the title of Emperor is reserved for those who are virtuous. To accept it on mere words and empty promises would be unworthy; I dare not assume such a position." The ministers all said: "Your Majesty rose from humble origins, punished tyrants and rebels, and pacified the four seas. Those who have rendered service are always rewarded with fiefs and titles as kings or marquises. If Your Majesty does not assume the imperial title, all will be doubtful and lose faith." "We ministers are willing to lay down our lives for this cause." King of Han made three refusals, but finally yielded and said: "If all of you believe it to be beneficial for the state, then I shall accept." On the Jiawu day, he ascended the throne as emperor at the southern bank of the Si River.

## 54

The Emperor declared that King Yi had no descendants. King Tian Xin of Qi, being familiar with the customs of Chu, was transferred to become King of Chu and established his capital at Xiaopi. Lord Chengjian Hou Peng Yue was enfeoffed as King of Liang with his capital in Dingtao. The former King Han Xin remained as King of Han, with his capital at Yangzhi. Lord Hengshan Wang Wu Rui was transferred and enfeoffed as the King of Changsha, with his capital in Linxiang. Fanjun's general Mei Xuan had rendered meritorious service by following the Emperor into Wu Pass, so the Emperor showed kindness to Fanjun. King Huainan Wang Bu, King Yan Wang Zang Tu, and King Zhao Wang Ao all remained in their original positions.

## 55

The empire was now at peace. Emperor Gaozu established his capital in Luoyang, and all the feudal lords became his subjects. Therefore, King Linjiang Wang Huan rebelled against Han on behalf of Xiang Yu; the Emperor ordered Lu Wan and Liu Jia to besiege him, but they could not capture the city. After several months he surrendered, and was executed in Luoyang.

## 56

In the fifth month, all troops were disbanded and sent back home. Feudal lords' sons residing in Guanzhong would be exempt from taxes for twelve years; those returning to their fiefs would be exempt for six years, and provided with food for one year.

## 57

Emperor Gaozu held a banquet at the Nangong Palace in Luoyang. Emperor Gaozu said: "All marquises and generals must not conceal anything from me, but speak the truth." "Why have I been able to gain control of the empire?" "And why did the Xiang clan lose the empire?" Gao Qi and Wang Ling replied: "Your Majesty is arrogant and disrespectful to others, while Xiang Yu was kind and loved his people. Yet Your Majesty sends men to attack cities and seize territory, and grants the surrendered lands to them; thus sharing benefits with all under heaven." "Xiang Yu was jealous of the virtuous and talented, he resented those who had rendered service and doubted the worthy. He claimed victories without giving credit to others, and seized territory without sharing its benefits; this is why he lost the empire." Emperor Gaozu said: "You know only one aspect, but not the other. 鲰 In devising strategies within the tent and determining victory from a distance of thousands of li, I am no match for Zi Fang. In stabilizing the state, pacifying the people, supplying provisions, and ensuring unbroken supply routes, I am no match for Xiao He. In commanding an army of a hundred thousand men, in battles always achieving victory and in sieges always capturing the enemy's position, I am no match for Han Xin. These three are all outstanding men; I have been able to use them, and this is why I have gained the empire. "Xiang Yu had one Fan Zeng but was unable to make proper use of him; that is why he fell into my hands."

## 58

Emperor Gaozu wanted to permanently establish his capital in Luoyang. A native of Qi named Liu Jing advised, and the Marquis Liu Bang was persuaded by the Retainer Hou to move the capital to Guanzhong; on that day, Emperor Gaozu set out for Guanzhong and established it as the imperial capital. In the sixth month, a general amnesty was proclaimed throughout the empire.

## 59

In the tenth month, King Yan Wang Zang Tu rebelled and captured Dai territory. Emperor Gaozu personally led an army to attack him and captured King Yan Wang Zang Tu. He immediately enfeoffed the Taiwei Lu Wan as King of Yan. He ordered Chancellor Cao Kui to lead troops in an attack on Dai.

## 60

That autumn, Li Ji rebelled; Emperor Gaozu personally led the army against him and Li Ji fled. Li Ji was a general of the Xiang clan. After the defeat of the Xiang clan, Li Ji became a retainer of Lord Chen and did not follow Xiang Yu. He fled and surrendered to Emperor Gaozu, who enfeoffed him as Marquis of Yingchuan. When Emperor Gaozu arrived in Luoyang and summoned Li Ji by reviewing the register of Tong Hou, Li Ji became fearful and thus rebelled.

## 61

In the sixth year, Emperor Gaozu paid homage to his father, Lord Tai Gong, once every five days, observing the rites of a son toward his father within a family. The household official advised Lord Tai Gong: "Heaven has no two suns, and the land has no two kings. "Now although Gaozu is your son, he is the sovereign of the people; "although you are his father, you are a subject. "How can one allow the sovereign to bow before a subject!" "If this continues, imperial authority will not be respected." Later, when Emperor Gaozu came to pay homage, Lord Tai Gong held a broom and met him at the gate, retreating backward. Emperor Gaozu was greatly surprised and got down from his carriage to support Lord Tai Gong. Lord Tai Gong said: "The Emperor is the sovereign of all people; how can I allow my personal status to disrupt the laws of the empire!" Thus, Emperor Gaozu honored Lord Tai Gong as Taishang Huang (the Grand Emperor Emeritus). He was secretly pleased with the advice of his household official and bestowed five hundred catties of gold upon him.

## 62

In the twelfth month, someone reported a change in circumstances, accusing King Chu Wang Xin of plotting rebellion. The Emperor consulted his attendants, who all vied to attack him. Following Chen Ping's strategy, the Emperor pretended to tour Yunmeng and convened a meeting with the feudal lords in Chen. When King Chu Wang Xin came to greet him, he was immediately arrested on the spot. On that day, a general amnesty was declared throughout the empire. Tian Ken congratulated him and took the opportunity to advise Emperor Gaozu: "Your Majesty has captured Han Xin, and now you have pacified Qinzhong. "Qin is a country of strategic advantage, with natural barriers of rivers and mountains, spanning a thousand li in isolation. With a hundred thousand soldiers armed with halberds, Qin could control the region with just one out of every two men." "The terrain is convenient and advantageous; when sending troops to attack the feudal lords, it would be like pouring water from a high rooftop—swift and unstoppable." "As for Qi, to the east it has the wealth of Langya and Jimo; to the south, the strength of Mount Tai; to the west, the natural barrier of the Zhu He River; and to the north, the advantages of Bohai Bay. "It spans two thousand li, with a hundred thousand soldiers armed with halberts, isolated by a distance of a thousand li; Qi could control the region with one out of every twelve men." "Therefore, this is the eastern counterpart to Qin." "If not a close relative or son of His Majesty, no one else should be allowed to rule Qi as king." Emperor Gaozu replied: "Well said." He bestowed five hundred catties of yellow gold.

## 63

More than ten days later, Han Xin was enfeoffed as Marquis Huaiyin, and his territory was divided into two states. Emperor Gaozu declared that General Liu Jia had rendered repeated services and enfeoffed him as King of Jing, ruling over Huaidong. His younger brother Liu Jiao was made King of Chu, ruling over Huaixi. Liu Fei was enfeoffed as the Prince of Qi, ruling more than seventy cities; all people who spoke in the language of Qi were subject to Qi. Then he evaluated their merits and conferred titles upon the marquises with engraved jade tokens as proof. King Han Xin was transferred to Taiyuan.

## 64

In the seventh year, the Xiongnu attacked King Han Xin at Maiyi; as a result, Xin conspired with them and rebelled in Taiyuan. Baitu Manqiu Chen and Wang Huang enfeoffed Zhao Li, a former general of the State of Zhao, as king to lead a rebellion. Emperor Gaozu personally went to attack them. However, it happened to be very cold at the time, and two or three out of every ten soldiers lost their fingers from frostbite. The army thus proceeded as far as Pingcheng. The Xiongnu surrounded our forces at Pingcheng, and it took seven days before they finally withdrew. He ordered Fan Kuai to pacify the territory of Dai. His elder brother Liu Zhong was enfeoffed as Prince of Dai.

## 65

In the second month, Emperor Gaozu passed through Zhao and Luoyang on his way from Pingcheng to Chang'an. The Changle Palace was completed, and officials down to the rank of Chancellor moved their offices to Chang'an.

## 66

In the eighth year, Emperor Gaozu marched eastward to attack the remaining rebels loyal to King Han Xin at Dongyuan.

## 67

Chancellor Xiao He oversaw the construction of Weiyang Palace, building the East Gate, North Gate, Front Hall, Armory, and Grand Granary. When Emperor Gaozu returned, he saw the grandeur of the palace and gates and became angry. He said to Xiao He: "After years of bitter warfare that has tormented the people throughout the empire, victory or defeat was still uncertain. Why have you built such excessive palaces?" Xiao He replied: "The empire is not yet stable; therefore, it is appropriate to take advantage of the situation and complete the palace. "Moreover, the Son of Heaven takes all under heaven as his home; without grandeur and splendor, imperial authority cannot be respected. Furthermore, we must not allow future generations to surpass us in this regard." Emperor Gaozu then smiled with approval.

## 68

When Emperor Gaozu traveled to Dongyuan, he passed through Bairen. Zhao's prime minister Guan Gao and others plotted to assassinate him; sensing something was wrong, the Emperor felt uneasy and decided not to stay there. Prince of Dai Liu Zhong abandoned his state and fled, returning voluntarily to Luoyang; he was deposed and enfeoffed instead as Marquis Heyang.

## 69

In the ninth year, the plot by Zhao's prime minister Guan Gao was uncovered, and he was executed along with three generations of his family. King Zhao Wang Ao was deposed and enfeoffed as Marquis Xuanping. In this year, noble families from the states of Chu—Zhuang, Qu, Jing, Huai—and the Qi Tian clan were relocated to Guanzhong.

## 70

The Weiyang Palace was completed. Emperor Gaozu convened a grand audience with the feudal lords and ministers, holding a banquet in the Front Hall of Weiyang Palace. Emperor Gaozu raised a jade cup and stood to offer it as a toast to the Taishang Huang, saying: "Initially, my father often considered me a good-for-nothing who could not manage property or work as diligently as my elder brother Liu Zhong. "Now, whose achievements are greater—mine or those of my elder brother?" The ministers in the hall all cried out "Ten thousand years!" and laughed joyfully.

## 71

In the tenth year, in the tenth month, King Huainan Wang Qing Bu, King Liang Wang Peng Yue, King Yan Wang Lu Wan, King Jing Wang Liu Jia, King Chu Wang Liu Jiao, King Qi Wang Liu Fei, and King Changsha Wang Wu Rui all came to pay homage at Changle Palace. There were no major events in spring and summer.

## 72

In the seventh month, Taishang Huang passed away at Linyang Palace. Kings of Chu and Liang both came to attend his funeral. The prisoners in Linyang were pardoned. He changed the name of Li Yi to Xin Feng.

## 73

In August, Chancellor Chen Xi of Zhao rebelled in Dai territory. The emperor said: "Xi once served as my envoy and was very trustworthy. "Dai territory is of vital importance to me, so I made Xi a marquis and appointed him as Chancellor in charge of Dai. Now he has joined Wang Huang and others to plunder Dai territory!" The officials and people of Dai territory are not guilty. Therefore, pardon the officials and people of Dai." In September, the emperor personally went east to attack him. Upon arriving at Handan, the emperor said joyfully: "Xi did not take control of Handan to the south and block the Zhang River. I now know he is incapable of much." Learning that Xi's generals were all former merchants, the emperor said: "Now I understand how to deal with them." He therefore gave much gold to bribe Xi's generals, many of whom surrendered.

## 74

In the eleventh year, while Emperor Gaozu was in Handan and had not yet finished executing Xi and others, Xi's general Hou Chang led more than 10,000 men on a raid. Wang Huang's forces were stationed at Quni, and Zhang Chun crossed the Yellow River to attack Liaocheng. The Han dynasty sent General Guo Meng along with Qi's generals to attack them, achieving a decisive victory. Grand Marshal Zhou Bo took the route through Taiyuan and entered, pacifying Dai territory. When they reached Mayi, the defenders of Mayi did not surrender, so they attacked and devastated it.

## 75

Xi's general Zhao Li defended Dongyuan; Emperor Gaozu attacked but could not capture it. After more than a month, the defenders insulted Emperor Gaozu, which enraged him. When the city surrendered, he ordered that those who had cursed be executed and those who did not curse be pardoned. At this time, they divided the area north of Shandong in Zhao and established Liu Heng as King of Dai, with his capital at Jinyang.

## 76

In spring, Marquis Huaiyin Han Xin plotted rebellion in Guanzhong and was executed along with three generations of his family.

## 77

In summer, King Liang Peng Yue plotted rebellion, was deposed, and exiled to Shu; Later he again planned to rebel, so his three generations were executed. Liu Hui was enfeoffed as King of Liang and Liu You as King of Huaiyang.

## 78

In autumn, the seventh month, King Huainan Qing Bu rebelled, moving east to annex the territory of King Jing Liu Jia and crossing northward over the Huai River. King Chu Liu Jiao fled into Xue. Emperor Gaozu personally went to attack him. Liu Chang was enfeoffed as the King of Huainan.

## 79

In the twelfth year, in October, Emperor Gaozu had already defeated Qing Bu's forces at Huizhou. Qing Bu fled, and orders were given to other generals to pursue him.

## 80

Emperor Gaozu returned home and passed through Pei, where he stayed. He held a banquet at the Pei Palace and summoned all his old friends, elders, brothers, and younger siblings to drink freely. He selected 120 boys from Pei and taught them songs. When the wine was flowing freely, Emperor Gaozu played the zhu and composed a song himself: "The great wind rises, clouds fly aloft; My might extends across the realm as I return to my native land. How can I find valiant warriors to guard the four directions!" He ordered the boys to learn and harmonize with it. Emperor Gaozu then rose to dance, his emotions stirred by a mix of pride and sorrow, tears streaming down his face in several lines. He said to the elders and brothers of Pei: "A wandering son laments for his native land." "Although I have made Guanzhong my capital, after ten thousand years, my soul will still find joy and yearn for Pei." "And since I began as the Magistrate of Pei to punish tyrants and rebels, thus attaining the empire, let Pei be my fief for ancestral rites. Exempt its people from taxes and corvée labor, generation after generation, without exception." The elders, brothers, mothers, and old friends of Pei rejoiced daily with drinking to the utmost delight, recalling past events for amusement and laughter. After more than ten days, Emperor Gaozu wanted to leave, but the elders and brothers of Pei earnestly requested him to stay. Emperor Gaozu said: "My entourage is large; my uncles and elder brothers cannot provide for them." He then departed. The entire county of Pei, emptied of its resources, contributed to the western fief. Emperor Gaozu stayed again and held a banquet for three days. The elders and brothers of Pei all kowtowed and said: "Pei is fortunate to have been restored, but Feng has not yet been restored. We humbly beg Your Majesty's compassion and mercy." Emperor Gaozu said: "Feng is where I was born and raised, so I have never forgotten it. I only refrained from restoring it because of the reason that Yong Chi rebelled against me to support Wei." The elders and brothers of Pei persistently pleaded, so he also restored Feng, granting it the same privileges as Pei. At this time, Liu Bi, Marquis of Pei, was enfeoffed as king of Wu.

## 81

Han generals separately attacked Qing Bu's forces on both sides of the Tao River, achieving decisive victories and eventually pursuing and beheading him in Poyang.

## 82

Fan Kuai led a separate force to pacify Dai and killed Chen Xi at Dangcheng.

## 83

In the eleventh month, Emperor Gaozu returned from his campaign against Qing Bu's forces to Chang'an. In the twelfth month, Emperor Gaozu said: "The First Emperor of Qin, King Yinwang Chen She of Chu, King Anli Wang of Wei, King Min Wang of Qi, and King Daoxiang Wang of Zhao all have no descendants. I shall assign ten families to guard each tomb; for the Emperor of Qin, twenty families; and for Marquis Wuji of Wei, five families." Pardon the officials and people in Dai territory who were robbed or seized by Chen Xi and Zhao Li; all shall be pardoned. A surrendered general of Chen Xi reported that when Chen Xi rebelled, King Yan Lu Wan had sent someone to his location to conspire secretly. The emperor sent Biyang Hou to summon Lu Wan, but Lu Wan claimed to be ill. Biyang Hou returned and fully reported that Lu Wan had already shown signs of rebellion. In February, he sent Fan Kuai and Zhou Bo to lead troops against King Yan Wan, pardoning the officials and people of Yan who had participated in the rebellion. Liu Jian was enfeoffed as Prince of Yan.

## 84

While Emperor Gaozu was attacking Qing Bu, he was struck by a stray arrow and fell ill on the journey. His condition worsened, so Empress Lu summoned skilled physicians. When a physician entered to see him, Emperor Gaozu asked the doctor about his illness, and the doctor said: "The illness is treatable." Emperor Gaozu insulted him and said, "I rose from a commoner with only a three-foot sword to seize the empire. Is this not ordained by Heaven? If fate is determined by Heaven, what good would Bian Que do!" He then refused to allow treatment and instead gave the physician fifty catties of gold as a dismissal. After some time, Empress Lu asked: "After Your Majesty passes away (i.e., after your death), if Xiao Xiangguo were to die, who should succeed him?" The emperor said: "Cao Can is suitable." When asked about the next in line, he replied: "Wang Ling is suitable. However, Ling is somewhat naive; Chen Ping can assist him." Chen Ping has sufficient wisdom, but he would be difficult to entrust with sole responsibility. Zhou Bo is solid and sincere though lacking in eloquence, but among those who will ensure the Liu family's stability, it must be Bo. He may be appointed as Grand Marshal." Empress Lu again asked about those after them, and the emperor said: "Those who come after will not be within your knowledge either."

## 85

Lu Wan, with several thousand cavalrymen, stayed at the frontier to await an opportunity, hoping that the emperor would recover and he could enter in person to apologize.

## 86

On the Jiachen day of the fourth month (April 25, 195 BC), Emperor Gaozu passed away at Changle Palace. The announcement of his death was withheld for four days. Empress Lu and Shen Shiqi plotted, saying: "The generals were once commoners alongside the emperor. Now they serve as his subjects from above, yet they have always been discontented. If now they must serve a young ruler, unless we eliminate all of them by exterminating their clans, the realm will not be at peace." Some people heard this and informed General Li. General Li went to see Shen Shiqi and said: "I have heard that the emperor has passed away, yet four days have passed without announcing his death. There are plans to execute the generals." If this is true, the realm will be in great danger. Chen Ping and Guan Ying command 100,000 troops defending Xingyang; Fan Kuai and Zhou Bo lead 200,000 to pacify Yan and Dai. If they hear of the emperor's death and learn that all generals are to be executed, they will surely raise their armies, return to their hometowns, and attack Guanzhong." "The ministers will rebel within the court, while the feudal lords will revolt from without. The empire's downfall would be imminent!" Shen Shiqi entered to report this, and so on the Dingwei day (April 29, 195 BC) they announced his death and issued a general pardon throughout the realm.

## 87

Upon hearing of Emperor Gaozu's death, Lu Wan fled to the Xiongnu.

## 88

On the Bingyin day (May 7, 195 BC), he was buried. On the Jisi day (May 20, 195 BC), the crown prince was enfeoffed and taken to the temple of Taishang Huang. The ministers all said: "Emperor Gaozu rose from humble origins, restored order to a chaotic world and returned it to righteousness, pacified the realm, and became the founding ancestor of Han. His achievements were the greatest." They bestowed upon him the honored title of Gao Huangdi (the High Emperor). The crown prince succeeded to the throne as emperor, known as Emperor Xiaohui. An edict was issued ordering all commanderies, states, and feudal lords to establish temples for Emperor Gaozu and perform seasonal sacrifices in his honor.

## 89

And during the fifth year of Emperor Xiaohui's reign, moved by remembrance of Emperor Gaozu's sorrow and joy in Pei, they designated the Pei Palace as the original temple for Emperor Gaozu. The 120 boys whom Emperor Gaozu had taught to sing were all ordered to serve as musicians. Whenever there was a vacancy, they would be promptly filled.

## 90

Emperor Gao had eight sons: the eldest, Liu Fei, posthumously honored as King Dohui of Qi; the second was Emperor Xiaohui, son of Empress Lu; the third was Liu Yurong, posthumously honored as the Hidden King of Zhao, son of Qi Fu Ren; the fourth was Liu Heng, who had already been enfeoffed as Emperor Xiaowen, son of Empress Dowager Bo; the fifth was Liu Huai, posthumously honored as Prince Gong of Zhao, who had been enfeoffed originally as King of Liang and later transferred to the state of Zhao during the reign of Empress Dowager Lu; the sixth was Liu You, posthumously honored as Wang of You, who had been enfeoffd originally as King of Huaiyang and later transferred to the state of S during the reign of Empress Dowher Lu; the seventh was Liu Chang, posthumously honored as Liwang of Huainan; the eighth was Liu Jian, King of Yan.

## 91

Grand Historian said: The administration of the Xia dynasty was loyal. When loyalty declined, petty men became unruly; thus, the Yin people followed with reverence. When reverence declined, petty men resorted to superstition; thus, the Zhou people followed with culture and civilization. When culture and civilization declined, petty men became cold and indifferent; therefore, to remedy indifference, nothing is better than loyalty. The ways of the three kings are like a cycle, ending only to begin anew. Between the Zhou and Qin dynasties, one might say that culture had declined completely. The Qin administration did not reform this situation but instead imposed harsh laws and severe punishments—was this not a grave error? Therefore, when the Han dynasty rose to power, it took over during a time of decline and made changes easily, making people not weary; thus, it gained the Mandate of Heaven. The court convened in the tenth month. Chariots and ceremonial robes bore yellow roofs with a left-facing standard. He was buried in Changling Mausoleum.
