# Liezhuan of General Wei Biaoqi

## 1

The great general Wei Qing was a native of Pingyang. His father, Zheng Ji, served as an official and worked for the household of Marquis Pingyang. He had a relationship with Lady Wei, a concubine of the marquis, and thus Wei Qing was born. Wei Qing had an elder half-brother, Wei Changzi, who shared the same mother. His sister, Wei Zifuch, gained favor with the emperor from the household of Princess Pingyang, so Wei Qing adopted the surname Wei as well. His courtesy name was Zhongqing. Changzi changed his courtesy name to Changjun. The mother of Changjun was known as Lady Wei. Lady Wei's eldest daughter was Wei Ru, her second daughter was Shaoer, and the third daughter was Zifuch. Later, Zifuch's younger brothers Bu and Guang also adopted the surname Wei.

## 2

Wei Qing was raised in the household of the marquis but at a young age returned to his father, who made him tend sheep. The sons of Wei Qing's stepmother treated him like a slave or livestock and did not regard him as their equal brother. Once, Wei Qing accompanied someone to the residence at Ganshan, where a blacksmith apprentice who read fortunes told him: "You are a noble person; your official rank will reach that of marquis." Wei Qing laughed and said, "A slave's life is such that one can be content if not beaten or scolded. How could I possibly attain the matter of being enfeoffed as a marquis!"

## 3

When Wei Qing grew up, he became a horseman in the marquis's household and followed Princess Pingyang. In spring of Jianyuan year two, Wei Qing's sister Zifuch was admitted to the palace and gained the emperor's favor. The empress was the daughter of Grand Princess Tangyi Dachanggong, who had no children, and she was jealous. Grand Princess Tangyi Dachang heard that Wei Zifuch had gained the emperor's favor and was pregnant, so she became jealous of her. Thus, she sent someone to capture Wei Qing. At that time, Wei Qing served at Jianzhang Palace but had not yet gained fame. Grand Princess Tangyi Dachang imprisoned Wei Qing and wanted to kill him. His friend Qilang Gongsun Ao, along with some strong men, went to rescue him, which is why he did not die. The emperor heard about this and summoned Wei Qing to serve as Jianzhangjian, a palace attendant. Because of his noble status and the favor shown to his full brothers, rewards accumulated in several days amounted to thousands of gold pieces. Wei Ru became the wife of Gong Sunhe, the Tai Pu. Shaoer had previously been in a relationship with Chen Zhang, so the emperor summoned and honored Chen Zhang. Gongsun Ao thus became even more prominent. Zifuch was made a fu ren (lady of the palace). Wei Qing became a Da Zhongdafa.

## 4

In the fifth year of Yuanguang, Wei Qing was appointed as Chaji Jiangjun (General of Chaji), and he attacked the Xiongnu, departing from Shanggu; Tai Pu Gong Sunhe was appointed as Qingche Jiangjun (Light Chariot General), and departed from Yunzhong; Da Zhongdafa Gongsun Ao was appointed as Qi Jiangjun (Cavalry General) and departed from Dai Jun; Wei Wei Li Guang was appointed as Xiaoqi Jiangjun (Brave Cavalry General), departing from Yanmen: each army had ten thousand cavalry. Wei Qing reached Longcheng and beheaded hundreds of the enemy. General Gongsun Ao lost seven thousand cavalry; Wei Wei Li Guang was captured by the enemy but managed to escape and return: they were all sentenced to death, but later commuted their sentences through redemption and made them commoners. Gong Sunhe also achieved no merit.

## 5

In spring of the first year of Yuanshuo, Lady Wei gave birth to a son and was enfeoffed as empress. That autumn, Wei Qing became Chaji Jiangjun and departed from Yanmen with thirty thousand cavalry to attack the Xiongnu, beheading thousands of enemies. The following year, the Xiongnu invaded and killed the governor of Liaoxi, captured over two thousand people from Yuyang, and defeated General Han's army. The Han dynasty ordered General Li Xi to attack them, departing from Dai; and ordered Chaji Jiangjun Wei Qing to depart from Yunzhong and proceed westward as far as Gaoque. Thus, they captured the southern lands of the Yellow River, reaching as far west as Longxi, capturing thousands of enemy soldiers and hundreds of thousands of livestock, driving away the kings of Baiyang and Loufan. They then established the southern Yellow River region as Shuofang Jun (Shuofang Commandery). Wei Qing was enfeoffed with 3,800 households and made the Marquis of Changping. Su Jian, a jiao wei under Wei Qing, had meritorious service; he was enfeoffed with one thousand and one hundred households as the Marquis of Pingling. They ordered him to construct Shuofang City. Zhang Cigong, a jiao wei of Wei Qing, also had meritorious service and was enfeoffed as the Marquis of Antou. The emperor said: "The Xiongnu defy the principles of heaven, disrupt human ethics, oppress the elderly and abuse the young, take banditry as their occupation, deceive various barbarian tribes with deceitful actions, devise plots to raise arms, and repeatedly cause border troubles. Therefore, we have raised an army and dispatched generals to punish their crimes. Is it not said in the Book of Songs, 'Lightly attack the Xianyun, reaching Taiyuan,' and 'The war chariots rattle on, building that Shuofang'?" Now, General Chaji Wei Qing crossed the western river to Gaoque, capturing 2,300 enemy heads. He seized all their chariots, supplies, livestock and goods as spoils of war; he had already been enfeoffed a marquis. Then, proceeding westward, he pacified the southern Yellow River region, restored the old Yuxi fortifications, cut off Ziling, bridged the northern Yellow River, attacked Puní, defeated Fuli, beheaded elite troops, captured 3,711 enemy soldiers who had surrendered or were taken by surprise, seized prisoners and obtained intelligence, drove away over one hundred thousand horses, cattle, and sheep, returned with all armor and weapons intact. Therefore, Wei Qing is to receive an additional enfeoffment of 3,000 households." The following year, the Xiongu invaded and killed Tai Jun governor You, raided Yanmen and captured over one thousand people. The following year, the Xiongnu launched a large-scale invasion of Dai, Dingxiang, and Shang Jun, killing and capturing thousands of Han people.

## 6

The following year, in spring of the fifth year of Yuanshuo (124 BC), the Han dynasty ordered Chaji Jiangjun Wei Qing with thirty thousand cavalry to depart from Gaoque; Wei Wei Su Jian was appointed as Youji Jiangjun (Roving General), Zuo Neishi Li Ju as Qiangnu Jiangjun (Strong Crossbow General), Tai Pu Gong Sunhe as Qi Jiangjun, Dai Xiang Li Cai as Qingche Jiangjun, and all were subordinated to Chaji Jiangjun. They departed together from Shuofang; Da Xing Li Xi and Marquis Antou Zhang Cigong were appointed as generals, departing from Youbeiping: they all attacked the Xiongnu. The Right Xian Wang of the Xiongnu faced Wei Qing's forces, believing that Han troops could not reach this far, and became drunk. The Han troops arrived at night, encircling the Right Xian Wang. Startled, the Right Xian Wang fled in the night with only one of his beloved concubines and a few hundred strong cavalrymen, breaking through the siege to escape northward. Han light cavalry jiao wei Guo Cheng and others pursued for hundreds of miles but failed to catch up. They captured more than ten Right Xian assistant kings, over 15,000 men and women, and millions of livestock. At this point, they withdrew their troops and returned. Upon reaching the frontier, the emperor sent an envoy carrying the seal of the great general to bestow it upon Wei Qing in the camp. All other generals transferred their troops under the command of General Wei Qing, who then assumed his title and returned. The emperor said: "General Wei Qing personally led the troops, achieved a great victory, captured more than ten Xiongnu kings, and is therefore to be granted an additional enfeoffment of six thousand households." His son Kang was enfeoffed as Marquis of Yichun, his son Buyi as Marquis of Yin'an, and his son Deng as Marquis of Fagan. Wei Qing firmly declined, saying: "I was fortunate to serve in the military and relied on Your Majesty's divine power for our great victory. It is all due to the efforts of my various jiao wei officers who fought bravely. Your Majesty has already granted me additional enfeoffments. My sons are still infants, having not contributed any labor. It is a great favor of Your Majesty to grant them three marquisates; this does not align with the intent of those who serve in the military to encourage soldiers through merit." "How could Kang and the other two dare accept enfeoffment!" The emperor said, "I have not forgotten the merits of your various jiao wei officers; I will now certainly reward them." He then issued an order to the imperial historian, saying: "Hujun Duyi Gongsun Ao has followed the great general three times in attacking the Xiongnu and often served as a protector of the army. He captured a king by conveying orders to jiao wei; he is therefore enfeoffed with 1,500 households as the Marquis Heqi." Duyi Han Yue followed the great general out of Yuhun, reaching the court of the Right Xian Wang, where he personally engaged in battle and captured a king; thus, he was enfeoffed with 1300 households as Marquis Longe. Qi Jiangjun Gongsun He, following the great general, captured a king and was therefore enfeoffed with one-thousand-three-hundred households as the Marquis Nanjiao. Qingche Jiangjun Li Cai, having followed the great general twice and captured a king, was enfeoffed with sixteen hundred households as Marquis Le'an. Jiao Wei Li Shuo, Jiao Wei Zhao Buyu, and Jiao Wei Gongsun Rongnu each followed the great general three times and captured a king. Therefore, Shuo was enfeoffed with thirteen hundred households as Marquis Shezhi; Buyu received thirteen hundred households as Marquis Suicheng; and Rongnu received thirteen hundred households as Marquis Congping. General Li Ju, General Li Xi, and Jiao Wei Dou Ruyi also had meritorious services; they were granted the title of Guannei Hou (Inner Pass Marquis) with fiefs of three hundred households each." That autumn, the Xiongnu invaded Dai and killed Duyi Zhu Ying.

## 7

The following spring, the great general Wei Qing departed from Dingxiang. Hejie Hou Gongsun Ao served as Zhong Jiangjun (Central General), Tai Pu Gong Sunhe as Zu Jiangjun (Left General), Xi Hou Zhao Xin as Qian Jiangjun (Front General), Wei Wei Su Jian as You Jiangjun (Right General), Langzhongling Li Guang as Hou Jiangjun (Rear General), and You Neishi Li Ju as Qiang Nu Jiangjun (Strong Crossbow General). All were subordinated to the great general. They returned after beheading several thousand enemies. More than a month later, they all departed again from Dingxiang to attack the Xiongnu and beheaded over ten thousand enemies. You Jiangjun Su Jian and Qian Jiangjun Zhao Xin, with a combined force of over three thousand cavalrymen, encountered the Chanyu's troops alone. They fought for more than one day until their Han forces were nearly exhausted. The Qian Jiangjun had previously been a Hu person who surrendered and was enfeoffed as Xi Hou. In the crisis, he was lured by the Xiongnu and led his remaining cavalry of about eight hundred to surrender to the Chanyu. You Jiangjun Su Jian lost all his troops and managed to escape alone, returning to report himself to the great general. The great general asked about Su Jian's crime with Zheng Hong, Changshi An, Yilang Zhou Ba and others: "What should be done to Su Jian?" Zhou Ba said, "Since the great general departed, he has never executed a subordinate commander. Now that Su Jian abandoned his troops, he can be beheaded to demonstrate the general's authority." Zheng Hong and An said: "Not so. The military doctrine says, 'A small force that is resolute will be captured by a larger enemy.' Now Su Jian, with several thousand troops facing the Chanyu's tens of thousands, fought bravely for over a day until his soldiers were exhausted. He did not lose faith and returned by himself. To execute him after he surrendered voluntarily would show future subordinates that there is no hope of returning if they are defeated. He should not be executed." The great general said, "I am fortunate to serve in the army with sincerity and loyalty; I do not fear a lack of authority. Yet Zhou Ba advised me to demonstrate authority through execution, which greatly misrepresents my intent as an officer. Moreover, even if my duty requires me to execute a general, given my high rank and favor, I would not dare to act unilaterally or carry out punishment beyond the borders. Instead, I should return him in full to the emperor for His Majesty's own judgment. In this way, it demonstrates that as an official I do not dare to wield power arbitrarily; is this not acceptable?" The military officials all said, "Well said." Thus, they imprisoned Su Jian and sent him to the emperor's current location. After entering the frontier, troops were disbanded.

## 8

In this year, the great general's nephew Huo Qubing, who was eighteen years old and favored by the emperor, served as a palace attendant (Yizhong). Skilled in horseback riding and archery, he twice followed the great general. By imperial decree, he was assigned to lead elite warriors as a Piaoyao Jiao Wei (Roving Scout Officer), commanding eight hundred light cavalrymen who advanced hundreds of miles ahead of the main army to seize opportunities for profit, beheading and capturing more enemies than their number. At this, the emperor said: "Piaoyao Jiao Wei Qubing beheaded 2,228 enemies, including a prime minister and Danghu. He also killed Chanyu's great-grandfather Xing Ji Ruohou Chan and captured his uncle Luogubi alive. Having twice led the vanguard to victory, he is enfeoffed with six thousand households as Marquis Guanjun." Governor of Shanggu Hao Xian followed the great general four times, capturing and beheading over two thousand enemies. He was enfeoffed with eleven hundred households as Marquis Zhongli." In this year, two generals lost their armies and Xi Hou defected. Since the overall military achievements were not great, the great general did not receive additional enfeoffment. When You Jiangjun Su Jian arrived, the emperor did not execute him but pardoned his crime and commuted it through redemption, making him a commoner.

## 9

After returning from campaign, the great general was granted one thousand gold pieces as reward. At that time, Lady Wang was favored by the emperor. Ning Cheng advised the great general: "The reason why your achievements are not yet numerous but you personally hold a ten-thousand-household fief and all three of your sons have been enfeoffed as marquises is solely because of the empress. Now that Lady Wang enjoys imperial favor but her family has not yet attained wealth or rank, I hope you will use the thousand gold pieces granted to you as a gift for Lady Wang's relatives in celebration of their longevity." The great general thus used five hundred gold pieces for the tribute. When the emperor heard of this, he asked the great general about it. The great general told him the truth, and so the emperor appointed Ning Cheng as Duyi of Donghai.

## 10

Zhang Qian, who followed the great general, had previously served as an envoy to Dayue and was detained in the Xiongnu for a long time. He guided the army, knowing where good water and grass were located, so that the troops did not suffer from hunger or thirst. Because of his previous mission to cut off contact with distant states, he was enfeoffed as Hou of Bowang.

## 11

Three years after Marquis Guanjuan Huo Qubing was enfeoffed, in spring of the second year of Yuanshou (121 BC), he was appointed as Biaoqi Jiangjun with the title of Marquis Guanjuan. He led ten thousand cavalry out from Longxi and achieved military success. The emperor said: "Biaoqi Jiangjun led the troops across Wuli, pacified Susu Pu, crossed Hunu, passed through five kingdoms of kings. He did not plunder the supplies and people who were frightened, hoping to capture the Chanyu's son. After six days of continuous fighting, he traveled more than a thousand li beyond Yanzhi Mountain. In close combat, he killed Zhe Lan Wang and Lu Hu Wang, destroyed the entire armor unit, captured the son of Hunye Wang as well as his prime minister and duwei, beheading over eight thousand enemies. He also seized the golden man used by Qiutu for sacrifices to heaven. Therefore, Qubing was granted an additional enfeoffment of two thousand households."

## 12

That summer, Biaoqi Jiangjun and Heqi Hou Ao both departed from Beidi, taking different routes; Bowang Hou Zhang Qian and Langzhongling Li Guang both departed from Youbeiping, taking different routes: they all attacked the Xiongnu. Langzhongling led four thousand cavalry and arrived first; Bowang Hou, leading ten thousand cavalry, arrived later. The Xiongnu Zuxian Wang led tens of thousands of cavalry to surround Langzhongling. Langzhongling fought for two days, and more than half his men were killed; however, the number of enemies he killed was also considerable. When Bowang Hou arrived, the Xiongnu forces withdrew. Bowang Hou was found guilty of delay and should have been executed, but he commuted his sentence by paying a fine and became a commoner. Meanwhile, Biaoqi Jiangjun departed from Beidi and advanced deep into enemy territory. He became separated from Heqi Hou and could not find each other. Biaoqi Jiangjun crossed Juyan to Qilian Mountain, capturing a large number of enemies. The emperor said: "Biaoqi Jiangjun crossed Juyuan, passed through Xiao Yuezhi, and attacked Qilian Mountain. He captured Choutu Wang, with 2,500 of his followers surrendering in large numbers. He beheaded over 30,200 enemies, capturing five kings, five royal mothers, the empress 阏氏 (Yan Zhi), fifty-nine princes, and sixty-three prime ministers, generals, Danghu, and duwei. His forces suffered heavy losses, with casualties amounting to one-third of his troops. Therefore, Qubing is granted an additional enfeoffment covering five thousand households." The emperor bestowed the title Zuo Shuzhang on the jiao wei who had accompanied him to Xiao Yuezhi. Yingji Sima Po Nu, who had twice followed Biaoqi Jiangjun, beheaded Susu Pu Wang and captured Jiju Wang. He also captured one thousand-riders general De Wang and his royal mother, forty-one princes and below, as well as 3,330 enemies. In the vanguard, he captured another 1,400 enemies. For these achievements, Po Nu was enfeoffed with fifteen hundred households as Hou Congbiao. Jiao Wei Gou Wang Gao Bushi, who followed Biaoqi Jiangjun in campaign, captured eleven people including the son of Huyutu Wang and below, as well as one thousand seven hundred and sixty-eight enemies. For this achievement, he was enfeoffed with one thousand one hundred households as Hou Yiguan. Jiao Wei Pu Duo also achieved military merit and was enfeoffed as Marquis Huiqu." Heqi Hou Ao was found guilty of delaying his march and failing to meet up with Biaoqi Jiangjun. He should have been executed but commuted the sentence by paying a ransom, becoming a commoner instead. The veteran generals' troops and horses were not as good as those of Biaoqi. The forces under Biaoqi's command were always carefully selected, yet he was also brave enough to advance deep into enemy territory, often leading the vanguard with elite cavalry before his main army. His campaigns were also favored by heaven; he had never been trapped or cut off. However, these veteran generals often incurred blame for delays and failing to join the campaign in time. From this point on, Biaoqi Jiangjun became increasingly favored and esteemed by the emperor, rivaling the great general.

## 13

That autumn, Chanyu was furious because Hunye Wang in the western region had been repeatedly defeated by Han forces and lost tens of thousands of men, all due to Biaoqi Jiangjun's troops. Enraged, Chanyu wanted to summon Hunye Wang for execution. Hunye Wang and Qiutu Wang plotted together to surrender to Han, sending envoys ahead to contact the frontier forces. At that time, Da Xing Li Xi was commanding troops along the Yellow River and captured an envoy sent by Hunye Wang. He immediately dispatched a fast courier to report this news. The emperor, upon hearing the news, was afraid that they might pretend to surrender and then attack the frontier. Therefore, he ordered Biaoqi Jiangjun to lead troops to meet them. After crossing the river, Biaoqi saw Hunye Wang's forces from a distance. Some of Hunye Wang's subordinate generals, upon seeing the Han army, were unwilling to surrender and began to flee. Biaoqi then galloped into the camp to meet Hunye Wang, beheading eight thousand of those who wanted to escape. He then sent Hunye Wang ahead by imperial carriage to where the emperor was stationed, while leading all his troops across the river; tens of thousands surrendered, claiming their number reached one hundred thousand. After arriving in Chang'an, the emperor bestowed rewards and gifts worth dozens of millions. Hunye Wang was enfeoffed with ten thousand households as Hou Laiyin. His subordinate kings Huduni was enfeoffed as Xiaomo Hou, Yingbi as Huiqu Hou, Qin as Heqi Hou, and the great Danghu Tongli as Changle Hou. The emperor praised Biaoqi Jiangjun for his achievements, saying: "Biaoqi Jiangjun Qubing led the army to attack Xiongnu's Western Region King Hunye. The king and all his people surrendered in droves. He provided military provisions to supply them, bringing together over ten thousand archers under control. He killed Tiaokan, beheaded more than eight thousand enemies, and captured thirty-two kings from foreign lands. His soldiers suffered no serious injuries; the entire force of one hundred thousand was united and submitted. He further worked with them in labor efforts, extending to the riverfronts and passes, bringing lasting peace and stability." Therefore, Biaoqi Jiangjun was granted an additional enfeoffement of one thousand seven hundred households." The emperor reduced the number of garrison troops in Longxi, Beidi, and Shangjun by half to ease the burden on the people.

## 14

After a short while, the surrendered groups were relocated to areas outside the five border commanderies and all settled in the region south of the Yellow River. They were allowed to maintain their traditional customs as vassal states. The following year, the Xiongnu invaded Youbeiping and Dingxiang, killing and capturing over a thousand Han people.

## 15

The following year, the emperor discussed with his generals: "Xi Hou Zhao Xin advised Chanyu that Han troops could never cross the desert lightly or remain for long. Now we have mobilized a large force; their strategy will surely achieve its goal." This was the fourth year of Yuanshou era.

## 16

In spring of the fourth year of Yuanshou, the emperor ordered the great general Qing and Biaoqi Jiangjun Qubing each to lead fifty thousand cavalry. The number of infantry providing logistical support followed behind in the tens of thousands. All the brave warriors willing to fight fiercely and advance deep into enemy territory were assigned under Biaoqi's command. Biaoqi initially set out from Dingxiang, directly facing Chanyu. After captured enemies reported that Chanyu had moved eastward, the emperor changed his orders and commanded Biaoqi to depart from Daijun while ordering the great general to leave from Dingxiang. Langzhongling served as Qian Jiangjun, Taifu as Zu Jiangjun, Zhuzhu Zhao Shiqi as You Jiangjun, and Pingyang Hou Xiang as Hou Jiangjun; all were subordinate to the great general. The forces crossed the desert, with a total of fifty thousand cavalrymen and horses, joining Biaoqi to attack Chanyu of Xiongnu. Zhao Xin advised Chanyu: "Once the Han troops have crossed the desert, their men and horses will be exhausted. The Xiongnu can simply sit back and capture them." Therefore, Chanyu moved all his supply wagons far to the north and stationed all his elite troops in the northern desert. At that moment, the great general's army had just advanced more than a thousand li beyond the frontier and encountered Chanyu's forces arrayed in formation to await them. The great general then ordered his Wugang chariots to form a circular camp for defense while sending five thousand cavalry forward to confront the Xiongnu. The Xiongnu also deployed about ten thousand cavalry in response. As the sun was setting, a strong wind arose, with sand and gravel striking their faces so that the two armies could no longer see each other. The Han forces then deployed additional left and right wings to encircle Chanyu. Seeing that the Han forces were numerous and still strong in men and horses, Chanyu found his side at a disadvantage. As dusk approached, he therefore rode away on six mares with about several hundred elite cavalrymen, directly breaking through the northwest part of the Han encirclement to flee. By then it was already dark, and the Han and Xiongnu forces were engaged in chaotic combat, with heavy casualties on both sides. A captured enemy from the Han left wing reported that Chanyu had fled before dusk, so the Han forces sent light cavalry to pursue him at night. The great general's main army then followed behind them. The Xiongnu troops also scattered and fled. By dawn, the pursuing forces had traveled over two hundred li but failed to catch Chanyu. However, they captured and beheaded more than ten thousand enemies. They eventually reached Zhaixin Cheng on Tianyan Mountain, where they found stored Xiongnu supplies for their troops. The army stayed one day and then returned, burning the remaining grain in the city before heading back.

## 17

When the great general confronted Chanyu, Qian Jiangjun Guang and You Jiangjun Shiqi's forces had taken a separate eastern route; some of them lost their way and arrived late to engage Chanyu. The great general led his forces back, crossing the southern side of the desert, where he finally met up with Qian Jiangjun and You Jiangjun. The great general wanted to send a messenger back to report the situation, and ordered his chief clerk to formally reprimand Qian Jiangjun Guang. Hearing this, Guang committed suicide. When You Jiangjun arrived, he was handed over to the authorities and later commuted his punishment by paying a fine, becoming a commoner. The great general's forces returned through the frontier, having beheaded or captured over 19,000 enemies in total.

## 18

At that time, the Xiongnu people had lost track of Chanyu for more than ten days. Yougouli Wang heard about this and declared himself as the new Chanyu. Later, when Chanyu rejoined his forces, the Right King abandoned the title of Chanyu.

## 19

Biaoqi Jiangjun also led fifty thousand cavalry, with supply wagons equal in number to those of the great general's forces, but without any subordinate generals. He appointed Li Gan and others as major commanders, serving in the roles of subordinate generals. Departing from Dai and Youbeiping for over a thousand li, they directly confronted the left-wing forces of Xiongnu, achieving more captured and beheaded enemies than the great general's troops. After the army returned, the emperor said: "Biaoqi Jiangjun Qubin led his troops in person and commanded the soldiers he had captured from Hunyu. With minimal supplies, they crossed the vast desert, passed through Zhangqu, to attack and kill Biche Qi, then turned to strike the Left General, capturing enemy banners and drums, and passing through the territory of Lihou. They crossed Gonglv, capturing Tun Tou Wang, Han Wang and three others, as well as eighty-three generals, prime ministers, Danghu and duwei. They offered sacrifices at Mount Wolfju Xu, performed the ritual of chán at Guyan, and ascended to Hanghai. They captured and beheaded a total of 70,443 enemies. The army suffered heavy losses, amounting to one-third its strength, yet sustained itself by taking supplies from the enemy. Despite traveling far distances without regular supply lines, their provisions never ran out. Therefore, Biaoqi Jiangjun received an additional enfeoffment for five thousand eight hundred households." Governor of Youbeiping Lu Bode, who was under the command of Biaoqi Jiangjun, arrived at the rendezvous on time and followed him to Taoyu Mountain. There he beheaded or captured 2,700 enemies. For this, Lu Bode was enfeoffed with sixteen hundred households as Hou Fuli. Duyi of Beidi Xing Shan, who followed Biaoqi Jianggun and captured a king, was enfeoffed with twelve hundred households as Hou Yiyang. The former Guiyi Yinchun Wang Fulu Zhi and Louzhuan Wang Yijie Jian, who both followed Biaoqi Jiangjun on campaign and achieved military merit, were enfeoffed: Fulu Zhi with thirteen hundred households as Hou Zhuang, and Yijiejian with eighteen hundred households as Hou Zhongli. Congbiao Hou Po Nu and Changwu Hou An Ji, who also followed Biaoqi on campaign with achievements, were granted additional enfeoffments of three hundred households each. Jiao Wei Gan captured enemy banners and drums and was made a Guannei Hou, with an estate of two hundred households. Jiao Wei Zi was granted the title Dashuzhang. Many officers and soldiers were appointed to official posts, receiving numerous rewards and gifts. However, the great general did not gain any additional enfeoffment, and none of his officers or soldiers were granted marquis titles.

## 20

When the two armies departed from the frontier, both official and private horses totaled 140,000. However, fewer than thirty thousand returned through the frontier. The emperor then added a new position of Dashima (Grand Marshal). Both the great general and Biaoqi Jiangjun were appointed as Dashima. An official decree was issued, setting the rank and salary of Biaoqi Jiangjun equal to that of the great general. From then on, the great general Qing gradually lost influence, while Biaoqi Jiangjun grew increasingly favored and powerful. Many of the great general's former associates and subordinates left to serve Biaoqi, who promptly granted them official positions and titles. Only Ren An refused to do so.

## 21

Biaoqi Jiangjun was a man of few words and strict confidentiality, possessing boldness and the courage to take on responsibilities. The emperor once wanted to teach him Sun Wu's military strategies, but Biaoqi replied: "It is the effectiveness of one's strategy that matters; there is no need to study ancient military treatises." The emperor built a residence for him and ordered Biaoqi to inspect it. In response, he said: "Until the Xiongnu are destroyed, I have no time or thought for a home." Because of this, the emperor valued and loved him even more. However, having been a Zhong since his youth and enjoying high status, he did not personally attend to or care for soldiers. When accompanying the army on campaign, the emperor sent Taiguan to supply dozens of carts with provisions. After returning, many of these heavily loaded wagons were abandoned, leaving surplus grain and meat wasted, while some soldiers still suffered from hunger. While the army was stationed beyond the frontier, troops often lacked food and some could barely sustain themselves, yet Biaoqi still indulged in playing cuju (ancient Chinese football) on specially prepared fields. There were many incidents of this kind. The great general was a kind and modest man, using his gentle and conciliatory nature to please the emperor. However, he did not receive widespread acclaim from the people of the empire.

## 22

Three years after his campaign in the fourth year (i.e., by the sixth year of Yuanshou), Biaoqi Jiangjun died in 120 BC. The emperor mourned him deeply, mobilizing the black-armored troops from vassal states and arranging them in formation from Chang'an to Maoling. A tomb was built for him resembling Qilian Mountain. He was posthumously honored with the title Jinghuan Hou, combining martial valor and extensive territory. His son Shanshan succeeded him as marquis. Shanshan was young, courtesy name Zihou; the emperor loved him and favored him by appointing him to a military command when he grew strong enough. After six years, in the first year of Yuanfeng (110 BC), Shanshan died and was posthumously honored as Ai Hou. He had no son; thus, his line ended and the marquisate was abolished.

## 23

After Biaoqi Jiangjun's death, the great general's eldest son Yichun Hou Kang lost his title for breaking the law. Five years later, two of Kang's younger brothers—Yin'an Hou Buyi and Faguan Hou Deng—also lost their titles for failing the zhoujin (ritual gold) test. Two years after losing his title, the marquisate of Guanjun Hou was abolished. Four years later, the great general Qing died and was posthumously titled Liehou. His son Kang succeeded him as Changping Hou.

## 24

Fourteen years after the great general encircled the Chanyu, he died. The Han did not launch further attacks against the Xiongnu because their cavalry was depleted, and they were simultaneously engaged in campaigns to the south against the two Yue states, to the east against Goguryeo, and against the Qiang and southwestern tribes. Hence, for a long time, there were no major expeditions against the Hu (Xiongnu).

## 25

## 27

The greatest general Qing led seven campaigns against the Xiongnu, killing or capturing more than 50,000 enemy soldiers. ## 28

General Gongsun He. He was a native of Yiqu, and his ancestors were of Hu origin. He's father Hunye was made Pingqu Hou during the reign of Emperor Jingdi but lost his title for violating the law. When the emperor was still a crown prince, He served as one of his attendants. Eight years after Emperor Wu ascended the throne, he appointed He Taipu as Qingche Jiangjun and stationed him at Mayi. Four years later, as Qingche Jiangjun, he led an expedition from Yunzhong. Five years after that, serving as Qijun Jiangjun under the great general and achieving success in battle, he was enfeoffed as Nanjiao Hou. One year later, serving again under the great general as Zuojun Jiangjun in a campaign from Dingxiang, he achieved no notable success. Four years after that, he lost his title for failing the zhoujin test. Eight years later, as Fuzhu Jiangjun leading an expedition more than 2,000 li from Wuyuan, he still achieved no success. Eight years later, he was appointed Chancellor through his position as Taipu and enfeoffed as Geyi Hou. He served seven times as a general, leading campaigns against the Xiongnu but achieving no great successes; yet he was twice enfeoffed and eventually became Chancellor. He was implicated in a case involving his son Jingsheng, who had an illicit relationship with Princess Yangshi and was involved in witchcraft. As a result, the entire family was executed, leaving no descendants.

## 29

## 30

General Gongsun Ao was a native of Yiqu. He served as an officer under Emperor Wu. Twelve years after Emperor Wu took the throne, Gongsun Ao served as (Biao)qi Jiangjun and led an expedition from Dai. He lost 7,000 soldiers in the campaign; he was sentenced to death but commuted his punishment by paying a fine, thus becoming a commoner. Five years later, serving as an officer under the great general and earning merit in battle, he was enfeofed as Heqi Hou. One year later, serving as Zhongjun Jiangjun under the great general, he led two expeditions from Dingxiang but achieved no success. Two years later, serving as a general in an expedition from Beidi and arriving late to rendezvous with Biaoqi, he was sentenced to death but again commuted his punishment by paying fines, thus becoming a commoner once more. Two years later, serving as an officer with the great general, he achieved no merit in battle. Fourteen years later, as Yinyu Jiangjun, he oversaw the construction of Shoujiang Cheng (the City of Surrender). Seven years later, he again served as Yinyu Jiangjun and led another expedition against the Xiongnu to Yuwu. He suffered heavy losses in troops, was handed over to officials for punishment, and sentenced to death. To escape execution, he faked his own death and lived incognito among civilians for five or six years. Later discovered, he was once again arrested. He was executed for witchcraft involving his wife; the entire family was exterminated. In total, he served four times as a general and led campaigns against the Xiongnu once earning an enfeoffment as marquis.

## 31

General Li Ju was a native of Yunzhong. He served under Emperor Jingdi.  Seventeen years after Emperor Wu took the throne he was appointed Qiangnu Jiangjun as Left Neishi. One year later, he once again served as Qiangnu Jiangjun.

## 32

General Li Cai was a native of Chengji. He served under Emperors Xiaowendi, Jingdi, and Wudi. Following the great general as Qingche Jiangjun with notable achievements in battle, he was enfeofed as Le'an Hou. After becoming Chancellor, he broke the law and was executed.

## 33

General Zhang Cigong was a native of Hedong. As an officer following General Wei Qing, he earned merit in battle and was enfeofed as Antou Hou. After the empress dowager's death, he served as a general commanding the northern army. One year later, serving once again as a general under the great general; he held this position twice before losing his title for breaking laws. Cigong's father Long was an officer in charge of light cavalry and archery. Because he was skilled at archery, Emperor Jingdi favored him highly.

## 34

General Su Jian was a native of Dulung. As an officer following General Wei Qing and earning merit in battle, Su Jian was enfeofed as Pingling Hou. Later he served as a general overseeing the construction of Shuping. Four years later, serving as Youji Jiangjun, he followed the great general on an expedition from Shuping. One year later, as Youjun Jiangjun he once again followed the great general from Dingxiang. He lost contact with his subordinate commander Xi Hou and was separated from his troops; sentenced to death, he commuted this by paying a fine and became a commoner. Later, he served as Taishou of Dai Jun and died; his tomb is located in Dayou Xiang.

## 35

General Zhao Xin surrendered to the Han as a former Xiongnu Prime Minister, and was enfeofed as Xi Hou. Seventeen years after Emperor Wudi took the throne, Zhao Xin served as Qianjun Jiangjun and fought against the Chanyu. He was defeated and surrendered to the Xiongnu.

## 36

General Zhang Qian, after serving as an envoy sent to establish contact with Daya, returned and was appointed as a lieutenant. Following the great general and earning military merit, he was enfeofed as Bowang Hou. Three years later, serving as a general on an expedition from Youbeiping, he arrived late and was sentenced to death. He commuted the sentence by paying a fine and thus became a commoner. Later, he was sent as an envoy to establish relations with Wusun, served as Daxing, and died; his tomb is in Hanzhong.

## 37

## 38

## 39

In the third year of Taichu, he served as Youji Jiangjun and was stationed at Wuyuan's outer defensive cities. ## 42

Zhi Biaoqi Jiangjun Wei Qubing, on six occasions he launched campaigns against the Xiongnu; four of these were as a general, in which he personally killed or captured over 110,000 enemy soldiers. When Hunye Wang surrendered with tens of thousands of his followers, the western Hexi region including Jiuquan was opened up, and thereafter the western frontier saw fewer raids by Xiongnu forces. He received four additional enfeoffments, totaling 15,500 households. Among his subordinate officers and clerks, six earned the title of marquis for their merits, and two later became generals.

## 43

General Lu Bode was a native of Pingzhou. As Taishou of Youbei Ping, he followed the Biaoqi Jiangjun and earned merits in battle, being enfeoffed as Fuli Hou. After the death of Biaoqi Jiangjun, Lu Bode served as Weiyi and was appointed Fubo Jiangjun. He defeated Nan Yue in battle and received additional enfeoffment. Later he lost his title for violating the law. He served as Qiangnu Duyi, stationed at Juyan, and died in office.

## 44

General Zhao Po'nu was originally from Jiuyuan. He once fled and joined the Xiongnu, but later returned to Han service as a Shima under Biaoqi Jiangjun. For his achievements during an expedition from Beidi, he was enfeoffed as Congbiao Hou. He lost his title for failing the zhoujin requirement. One year later, he served as Xionghé Jiangjun and led a campaign against the Xiongnu to the Xionghe River but achieved no success. Two years later, he defeated the king of Loulan and was again enfeoffed as Chuye Hou. Six years later, he served as Junji Jiangjun and led 20,000 cavalry to attack the Xiongnu's Zuoxian Wang. Zuoxian Wang engaged in battle with him, surrounding Zhao Po'nu with an army of 80,000 horsemen. Zhao Po'nu was captured alive by the enemy forces and his entire army was lost as a result. He remained in Xiongnu territory for ten years, after which he fled back to Han along with the Xiongnu crown prince An Guo. Later, he was implicated in a witchcraft case and his entire clan was exterminated.

## 45

Since the rise of the Wei family, the great general Wei Qing received the first enfeoffment; afterward, his descendants and relatives were granted five marquis titles. Within twenty-four years, all five marquises lost their titles, and no member of the Wei family held a title thereafter.

## 46

Grand Historian said: Su Jian told me, "I once advised the great general that he was so highly esteemed and respected, yet no virtuous officials in the land were being praised. I hope you will observe how ancient renowned generals selected and recruited worthy individuals, and strive to do likewise." The great general replied, "Since Wei Qi and Wu An entertained numerous guests with lavish hospitality, the Son of Heaven was always deeply resentful." To personally associate with officials, to recruit the virtuous and dismiss the unworthy—this is a matter of royal authority. "As an official, I am merely expected to abide by laws and fulfill my duties; how could I possibly be involved in recruiting scholars!" Biaoqi Jiangjun also held similar views. This was the manner in which they conducted their military leadership.
