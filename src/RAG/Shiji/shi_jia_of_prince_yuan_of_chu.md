# Shi Jia of Prince Yuan of Chu

## 1

Prince Yuan of Chu, Liu Jiao, was the younger half-brother of Emperor Gaozu, courtesy name You.

## 2

Emperor Gaozu had four brothers; his eldest brother was Bo, who died at an early age. In the beginning, when Emperor Gaozu was obscure and lowly, he once avoided official duties; at that time, he often visited his sister-in-law Ju's house to eat with guests. His sister-in-law disliked her brother-in-law, and whenever he came with his guests, she pretended that the soup was all gone, breaking the pot, so the guests left because of this. Later, when they looked into the pot, there was still soup inside; Emperor Gaozu therefore resented his sister-in-law. When Emperor Gaozu became emperor and bestowed titles on his brothers, only the son of Bo did not receive a title. The Emperor Taishang Huang mentioned this matter; Emperor Gaozu said, "I have not forgotten to bestow a title on him, but it is because his mother is an unscrupulous woman." Thus he bestowed the title of Gongjie Hou (Gongjie Marquis) upon his son Xin. He also made his second elder brother Zhong king of Dai.

## 3

In the sixth year of Emperor Gaozu's reign, after capturing King Han Xin of Chu at Chen, he appointed his younger brother Jiao as king of Chu, with his capital in Pengcheng. Twenty-three years after ascending the throne, he died; his son Yiwang Ying succeeded him. In the fourth year of King Yi's reign, he died and was succeeded by his son Wang Wu.

## 4

Twenty years after King Wu ascended the throne, in winter, because of committing adultery while mourning for Empress Dowager Bo, he was stripped of Donghai Commandery. In spring, Wu conspired with King Wu of Wu to rebel; his prime minister Zhang Shang and Grand Tutor Zhao Yiwu advised against it, but he did not listen. Wu then killed Shang and Yiwu, raised troops with Wu to attack Liang in the west, and captured Jibi. When they reached the south of Changyi, they fought against General Zhou Yafu of Han. Han cut off the supply lines of Wu and Chu, and their soldiers suffered from hunger; King Wu of Wu fled, while King Wu of Chu committed suicide, after which his army surrendered to Han.

## 5

After Han had pacified Wu and Chu, Emperor Jing of Han wanted to appoint the son of Duke De to succeed Wu, and the son of Prince Yuanwang to succeed Chu. Empress Dowager Dou said: "King Wu was an old man; it is appropriate for him to be succeeded by a member of the royal family who is virtuous and in line with tradition. Now he was the first to lead seven states, causing chaos throughout the world; how can we continue his descendants!" She refused to allow Wu's succession but permitted the establishment of a successor for Chu. At that time, Li was the Han official in charge of the imperial clan records. He then appointed Li as King of Chu to uphold the ancestral temple of Prince Yuanwang; this was known as King Wen of Chu.

## 6

King Wen reigned for three years before he died, and his son Dao succeeded him as King An of Chu. In the twenty-second year of King An's reign, he died; his son Zhu was enthroned as King Xiang of Chu. King Xiang reigned for fourteen years before dying; his son Wang Chun succeeded him to the throne. After King Chun ascended the throne, in the second year of Dijie, an imperial official submitted a memorial reporting that King Chu plotted rebellion; the king committed suicide, and his state was abolished, incorporated into Han as Pengcheng Commandery.

## 7

King Zhao Liu Sui was the son of Emperor Gaozu's second son, whose name was You and posthumous title was "You." Prince You died from grief, hence the posthumous title "You." Empress Dowager Gao enthroned Lu Lu as king of Zhao; one year later Empress Dowager Gao died. The ministers executed the Lü clan, including Lu Lu, and then enthroned Youwang's son Sui as King of Zhao.

## 8

In the second year after Emperor Wen took the throne, he established Sui's younger brother Piqiang as king of Hejian, taking the Hejian Commandery from Zhao; this was King Wen. After reigning for thirteen years, he died and his son Fu succeeded him as the Marquis of Ai. One year later he died without a male heir; the line became extinct, and the state was abolished and incorporated into Han.

## 9

Sui had ruled Zhao for twenty-six years when during Emperor Jing's reign, due to Chao Cuo's policies, the state of Zhao was stripped of Changshan Commandery. When Wu and Chu rebelled, King Sui of Zhao conspired with them and raised troops. His prime minister Jiande and the Minister-in-Charge Wang Han advised against it, but he would not listen. Sui burned to death Jiande and Wang Han, mobilized troops to station at his western border, intending to wait for Wu and march west together. He sent envoys north to the Xiongnu to form an alliance and attack Han. Han dispatched Hou Quzhou Li Ji to attack him. King Sui of Zhao returned and defended the city of Handan, holding out for seven months. Wu and Chu were defeated at Liang and could not advance westward. The Xiongnu, hearing of this, also halted their advance and refused to enter Han territory. Luan Bu, having defeated Qi on his return, then combined forces and led water to flood the city of Zhao. The walls of Zhao's capital were breached; King Zhao committed suicide, and Handan surrendered accordingly. The line of Prince Youwang of Zhao became extinct.

## 10

Grand Historian said: When a state is about to flourish, there must be auspicious omens; virtuous men are employed and the wicked are driven away. When a state is on the verge of collapse, the worthy hide themselves while corrupt ministers gain power. Had King Wu of Chu not executed Shen Gong, and had he followed his advice; if Zhao had appointed Fang Yu and the Master, would there have been plots of usurpation and murder that brought disgrace upon them before all under heaven? O Virtuous men! O Virtuous men! If a ruler does not possess virtue within, how could he possibly employ them? Indeed, the saying "Safety and danger depend on issuing commands; survival or destruction depends on whom one appoints" is truly accurate!
