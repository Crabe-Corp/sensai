# Hereditary House of Confucius

## 1

Confucius was born in Zouyi, Changping Xiang, Lu. His ancestors were from Song; his name was Kong Fangshu. Fangshu gave birth to Boxia, and Boxia gave birth to Shulianghe. Shulianghe had an extramarital union with a woman of the Yan family and thus gave birth to Confucius; they prayed at Niku Hill and obtained Confucius. Confucius was born in the twenty-second year of Duke Xiang of Lu's reign. He was born with a prominent forehead and a rounded crown on his head, so he was named Qiu accordingly. His courtesy name was Zhongni; his surname was Kong.

## 2

When Qiu was born, Shulianghe had already died and was buried on Fangshan Mountain. Fangshan is located to the east of Lu; thus, Confucius doubted where his father's tomb was, as his mother concealed it. When he was a child playing games, Confucius often arranged ritual vessels and practiced ceremonial etiquette. After his mother's death, Confucius had her coffined at Wufu Qu; this was because he was cautious. The mother of Wanfu from Zou told Confucius about his father's tomb, and then they proceeded to bury them together at Fangshan.

## 3

Confucius tied a mourning cord; the Ji family held an offering banquet for scholars, and Confucius went along. Yang Hu rebuked him, saying, "The Ji family is holding a banquet for scholars; they are not daring to entertain you." Because of this, Confucius withdrew.

## 4

When Confucius was seventeen years old, Lu's official Meng Lizi fell ill and was about to die. He admonished his son Yizi, saying: "Kong Qiu is a descendant of the sage, but his lineage perished in Song." His ancestor Fufu He was the one who first possessed Song and passed on the succession to Duke Li. When Zheng Kao Fu assisted Dais, Wus, and Xuans of the Song state, he became increasingly respectful with each appointment; thus, the bronze tripod inscription reads: "Bowing once at the first appointment, stooping further at the second, bowing deeply at the third. Walking along the wall, no one dared to show me contempt." "I eat porridge here and drink gruel here, just enough to feed my mouth." Such was his respectfulness. I have heard that the descendants of sages, even if they do not hold office in their time, must include someone who attains eminence. Now Kong Qiu is young and fond of rites; could he be the one destined for greatness? Even if I die, you must take him as your teacher." After Lizi died, Yizi and Lu's Nan Gong Jingshu went to study rites with him. In this year, Duke Wu of Ji died, and Pingzi took his place.

## 5

Confucius was poor and lowly in status. When he grew up, he once served as an official for the Ji family, measuring and weighing goods fairly; he also once served as a livestock officer, during which time the animals multiplied. Because of this, he became an official in charge of public works (Sikong). Later, he left Lu and was rejected by Qi, driven out by Song and Wei, and trapped between Chen and Cai. At that time, he returned to Lu. Confucius was nine chi and six cun tall; people all called him a "tall man" and regarded him as extraordinary. Lu treated him well again, so he returned to Lu.

## 6

Nan Gong Jingshu of Lu said to the ruler of Lu: "Please allow me and Confucius to go to Zhou." The ruler of Lu gave him one carriage, two horses, and a young attendant who went with them. They traveled to Zhou to inquire about rites; it was during this time that he met Laozi. Before parting, Laozi sent him off and said: "I have heard that those who are rich and noble send people away with wealth, while those who are benevolent send them away with words. I am neither rich nor noble, yet I secretly bear the title of a benevolent person. I will send you away with words: 'Those who are intelligent and perceptive but approach death are those who like to criticize others. Those whose eloquence is broad and extensive yet endangers themselves are those who expose the evils of others." "As a son, do not act as if you have your own will; as a minister, do not act as though you are independent." After Confucius returned from Zhou to Lu, his disciples gradually increased in number.

## 7

At this time, Duke Ping of Jin was indulgent and corrupt; the six high-ranking officials held power arbitrarily, waging wars against the feudal lords in the east; 략 King Ling of Chu had a strong army and bullied the Central States; Qi was large in size and close to Lu. Lu, being small and weak, would provoke the anger of Jin if it allied with Chu; if it allied with Jin, then Chu would come to attack. Without being prepared against Qi, the army of Qi invaded Lu.

## 8

In the twentieth year of Duke Zhao of Lu's reign, Confucius was about thirty years old. Duke Jing of Qi and Yan Ying came to Lu, and Duke Jing asked Confucius: "In the past, Duke Mu of Qin had a small state in an isolated location; how did he become a hegemon?" Confucius replied: "Qin was indeed a small country, but its ambitions were great; its location may have been remote, but its conduct followed the straight and proper path." He personally appointed Wu Gu, granting him the rank of Da Fu (a high official), elevating him from among those in chains. After speaking with him for three days, he entrusted him with state affairs. By achieving success through such means, even becoming a king would be possible; to become merely a hegemon is small indeed." Duke Jing was pleased.

## 9

When Confucius was thirty-five years old, Ji Pingzi and Hou Zhaobo incurred the anger of Duke Zhao of Lu due to a cockfighting incident. Duke Zhao led his army to attack Pingzi, who, together with the Meng family and the Shusun family, launched a joint assault on Duke Zhao. Duke Zhao's forces were defeated, and he fled to Qi, where Qi granted him residence at Qianhou. After some time, Lu fell into chaos. Confucius went to Qi and became a retainer in the household of Gao Zhaozi, hoping thereby to gain access to Duke Jing. While discussing music with Qi's Grand Music Master, he heard the Shao music and studied it; for three months afterward, he did not know the taste of meat. The people of Qi praised him for this.

## 10

Duke Jing asked Confucius about governance, and Confucius said: "A ruler should be a proper ruler; a minister should be a proper minister; a father should be a proper father; a son should be a proper son." Duke Jing said, "That is good! If indeed a ruler is not a proper ruler, a minister not a proper minister, a father not a proper father, and a son not a proper son, even if there were grain, how could I possibly eat it!" On another occasion, Duke Jing again asked Confucius about governance. Confucius said: "The key to governance lies in frugality." Duke Jing was pleased and intended to grant the fields of Nixi to Confucius as a fief. Yan Ying advised, saying: "Scholars of the Ru school are eloquent and witty but cannot be governed by established laws; they are arrogant and self-indulgent, and thus cannot serve as subordinates; they elevate mourning to excessive sorrow, squander property for lavish burials, which cannot be a model for society; they travel from place to place making speeches and begging for support, which is not suitable for the state." Since the time of great sages passed away, the Zhou dynasty had already declined, and there were gaps in rites and music. Now Confucius emphasizes elaborate attire, complicated rituals of ascending and descending, and detailed courtesies; generations cannot exhaust his teachings, and a lifetime cannot fully grasp his rites. If the ruler wishes to use him in order to change Qi's customs, it is not suitable as an example for common people." Later, Duke Jing received Confucius with respect but did not inquire about his rites. On another day, Duke Jing stopped Confucius and said: "I cannot treat you as the Ji family treats their officials." He treated him somewhere between the ranks of the Ji and Meng families. Some officials from Qi wanted to harm Confucius, and Confucius learned about it. Duke Jing said: "I am old now; I cannot employ you." Confucius then left and returned to Lu.

## 11

When Confucius was forty-two years old, Duke Zhao of Lu died at Qianhou, and Duke Ding took the throne. Five years after Duke Ding ascended the throne, in summer, Ji Pingzi died, and Huanzi succeeded him. Ji Huanzi was digging a well when he found an earthen jar; inside it, there appeared to be something like a sheep. He asked Zhongni and was told: "It is a dog." Zhongni said: "According to what I have heard, it is a sheep. I have heard that among the strange creatures of wood and stone are Kuí and Wanglang; in water, Long and Wangxiang; and in earth, Fen Yang."

## 12

Wu attacked Yue, destroyed Kuaiji, and found a bone so large that it required an entire cart to transport just one section. The state of Wu sent envoys to ask Zhongni: "Which is the largest kind of bone?" Zhongni said: "Yu summoned the gods and spirits to Kuaiji Mountain. Fangfeng Shi arrived late, so Yu killed and executed him; his bones were so large that one section required an entire cart for transport—this was the largest." The envoy from Wu asked: "Who is a god?" Zhongni said: "The gods of mountains and rivers are sufficient to govern the world; those who guard them are considered deities, while the gods of Sheji (soil and grain) are like marquises and dukes, all belonging to the king." The guest asked: "What did Fangfeng guard?" Zhongni said: "The ruler of Wangwang Shi guarded Feng and Yu Mountains, belonging to the Li surname. In the time of Yu, Xia, and Shang dynasties, they were known as Wangwang; in Zhou times, they were called Changzhi, and now they are referred to as Daren (great people)." The guest asked: "How tall can a person be?" Zhongni said: "The people of Jiaoju Shi were three chi tall, the shortest possible. Those who are tall do not exceed ten times that height; this is the maximum limit." At this, the guest from Wu said: "How wise a sage you are!"

## 13

Huanzi's favorite retainer was Zhongliang Huai, who had a conflict with Yang Hu. Yang Hu wanted to drive out Huai, but Gongsan Butniu stopped him. That autumn, Huai became even more arrogant, and Yang Hu arrested him. Huanzi was angry, so Yang Hu took advantage of the situation to imprison Huanzi, made an oath with him, and then released him. Yang Hu thus became even more contemptuous toward the Ji family. The Ji family also overstepped their status in relation to the royal house, as a retainer of a minister held state power; therefore, from officials down in Lu, all transgressed and deviated from the proper path. Therefore, Confucius did not hold office but withdrew to study and revise the Book of Poetry, the Book of Documents, rites, and music. His disciples grew increasingly numerous, coming from distant places, none of whom failed to receive instruction from him.

## 14

In the eighth year of Duke Ding's reign, Gongsan Butniu was dissatisfied with the Ji family and joined Yang Hu in rebellion. They wanted to depose the legitimate heirs of the three Huan families and replace them with a collateral branch favored by Yang Hu, so they seized Ji Huanzi. Huanzi tricked them and managed to escape. In the ninth year of Duke Ding's reign, Yang Hu was defeated and fled to Qi. At that time, Confucius was fifty years old.

## 15

Gongsan Butniu rebelled against the Ji family in Fei and sent someone to summon Confucius. Confucius had adhered to the Way for a long time, was gentle and unassuming without any opportunity to demonstrate his abilities, and no one could employ him. He said: "The Zhou Wenwang and Wuwang rose from Feng and Hao to become kings; now although Fei is small, perhaps something similar might happen!" He wanted to go. Zilu was displeased and stopped Confucius. Confucius said, "Could the one who summoned me have done so without purpose?" "If they employ me, perhaps I could become an Eastern Zhou!" Nevertheless, he ultimately did not go.

## 16

Later, Duke Ding appointed Confucius as the administrator of Zhongdu. In one year, all regions followed his example. From being an administrator of Zhongdu, he became Minister of Works, and from Minister of Works, he became Grand Magistrate.

## 17

In spring of the tenth year of Duke Ding's reign, peace was made with Qi. In summer, Li Chu, a minister of Qi, said to Duke Jing: "Lu is employing Kong Qiu; his influence poses a threat to Qi." So he sent envoys to inform Lu of a friendly meeting, which took place at Jiagu. Duke Ding of Lu was about to go in great splendor. Confucius, acting as prime minister, said: "I have heard that where there are civil affairs, military preparedness is essential; and where there are military affairs, civil preparedness is also necessary. "In ancient times, when feudal lords left their territories, they were always accompanied by officials in full attendance. Please provide the Left and Right Marshals." Duke Ding said, "Agreed." The Left and Right Marshals were provided. At the meeting with Duke of Qi at Jiagu, an altar was set up with a three-tiered earthen platform. They met according to the rites for equal states, bowing and yielding as they ascended the platform. After the rituals of offering and returning wine were completed, an official from Qi hurriedly stepped forward and said: "Please present the music of the four directions." Duke Jing said, "Agreed." At this, banners with plumes and feathers, spears, halberds, swords, whips, drums, and shouting troops arrived. Confucius hurried forward, ascended the steps one by one, not reaching the topmost step, and raising his sleeve to speak said: "Our two rulers have convened a friendly meeting; what business do the music of barbarians have here! I request that officials be ordered to remove it!" The officials moved them back, but when they did not leave, Confucius looked toward Yanzi and Duke Jing on both sides. Duke Jing felt ashamed inwardly and waved for them to be dismissed. After a short while, an official from Qi hurried forward and said: "Please present the music of the palace." Duke Jing said: "Agreed." Acrobats and midgets performed a play and advanced. Confucius hurried forward, climbed the steps one by one without reaching the topmost step and said: "A commoner who deceives feudal lords deserves to be executed! I request that officials carry out the punishment!" The officials applied the law, and their hands and feet were severed. Duke Jing was frightened and shaken, realizing that righteousness was beyond his grasp. Upon returning, he was greatly alarmed and told his ministers: "Lu uses the Way of the gentleman to assist its ruler, yet you alone taught me with the ways of barbarians, causing me to offend Duke Lu. What shall I do about this?" An official stepped forward and replied: "When a gentleman makes a mistake, he apologizes with sincerity; when a petty person makes a mistake, they apologize with words. If Your Majesty regrets it, you should apologize sincerely." Thus, Duke of Qi returned the territories of Yun, Wenyang, and Guiyin that had been encroached upon from Lu as an apology for his mistake.

## 18

In the summer of the thirteenth year of Duke Ding's reign, Confucius said to Duke Ding: "Ministers must not store weapons, and high officials must not have cities with walls over a hundred chi in height." He sent Zhongyou as administrator of the Ji family to dismantle the three walled cities. At this time, the Shusun clan first dismantled Hou. The Ji family was about to dismantle Fei, but Gongsan Butniou and Shusun Zhe led the people of Fei in an attack on Lu. The duke and the three ministers entered the residence of the Ji family and ascended Wuzi's terrace. The people of Fei attacked them, but failed to overcome their defenses; they penetrated as far as the duke's side. Confucius ordered Shen Juxu and Yue Qi to descend and attack them, and the people of Fei retreated northward. The citizens pursued them and defeated them at Gumie. The two men fled to Qi, and thus Fei was dismantled. As they were about to dismantle Cheng, Gong Lian Chufu said to Mengsun: "If we dismantle the walls of Cheng, the people of Qi will surely reach our northern gate. Moreover, Cheng is the stronghold protecting the Meng family; to have no Cheng means there would be no Meng family. I will not dismantle it." In the twelfth month, the duke besieged Cheng but failed to capture it.

## 19

In the fourteenth year of Duke Ding's reign (526 BC), Confucius was fifty-six years old. He served as Grand Magistrate acting in the role of prime minister and showed a look of joy. His disciples said: "We have heard that gentlemen do not fear when misfortune comes, nor rejoice when fortune arrives." Confucius said: "There is such a saying. Is it not also said, 'Doesn't one rejoice in being noble yet humble toward others'?" At this time, he executed Shaozheng Mao, a Lu official who had been causing political disorder. After three months of participating in state affairs, those selling lambs and piglets no longer inflated their prices; men and women walking on the roads kept separate paths; nothing lost in the road was picked up by others; and guests from all directions who arrived at the city did not need to seek out officials, for they were all given what was needed and sent on their way.

## 20

The people of Qi heard this and were alarmed, saying: "If Kong Qiu governs, Lu will surely become dominant. If Lu becomes dominant, our territory is nearby, so we shall be the first to be annexed. Why not give them land?" Li Chu said: "Let me try to dissuade them first; if that fails, then we can offer land. It is better late than never!" Thus, they selected eighty beautiful women from Qi who were skilled in dancing the Kang music and dressed them in colorful robes. They also presented thirty teams of four fine horses as a gift to Duke Lu of Lu. They displayed the dancing women and fine horses outside the high gate to the south of Lu's city. Ji Huanzi, dressed in plain clothes, went to watch them several times. On the verge of accepting the gifts, he told Duke Lu that they were touring along the Zhou Road, spending entire days watching and neglecting official duties. Zilu said: "Master, you may now leave." Confucius said: "Lu is about to hold a suburban sacrifice. If they offer the sacrificial meat to the ministers, then I can still remain." Ji Huanzi finally accepted the dancing women from Qi and did not attend to government affairs for three days; During the suburban sacrifice, he also failed to present the sacrificial meat on the ritual platters to the ministers. Confucius then left and stayed overnight in Tun. Shi Ji accompanied him, saying: "Master is certainly not at fault." Confucius said: "Shall I sing?" He sang: "A wife's words can drive one away; her petitions may lead to death and failure. "Ah, how leisurely it is to wander about, just to pass the years!" Shi Ji returned, and Ji Huanzi asked: "What did Confucius say?" Shi Ji told him the truth. Ji Huanzi sighed and said: "The Master is blaming me because of those concubines, isn't he!"

## 21

Confucius thus went to Wei and stayed at the home of Yanzuo Zou, his sister-in-law's brother-in-law to Zilu. Duke Ling of Wei asked Confucius: "What salary did you receive while in Lu?" Confucius replied: "Six hundred dan of millet." The people of Wei also provided six hundred dan of millet. After a short time, someone slandered Confucius to Duke Ling of Wei. Duke Ling sent Gongsun Yujia to go out and in once. Confucius feared being accused of a crime, so after staying for ten months, he left Wei.

## 22

He was about to go to Chen and passed through Kuang. Yankuo, serving as his attendant, pointed with his whip and said: "In the past, I entered here from that gap." The people of Kuang heard this and thought he was Yang Hu of Lu. Since Yang Hu had once oppressed the people of Kuang, they now detained Confucius. Confucius resembled Yang Hu in appearance and was thus held for five days. When Yan Yuan arrived later, Confucius said: "I thought you were dead." Yan Yuan replied: "As long as the Master is alive, how dare I die!" The people of Kuang detained Confucius more urgently, and his disciples became frightened. Confucius said: "Since King Wen is already dead, isn't the culture of Zhou now in us?" "If Heaven were about to destroy this culture, it would not entrust it to those who come after." "But since Heaven is not yet destroying this culture, what can the people of Kuang do to me!" Confucius had his followers serve as retainers of Ning Wuzi in Wei; only then was he allowed to leave.

## 23

After leaving, he passed through Pu. More than a month later, he returned to Wei and stayed at Qu Boyu's house. There was a woman named Nanzi, the wife of Duke Ling, who sent someone to tell Confucius: "If gentlemen from all directions do not consider it an insult and wish to be brothers with my lord, they must meet me, his minor lady." "I would like to see you." Confucius declined and expressed gratitude, but eventually met her out of necessity. The lady was inside a curtain made of chī fabric. Confucius entered the door, bowed deeply facing north. The lady herself bowed twice from within the curtain, and the sound of her jade pendants was clear and resonant. Confucius said: "Earlier I refrained from seeing her; now that I have seen her, I must respond with proper courtesy." Zilu was displeased. Confucius swore an oath, saying: "If I have done anything improper, may Heaven abhor me!" "May Heaven abhor me!" After staying in Wei for more than a month, Duke Ling traveled with his wife in the same carriage, accompanied by the eunuch Yong Qu as a passenger. They went out and had Confucius ride in the following carriage, parading through the marketplace. Confucius said: "I have never seen anyone who loves virtue as much as he loves beauty." At this, Confucius was offended and left Wei, passing through Cao. In that year, Duke Ding of Lu died.

## 24

Confucius left Cao and went to Song, where he practiced rituals with his disciples under a large tree. Sima Huantui of Song wanted to kill Confucius and uprooted the tree. Confucius left. His disciples said: "We can leave quickly now." Confucius said: "Heaven has endowed me with virtue; what can Huantui do to me!"

## 25

Confucius went to Zheng, but became separated from his disciples. Confucius stood alone at the eastern gate of the outer city. ල A man from Zheng told Zigong: "There is a person at the eastern gate. His forehead resembles Yao, his neck resembles Gao Tao, and his shoulders resemble Zi Chan; yet from the waist down he falls short of Yu by three cun." "He looks disheveled like a lost dog from a ruined home." Zigong told Confucius the truth. Confucius smiled and said: "Appearance is unimportant." "And to say I look like a lost dog from a fallen household—how true that is!" "How true that is!"

## 26

Confucius then went to Chen and stayed at the house of Shicheng Zhenzi. After more than a year, King Fuchai of Wu attacked Chen, captured three towns, and then withdrew. Zhao Mang attacked Chaoguo. Chu besieged Cai, and the state of Cai moved to Wu. Wu defeated King Goujian of Yue at Kuaiji.

## 27

A falcon landed in the court of Chen and died, pierced by a shaft made of kǔ wood with a stone arrowhead. The arrow was one chi and zhi long. Duke Min of Chen sent an envoy to inquire from Zhongni. Zhongni said: "This falcon has come from afar; this is an arrow of the Su Shen." "In ancient times, King Wu defeated Shang and opened roads to the Nine Yi and Hundred Man. He made them each bring tribute from their regions so that they would not forget their duties." At that time, Su Shen offered kǔ arrows with stone arrowheads, one chi and zhi in length. The former kings wished to demonstrate their great virtue by giving a Su Shen arrow to Da Ji, who was married to Hu Gong of Yu and enfeoffed in Chen. They distributed precious jade among those of the same surname to demonstrate closeness; they gave distant duties to those of different surnames, so that they would not forget submission. "Therefore, Chen was given the Su Shen arrow." They searched in the old treasury and indeed found it.

## 28

Confucius stayed in Chen for three years, when the states of Jin and Chu vied for dominance and alternately attacked Chen. In addition, Wu invaded Chen, so Chen was often under attack. Confucius said: "Let us return!" "Let us return!" "My disciples are reckless and impetuous, but they strive forward without forgetting their original aspirations." At this time, Confucius left Chen.

## 29

He passed through Pu and encountered the Gongsu clan rebelling with Pu; the people of Pu detained Confucius. Among his disciples was Gongliang Ru, who followed Confucius with five private chariots. He was tall, virtuous, and brave, and said: "I once followed my master into trouble in Kuang; now we are again encountering hardship here. This must be fate." "I have suffered misfortune with my master twice; I would rather fight and die." The battle was very fierce. Fearing the situation, the people of Pu told Confucius: "If you promise not to go to Wei, we will release you." They made an oath with him and let Confucius leave through the eastern gate. Confucius then went to Wei. Zigong asked: "Can an oath be broken?" Confucius said: "An oath made under duress, the gods will not heed."

## 30

Duke Ling of Wei heard that Confucius had arrived and was pleased; he welcomed him at the outskirts. He asked: "Can Pu be attacked?" Confucius replied: "Yes." Duke Ling said: "My officials think it is not possible." "Now, Pu is what Wei uses to guard against Jin and Chu. If Wei attacks it, would that not be inappropriate?" Confucius said: "The men there have the resolve to die for their cause, and the women are determined to defend the western river." "The people I would attack are no more than four or five individuals." Duke Ling said: "Well spoken." Yet he did not attack Pu.

## 31

Duke Ling was old and neglectful of state affairs, so he did not employ Confucius. Confucius sighed and said: "If there were someone who would use me, within a year things could be set in order, and after three years, success would be achieved." Confucius departed.

## 32

Bo Xi was the administrator of Zhongmou. Zhao Jianzi attacked Fan and the Zhonghang clan, waging war on Zhongmou. Bo Xi rebelled and sent someone to summon Confucius. Confucius wanted to go. Zilu said: "I have heard from my master that 'a gentleman does not enter a place where the ruler personally engages in evil deeds.'" "Now, Bo Xi has personally rebelled with Zhongmou; you want to go there. How can that be?" Confucius said: "Yes, I have made such a statement." Confucius said: "That has been said before. Is it not called firmness? Even when ground, it does not become scaleless." Is it not called whiteness? Even when dyed, it does not turn black. "Am I a gourd melon? How could I just hang there without being used?"

## 33

Confucius played the qing. A man carrying a basket passed by his door and said: "How earnest he is, playing the qing!" "Strident and forceful! It's just that no one knows him anymore!"

## 34

Confucius studied the guqin with Shixiangzi, and for ten days he made no progress. Shixiangzi said: "You may now advance further." Confucius said: "I have already become familiar with the melody, but I have not yet grasped its rhythm." After some time, he said: "I have now mastered its rhythm. You may now advance further." Confucius said: "I have not yet understood the intent behind it." After some time, he said: "I have now grasped its intent. You may now advance further." Confucius said: "I have not yet come to understand the character of the person behind it." After some time, [he] was seen in deep and solemn contemplation, then with a joyful expression gazing upward as if harboring lofty aspirations. He said: "I have come to understand the kind of person it represents. He is dark-complexioned and tall, with eyes like those gazing at a sheep, as if he were a king ruling over four states. If not King Wen, who else could compose such music?" Shixiangzi rose from his seat and bowed twice, saying: "The teacher has indeed said this is the composition of King Wen."

## 35

After Confucius failed to gain employment in Wei, he planned to travel westward to meet Lord Zhao Jianzi. When he reached the river, he heard of the deaths of Dou Mingtu and Shun Hua. Standing by the riverbank, he sighed: "How beautiful is this water, so vast and flowing!" "If I cannot cross this river, it must be my fate!" Zigong hurried forward and asked: "May I ask what you mean by that?" Confucius said: "Dou Mingtu and Shun Hua were virtuous ministers of the Jin state." When Lord Zhao Jianzi had not yet achieved his ambitions, he needed these two men before he could govern. But after he had attained power, he killed them and then began to govern. I have heard it said: If one dissects pregnant animals or kills young ones, the qilin will not come near the suburbs; If one drains a lake to catch all its fish, then the jiaolong will no longer harmonize with yin and yang; If one destroys nests and breaks eggs, then the fenghuang will not soar. Why is that? Because gentlemen avoid harming their own kind. Birds and beasts still know to avoid injustice, so how much more should I, Confucius!" He then returned to rest in Zouxiang and composed the "Zoucao" as a lament for them. And he returned to Wei, entering the household of Qu Boyu.

## 36

On another day, Duke Ling asked about military formations. Confucius said: "I have heard of rituals and ceremonies, but I have not studied matters of the army." The next day, when speaking with Confucius, he saw a flock of geese flying by. Looking up at them, his expression was no longer on Confucius. Confucius then left and returned to Chen once more.

## 37

In summer, Duke Ling of Wei died; his grandson Zhe was installed as ruler, known as Duke Chugong of Wei. On the sixth month, Zhao Mang brought Crown Prince Kuaike into Qi. Yang Hu had the crown prince wear mourning clothes, with eight men dressed in black and wearing cords of hemp as if they were mourners. Pretending to be people coming from Wei to welcome him, they wept and entered, then took up residence there. In winter, the state of Cai moved its capital to Zhoulai. This year was the third year of Duke Ai's reign in Lu, and Confucius had reached the age of sixty. Qi assisted Wei in besieging Qi because the Crown Prince Kuaike was there.

## 38

In summer, a fire broke out at the temple of Duke Huanli of Lu; Nan Gong Jingshu rushed to put it out. Confucius, who was in Chen at the time, heard about it and said: "The disaster must have occurred at the temple of Duke Huanlí, right?" Soon after, this proved to be true.

## 39

In autumn, Ji Huanzi fell ill and was carried in a palanquin to view the city of Lu. He sighed deeply and said: "Once this state was on the verge of prosperity, but because I incurred Confucius's displeasure, it did not flourish." He turned to his son Kangzi and said: "If I die, you must become the prime minister of Lu; when you serve as prime minister, you must surely summon Zhongni." Several days later, Huanzi died and Kangzi took his place. After the funeral rites were completed, he wanted to summon Zhongni. Gong Zhiyu said: "In the past, our late lord employed him but did not see it through to the end, and in the end we were laughed at by the feudal lords. If we now employ him again and still fail to carry it through, we will be ridiculed once more by the feudal lords." Kangzi asked: "Then who else should I summon?" He said: "You must summon Ran Qiu." Thus, he sent envoys to summon Ran Qiu. As Ran Qiu was about to depart, Confucius said: "The people of Lu are summoning you. This is not for a minor appointment; they intend to give you an important role." On that day, Confucius said: "Return! Return!" "My disciples are wild and impetuous, yet they display a colorful mastery of writing. I do not know how to guide them." Zigong, knowing that Confucius longed to return home, accompanied Ran Qiu on his journey and thus warned him: "If you are appointed, use Confucius as your recommendation," he said.

## 40

After Ran Qiu had left, the following year, Confucius moved from Chen to Cai. Duke Zhao of Cai was about to go to Wu; he had been summoned by Wu. Previously, Duke Zhao had deceived his ministers and moved the capital to Zhoulai; now he was about to leave again. The officials feared another relocation, so Gongsun Pian shot and killed Duke Zhao. Chu invaded Cai. In autumn, Duke Jing of Qi died.

## 41

The following year, Confucius left Cai and went to Ye. Ye Gong asked about governance; Confucius said: "Governance lies in attracting the distant and uniting the near." On another day, Ye Gong asked Zigong about Confucius; Zigong did not answer. Confucius heard about it and said: "You, why didn't you say 'He is a person who studies the Way without tiring, teaches others without growing weary, works with such determination that he forgets to eat, finds joy so great that he forgets his worries, and does not even realize old age is approaching'?"

## 42

After leaving Ye, Confucius returned to Cai. Chang Ju and Jie Ni were plowing together; Confucius regarded them as recluses, so he sent Zigong to ask for directions. Chang Ju asked: "Who is the man holding the reins?" Zigong said: "He is Kong Qiu." Chang Ju asked: "Is this the Kong Qiu of Lu?" Zigong said: "Yes." He said: "Then he knows the ford already." Jie Ni asked Zigong: "Who are you?" Zigong said: "I am Zhong You." Jie Ni asked: "Are you a disciple of Kong Qiu?" Zigong said: "Yes." Jie Ni said: "The world is full of people who are idle and indifferent. Who will change this situation? "And furthermore, would it not be better to follow those who retreat from the world rather than those who merely avoid bad people?"! They continued tilling without stopping. Zigong reported this to Confucius, who sighed and said: "Birds and beasts cannot be kept in the same flock. "If the world were just, I would not seek to change it."

## 43

On another occasion, Zigong was walking and met an old man carrying a bamboo basket. The old man asked: "Have you seen the master?" The old man said: "If one does not work with his four limbs and cannot distinguish the five grains, who is this 'master'?"! He planted his staff in the ground and continued weeding. Zigong reported this, and Confucius said: "This is a recluse." When Zigong returned to visit him again, the old man had already left.

## 44

After three years in Cai, Confucius was still there when Wu attacked Chen. Chu came to the aid of Chen and stationed its army at Chengfu. Hearing that Confucius was between Chen and Cai, Chu sent envoys to invite him. Confucius was about to go and pay his respects, but the ministers of Chen and Cai plotted: "Confucius is a man of virtue; all his criticisms strike at the ills of the feudal lords. Now that he has been staying for a long time between Chen and Cai, all the actions proposed by the ministers do not align with Zhongni's intentions. Now Chu is a great state that has come to invite Confucius. If Confucius were employed by Chu, then the influential ministers of Chen and Cai would be in danger." Therefore, they conspired together to mobilize their men and surround Confucius in the wilderness. Unable to proceed, they were cut off from food supplies. His followers fell ill and could not rise. Confucius continued to lecture, recite poetry, and play the zither without faltering. Zilu angrily said: "Can a man of virtue also fall into hardship?" Confucius replied: "A man of virtue remains steadfast in adversity, but when a petty person is in distress, he becomes reckless."

## 45

Zigong's expression changed. Confucius said: "Cai, do you think I am a person who learns much and remembers it?" He replied: "Yes." "Is that not so?" Confucius said: "No, it is not." "I have one principle that runs through all things."

## 46

Confucius, knowing his disciples were resentful, summoned Zigong and asked: "The Book of Songs says 'Not rhinoceroses nor tigers, yet they lead in the wilderness.'" "Is my way wrong?" "Why am I here then?" Zilu replied: "Perhaps we are not yet benevolent? That is why people do not believe in us." "Perhaps we are not wise? That is why people do not follow us." "That is why people do not put our teachings into practice." Confucius said: "Is that so!" "You, if a benevolent person were always trusted, how could there be Boyi and Shuqi?" If a wise person were always followed, how could there have been Prince Wizi Bingan?"

## 47

Zilu went out and Zigong entered to see him. Confucius said: "Cai, the Book of Songs says 'Not a rhinoceros nor a tiger, yet they roam in that wilderness.'" "Is my way wrong?" "Why am I here then?" Zigong replied: "Master's way is so great, hence the world cannot accommodate Master. Perhaps you should slightly lower your standards?" Confucius said: "Cai, a good farmer can cultivate but cannot guarantee harvests; a skilled craftsman can be clever but cannot always meet expectations. A man of virtue can cultivate his way, establish its framework and principles, organize and manage it, but cannot force the world to accept him. Now you do not cultivate your own way yet seek acceptance from others. Cai, your aspirations are not far-reaching!"

## 48

Zigong went out and Yan Hui entered to see him. Confucius said: "Hui, the Book of Songs says, 'Not a rhinoceros, not a tiger, yet leading in that wilderness.'" "Is my way wrong?" "Why am I here then?" Yan Hui replied: "Master's way is extremely great, so the world cannot contain it. Nevertheless, Master continues to promote and practice it; not being accepted is no disgrace. It is precisely when one is not accepted that a man of virtue becomes evident!" If the Way is not cultivated, that is my shame. The Way has been greatly cultivated yet remains unused; this is the disgrace of those who hold power in a state. "Not being accepted causes no harm; it is precisely when one is not accepted by others that a man of virtue reveals himself!" Confucius smiled happily and said: "How true this is, the son of the Yan family! "If you had more wealth, I would be your steward."

## 49

At this point, Confucius sent Zigong to Chu. The King of Chu mobilized his army to welcome Confucius, and only then was he rescued from the predicament.

## 50

King Zhao would have granted Confucius a fief of seven hundred li of land with written confirmation. Chu's Lingyi Zixi asked: "Does the king have an envoy to the feudal lords as capable as Zigong?" He replied: "No." "Do you have a minister as virtuous as Yan Hui?" He replied: "No." "Do you have a general as capable as Zilu?" He replied: "No." "Do you have an official as capable as Zaiyu?" He replied: "No." "Moreover, the ancestors of Chu were granted a fief by the Zhou dynasty with the title of Zi Nan covering fifty li. Now Confucius expounds the laws of the Three Dynasties and Five Emperors, clarifies the achievements of Zhou Gong and Shao Kang. If King were to employ him, how could Chu maintain for generations a vast territory spanning thousands of li?" King Wen ruled from Feng and King Wu from Hao; rulers of only a hundred li eventually became kings over the whole world. If Confucius now gains land with his virtuous disciples as assistants, it will not be a blessing for Chu." King Zhao then stopped the plan. That autumn, King Zhao of Chu died in Chengfu.

## 51

Chu Kuang Jieyu sang as he passed by Confucius and said: "Phoenix! Phoenix!" "Phoenix! Phoenix!" "Why has virtue declined? "The past cannot be changed, but the future is still within reach!" "Already gone, already gone! Those who govern today are in peril!" Confucius stepped down from his carriage, wanting to speak with him. But the man hurried away and Confucius could not catch up to speak with him.

## 52

At this time, Confucius returned from Chu back to Wei. In this year, Confucius was sixty-three years old, and it was the sixth year of Duke Ai's reign in Lu.

## 53

The following year, Wu met with Lu at Zeng and demanded a hundred sacrificial offerings. Tai Tsai Pi summoned Ji Kangzi. Ji Kangzi sent Zigong to go, and only then did the matter cease.

## 54

Confucius said: "The politics of Lu and Wei are like those of brothers." At this time, the ruler of Wei, Zhu Zhefu, could not ascend to power and was outside the state; many feudal lords frequently criticized him for it. Many of Confucius's disciples were employed in Wei, and the ruler of Wei wished to have Confucius govern his state. Zilu said: "If the ruler of Wei awaits you to take charge of governance, what will you do first?" Confucius said: "It must be to correct names!" Zilu said: "Is that true? How impractical you are, Master! "Why must we start with correcting names?" Confucius said: "How rustic you are, You! "If names are not correct, words will be unconvincing; if words are unconvincing, affairs cannot succeed; if affairs cannot succeed, rites and music will not flourish; if rites and music do not flourish, punishments and penalties will be misapplied; and if punishments and penalties are misapplied, the people will have no idea where to place their hands or feet." A man of virtue must act in a way that can be given proper names and speak words that are sure to be carried out. For a gentleman, there is no room for carelessness or compromise in his words."

## 55

The following year, Ran You served as general under Ji Shi and fought against Qi at Lang, achieving a victory. Ji Kangzi asked: "Did you learn about military affairs?" "Or is it innate to you?" Ran You replied: "I learned it from Confucius." Ji Kangzi asked: "What kind of person is Confucius?" Ran You replied: "He uses them with proper names; he proclaims it to the people, and when tested before spirits and deities, there is no regret." "To pursue this way so thoroughly, even if a thousand communities were offered, the Master would not be tempted by it." Ji Kangzi said: "I wish to summon him. Is that possible?" Ran You replied: "If you wish to summon him, do not let petty people obstruct it, and then it will be possible." Meanwhile, Wei Kongwuzi was about to attack Doushi and consulted Confucius for strategy. Confucius pretended not to know, then ordered his carriage prepared and said: "A bird can choose its tree; how could a tree choose the bird?" Kongwuzi insisted on keeping him. At that time, Ji Kangzi expelled Gong Hua, Gong Bin, and Gong Lin, and sent gifts to welcome Confucius back; thus, Confucius returned to Lu.

## 56

Confucius had been away from Lu for a total of fourteen years before returning to Lu.

## 57

Duke Ai of Lu asked about governance, and Confucius replied: "The key to government lies in selecting the right ministers." Ji Kangzi asked about governing, and he said: "Promote upright people over crooked ones, and the crooked will become straight." Ji Kangzi was troubled by thieves. Confucius said: "If you yourself have no desire for improper things, even if rewards are offered, people will not steal." However, the State of Lu ultimately did not employ Confucius, and he also did not seek office.

## 58

In Confucius's time, the Zhou dynasty was weak and rites and music were neglected; the Book of Songs and the Book of Documents were incomplete. He traced back to the rites of the Three Dynasties, arranged the historical records, recording events from the time of Tang and Yu upwards to the period of Qin Miao. He said: "I can speak about Xia rites, but the state of Qi is insufficient as evidence. "I can speak about Yin rites, but the state of Song is insufficient as evidence." "If there were enough records, then I could provide evidence." Observing the changes and additions made by Yin and Xia, he said: "Even a hundred generations later, it can be known, because of alternating ceremonial forms and substantive principles. "Zhou observed the two previous dynasties; how rich in ceremony its culture is!" "I follow Zhou." Therefore, the "Book of Documents" and the "Record of Rites" originate from Kong's family.

## 59

Confucius told the chief musician of Lu: "Music can be understood. At first, it begins with a sudden flourish; then it flows smoothly, clearly and distinctly, unfolding in sequence to reach completion." "I returned from Wei to Lu, and only then did music become properly regulated; the Ya and Song were each placed in their proper positions."

## 60

In ancient times, there were more than three thousand poems. By the time of Confucius, he removed duplicates and selected those suitable for rites and righteousness. He drew from the achievements of Qi and Houji in antiquity, described the prosperity of Yin and Zhou dynasties in the middle part, and reached down to the decline during Youli's reign. Starting with what was close at hand, hence it is said: "The disorder of 'Guanju' marks the beginning of the Feng section; 'Lüming' marks the start of Xiao Ya; 'Wenwang' marks the start of Da Ya; and 'Qingmiao' marks the start of Song." Confucius played all 305 poems on the zither and sang them, seeking to harmonize with the tones of Shao, Wu, Ya, and Song. From this point onward, rites and music could be properly described, to prepare for the way of kingship and complete the Six Arts.

## 61

In his later years, Confucius took great interest in the Yijing (Book of Changes), arranging the Tuan, Xi, Xiang, Shuogua, and Wenyan. While reading the Yijing, he wore out its leather cords three times. He said: "If I were given a few more years, if so, then my understanding of the Yijing would be thorough and refined."

## 62

Confucius taught his disciples with the Book of Songs, the Book of Documents, rites, music, and other subjects. His students numbered about three thousand, among whom seventy-two had mastered all six arts thoroughly. People like Yan Zuozi and others received instruction from him; many students were taught in his school.

## 63

Confucius taught through four aspects: literature, conduct, loyalty, and trustworthiness. He cut off four things: no arbitrary assumptions, no rigid certainties, no stubbornness, and no self-centeredness. What he was cautious about were fasting for rituals, battle, and illness. Zi Han rarely spoke of profit, fate, or benevolence. He did not enlighten a student who was not eager to learn; if someone could not infer the other three corners from one corner presented, he would no longer repeat it.

## 64

In his native district, he appeared modest and as though unable to speak. But in the ancestral temple or court, he spoke clearly and eloquently, yet always with caution. In the morning court session, when speaking with high-ranking officials, his manner was respectful and earnest; when speaking with lower-ranking officials, he was composed and dignified.

## 65

Upon entering the public hall, he bowed as if showing deep respect; When hurrying forward, his posture was like that of a bird spreading its wings. When summoned by the ruler to serve as an attendant at a reception, he looked alert and composed. If summoned by the lord's command, he would set out immediately without waiting for his carriage to be prepared.

## 66

When fish was spoiled, meat decayed, or meat cut improperly, he did not eat it. If the mat was not properly arranged, he would not sit on it. When eating near someone mourning a death, he never ate to his full satisfaction.

## 67

On the day he wept, he did not sing. When seeing someone in mourning clothes or a blind person, even if they were children, his expression would change with respect.

## 68

"Among three people walking together, there must be one who can be my teacher." "Not cultivating virtue, not discussing learning, failing to act upon righteousness when heard, and being unable to correct one's faults—these are my concerns." When he had someone sing a song, if it was good, he would ask them to repeat it and then harmonize with it.

## 69

Zi Bu spoke little about strange phenomena, strength, chaos, or spirits.

## 70

Zigong said: "The Master's literary works and teachings can be heard." "But the Master's words about the principles of Heaven, human nature, and destiny are no longer to be heard." Yanyuan sighed and said: "The higher I look up at it, the more exalted it appears; the deeper I study into it, the firmer it becomes. Looking ahead, it is before me; suddenly, it seems to be behind." The Master guided people step by step and skillfully. He enriched me with literature and restrained me with rites, making it impossible for me to stop learning. After exhausting my abilities, as if something had been established, standing out distinctly. Though I wish to follow it, there is no way to do so anymore." A young man from Daxiang said: "Great indeed is Confucius! He has extensive learning but no single field in which he is renowned." Confucius heard this and said: "What should I cling to? Should it be driving a chariot?" Or archery?" "I will say I am good at driving." Lao said: "The Master said, 'He has not been tested, hence his skills.'"

## 71

In the spring of the fourteenth year of Duke Ai of Lu's reign, he went hunting in Daye. Shusun Shi's charioteer Chushang captured an animal and considered it an ill omen. lık Confucius examined it and said: "It is a qilin." He took it. He said: "The Yellow River has not produced the map, and the Luo River has not brought forth the book; I am finished!" When Yanyuan died, Confucius said: "Heaven is bereaving me!" And when he saw the qilin during his western hunting expedition, he said: "My way has come to an end!" He sighed deeply and said: "No one understands me!" Zigong asked: "Why do people not understand you?" Confucius said: "I do not blame Heaven, nor do I accuse others. Learning from the humble and reaching up to the lofty—perhaps only Heaven understands me!"

## 72

"He who does not lower his aspirations nor disgrace himself—this is Boyi and Shuqi, isn't it?" He said: "Liu Xiaohui and Shaolian lowered their aspirations and disgraced themselves." He referred to "Yu Zhong and Yiyi, who lived in seclusion and spoke freely; their conduct was upright and clean, and they abandoned things with wisdom." "As for me, I am different from them—I find nothing absolutely acceptable or unacceptable."

## 73

Confucius said: "Alas! Alas! A gentleman is distressed when he dies without his name being honored. "My way will not be followed; how can I make myself known to future generations?" Thus, he based on historical records and compiled the Spring and Autumn Annals, beginning with Duke Yin and ending with the fourteenth year of Duke Ai's reign, covering twelve dukes. He took Lu as his base, honored Zhou, and respected Yin, drawing from the Three Dynasties. He condensed its language while making its meaning broad. Therefore, the rulers of Wu and Chu called themselves kings, but the Spring and Autumn Annals belittled them by calling them "zi"; although the meeting at Jiantu was actually convened by them, the Spring and Autumn Annals concealed this by saying "the Son of Heaven hunted in Heyang"—by applying such principles to regulate his contemporaries. The meaning behind censure and restraint will, in the future, be taken up and implemented by a true ruler. When the principles of the Spring and Autumn Annals are put into practice, then treacherous ministers and rebellious sons throughout the world will be filled with fear.

## 74

When Confucius was in office and presiding over lawsuits, if there were words and expressions that could be shared with others, he did not claim them as his own alone. But when it came to compiling the Spring and Autumn Annals—he wrote what he deemed necessary, omitted what he thought unfit—and even Zixia and his disciples could not add a single word. The disciples received the Spring and Autumn Annals; Confucius said, "Posterity will know me because of the Spring and Autumn Annals—and posterity will also hold me accountable because of it."

## 75

The following year, Zilu died in Wei. Confucius fell ill; Zigong requested to visit him. Confucius was leaning on a staff, strolling leisurely at the gate, and said: "Ci, how late you have come!" Confucius sighed and sang: "The great mountain collapses! The beams and pillars break!" "The wise man withers away!" He wept as he said this. He said to Zigong: "For a long time now, the world has been without virtue; no one can follow my teachings. The Xia people placed coffins on the eastern steps, the Zhou people on the western steps, and the Yin people between two pillars. "Last night I dreamed of sitting in a funeral rite between two columns; I must have originally been a descendant of the Yin people." Seven days later, he died.

## 76

Confucius was seventy-three years old and passed away on the Jichou day of the fourth month in the sixteenth year of Duke Ai of Lu's reign.

## 77

Duke Ai eulogized him, saying: "The heavens are unkind; they have not spared even one old man to shield me in my position of power. I am left alone and burdened with sorrow." Alas! How sad it is! "Master Ni, you have no laws to restrain yourself!" Zigong said: "Will the lord not be honored in Lu?" The Master once said, 'When rites are lost, confusion follows; when names are misused, errors arise. Losing one's aspirations leads to confusion, and losing one's purpose leads to mistakes.'" To not employ him when he was alive but eulogize him after his death is against the rites. 邏 Calling oneself "I, one man alone" is not a proper name."

## 78

Confucius was buried on the banks of the Si River north of Lu city; all his disciples mourned for three years. After completing their three-year mourning period, when they bade each other farewell with heartfelt farewells and wept, everyone once again expressed their deepest sorrow; some chose to remain behind. Only Zigong built a hut by the tomb and stayed there for six years, after which he finally left. Disciples and Lu people who went to settle near his tomb numbered more than a hundred households; the place was thus named Kongli. The people of Lu passed down through generations to offer sacrifices at Confucius's tomb according to the seasons, and scholars also conducted rites such as the banquet for the local community and archery ceremonies there. Confucius's tomb was one qing in size. Therefore, within the hall where he once lived and his disciples resided, later generations built a temple to house Confucius's clothing, hats, zither, carriage, and books; this tradition continued for more than two hundred years until the Han dynasty without interruption. Emperor Gaozu passed through Lu and offered a grand sacrifice to him with a "tai lao." Whenever marquises, ministers, or high officials arrived in Lu, they would always visit the temple first before attending to official business.

## 79

Confucius had a son named Li, courtesy name Boyu. Boyu was fifty years old when he died before Confucius.

## 80

Boyu had a son named Ji, courtesy name Zisi, who was sixty-two years old. He once suffered hardship in Song. Zisi wrote the Zhongyong (Doctrine of the Mean).

## 81

Zisi had a son named Bai, courtesy name Zishang, who was forty-seven years old. Zishang had a son named Qiu, courtesy name Zijia, who was forty-five years old. Zijia had a son named Ji, courtesy names Zijing, who was forty-six years old. Zijing had a son named Chuan, courtesy name Zigao, who was fifty-one years old. Zigao had a son named Zhenshen, who was fifty-seven years old and once served as prime minister of the state of Wei.

## 82

Zhenshen had a son named Fu, who was fifty-seven years old; he served as a doctor (scholar-official) for King Shang of Chen and died in the service of Chen.

## 83

Fu had a son named Xiang, who was fifty-seven years old. He once served as a doctor to Emperor Xiaohui and later was transferred to serve as governor of Changsha. He was nine chi six cun tall.

## 84

Xiang had a son named Zhong, who was fifty-seven years old ． Zhong had a son named Wu; Wu had sons named Yannian and Anguo. Anguo served as a doctor for the current emperor, later becoming governor of Linhuai, but died at an early age. Anguo had a son named Yang; Yang had a son named Huan.

## 85

Grand Historian said: The Book of Songs says, "High mountains are to be revered; noble paths are to be followed." Though one may not reach them, still the heart yearns toward them. I have read Kong's writings and can imagine what kind of person he was. When I visited Lu, I observed the temple hall where Confucius once lived, his chariots and ceremonial robes, as well as ritual objects. The scholars practiced rites in their homes according to the seasons; I lingered there with reverence, unable to leave. Throughout the world, there have been many kings and sages—when they were alive, they enjoyed honor; but after their deaths, that was all. Confucius, a commoner, has been passed down for more than ten generations, and scholars have revered him. From the Son of Heaven to marquises and dukes, those in China who speak of the Six Arts all refer back to Master Kong for guidance. He can truly be called the supreme sage!
