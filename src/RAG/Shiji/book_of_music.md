# Book of Music

## 1

Tai Shigong said: Whenever I read the Yu shu, and come to passages about rulers and ministers admonishing each other, maintaining stability in this way, yet when the supporting officials are not virtuous, all affairs fall into ruin, I have never failed to weep. King Cheng composed a hymn, reflecting on his own lessons and warnings, grieving over the family's calamity; could this not be called being constantly vigilant and cautious, skillfully preserving virtue until the end? A junzi, when not bound by constraints, cultivates virtue; when full of success, he does not abandon propriety. When at leisure, he can reflect on the beginning; in peace and security, he remembers the start. He bathes in the nourishing grace and sings praises of diligence and hardship—without great virtue, who could be like this! The Zhuan says: "When governance is stable and achievements are made, rites and music will then flourish." Within the seas, human conduct becomes increasingly profound, their virtue ever more refined, and what they find joy in becomes ever more different. When full but not reduced, it will overflow; when abundant but not held steady, it will topple. All music is created to regulate and moderate joy. A junzi regards humility and withdrawal as propriety, and sees reduction and restraint as joy; he finds happiness in being like this. Because regions differ and states vary, their sentiments and customs are not the same; therefore, they broadly collect folkways and harmonize musical tones to make comparisons, in order to remedy shortcomings, transform people's dispositions, and assist in spreading political instruction and moral education. The Son of Heaven personally appears at the Mingtang to observe, and all the people are cleansed of evil and filth, tempered and filled with virtue, so as to cultivate their nature. Hence it is said that when the tones of Ya and Song are orderly, the people become upright; when the loud and stirring sounds arise, the scholars become inspired; but when the melodies of Zheng and Wei stir, the hearts grow licentious. When their tones are harmonized and in accord, even birds and beasts are moved; how much more so for those who possess the Five Constants, who harbor feelings of like and dislike—this is the natural order!

## 2

When governance falters and lacks integrity, Zheng music arises; feudal lords of successive generations, their names renowned in neighboring states, compete to surpass one another. From Zhongni (Confucius), who could not tolerate Qi's court musicians and thus refused to be accommodated in Lu, even though he withdrew the proper music to guide society and composed five chapters to satirize his time, still no one was transformed. Declining gradually until the Six States, drifting and indulging in excess, proceeding without return, ultimately leading to loss of life and destruction of lineage, with all states falling into Qin.

## 3

Qin Ershi especially regarded it as a source of entertainment. The Chancellor Li Si advised, "Abandoning the Shijing and Shangshu, indulging completely in music and beauty—this is why Zuyi was afraid; lightly accumulating minor faults, giving free rein to the mind through long nights—this is how Zhou Wang perished." Zhao Gao said: "The music of the Five Emperors and Three Kings each had different names, showing that they did not follow one another. From above in the court to below among the people, they could connect through joy and unite with earnestness. Without this harmonious persuasion, understanding would not be achieved, grace would not flow; these were each the transformation of one age, the music suited to its time—why must it necessarily be a swift horse like the Luli from Huashan in order to travel far?" Qin Ershi agreed with this.

## 4

Emperor Gaozu passed through Pei and composed the three chapters of "Sanhou," ordering children to sing them. łoż After Emperor Gaozu's death, the people of Pei were allowed to perform songs and dances in honor of his ancestors at the temple during all four seasons. Emperor Xiaohui, Emperor Xiaowen, and Emperor Xiaojing made no additions or changes; they merely practiced the usual and traditional music at the Yuefu.

## 5

When the current emperor ascended the throne, he composed nineteen chapters and ordered Attendant-in-Attendance Li Yannian to arrange their melodies. He was then appointed as Director of Harmonizing Tones (Xie Lü Duyi). Scholars who had mastered only one of the Classics could not understand its words alone; they all gathered together with experts from the Five Classics, discussed and studied it collectively, and thus were able to fully grasp its meaning. The text is rich in classical and refined expressions.

## 6

The Han dynasty regularly performed sacrifices to Taiyi at Ganquan on the first Xin day of the first lunar month, holding night ceremonies from dusk until dawn. There was often a shooting star passing over the altar of sacrifice. Seventy boys and girls were ordered to sing together. Songs for spring: "Qingyang"; songs for summer: "Zhuming"; songs for autumn: "Xihao"; songs for winter: "Xuanming." There were many such instances, so they are not discussed here.

## 7

They had also once obtained a divine horse from the Wokou Waters and composed another song for Taiyi in sequence. The lyrics of the song said: "Taiyi offers tribute, as the heavenly horse descends; its sweat is red and moist, its foam flows like cinnabar." "Graceful in movement, it gallops ten thousand li; now who can match it, with the dragon as its companion?" Later, after conquering Dayuan and obtaining a horse capable of traveling a thousand li, named Pusao, they composed another song in sequence. The poem said: "The heavenly horse comes from the westernmost reaches; having traveled ten thousand li, it returns to one of virtue. Bearing divine might, it descends upon foreign lands; crossing the shifting sands, all four barbarian regions submit." The Zhongwei Ji An said: "Whenever a Son of Heaven creates music, above it is to honor ancestors and below it is to transform the people. Now that Your Majesty has obtained a horse, composing poetry for song and coordinating it with ancestral temple rites—could the ancestors of former emperors or the common people possibly understand its melody?" The emperor fell silent and was displeased. The Chancellor Gongsun Hong said: "An has slandered the sacred institution; he should be executed along with his clan."

## 8

All music arises from human hearts. The movement of the heart is caused by external things. Stirred by external objects, the heart moves, thus giving rise to sound; Sounds correspond with one another, hence variations arise; Variations take form and are called music; When these sounds are arranged into harmony, and accompanied by ritual implements such as shields, axes, feathers, and banners, it is called "Yue," or music. Music originates from sound; its root lies in the human heart being stirred by external objects. Therefore, when sorrow stirs the heart, the resulting sound is sharp and repressed; when joy stirs the heart, its sound becomes relaxed and gentle; when happiness stirs the heart, the sound is expansive and diffused; when anger stirs the heart, its tone becomes rough and sharp; when reverence stirs the heart, the voice is upright and clear; and when love stirs the heart, its melody becomes harmonious and gentle. These six emotions are not inherent in human nature; they arise only after being stirred by external things. Therefore, the sage kings of old were cautious about what stirred people's hearts. Therefore, rites are used to guide their aspirations; music is employed to harmonize their voices; government ensures consistency in conduct; and punishments serve as a defense against wrongdoing. Rites, music, punishment, and governance all ultimately aim at the same goal: to unify the people's hearts and bring forth good governance.

## 9

All sound originates from the human heart. Emotion stirred within gives rise to sound; when sounds form patterns, they are called music. Therefore, the music of a well-governed age is peaceful and joyful—its tone upright and harmonious; the music of an age in disorder is resentful and angry—its tone deviant and discordant; the music of a fallen state is sorrowful and pensive—its people are oppressed. The principles of sound and melody are connected to uprightness. Gong represents the ruler, Shang the minister, Jue the people, Zhi affairs, and Yu objects. If these five elements are not in disorder, there will be no discordant or chaotic sounds. When Gong is disordered, it becomes wild—this indicates a proud ruler; when Shang is chaotic, it becomes hollow and weak—its ministers are corrupt; when Jue is disordered, sorrow arises—the people are resentful; when Zhi is chaotic, sadness results—affairs become burdensome; and when Yu is disordered, danger follows—the state's wealth is exhausted. When all five are in disorder and each dominates the other, it is called "man," or disrespect. In such a case, there will be no day left for the state to avoid its downfall. The music of Zheng and Wei is the music of an age in chaos; it is close to disrespect. The music from Sangjian and Poshang is the music of a fallen state; when governance collapses, the people drift away, deceiving superiors and acting for private gain without restraint.

## 10

All sound originates from human hearts; Music is connected to moral principles. Therefore, those who know sound but not music are like beasts; those who understand music yet do not grasp its deeper meaning are the common people. Only a junzi can truly know and appreciate music. Therefore, by examining sound one understands music; by understanding music one grasps the essence of harmony; by grasping this harmony one comprehends governance—and thus the principles of good rule are complete. Therefore, those who do not understand sound cannot be discussed about music; those who do not know music cannot be spoken to about the deeper meaning of harmony. To understand this harmony is nearly equivalent to understanding rites. To possess both rites and music fully is called having virtue. Virtue means attainment. Therefore, the height of music is not about reaching the extreme in sound; the grandeur of sacrificial rites is not about ultimate flavor. The se zither in the Qingmiao Temple, with its red strings and loosely spaced bridges, produces a melody where one voice is followed by three sighs—this leaves behind lingering music. The grand sacrificial rites value xuanjiu (symbolic wine) and place raw fish on the zǔ, serving strong broth without blending—this leaves behind a lingering flavor. Therefore, the sage kings established rites and music not to satisfy the desires of the mouth, belly, ears, or eyes, but in order to teach the people to regulate their likes and dislikes and return to the proper way of humanity.

## 11

Human beings are born with stillness—this is Heaven's nature; Stirred by external things, they move—this is the expression of human nature. When objects come and knowledge arises, then likes and dislikes take form. When one's likes and dislikes are without restraint internally, and external influences lead to temptation, if one cannot reflect inwardly, the principles of Heaven will be extinguished. External things can endlessly move people; if human likes and dislikes have no limits, then when external objects arrive, people become transformed by them. When people are transformed by external things, it means they extinguish the principles of Heaven and indulge human desires to their extremes. Thus arise thoughts of rebellion and deceit, as well as acts of licentiousness and disorder. Therefore, the strong intimidate the weak; the many oppress the few; the wise deceive the foolish; the brave torment the timid. The sick and afflicted are not cared for, and the elderly, young, widows, and orphans find no place—this is the path to great disorder. Therefore, sage kings of old established rites and music to regulate human conduct: wearing mourning clothes and weeping—this was to regulate funeral customs; bells, drums, shields, and axes—these were used to harmonize joy and peace; marriage and the capping/jewel ceremonies for women—these served to distinguish between men and women; archery, ancestral sacrifices, and sacrificial feasts—these were used to regulate social interactions. Rites restrain the people's hearts; music harmonizes their voices; government enforces these, and punishments prevent transgressions. When rites, music, punishments, and governance are fully implemented without contradiction, then the way of kingship is complete.

## 12

Music brings people together; rites distinguish them. When united, they become affectionate; when differentiated, they show respect. If music prevails too much, people will become indulgent; if rites prevail too much, people will grow estranged. To harmonize inner feelings and refine outward appearances—this is the function of rites and music. When propriety and righteousness are established, distinctions between noble and humble become clear; when the cultural expressions of music align, harmony is achieved between superiors and inferiors; when likes and dislikes are clear, distinctions between the virtuous and the unworthy become evident; punishments restrain violence, ranks honor virtue—thus governance becomes balanced. With benevolence to love the people and righteousness to correct them, in this way the people will be well governed.

## 13

Music arises from within; rites originate from without. Because music arises from the inner self, it brings tranquility; because rites originate externally, they bring outward form. The grandest music is always simple; the grandest rites are necessarily plain. When music reaches its perfection, resentment disappears; when rites reach their perfection, contention ceases. To govern the world through courtesy and yielding—this is what rites and music mean. When the people are not violent, feudal lords submit in peace, weapons and armor remain unused, five punishments are unnecessary, the common people suffer no hardship, and the Son of Heaven is never angry—thus music reaches its full harmony. Harmonizing the affection between father and son, clarifying the order of seniority among elder and younger, thus showing reverence throughout all under Heaven. When a Son of Heaven acts in this way, rites will be properly observed.

## 14

The grandest music harmonizes with the harmony of Heaven and Earth; the grandest rites align with their rhythms. Harmony, therefore, ensures that all things remain in balance; Rhythm, therefore, leads to sacrifices to Heaven and offerings to Earth. When things are clear, rites and music arise; when they are obscure, spirits and deities appear—thus throughout all under Heaven reverence is united with love. Rites are the means by which different matters unite in reverence; Music is the means by which diverse expressions unite in love. The essence of rites and music is the same; therefore, enlightened kings transmit them together. Thus, affairs proceed with the times, names accompany achievements. Therefore, bells, drums, flutes, stone chimes, feathers, yue, shields, and axes are instruments of music; bowing, stretching, looking up and down, steps, omens, slow or quick movements—these form the expressions of music. food vessels, sacrificial stands, ritual utensils, systems, and ceremonial patterns—these are the instruments of rites; ascending and descending, moving up and down, circling around, wearing or removing outer garments—these form the expressions of rites. Therefore, those who understand the essence of rites and music can create them; those who recognize their forms can master their techniques. To create is to be sage; to master technique is to be enlightened. Enlightened sages are those who combine mastery and creation.

## 15

Music is the harmony of Heaven and Earth; rites are the order of Heaven and Earth. Harmony, therefore, transforms all things; Order, therefore, distinguishes among all beings. Music originates from Heaven; rites are established by Earth. Excessive regulation leads to disorder; excessive creation leads to violence. Only when one understands Heaven and Earth can one properly establish rites and music. Clarifying moral relationships without hardship—this is the essence of music; Joy, delight, and affection—these are the expressions of music. Uprightness without deviation—this is the nature of rites; Dignified, respectful, and obedient—these are the forms of rites. As for how rites and music are applied to metal and stone, transmitted through sound and voice, used in ancestral temples and state altars, and performed before mountains, rivers, spirits, and deities—this is why they share a common bond with the people.

## 16

When a king achieves success, he creates music; when order is established, he establishes rites. The greater the achievement, the more complete the music; the clearer the governance, the better prepared the rites. Dances with shields and axes are not yet complete music; Offering cooked food in sacrifice is not yet full understanding of rites. The Five Emperors lived in different times and did not follow the same music; the Three Kings ruled in different ages and did not inherit the same rites. When music reaches excess, sorrow follows; when rites become crude, they become biased. And who could achieve joy through profound music and complete rites without bias? Could it be only the greatest sage? Heaven is high, Earth is low; all things are dispersed and distinct—thus rites are established. Flowing without ceasing, uniting to transform—this is how music arises. Spring brings birth and summer growth—this is benevolence; Autumn gathers and winter stores—this is righteousness. Benevolence is close to music; righteousness is close to rites. Music brings deep harmony, guiding spirits in accordance with Heaven; Rites distinguish appropriateness, residing among spirits and following Earth. Therefore, sages created music to correspond with Heaven, and established rites to harmonize with Earth. When rites and music are clearly complete, the functions of Heaven and Earth are fulfilled.

## 17

Heaven is honored, Earth is humble—thus the relationship between ruler and minister is established. High and low are already arranged—thus distinctions of noble and humble are set. Movement and stillness follow fixed patterns—thus small and great become distinct. Things gather by kind, beings divide into groups—thus their natures and fates differ. In Heaven they form images; on Earth they take shape—thus rites represent the distinctions of Heaven and Earth. The Earth's qi rises, Heaven's qi descends; yin and yang rub against each other; Heaven and Earth resonate. Thunder and lightning stir them, wind and rain invigorate them, the four seasons move them, sun and moon warm them—thus all things transform and flourish. In this way, music is the harmony of Heaven and Earth.

## 18

If transformation does not follow its proper time, life will not arise; if distinctions between men and women are absent, disorder will increase—this is the principle of Heaven and Earth. When rites and music reach up to Heaven and extend down to Earth, operate through yin and yang, and communicate with spirits and deities; when they explore the highest heights and furthest distances while measuring depth and thickness—music originates from the primordial beginning, and rites reside in completed things. That which is ceaseless is Heaven; that which remains unmoving is Earth. One movement, one stillness—this is the space between Heaven and Earth. Therefore, sages said, "Rites and music."

## 19

In the past, Shun made a five-stringed qin to sing of the southern wind; Kui first created music to reward feudal lords. Therefore, the Son of Heaven creates music in order to reward among the feudal lords those who possess virtue. When virtue is abundant and instruction honored, when five grains mature at their proper times—then music is used to reward them. Therefore, for those who govern the people with diligence, the dancers' steps are long and far-reaching; for those who rule the people in idleness, their dance steps are short. Thus, by observing the dance one knows virtue; by hearing the posthumous title, one understands conduct. Dazhang—this signifies clarity; Xianchi—this means completeness; Shao—this denotes succession; Xia—this signifies grandeur; The music of the Yin and Zhou dynasties was fully developed.

## 20

The way of Heaven and Earth: if cold and heat do not follow their seasons, disease arises; if wind and rain are without measure, famine follows. Education is the people's cold and heat; when teaching does not follow proper timing, it harms society. Affairs are the people's wind and rain; if affairs proceed without restraint, no achievement is made. Therefore, when former kings created music, they did so in accordance with law and order; if it was good, its performance would resemble virtue. Raising pigs for wine is not intended to bring disaster; yet if lawsuits become more frequent, then the flow of wine gives rise to calamity. Therefore, former kings established wine rituals; in the ritual of one offering, guests and hosts exchanged a hundred bows, yet after drinking all day, they could not become drunk. This was how former kings prepared against the calamity of alcohol. Therefore, wine and food are used to unite in joy.

## 21

Music is meant to resemble virtue; Ritual is meant to restrain excess. Therefore, when former kings had great affairs, they necessarily observed rituals to express mourning; when there was great fortune, they necessarily observed rituals for joy: the distinction between sorrow and joy all concluded with ritual.

## 22

Music is about giving; ritual is about reciprocation. Joy—joy in what arises from oneself; and ritual, returns to its original beginning. Music expresses virtue; ritual reciprocates emotion and returns to the origin. What is called Dalu is the chariot of the Son of Heaven; the long qi with nine tassels is the banner of the Son of Heaven; those with blue-black borders are the headdresses of the Son of Heaven's tortoise; followed by herds of oxen and sheep, these were what was given to feudal lords.

## 23

Music is the unchangeable aspect of emotion; Ritual is the immutable principle. Music unifies similarities, ritual distinguishes differences; thus, the principles of music and ritual permeate human relationships. Understanding origins and knowing change is the essence of music; Establishing sincerity and removing falsehood is the principle of ritual. Ritual and music follow the sincerity of Heaven and Earth, express the virtue of spirits and deities, summon the gods above and below, and unify both refined and coarse substances, leading to the proper conduct between fathers and sons, rulers and ministers.

## 24

Therefore, when a great person establishes ritual and music, Heaven and Earth will become luminous. Heaven and Earth harmonize joyfully, yin and yang unite in accord; the nurturing warmth covers and fosters all things. Then grasses and trees flourish, roots sprout, birds spread their wings, horns grow, hibernating creatures emerge from dormancy, feathered beings nestle close, furred ones conceive and give birth, live-bearing animals do not miscarry, and egg-laying ones do not have broken eggs—this is the return of music's way.

## 25

Music does not mean merely the playing of Huangzhong and Dalv, singing with strings or beating drums—it is but a minor aspect of music. Hence, children dance to it; arranging mats and seats, setting out wine vessels and sacrificial offerings, arranging 笾 and dòu, using rising and falling movements as ritual—these are but minor aspects of ritual; therefore, officials oversee them. Music masters who distinguish between tones and poetry face north while playing the stringed instruments; priests and attendants who understand ancestral temple rituals, therefore follow behind the shi (symbolic ancestor); funeral priests who distinguish mourning rites, therefore follow after the family head. Therefore, virtue ascends to the higher level, while skill descends to the lower; conduct takes precedence and comes first, affairs follow and come later. Therefore, former kings recognized the higher and lower, the prior and subsequent; only then could they establish order throughout the world.

## 26

Music is what sages find joy in, and it can cultivate virtue among the people. It deeply moves people; its influence changes customs and transforms society—thus former kings emphasized its teaching.

## 27

Human beings possess a nature of blood, qi, mind, and knowledge, yet they do not have fixed patterns of sorrow, joy, happiness, or anger. These emotions arise in response to external stimuli; only then does the state of one's heart become evident. Therefore, when subtle and sorrowful tones are played, the people feel anxious and worried; when smooth, leisurely, and gently flowing music with elaborate melodies but simple rhythms is performed, the people enjoy peace and happiness; when harsh, abrupt, vigorous, and expansive tones are played, the people become resolute and determined; when upright, just, solemn, and sincere tones are performed, the people become respectful and reverent; when broad, generous, harmonious, and gentle tones are played, the people show kindness and love; when licentious, deviant, chaotic, and corrupting tones are performed, the people fall into lustful disorder.

## 28

Therefore, former kings based their understanding on human nature and emotions, examined them in terms of principles and measurements, established ritual and righteousness to regulate them, harmonized the vital energies, guided the Five Constant Virtues, ensuring that yang was active but not dispersed, yin was restrained but not overly tight, strong qi did not become angry, and gentle qi did not shrink. Thus, these four aspects flowed freely within and manifested outwardly, each occupying its proper place without encroaching upon one another. Only then did they establish schools and grades of learning, expand the rhythms, simplify the ornate elements, in order to cultivate moral depth. They categorized the relative sizes, compared the order of beginning and end, to resemble affairs and conduct, so that relationships between close and distant relatives, noble and humble, elders and juniors, men and women all became evident in music: thus it is said, "Music reveals profound truths."

## 29

When the soil becomes exhausted, grasses and trees do not grow; when water is disturbed, fish and turtles do not thrive; when vital energies decline, living things fail to reproduce; when society falls into disorder, ritual collapses and music becomes licentious. Therefore, the tones are sorrowful yet disrespectful, joyful yet uneasy, careless and disorderly in rhythm, indulgent to the point of forgetting one's roots. When music is overly expansive, it accommodates wickedness. If too narrow and restrictive, desires arise; the corrupting influences stir while the virtue of harmony is extinguished. Therefore, gentlemen despise such music.

## 30

Whenever licentious tones affect people, they provoke discordant energies; when these energies take form, licentious music arises. When proper tones affect people, harmonious energies respond; when such energies take shape, harmonious music arises. When a leader's tone is answered in harmony, the crooked and straight return to their proper places; thus, the principles governing all things act upon each other according to their categories.

## 31

Therefore, gentlemen regulate emotions to harmonize their wills, and by comparing similarities, they cultivate proper conduct. Lascivious tones and corrupt colors do not cloud the mind's clarity; licentious music, which neglects ritual, does not take root in one's inner disposition. Slothful and deviant energies do not settle within the body; thus, ears, eyes, nose, mouth, mind, knowledge, and all limbs follow proper order, enabling righteous conduct to be carried out. Only then, through sound and voice, refined by qin and se zithers, animated by drums and axes, adorned with feathers and banners, accompanied by xiao and guan flutes, can the radiance of virtuous conduct be stirred, the harmony of the four seasons be activated, and thus the principles governing all things be revealed. Therefore, clarity and brightness resemble Heaven; vastness resembles Earth; beginning and end resemble the four seasons; circular movement resembles wind and rain; the five colors form patterns without disorder, the eight winds follow pitch standards without corruption, all measurements conform to numerical principles and remain constant; the small and large complement each other, the beginning and end give rise to one another, clear and murky tones alternate in harmony, taking turns as principles. Therefore, when music is practiced, social order becomes clear; ears and eyes become sharp, blood and qi remain harmonious; customs change for the better, and peace prevails throughout the world. Hence it is said: "Music is joy." Gentlemen find joy in the Way; small men find joy in their desires. When desire is controlled by the Way, one experiences joy without disorder; when the Way is forgotten for desire, one becomes confused and finds no true joy. Therefore, gentlemen regulate their emotions to harmonize their will, expand music to complete moral instruction; when music is practiced, the people turn toward virtue—this reveals virtue indeed.

## 32

Virtue is the beginning of human nature; music is the flourishing expression of virtue; metal, stone, silk, and bamboo are instruments of music. Poetry expresses one's will; song echoes its tone; dance moves the body's form: all three originate from the heart, and only then do musical energies follow. Therefore, deep emotion gives rise to refined culture; abundant energy transforms into divine influence. Harmony and gentleness accumulated within manifest as brilliance outwardly—only music cannot be feigned.

## 33

Music is the movement of the heart; sound is the manifestation of music; ornamentation and rhythm are embellishments of sound. Gentlemen move from the root, take delight in its manifestation, and then regulate its embellishment. Therefore, the drum is first struck to serve as a warning; three steps are taken to show direction; two beginnings mark departure clearly; returning in disorder disciplines one's return. Vigorous yet not reckless, reaching into the profound without concealment. One finds joy in his aspirations and does not tire of the Way; He fully upholds the Way, and does not favor personal desires. Thus, emotion is revealed and righteousness established; music concludes with virtue honored; gentlemen delight in goodness, while small men cease from wrongdoing: hence it is said, "Among the ways of nurturing the people, music holds the greatest place."

## 34

Gentlemen say: Ritual and music cannot for a moment be separated from one's person. Devoting oneself to music to cultivate the mind, then easygoing, upright, filial, and trustworthy dispositions arise naturally. When such a disposition arises, one finds joy; with joy comes peace; with peace comes endurance; with endurance comes harmony with Heaven; and with harmony with Heaven, the divine is attained. Heaven acts without words yet commands belief; the divine exerts no anger yet inspires awe. To devote oneself to music is to cultivate one's mind; to devote oneself to ritual is to regulate one's conduct. Regulating one's conduct brings dignity and reverence; dignity and reverence bring solemn authority. If for a moment the mind is not harmonious or joyful, then petty and deceitful thoughts will enter it. If for a moment one's appearance is not dignified or reverent, then careless and frivolous thoughts will enter it. Therefore, music originates from within; ritual is expressed outwardly. Music achieves the utmost harmony; ritual achieves the utmost order. When inner harmony and outer order are achieved, then the people will look upon one's countenance without contention, and seeing one's bearing, they will not feel casual or disrespectful. Virtue shining from within moves the people, who all listen attentively; propriety expressed outwardly leads the people to follow without resistance. Hence it is said: "He who understands the Way of ritual and music can act in accordance with it, and there will be no difficulty in governing the world."

## 35

Music originates from within; ritual is expressed outwardly. 故樂有《詩》、《書》、《舞》。 Therefore, ritual emphasizes humility, while music emphasizes fullness. Ritual is humble yet advancing, and advancement becomes decorum; music is full yet returns, and return becomes decorum. If ritual is humble but does not advance, it will wither; if music is full yet does not return, it becomes unrestrained. Therefore, ritual involves reciprocity and music involves return. When ritual receives its due response, there is joy; when music achieves its proper return, there is peace. The reciprocity of ritual and the return of music are but one in meaning.

## 36

Music is joy, an emotion from which human nature cannot escape. Joy must be expressed through sound and voice, manifested in movement and stillness; this is the way of humanity. Sound, voice, movement, and stillness—these are transformations of human nature and method, and they are fully expressed in these aspects. Therefore, people cannot be without joy, and joy cannot exist without form. If the form of music does not conform to the Way, disorder is inevitable. The sage kings abhorred disorder, so they established the tones of ya and song to guide it. They ensured that its sounds were sufficient for joy without excess, that its forms were refined enough to be elegant yet not tedious, and that its modulations—whether straight or winding, elaborate or simple, sharp or mellow, rhythmic or measured—were adequate merely to move the good heart of people. Thus they prevented wayward desires and evil influences from taking hold; this was the method by which sage kings established music. Therefore, when music is performed in ancestral temples and rulers and ministers of all ranks listen together, none but feel harmony and reverence; when it is played among clan elders and in villages, with the young and old listening together, then none but feel harmonious and obedient; when it is performed within the household, by father and son or brothers together, then none but feel harmony and affection. Therefore, music examines the fundamental principle to establish harmony, compares things to embellish rhythm, and combines rhythms into a patterned form. Thus it serves to unite father and son, ruler and minister, and to bind closely all the people; this is how sage kings established the Way of music. Therefore, when one listens to the tones of ya and song, one's aspirations are broadened; when one holds the gān and qī, practices bowing, looking up and down, bending and stretching, then one's deportment becomes dignified; when one follows the formations and movements, observes the rhythms and cadences, then ranks are properly aligned and advances and retreats are orderly. Therefore, music is the standard of Heaven and Earth, the principle of harmony, an expression from which human emotion cannot escape.

## 37

Music is how sage kings adorned joy; military campaigns and weapons of war are how sage kings expressed anger. 故其喜，人莫不欲得之；其怒，人莫不畏之。 Therefore, the joys and angers of sage kings all conformed to proper standards. When they were joyful, the whole world harmonized with them; when they were angry, those who committed violence and disorder feared them. The Way of ritual and music practiced by sage kings can truly be called flourishing.

## 38

Wei Wenhòu asked Zi Xia, "When I wear my ceremonial cap and robes to listen to ancient music, I fear that I will fall asleep; but when I listen to the music of Zheng and Wei, I do not know fatigue. I dare ask: Why is ancient music like that?" "Why is new music like this?"

## 39

Zi Xia replied, "Now, as for ancient music: when the performers advance together and retreat together, harmony is maintained in a broad manner. Zithers, gourds, sheng mouth organs, and huang flutes are played in coordination with clappers and drums. The performance begins with civil tones and concludes with martial rhythms; order and disorder are regulated by the xiang dance staffs, while swift movements follow the ya style. Gentlemen speak in this way and thus expound on antiquity; they cultivate their own conduct, manage their families, and bring balance to the world: such is the origin of ancient music. Now, as for new music: when performers bow forward and backward in a hasty manner, the sounds are licentious and corrupting; they indulge without restraint. It includes acrobats, midgets, and mixed performances of men and women, to the point where one no longer recognizes father from son. Such music cannot be discussed in terms of moral instruction or used to expound on antiquity: this is the origin of new music. Now, what your lordship has asked about is music (yue), but what you truly enjoy is sound and melody (yin). Music (yue) and sound/melody (yin) are similar yet distinct."

## 40

Wenhou said, "I dare to ask: how so?"

## 41

Zi Xia replied, "In ancient times, Heaven and Earth were in harmony and the four seasons followed their proper order; the people had virtue and the five grains flourished; diseases did not arise and there were no omens of calamity. This is called great appropriateness (da dang). Only then did sage men establish the relationships of father and son, ruler and subject as the framework for society. Once these frameworks were properly ordered, the world was at great peace; when the world was thus at peace, they regulated the six pitch pipes, harmonized the five tones, played zithers while singing poetry and odes—this is called moral music (de yin), and such moral music is what we call true music (yue). The Shi says: "Let there be no cessation of moral music; virtue shall become clear, and when it is clear, it shall be exemplary. When it is exemplary, one becomes a leader and a ruler." "Ruling this great state, he brings order and harmony to all." "He follows Wenwang; his virtue is without regret." "Having received Heaven's blessings, he extends them to future generations." This is what it means. "Is the music your lordship truly enjoys not this indulgent and corrupting sound?"

## 42

Wenhou said, "I dare to ask: from where does such indulgent music originate?"

## 43

Zi Xia replied, "The music of Zheng is excessive and corrupts the will; the music of Song is soft and seductive, indulging the will; the music of Wei is fast-paced and agitates the mind; the music of Qi is unrestrained and arrogant. All four are excessive in sensuality and harmful to virtue, which is why they are not used in sacrifices. The Shi says: 'Respectful and harmonious sounds resound; thus the ancestors listen.' "Susu" means reverence; "Yongyong," harmony. With reverence and harmony, what task cannot be accomplished? For a ruler, it is merely a matter of being cautious about his likes and dislikes. If the ruler likes something, ministers will follow suit; if those above act in a certain way, the people below will emulate them. The Shi says: "It is very easy to lead the people," this is what it means. Then, sage men created the tao drum, jian wooden clapper, xun ocarina, and chi flute. These six instruments are the sounds of moral music. Then they added bells, stone chimes, yu reed pipes, and se zithers to harmonize with them, and used the gān halberd, qi axe, mao banner, and di standard for dancing. This is why such music was used in ancestral temple sacrifices, for presenting wine and toasting at banquets, and for establishing the proper order of officials, distinguishing between noble and humble so that each receives his due. This is how later generations were shown the sequence of respect, hierarchy, seniority, and youth. The sound of bells is keng; with keng, commands are established; from commands, formations arise; from formations, martial order is achieved. When a gentleman hears the sound of bells, he thinks of his valiant ministers. The sound of stone chimes is keng; with this keng, distinctions are made; from distinctions arises the resolve to die for one's duty. When a gentleman hears the sound of stone chimes, he thinks of ministers who died defending the borders. The sound of silk strings is sorrowful; from sorrowfulness arises integrity; from integrity, firm resolve. When a gentleman hears the sounds of qin and se zithers, he thinks of ministers devoted to righteousness and principle. The sound of bamboo instruments is abundant; from abundance arises coordination; from coordination, the gathering of people. When a gentleman hears the sounds of yu reed pipes and xiao flutes, he thinks of ministers responsible for accumulating resources and gathering the people. The sound of drums is jubilant; from jubilation arises action; from action, the advancement of the masses. When a gentleman hears the sounds of drums and pi war-drums, he thinks of generals and commanders. A gentleman's listening to music is not merely about hearing the clangs and crashes; there are also meanings that resonate within.

## 44

Bin Moujia was sitting in attendance with Confucius, and when Confucius spoke to him about music, he said: "The preparations for Wu have been long-standing; why is that?"

## 45

He replied, "Because the people do not support it."

## 46

"Why is there prolonged sighing and excessive weeping?"

## 47

He replied, "Because he fears failing to accomplish his task."

## 48

"Why is the vigorous and spirited performance so early?"

## 49

He replied, "Because it is timely for action."

## 50

"Why does Wu sit with the right hand in a formal position and the left hand relaxed?"

## 51

He replied, "That is not how Wu sits."

## 52

"Why does the sound extend to the shang tone?"

## 53

He replied, "That is not the music of Wu."

## 54

Confucius said, "If it is not the music of Wu, then what kind of music is it?"

## 55

He replied, "It is due to officials losing their proper transmission. If it were not for the officials' loss of proper transmission, then King Wu's aspirations would have been in vain."

## 56

Confucius said, "Only what I heard from Chang Hong is also like your words."

## 57

Bin Moujia rose, removed his mat, and respectfully asked, "The preparations for Wu have been long in the making; I have already heard your instructions. I dare to ask why it is delayed so long?"

## 58

Confucius said, "Sit down; I will tell you. Music is the representation of completion. Holding shields and standing as mountains, this was King Wu's endeavor; Vigorous and spirited performance reflects Tai Gong's aspirations; When Wu's chaos subsided, all sat in order—this was the governance of Zhou and Zhao. Moreover, regarding Wu: in the first section it moves northward; in the second section, Shang is destroyed; in the third section, it turns south; in the fourth section, the southern regions are established as territories; in the fifth section, the land is divided at Shaan, with Duke of Zhou on the left and Zhao Gong on the right; in the sixth section, the music resumes to honor the Son of Heaven. The movements vibrate outward four times, displaying great (vibrant) authority within the central lands. Divided and advancing together, this signifies that affairs are completed early. Remaining in place for a long time after the music resumes is to await the arrival of feudal lords. Have you not heard the words spoken at Muye? After King Wu defeated Yin and returned to Shang, before he had even dismounted his chariot, he enfeoffed the descendants of Huangdi at Ji, those of Emperor Yao at Zhu, and those of Emperor Shun at Chen; After dismounting, he enfeoffed descendants of the Xia Houshi at Qi, those of Yin at Song, honored the tomb of Prince Bi Gan, released Ji Zi from imprisonment, and allowed him to follow Shang Rong in restoring his position. Common people were relieved of burdensome policies, and common officials received double their salaries. After crossing the Yellow River to the west, he dispersed his horses on the southern side of Mount Hua and never rode them again; he released his oxen into the plains of Taolin and never used them for labor again; he stored his chariots, armor, and weapons in government treasuries and never used them again; he reversed the weapons on his chariot, wrapping them in tiger skins; he appointed military officers as feudal lords and named this practice "Jian Gao"—only then did the world know that King Wu would never again use force. He dispersed his troops and held archery ceremonies in the suburbs, shooting to the left at Lishou and to the right at Zoushi, thus ending the practice of penetrating leather targets; He wore ceremonial robes and held a tablet in his belt, while the elite warriors returned their swords to the sheathes; Offering sacrifices at the Mingtang temple made the people aware of filial piety; After court audiences, feudal lords understood how to be ministers; The ritual plowing made them understand reverence: these five practices were the great teachings of the world. Feasting the Three Elders and Five Elders at the Taixue, the Son of Heaven personally slaughtered sacrificial animals with his upper garment open, carried sauce to offer food, held a cup for the libation, and wore ceremonial headdress while holding shields—this was how he taught feudal lords fraternal respect. If this were the case, then the Zhou way would reach in all directions, and rites and music would circulate freely. Therefore, that Wu's performance was delayed for so long, is it not appropriate?"

## 59

Zi Gong met with Shiyi and asked him, "I have heard that singing and music each has its appropriate form. For someone like me, what kind of song should I sing?"

## 60

Shiyi said, "I am a humble artisan; how could I possibly know the appropriate songs to recommend. Please allow me to recite what I have heard, and you may choose for yourself." Those who are broad-minded and tranquil, gentle yet upright should sing the Odes; Those who are expansive and calm, open-minded and trustworthy should sing the Da Ya; Those who are respectful, frugal, and fond of rites should sing the Xiao Ya; Those who are upright, clean, and humble should sing the Feng; Those who are forthright and compassionate should sing Shang; Those who are warm-hearted, kind, yet decisive should sing Qi. Singing is a way to express one's integrity and display virtue; When one moves with sincerity, heaven and earth respond, the four seasons harmonize, stars align in order, and all things flourish. Therefore, Shang music is the lingering sound of the Five Emperors; the people of Shang cherished it, hence it was called "Shang"; Qi music is the lingering sound from the Three Dynasties; the people of Qi cherished it, hence it was named "Qi." Those who understand the poetry of Shang can make repeated decisions when facing affairs; Those who understand the poetry of Qi will yield in the face of profit. Making repeated decisions when facing affairs is courage; Yielding in the face of profit is righteousness. With courage and righteousness, who else but through song could preserve these virtues? Therefore, in singing, the voice rises as if ascending, falls as if descending, turns like a sharp angle, stops as still as withered wood, stays centered within the square, and each phrase follows the curve of a hook—layer upon layer, it flows smoothly like pearls strung together. Therefore, "song" means to prolong one's words. Because one is pleased by it, he speaks of it; If speaking is insufficient, therefore one prolongs the words; If prolonging the words is still insufficient, then one sighs and laments; If sighing and lamenting are still insufficient, therefore one cannot help but raise hands to dance and stomp feet in rhythm." Zi Gong asked about music.

## 61

All sounds originate from the human heart; heaven and humanity are connected in this way, just as a shadow mirrors an object's form, or an echo responds to sound. Therefore, those who do good are blessed by heaven with fortune, while those who commit evil are visited by calamity from heaven; this is the natural order.

## 62

Thus, when Shun played the five-stringed qin and sang the poem of Nanfeng, the world was governed in harmony; Zhao composed the music of Chaoge Beibi, and perished with his life and state. How great was Shun's way! How narrow was Zhao's path! The poem of Nanfeng is a sound of growth and vitality. Shun's music cherished it; his joy aligned with the harmony of heaven and earth, winning the heartfelt approval of all nations, hence the world was at peace. The music of Chaoge was inappropriate; "north" signifies defeat, and "bi" means narrow-mindedness. Zhao loved such music, which diverged from the hearts of all nations. Feudal lords did not submit to him, common people did not support him, and the world rebelled against him—thus he lost his life and state.

## 63

During Duke Ling of Wei's time, when he was traveling to Jin, they stopped for the night by the Pusu River. At midnight, he heard the sound of a zither being played and asked his attendants; they all replied that they had not heard anything. He then summoned Shijuan and said, "I have heard the sound of zither music; I asked my attendants, but none of them heard it. Its form seems supernatural—listen to it and transcribe it for me." Shijuan said, "Yes, my lord." He then sat upright, took up the zither, listened carefully, and transcribed it. The next day he said, "I have obtained it now, but I am not yet familiar with it; please allow me to stay and practice it." Duke Ling said, "Go ahead." So he stayed overnight again. The next day, he reported, "I have mastered it." They then departed for Jin and presented themselves to Duke Ping of Jin. Duke Ping held a banquet on the Shuihui Terrace. When the wine was flowing freely, Duke Ling said, "On our way here, we heard a new melody; please allow me to perform it." Duke Ping said, "Go ahead." He then ordered Shijuan to sit beside Shikuang and play the zither. Before it was finished, Shikuang stopped him by touching his shoulder and said, "This is the sound of a fallen kingdom; you must not continue." Duke Ping asked, "From where does this melody originate?" Shikuang said, "It was composed by Shiyuan. He composed it together with Zhao for the decadent music. When King Wu attacked Zhao, Shiyuan fled eastward and threw himself into the Pusu River; therefore, this melody can only be heard by the banks of Pusu. Whoever first hears this sound will see their state weakened." Duke Ping said, "What I love is music; I wish to hear it in full." Shijuan played the entire piece.

## 64

Duke Ping said, "Is there no sound more sorrowful than this?" old Shikuang replied, "Yes." Duke Ping asked, "May I hear it?" Shikuang said, "Your virtue and righteousness are shallow; you cannot bear to listen to it." Duke Ping said, "What I love is music. I wish to hear it." Shikuang had no choice but to take up the zither and play it. After one performance, thirty-two black cranes gathered at the hall's entrance; after a second performance, they stretched their necks and cried out, spread their wings and danced.

## 65

Duke Ping was greatly delighted and rose to offer a toast to Shikuang's longevity. Returning to his seat, he asked, "Is there no sound more sorrowful than these?" Shikuang said, "Yes." "Long ago, the Yellow Emperor used it to greatly harmonize with spirits and deities. Now your virtue and righteousness are shallow, insufficient for you to listen to it; if you do hear it, calamity will follow." Duke Ping said, "I am old now, and what I love is music. Please let me hear it in full." Shikuang had no choice but once again to take up the zither and played it. After one performance, white clouds arose from the northwest; after a second performance, a strong wind arrived followed by rain, tiles flew off the eaves of the hall, and those around him all ran about in alarm. Duke Ping was terrified and crouched down between the corridor and the building. The state of Jin suffered a great drought, with red, barren land for three years.

## 66

Those who listened experienced either good fortune or misfortune. Music must not be arbitrarily performed.

## 67

Tai Shigong said: The enlightened kings of the ancient past who performed music did so not for their own pleasure or to indulge their desires, but in order to achieve good governance. Those who correct moral instruction all begin with sound; when the sound is upright, conduct becomes upright as well. Therefore, music and sound are used to stir the blood vessels, circulate the spirit, and harmonize the mind with righteousness. Hence, the Gong mode affects the spleen and harmonizes with righteousness and sainthood; the Shang mode affects the lungs and harmonizes with justice; the Jue mode affects the liver and harmonizes with benevolence; the Zhi mode affects the heart and harmonizes with propriety; the Yu mode affects the kidneys and harmonizes with wisdom. Therefore, music is used internally to assist in cultivating a righteous mind and externally to distinguish between noble and humble ranks; it is employed above to serve ancestral temples, and below to transform the common people. The qin is eight chi and one cun long, which represents the correct standard of measurement. The thickest string is Gong, placed in the center, representing the ruler. Shang is stretched to the right side; the rest of the strings follow in order by thickness, not deviating from their sequence—thus the positions of ruler and minister are properly established. Therefore, hearing the Gong tone makes people warm, relaxed, and expansive; hearing the Shang tone makes people upright and fond of righteousness; hearing the Jue tone makes people compassionate and loving toward others; hearing the Zhi tone makes people joyful in virtue and fond of giving; hearing the Yu tone makes people orderly and fond of propriety. Ritual comes from outside, while music arises from within. Therefore, a gentleman cannot for an instant be separated from ritual; if he is even momentarily apart from it, his rude and arrogant behavior will manifest outwardly; he cannot for an instant be without music; if he is even briefly apart from it, his deceitful and wicked conduct will arise within. Therefore, musical tones are what cultivate righteousness in a gentleman. In ancient times, emperors and feudal lords never left the court without listening to bells and stone chimes; high-ranking officials and gentlemen never went anywhere without hearing the sounds of qin and se—this was done in order to cultivate moral conduct and prevent licentiousness. Licentiousness arises from the absence of ritual; therefore, sage kings ensured that people heard refined and solemn music with their ears, saw dignified rituals with their eyes, practiced respectful behavior with their feet, and spoke words of benevolence and righteousness. Therefore, a gentleman can speak all day without any opportunity for evil or deviant thoughts to enter.
