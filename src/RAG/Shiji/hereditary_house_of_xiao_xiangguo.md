# Hereditary House of Xiao Xiangguo

## 1

Xiao Xiangguo He was a native of Pei Feng. Wu Hai was appointed as the chief clerk (zhu li yuan) of Pei by Xiao Xiangguo He.

## 2

When Gaozu was still a commoner, He often protected him in official matters. When Gaozu served as a postmaster (ting zhang), he often had He at his side. When Gaozu was assigned to serve in Xianyang, the officials all presented him with three coins as a gift; only He gave five.

## 3

The Qin dynasty's imperial censors who supervised the commanderies and their attendants were often scrutinized by him. He was then assigned to handle matters as a military officer (zushi) of Sishui, and he ranked first in his performance. The Qin dynasty's imperial censor wanted to recommend He for imperial service, but He firmly requested not to go, and thus was excused from doing so.

## 4

When Gaozu rose as the Lord of Pei, He often served as an assistant magistrate (cheng) overseeing affairs. When Lord Pei arrived at Xianyang, all the generals rushed to seize gold, silk, and other valuables from the treasury; only He entered first to collect the Qin dynasty's chancellor and imperial censor laws, regulations, maps, and records and stored them away. When Lord Pei became King of Han, he appointed He as his prime minister (chengxiang). Xiang Wang and the feudal lords sacked and burned Xianyang before leaving. This is why King Han was able to fully understand the strategic passes and obstacles of the empire, the number of households, the strengths and weaknesses in different regions, and the hardships endured by the people: because He had obtained all the Qin records and maps. He recommended Han Xin to King Han, who then appointed Han Xin as his supreme general (dajiangjun). This is recorded in the account of the Marquis of Huaiyin.

## 5

King Han led his troops eastward to pacify the Three Qins, while He remained behind as prime minister to collect supplies from Ba and Shu, pacifying and reassuring the people, ensuring that military provisions were available. In the second year of Han (205 BCE), King Han and the feudal lords attacked Chu, while He defended Guanzhong, served as regent for the crown prince, and administered Linyang. He established laws and regulations, built ancestral temples, altars of land and grain, palaces, and county towns; each time he submitted proposals for approval, and upon receiving permission, proceeded with the tasks. If there was no time to submit a report first, he acted according to what seemed most expedient and then informed King Han afterward. In Guanzhong, He managed the logistics of population records and grain transport to supply the army. On several occasions when King Han lost his troops and fled, He consistently raised new soldiers from Guanzhong to replenish the losses. Because of this, the emperor entrusted all affairs in Guanzhong exclusively to him.

## 6

In the third year of Han (204 BCE), when King Han and Xiang Yu confronted each other between Jing and Suo, the emperor frequently sent envoys to personally attend to and comfort the prime minister. Bao Sheng said to the prime minister, "The king is exposed to harsh weather with his canopy open. He frequently sends envoys to attend to you personally because he has suspicions about you." "For your sake, there is no better plan than to send all of your capable male descendants and nephews who can bear arms directly to the army's location; this will surely make the king more confident in you." He followed his advice, and King Han was greatly pleased.

## 7

In the fifth year of Han (202 BCE), after Xiang Yu had been killed and the empire pacified, rewards were conferred according to military contributions. The ministers vied for credit, and it took over a year before their merits could be decided. Gaozu considered Xiao He's contributions the greatest and enfeoffed him as Marquis of Zan, granting him a large fief. The meritorious ministers all said, "We personally donned armor and wielded sharp weapons; some of us fought in over a hundred battles, others in dozens. We captured cities and seized territory, with varying degrees of achievement in major and minor campaigns." "Now Xiao He has never had the toil of a single horse or drop of sweat; he merely holds discussions with pen and ink, without ever engaging in battle. Yet his rank is placed above ours—why is this?" Emperor Gao asked, "Do you all know about hunting?" They replied, "We do." "Do you know the hunting dogs?" They replied, "We do." Emperor Gao said, "In hunting, it is the dogs that chase and kill the game or hares; but it is people who track down their trails and indicate where the prey is." "Now you are only capable of capturing running beasts—you are like hunting dogs in terms of merit." "As for Xiao He, he is the one who tracks down trails and indicates where to strike—he is a man of strategic contribution." "Moreover, each of you followed me with only two or three men at most. "But Xiao He brought dozens from his entire clan to follow me—his contribution cannot be forgotten." The ministers all fell silent and dared not speak further.

## 8

After the marquises had all received their enfeoffments, and when it came to determining their ranks, they said, "Marquis of Pingyang Cao Can personally bore seventy wounds; he captured cities and seized territory with the most contributions—he should be ranked first." The emperor had already adjusted the rankings of the meritorious ministers and awarded Xiao He many additional honors, but when it came to their official positions there was no way to dispute this further; yet in his heart he wanted Xiao He to be ranked first. Guannei Hou E Jun said, "All the ministers' discussions are mistaken." Although Cao Can has achieved merits in open-field battles and territorial conquests, these were merely accomplishments of a single era. The emperor faced Chu for five years, often losing his army and retreating alone on many occasions. Yet Xiao He consistently sent reinforcements from Guanzhong to fill the gaps, even without imperial orders—on multiple occasions, he mustered tens of thousands of troops to relieve the emperor's dire shortages. During the years when Han and Chu confronted each other at Xingyang, the army had no visible supplies, yet Xiao He transported provisions from Guanzhong without fail, ensuring that food was never lacking. Although the emperor often fled to Shandong, Xiao He always preserved Guanzhong intact for His Majesty—this is a contribution that will last through the ages. Now even if hundreds of Cao Cans were to be lost, what would Han lack? Han does not necessarily need them all in full. How can one possibly place the merit of a single day above that which lasts through the ages! "Xiao He should be first, and Cao Can second." Emperor Gao said, "Well spoken." Thus he ordered Xiao He to be ranked [first], granting him the privilege of wearing a sword and slippers in court, and allowing him to enter the palace without hurrying.

## 9

The emperor said, "I have heard that recommending virtuous men is rewarded most highly. "Xiao He's contributions were indeed great, but it was only through E Jun's recommendation that his merits became even clearer." Thus, he elevated E Jun to the title of Marquis of Anping based on the fief previously granted as Guannei Hou. On that day, over a dozen of Xiao He's sons and brothers were all enfeoffed with fiefs. He was further granted an additional 2,000 households for the reward, because when the emperor had once served in Xianyang and officials gave him three coins as a gift, He alone had given five.

## 10

In the eleventh year of Han (196 BCE), Chen Xi rebelled; Emperor Gao personally led his army and advanced to Handan. Before the campaign was over, the Marquis of Huaiyīn plotted a rebellion in Guanzhong. Empress Dowager Lü used Xiao He's plan to execute the Marquis of Huai Yin; this is recorded in the account concerning Huai Yin. After learning of the execution of the Marquis of Huaiyin, the emperor sent envoys to bestow upon Chancellor He the title of Xiangguo (a higher rank than chancellor), added 5,000 more households to his fief, and ordered 500 soldiers with one Duwei officer as personal guards for the Xiangguo. All the ministers came to congratulate him, but only Zhao Ping expressed condolences. Zhao Ping was a former marquis of Dongling from the Qin dynasty. After the fall of Qin, he became a commoner and was poor; he grew melons east of Chang'an city. His melons were so delicious that people in later generations called them "Dongling melons," naming them after Zhao Ping. Zhao Ping said to the Xiangguo, "Misfortune begins from here." "The emperor is exposed to hardships outside, while you remain in the capital. You have not personally faced arrows and stones on the battlefield yet still received additional fiefs and guards—this is because the Marquis of Huaiyín recently rebelled within the empire, and now they suspect your loyalty." Granting you bodyguards to protect you is not an act of favor—it is a sign of suspicion. I hope you will refuse the fief and not accept it, and instead contribute all your private wealth to support the army—then the emperor's mind will be at ease." The Xiangguo followed his advice, and Emperor Gao was greatly pleased.

## 11

In the autumn of the twelfth year of Han (195 BCE), Jing Bu rebelled, and Emperor Gao personally led an army to attack him. He frequently sent envoys asking what the Xiangguo was doing. While the emperor was in the field, the Xiangguo comforted and encouraged the common people, mobilizing all available resources to support the army, just as he had done during Chen Xi's rebellion. A guest advised the Xiangguo saying, "Your family will not be long for this world." "Your position is that of Xiangguo and your contributions are the greatest—can there possibly be any more rewards to give?" "Yet from your initial entry into Guanzhong, you have won the people's hearts for over a decade; they all support you and continue to work hard to maintain their favor." "The reason the emperor repeatedly inquires about your activities is because he fears that you might stir up unrest in Guanzhong." "Now, why don't you buy more land and make cheap loans to yourself in order to sully your reputation?" "Then the emperor's mind would be at ease." The Xiangguo then followed his advice, and the emperor was greatly pleased.

## 12

After Emperor Gao returned from defeating Jing Bu's forces, people along the road blocked his way and submitted petitions stating that the Xiangguo had forcibly bought thousands of acres of land and homes at low prices. When the emperor arrived, the Xiangguo paid his respects. The emperor laughed and said, "Indeed, this prime minister is greedy for the people!" He gave all the petitions submitted by the people to the Xiangguo and said, "You should personally apologize to them." The Xiangguo therefore petitioned on behalf of the people, saying, "Chang'an has limited land. There are many vacant areas in Shanglin that are being wasted; I request permission for the people to cultivate them, and not collect the straw as food for animals." The emperor was furious and said, "This prime minister has accepted many gifts from merchants and now dares to petition for my hunting grounds!" He then ordered the Xiangguo to be taken before the imperial judge, shackled and imprisoned. After several days, Wang Weiwei served as attendant and asked in front of the emperor, "What great crime has the Xiangguo committed that Your Majesty is treating him so harshly?" The emperor said, "I have heard that Li Si served as prime minister under Emperor Qin Shi Huang. When there was good to be credited, he gave it to the ruler; when there were faults, he took them upon himself." "Now this Xiangguo has accepted much money from merchants and petitioned for my hunting grounds on behalf of the people in order to ingratiate himself with them, so I have had him arrested." Wang Weiwei said, "If an official's duty involves doing something beneficial for the people and petitioning on their behalf, this is indeed a prime minister's responsibility. How can Your Majesty suspect that the Xiangguo accepted money from merchants?" "Moreover, during the years when Your Majesty confronted Chu, and Chen Xi and Jing Bu rebelled, you personally led your army to fight. At that time, the Xiangguo defended Guanzhong; had he wavered even slightly, all of Guanzhong west of the pass would no longer have been yours." "Could the Xiangguo not have taken advantage during that time for his own benefit, and yet now he would be tempted by merchants' money?" "Moreover, the Qin dynasty lost its empire precisely because it did not hear of its faults. Li Si's practice of taking blame upon himself—what is there to emulate in that?" "How shallow Your Majesty's suspicion of a prime minister is." Emperor Gao was not pleased. On that day, he sent an envoy with a token of authority to release the Xiangguo from prison. The Xiangguo was old and had always been respectful and cautious; upon entering, he knelt barefoot in apology. Emperor Gao said, "Prime Minister, you may rest now!" "The Xiangguo petitioned on behalf of the people for my hunting grounds, and I refused. If I were a tyrant like Jie or Zhou, then he would have been a virtuous prime minister." "I deliberately imprisoned the Xiangguo in order for the people to hear of my faults."

## 13

He Su had never been on good terms with Cao Can. When He fell ill, Emperor Xiaohui personally visited the Xiangguo to see his condition and asked, "After you pass away, who could succeed you?" He replied, "No one knows a subject better than the ruler." Emperor Xiaohui asked, "What about Cao Can?" He kowtowed and said, "The emperor has chosen well!" "I shall have no regrets even if I die now!"

## 14

Whenever He acquired farmland or a residence, he always chose the most remote locations; when establishing his household, he did not build walls or houses. He said, "If future generations are virtuous, they will take my frugality as a model; if they are not virtuous, let them not become targets for powerful families to seize."

## 15

In the second year of Emperor Xiaohui's reign, the Xiangguo He died and was posthumously honored as Wen Zhong Hou.

## 16

Four generations of his descendants lost their marquisates due to crimes, and the title was extinguished; however, whenever an emperor came to power, he would seek out He's descendants again, bestowing them with the title of Zan Hou. No other meritorious subject could match this honor.

## 17

Grand Historian said: Xiao Xiangguo He served as a clerk in the Qin dynasty, unremarkable and without any distinguished conduct. With the rise of Han, relying on the waning light of the old order, He carefully safeguarded the seals and keys. Because the people resented Qin laws, he followed public sentiment to initiate a new beginning in harmony with the times. Huaiyin, Qing Bu and others were all eliminated by execution, yet He's meritorious deeds remain illustrious. His position was the highest among ministers, and his reputation spread to later generations; he vied in glory with Hong Yao, Sanyi Sheng, and others.
