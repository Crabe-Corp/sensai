# Annals of Yin

## 1

Yin Qi, whose mother was Jian Di, a daughter of the You Song clan, was the second concubine of Emperor Ku. The three women went to take a bath and saw a xuan bird drop an egg; Jian Di took it and swallowed it, becoming pregnant and giving birth to Qi. When Qi grew up, he assisted Yu in controlling the floods and achieved success. Emperor Shun then gave orders to Qi, saying: "The people do not show affection for one another, and the five categories of society are not properly instructed. You shall serve as a Sikou (Minister of Rites) and diligently promote the Five Teachings; the Five Teachings lie in kindness." He was enfeoffed at Shang and granted the surname Zi Shi. Qi rose to prominence during the time of Tang, Yu, and Da Yu; his achievements brought great benefit to the people, who thus lived in peace.

## 2

After Qi's death, his son Zhao Ming succeeded him. After Zhao Ming died, his son Xiang Tu succeeded him. After Xiang Tu died, his son Chang Ruo succeeded him. After Chang Ruo died, his son Cao Yu succeeded him. After Cao Yu died, his son Ming succeeded him. After Ming died, his son Zhen succeeded him. After Zhen died, his son Wei succeeded him. After Wei died, his son Bao Ding succeeded him. After Bao Ding died, his son Bao Yi succeeded him. After Bao Yi died, his son Bao Bing succeeded him. After Bao Bing died, his son Zhu Ren succeeded him. After Zhu Ren died, his son Zhu Gui succeeded him. After Zhu Gui died, his son Tian Yi succeeded him; he is known as Cheng Tang.

## 3

Cheng Tang: from Qi to Tang, there were eight relocations of the capital. Tang first settled in Bo, following the residence of previous kings, and composed the "Di Gao."

## 4

Tang campaigned against the feudal lords. Ge Bo did not perform sacrifices, so Tang began to attack him. Tang said: "I have a saying: when people look at water they see their reflection; by observing the people one knows whether governance is good or not." Yi Yin said, "How wise!" If words can be heeded, the Way will then advance. loff The ruler and his subjects must serve the people; those who do good are all found in the king's officials. Strive, strive!" Tang said: "If you cannot respect the mandate, I will impose a great punishment and execute you, with no possibility of pardon." The Records of Tang's Campaign were composed.

## 5

Yi Yin, whose personal name was E Heng. E Heng wanted to advise Tang but had no opportunity, so he became a retainer of the You Shen clan sent as an attendant, carrying a cauldron and serving utensils, using the art of flavor to persuade Tang, leading him toward the royal way. Some say that Yi Yin was a recluse, and Tang sent envoys to invite him; after five rounds of invitations he finally agreed to go with Tang, speaking about the matters of the "Plain King" and the Nine Rulers. Tang appointed him to oversee state affairs. Yi Yin left Tang and went to Xia. After finding the state of Xia corrupt, he returned again to Bo. He entered through the northern gate and met Nü Jiu and Nü Fang; the "Nü Jiu Nü Fang" was composed.

## 6

Tang went out and saw a hunter setting up a net in all four directions, who made an incantation: "From above and below, from all four quarters, may they all enter my net." Tang said: "Ah! That is too much!" He then removed the net from three sides and made an incantation: "Those who wish to go left, go left." "Those who wish to go right, go right. "If you do not heed the mandate, then enter my net." The feudal lords heard of this and said: "Tang's virtue is profound indeed, reaching even to birds and beasts."

## 7

At that time, Xia Jie was enforcing tyrannical policies and indulging in debauchery, while the feudal lord Kunwu Shi was causing chaos. Tang then raised an army and led the feudal lords, with Yi Yin following him; Tang personally wielded a halberd to attack Kunwu, and thus proceeded to attack Jie. Tang said: "Listen, all of you common people! Come here, and you shall all hear my words." "I, the humble one, dare not act in rebellion. The Xia dynasty has committed many crimes; I have only heard your people's complaints and know that the Xia clan is guilty." I revere Heaven above all, and dare not fail to act justly. Now the Xia has committed many crimes, and Heaven's mandate decrees its punishment. "Now you have your people, yet you say: 'My ruler does not care for my people; he abandons our agricultural duties and imposes harsh policies.'" "You may ask: 'If we are guilty, what shall we do?'" "The Xia king led the people to resist and seize the state of Xia." The people, following a leader who was lazy and uncooperative, said: "On what day will this time be lost?" "You and I shall both perish!" "If the Xia's virtue is like this, then today I must go forth." "You should still assist me in carrying out Heaven's punishment; I shall surely deal justly with you." "Do not doubt my words, for I do not speak falsely." "If you do not follow the oath, I shall punish and execute you without any pardon." He announced this to his troops; thus was composed "The Oath of Tang." At this time, Tang said: "I am very martial," and took the title King Wu.

## 8

Jie was defeated at You Song's ruins, fled to Ming Tiao, and the Xia army suffered a crushing defeat. Tang then attacked San Zong, captured their precious treasures, and Yi Bo and Zhong Bo compiled the records of these treasures. After Tang had defeated Xia, he wished to replace its ancestral shrine, but could not; thus was composed "The Xia Ancestral Shrine." Yi Yin made a report. At this time, all the feudal lords submitted; Tang then ascended to the throne of Son of Heaven and pacified the realm.

## 9

Tang returned to Tai Juan Tao, where Zhong Lei composed a "Gao" (edict). After ending the Xia dynasty's mandate, he returned to Bo and composed the "Gao of Tang": "In the third month, the king personally arrived at the eastern suburbs." He addressed the feudal lords and their rulers: "None of you should fail to serve the people with achievements; diligently apply yourselves to your duties. "If I must impose a great punishment upon you, do not hold me in resentment." He continued: "In the past, Yu and Gao Yao toiled for a long time outside; they achieved great service for the people, so the people found peace. "To the east was the Yangtze River, to the north the Ji River, to the west the Yellow River, and to the south the Huai River; when these four great waterways were controlled, all the people had a place to dwell. "Hou Ji descended to sow seeds, cultivating a hundred grains for agriculture." "The Three Dukes all achieved great service for the people; therefore, they were honored with posthumous titles. "In ancient times, Chi You and his ministers caused chaos among the people, so Heaven did not grant them approval; they were recorded as criminals. "Therefore, one cannot be remiss in following the words of former kings." He said: "Those who do not follow the Way must not hold positions within the state; you must not resent me for this." With these words, he issued orders to the feudal lords. Yi Yin composed "Xian You Yi De," and Jiu Dan composed "Ming Ju."

## 10

Tang then changed the calendar, altered the color of ceremonial robes to white as the supreme symbol, and held court meetings during the day.

## 11

After King Tang died, Crown Prince Tai Ding had not yet ascended the throne when he also passed away; thus they enthroned Tai Ding's younger brother Waibing, who became Emperor Waibing. Emperor Waibing reigned for three years before dying; his younger brother Zhong Ren was then enthroned, becoming Emperor Zhong Ren. Emperor Zhong Ren reigned for four years before dying; Yi Yin then placed Tai Ding's son Tai Jia on the throne. Tai Jia was Cheng Tang's legitimate grandson, and became Emperor Tai Jia. In the first year of Emperor Tai Jia's reign, Yi Yin composed "Yi Xun," "Si Ming," and "Cu Hou."

## 12

Three years after Emperor Tai Jia ascended the throne, he became corrupt and tyrannical, failing to follow Tang's laws and disrupting virtue; thus Yi Yin exiled him to Tong Gong. For three years, Yi Yin assumed the role of regent, governing the state and receiving feudal lords in court.

## 13

After three years at Tong Gong, Emperor Tai Jia repented for his past misdeeds and took responsibility for himself, returning to virtue; thus Yi Yin welcomed Emperor Tai Jia back and restored him to power. Emperor Tai Jia cultivated virtue, and all the feudal lords returned to the Yin dynasty; the people thus lived in peace. Yi Yin praised him, composing three chapters of "Tai Jia Xun," extolling Emperor Tai Jia and bestowing on him the posthumous title Taizong.

## 14

After Taizong died, his son Wo Ding succeeded him. During the reign of Emperor Wo Ding, Yi Yin passed away. After Yi Yin was buried in Bo, Jiu Dan then instructed on the deeds of Yi Yin and composed "Wo Ding."

## 15

After Wo Ding died, his younger brother Tai Geng succeeded him, becoming Emperor Tai Geng. After Emperor Tai Geng died, his son Emperor Xiao Jia succeeded him. After Emperor Xiao Jia died, his younger brother Yong Ji succeeded him, becoming Emperor Yong Ji. The Yin dynasty declined, and some feudal lords no longer came to court.

## 16

After Emperor Yong Ji died, his younger brother Tai Wu succeeded him, becoming Emperor Tai Wu. Emperor Tai Wu appointed Yi Zhi as prime minister. In Bo, an auspicious mulberry and millet plant grew together in the court; by morning they had grown large enough to form a canopy. Emperor Tai Wu was frightened and asked Yi Zhi about it. Yi Zhi said, "I have heard that omens of evil cannot overcome virtue; perhaps there is a deficiency in Your Majesty's governance? The emperor should cultivate virtue." Tai Wu followed his advice, and the auspicious mulberry withered and died. Yi Zhi made recommendations to Wu Xian. Wu Xian successfully managed the royal affairs and composed "Xian Ai" and "Tai Wu." Emperor Tai Wu praised Yi Zhi in the ancestral temple, saying he was not merely a subject; Yi Zhi declined the honor, and thus "Yuan Ming" was composed. The Yin dynasty revived, and feudal lords returned to its allegiance, hence he was posthumously honored as Zhong Zong.

## 17

After Emperor Zhong Zong died, his son Emperor Zhong Ding succeeded him. Emperor Zhong Ding moved the capital to Ao. He Dan Jia resided in Xiang. Zu Yi moved the capital to Xing. After Emperor Zhong Ding died, his younger brother Wai Ren succeeded him, becoming Emperor Wai Ren. The records of Zhong Ding are incomplete and not fully available. After Emperor Wai Ren died, his younger brother He Dan Jia succeeded him, becoming Emperor He Dan Jia. During the reign of Emperor He Dan Jia, the Yin dynasty declined once again.

## 18

After Emperor He Dan Jia died, his son Emperor Zu Yi succeeded him. When Emperor Zu Yi ascended the throne, the Yin dynasty revived once more. Wu Xian assumed office.

## 19

After Emperor Zu Yi died, his son Emperor Zu Xin succeeded him. After Emperor Zu Xin died, his younger brother Wo Jia succeeded him, becoming Emperor Wo Jia. After Emperor Wo Jia died, the throne was given to Zuxin's son, Zuding, who was the elder brother of Wo Jia, becoming Emperor Zuding. After Emperor Zu Ding died, his younger brother Nan Geng, the son of Emperor Wo Jia, succeeded him, becoming Emperor Nan Geng. After Emperor Nan Geng died, the throne passed to Yang Jia, the son of Emperor Zu Ding, becoming Emperor Yang Jia. During the reign of Emperor Yang Jia, the Yin dynasty was in decline.

## 20

Since the reign of Emperor Zhong Ding, the legitimate heir was abandoned and replaced by various sons and grandsons. These descendants often vied with one another for succession, leading to nine generations of turmoil; as a result, no feudal lords came to court anymore.

## 21

After Emperor Yang Jia died, his younger brother Pangeng succeeded him, becoming Emperor Pangeng. During the reign of Emperor Pangeng, the capital of Yin had already been in Hebei. Pangeng crossed the Yellow River to the south and returned to the former residence of King Cheng Tang, marking the fifth relocation with no fixed location. The people of Yin expressed complaints and resentment; they did not wish to relocate. Pangeng then addressed the feudal lords and ministers, saying: "In the past, our great ancestor King Cheng Tang and your ancestors together established this realm; its laws and regulations are worth upholding. If we abandon them without striving to uphold them, how can virtue be achieved!" He then proceeded across the Yellow River southward, governed Bo, and implemented the policies of King Tang. Only after this did the people find peace once more, and the Yin dynasty revived. Feudal lords came to court because they respected the virtue of King Cheng Tang.

## 22

After Emperor Pangeng died, his younger brother Xiao Xin succeeded him, becoming Emperor Xiao Xin. When Emperor Xiao Xin ascended the throne, the Yin Dynasty declined once again. The people missed Pangeng and thus composed three chapters titled "Pangeng." After Emperor Xiao Xin died, his younger brother Xiao Yi succeeded him, becoming Emperor Xiao Yi.

## 23

After Emperor Xiao Yi died, his son Emperor Wu Ding succeeded him. When Emperor Wu Ding ascended the throne, he wished to revive Yin but had not yet found an able assistant. For three years, he remained silent; all political affairs were decided by the chief minister in order to observe the customs of the realm. Emperor Wu Ding dreamed at night that he had found a sage named Yue. He examined all his ministers and officials according to the image from his dream, but none matched. Thus he ordered craftsmen and workers to search in the countryside, and they found Yue in the area of Fu Xian. At that time, Yue was a slave laborer, building structures at Fu Xian. When presented to Wu Ding, Wu Ding said, "This is the one." After speaking with him, it was indeed confirmed that he was a sage. He was appointed as prime minister, and Yin state was greatly governed. Therefore, the place Fu Xian was used to name his clan, and he was called Fu Yue.

## 24

Emperor Wu Ding offered sacrifices to King Cheng Tang; the next day, a pheasant flew up and perched on the ear of a tripod while making noises. Wu Ding was frightened. Zu Ji said, "Your Majesty need not worry; first, improve governance." Zu Ji then instructed the king: "Only Heaven observes below and examines righteousness; it grants longevity or shortens years. It is not Heaven that shortens people's lives, but rather they cut their own fate short in the middle. If the people do not follow virtue and refuse to accept punishment, Heaven has already assigned a mandate to correct their virtue. It is then said, 'What can be done?' Alas! "Your Majesty, if you succeed in governing the people with reverence, nothing is not continued by Heaven. Regular sacrifices must never be neglected as a way of abandoning the path." Wu Ding improved governance and practiced virtue; all under heaven rejoiced, and the Yin dynasty once again revived.

## 25

After Emperor Wu Ding died, his son Emperor Zu Geng succeeded him. Zu Ji praised Wu Ding for regarding the auspicious pheasant as a sign of virtue, and established his temple with the title Gao Zong. Thus, "Gao Zong Rong Ri" and its commentary were composed.

## 26

After Emperor Zu Geng died, his younger brother Zu Jia succeeded him, becoming the Emperor Jia. Emperor Jia was licentious and corrupt; the Yin dynasty declined again.

## 27

After Emperor Jia died, his son, Emperor Lin Xin, succeeded him. After Emperor Lin Xin died, his younger brother Geng Ding succeeded him, becoming Emperor Geng Ding. After Emperor Geng Ding died, his son Wu Yi succeeded him as emperor. The Yin dynasty moved away from Bo once again and relocated to Hebei.

## 28

Emperor Wu Yi was tyrannical; he made a doll, calling it the god of Heaven. He played board games with it and had others act out its moves. When the god of Heaven lost, Wu Yi insulted and humiliated it. He filled a leather bag with blood, held it up, and shot at it, naming the act "Shooting Heaven." While hunting between the He and Wei rivers, a sudden thunderstorm struck, and Wu Yi was killed by lightning. His son Emperor Tai Ding succeeded him. After Emperor Tai Ding died, his son Emperor Yi succeeded him. When Emperor Yi ascended the throne, Yin declined further.

## 29

Emperor Yi's eldest son was Wei Zi Qi; however, because Qi's mother was of low status, he could not succeed to the throne. His younger son Xin had a mother who was the principal queen, so Xin became the heir. After Emperor Yi died, his son Xin succeeded him and became Emperor Xin; he is known in history as Zhou.

## 30

Emperor Zhou was naturally intelligent, quick-witted, and had a sharp perception; his hearing and sight were extremely keen; his physical strength surpassed that of ordinary people, and he could wrestle fierce beasts with his bare hands; his wisdom was sufficient to resist remonstrances, and his eloquence enough to justify wrongdoings; he boasted of the abilities of his ministers and officials, elevated himself above all others with his reputation, believing that everyone was beneath him. He loved drinking and indulgent pleasures, and favored concubines. He loved Daji, and followed her words without question. Thus he ordered Shi Juan to compose new licentious music, the "Beili" dance, and soft, sensuous melodies. He imposed heavy taxes to fill the treasury of Lutai with money and stockpile grain at Juqiao until it overflowed. He collected more exotic dogs, horses, and rare objects, filling his palaces to overflowing. He further expanded the Shachu Yuan Tai hunting grounds, capturing many wild beasts and flying birds to place within them. He was disrespectful toward ghosts and gods. He held grand feasts and entertainments at Shachu, filling pools with wine and hanging meat like trees; he made men and women run naked among them, indulging in drinking all night long.

## 31

As the people resented him and some feudal lords rebelled, Zhou then imposed severe punishments, including the method of roasting on a gridiron. He appointed Xi Bo Chang, Ji Hou, and E Hou as his three top ministers. Ji Hou had a beautiful daughter, whom he presented to Zhou. The Ji Hou's daughter did not enjoy licentiousness; Zhou became angry, killed her, and then minced Ji Hou into pieces. E Hou strongly protested and argued fiercely in defense of Ji Hou, so Zhou had E Hou dried like meat. Xi Bo Chang heard about this and secretly sighed with sorrow. Chong Hou Hu learned of it and reported to Zhou, who then imprisoned Xi Bo at Youli. Xi Bo's ministers, including Hong Yao and others, sought beautiful women, rare objects, and fine horses to present to Zhou, who then released Xi Bo. Xi Bo, upon his release, offered the western territory of Luo in exchange for the abolition of the gridiron punishment. Zhou agreed and granted him bows, arrows, axes, and halberds, allowing Xi Bo to conduct military campaigns. He appointed Fei Zhong as his chief minister. Fei Zhong was good at flattery and loved wealth, so the people of Yin did not trust him. Zhou also used Wu Lai. Wu Lai was good at slander and calumny, so the feudal lords became even more estranged from Zhou because of this.

## 32

After returning home, Xi Bo secretly cultivated virtue and performed good deeds; many feudal lords defected from Zhou and went to follow Xi Bo. Xi Bo grew increasingly powerful, so Zhou gradually lost authority and influence. Prince Bi Gan advised him, but he did not listen. Shang Rong was a virtuous man beloved by the people; Zhou deposed him. When Xi Bo attacked and destroyed the state of Ji, Zuyi, a minister of Zhou, heard about it and blamed Zhou. Fearing for his kingdom, he hurried to report to Zhou: "Heaven has already ended our Yin dynasty's mandate; even when we consulted the great tortoise shells, no one dared predict good fortune. It is not that the former kings failed to assist us later generations, but because Your Majesty indulged in licentiousness and cruelty, cutting off your own destiny. Therefore Heaven has abandoned us. We do not have peace or food security; we are unaware of heavenly principles and fail to follow established norms." Now our people all wish for destruction, saying, "Why does Heaven not bring down its wrath? Why has the great mandate not yet come?" What is Your Majesty going to do about it?" Zhou said, "Is not my life determined by Heaven's mandate?" Zuyi returned and said, "Zhou cannot be advised anymore." After Xi Bo died, King Wu of Zhou led his eastern expedition and arrived at Mengjin, where eight hundred feudal lords who had defected from Yin joined him. lando The feudal lords all said, "Zhou can be attacked now." King Wu said, "You do not yet know the will of Heaven." So they returned home.

## 33

Zhou became even more licentious and disorderly, without restraint. Wei Zi repeatedly advised him but was not listened to; so he conspired with the Grand Tutor and Junior Tutor and then left. Bi Gan said, "As a subject, one cannot help but die in an attempt to remonstrate." He then strongly advised Zhou. Zhou became angry and said, "I have heard that the heart of a sage has seven orifices." He dismembered Bi Gan to examine his heart. Ji Zi, fearing for his life, pretended to be mad and acted like a slave; Zhou then imprisoned him as well. The Grand Tutor and Junior Tutor of Yin then took the ritual musical instruments and fled to Zhou. King Wu of Zhou therefore led his feudal lords in a campaign against Zhou. Zhou also mobilized troops to resist them at Musye. On the Jiazi day, Zhou's army was defeated. Zhou fled and climbed onto Lütai, wearing his precious jade robes, then threw himself into the fire and died. King Wu of Zhou then beheaded Zhou, impaling his head on a [large] white flag. He killed Daji. He released Ji Zi from prison, built a tomb for Bi Gan, and honored the neighborhood of Shang Rong with a marker. He enfeoffed Zhou's son Wu Geng and Lu Fu to continue the Yin ancestral rites, ordering them to follow the policies of Pan Geng. The people of Yin were greatly pleased. At this time, King Wu of Zhou became the Son of Heaven. Later generations devalued the imperial title and referred to him as a king. He enfeoffed descendants of Yin as feudal lords, subject to Zhou.

## 34

After King Wu of Zhou died, Wu Geng conspired with Guan Shu and Cai Shu to rebel. King Cheng ordered Duke of Zhou to punish them, and then enfeoffed Wei Zi at Song to continue the descendants of Yin.

## 35

Grand Historian said: I have compiled a eulogy on Qi's deeds, from Cheng Tang onward, drawing materials from the Book of Documents and the Book of Songs. Qi belonged to the Zi clan; later, after being enfeoffed in different states, his descendants took their state names as surnames, including Yin Shi, Lai Shi, Song Shi, Kongsong Shi, Zhi Shi, Bei Yin Shi, and Mu Yi Shi. Confucius said: The chariots of Yin were well made, and they favored the color white.
