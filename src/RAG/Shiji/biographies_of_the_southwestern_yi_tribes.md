# Biographies of the Southwestern Yi Tribes

## 1

The chieftains of the southwestern Yi numbered in the dozens, and Yelang was the largest. To its west, the tribes belonging to Mimo also numbered in the dozens, with Dian being the largest; From Dian northward, the chieftains also numbered in the dozens, among whom Qiongdu was the largest: all of these people wore their hair in topknots, cultivated fields, and had villages and settlements. Beyond them, to the west from Tongshi in the east and northward to Yeyu, the people known as Gui and Kunming wore their hair braided, moved about with their herds, had no fixed dwellings or chieftains, and occupied a territory spanning several thousand li. From Ranzhi in the northeast, the chieftains numbered in the dozens, of whom Xitu and Zuo Du were the largest; From Zuo Du in the northeast, the chiefts numbered in the dozens, Ranzhong being the largest. Their customs were either sedentary or nomadic; they lived to the west of Shu. From Ranzhong in the northeast, there were dozens of chieftains; Baima was the largest among them. They all belonged to the Di people. These were all the southern and western barbarian tribes outside Ba and Shu.

## 2

At the beginning, during the reign of King Wei of Chu, he sent General Zhuang Qiao to lead troops along the river, conquering areas west of Ba and Qinzhong. Zhuang Qiao was a descendant of King Zhuang of Chu. Qiao reached Dianchi, where the territory was three hundred li in area; on its sides were level lands stretching for several thousand li of fertile and abundant land. He pacified it with military might and brought it under the rule of Chu. He wanted to return and report, but just then the Qin attacked and seized from Chu the commanderies of Ba and Qinzhong; the roads were blocked and impassable. Therefore, he returned and declared himself king of Dian, changed his attire, conformed to their customs, and ruled over them accordingly. During the Qin dynasty, Chang'e roughly opened a five-chi road; in these states, officials were somewhat appointed. After more than ten years, the Qin was destroyed. When the Han dynasty rose to power, they abandoned these states and opened up the old frontier of Shu. Some people from Ba and Shu secretly went out to trade, obtaining Zuo horses, Bo slaves, and Mao cattle; because of this, the regions of Ba and Shu became prosperous.

## 3

In the sixth year of Jianyuan, Taixing Wang Hui attacked Dongyue; in response, Dongyue killed King Ying. Taking advantage of the military momentum, Wang Hui instructed Fan Yang Ling Tang Meng to convey a message to Nan Yue indirectly. The Southern Yue people served Meng with Shu Goujiang sauce. Meng asked where it came from, and they said, "It comes through the northwest via Zangke; the Zangke River is several li wide and flows under Fanyu City." After returning to Chang'an, Meng asked merchants from Shu, and the merchants said, "Only Shu produces Goujiang sauce; many people secretly take it to sell in Yelang. Yelang is situated by the Zangke River, which is more than a hundred bu wide and sufficient for boat navigation. The Southern Yue used wealth to subjugate Yelang, reaching as far west as Tongshi; yet they were still unable to fully enslave and control them." Tang Meng then submitted a memorial to the emperor, saying: "The King of Southern Yue has yellow-roofed palaces and a left-facing banner; his territory spans more than ten thousand li from east to west. Though he is nominally an outer vassal, in reality he is the ruler of one province. Now, if we go from Changsha and Yuzhang, many water routes are cut off and difficult to travel. I have secretly learned that Yelang has a capable army of more than ten thousand men. If we float ships down the Zangke River and strike unexpectedly, this would be an extraordinary strategy to subdue Yue." If we truly use the strength of Han and the wealth of Ba and Shu to open a route through Yelang and establish officials there, it would be very easy." The emperor approved this. He then appointed Tang Meng as Langzhongjiang, commanding a thousand soldiers and provisions for more than ten thousand people. He entered through the Zuo Pass of Ba and Shu and thus met Yelang Hou Duotong. Meng gave generous gifts, explained Han's power and virtue to him, made an agreement to establish officials, and appointed his son as a county magistrate. The small towns near Yelang were all greedy for Han silk and cloth, believing the Han route to be perilous and ultimately unattainable; thus they provisionally accepted Meng's agreement. After returning with a report, it was then established as Jianwei Commandery. They mobilized soldiers from Ba and Shu to build roads, starting from Budaodao toward the Zangke River. Shu native Sima Xiangru also proposed that the western Yi tribes of Qiong and Zhuo could be established as a commandery. He sent Sima Xiangru, in the capacity of Langzhongjiang, to go and explain; they were all treated like the southern Yi tribes, with one Duwei officer and more than ten counties established, subject to Shu.

## 4

At that time, the four commanderies of Ba and Shu maintained a route connecting the southwestern Yi tribes; garrisons were established for mutual support in troop movements and supply. After several years, the road remained impassable, and many soldiers died from exhaustion, starvation, or exposure; the southwestern Yi tribes rebelled repeatedly. Troops were sent to suppress them, but the campaigns consumed great resources and achieved no success. The emperor was troubled by this situation and dispatched Gong Sun Hong to go and investigate the matter. After returning, he reported back, stating that it would be disadvantageous. When Gong Sun Hong became the Grand Censor, at that time the court was just beginning to build Shuofang in order to control the Yellow River and drive out the Xiongnu. Hong then repeatedly argued that the southwestern Yi tribes were a threat and could be temporarily abandoned so that all efforts might be concentrated on dealing with the Xiongnu. The emperor withdrew from the western Yi, establishing only two counties and one Duwei officer in Nan Yi Yelang, gradually allowing Jianwei to maintain itself independently.

## 5

In the first year of Yuanshou, Lord Bolang Hou Zhang Qian returned from a mission to Dayu. He said that while staying in Dayu he had seen Shu brocade and 邛 bamboo canes, and upon inquiry about their origin, was told: "They come from the country of Shendu to the southeast, which is several thousand li away, where Shu merchants trade." Some had heard that about two thousand li west of Qiong lay the country of Shendu. Zhang Qian then strongly argued that Dayu lay to the southwest of Han, and admired Chinese civilization. They were troubled by the Xiongnu blocking their route. If a connection could be made with Shu, the route to the country of Shendu would become much shorter and more convenient, bringing benefits without harm. Thus the Son of Heaven ordered Wang Ranyu, Bai Shichang, Lü Yue Ren and others to secretly go west through the western Yi territories in search of the country of Shendu.  They reached Dian, where King Cang Qiang detained them and sent more than ten groups to seek a western route. After more than a year, they were all blocked by the Kunming people and could not reach Shendu.

## 6

King of Dian asked the Han envoys: "Which is greater, Han or me?" The Yelang Hou also thought similarly. Because the roads were not connected, they each considered themselves rulers of a province and did not know how vast Han was. When the envoys returned, they strongly praised Dian as a great state capable of being brought under close allegiance. The Son of Heaven took special notice of this.

## 7

When the Southern Yue rebelled, the emperor ordered Chiyi Hou to mobilize troops from the southern Yi through Jianwei. The ruler of Qulan feared that a distant campaign would leave his people vulnerable to attacks on the elderly and weak by neighboring states, so he conspired with his followers to rebel, killing the envoy and the governor of Jianwei. The Han dynasty then mobilized eight commandant units composed of convicts from Ba and Shu who had previously fought against the Southern Yue to crush them. Just then, the Southern Yue had already been defeated; since the eight Han commandant units did not receive further orders, they withdrew their troops and proceeded to execute punitive measures against Toulan. Toulan was a people who often blocked the route to Dian. After pacifying Toulan, they then subdued the southern Yi and established Zangke Commandery. The Yelang Hou had initially relied on the Southern Yue; after the Southern Yue was destroyed, when the Han forces returned to punish rebels, Yelang submitted and came to court. The emperor appointed him as King of Yelang.

## 8

After the destruction of Southern Yue, and when Han executed Qulan's ruler and the chieftain of Qiong, as well as killed Zuo Hou, Ranzhong and others were greatly frightened and requested to submit and have officials appointed. Thus, Qiongdu was established as Yue Xi Commandery, Zhuodu as Shenli Commandery, Ranzhong as Wenshan Commandery, and the Bai Ma region to the west of Guanghan as Wudu Commandery.

## 9

The emperor sent Wang Ranyu to use the momentum from the defeat of Yue and the suppression of the southern Yi tribes to persuade King Dian to come to court. King of Dian had a following of several ten thousand people, and to the northeast of him were Laojin and Mimo, who were all of the same surname and supported each other; they refused to listen. Laojin and Mimo repeatedly harassed Han envoys and soldiers. In the second year of Yuanyou, the Son of Heaven mobilized troops from Ba and Shu to attack and annihilate Laojin and Mimo. With his army he approached Dian. The King of Dian initially showed good conduct, so the Han did not execute him. King of Dian, having suffered hardships from the southwestern Yi tribes, surrendered with his entire state and requested that officials be appointed and he be allowed to come to court. Thus it was established as Yizhou Commandery, the King of Dian was granted a royal seal, and he continued to rule over his people.

## 10

There were hundreds of chieftains among the southwestern Yi tribes, but only Yelang and Dian received royal seals as kings. Dian was a small town, yet it was most favored.

## 11

Grand Historian said: Could the ancestors of Chu have been deprived of heavenly blessings? They served as a teacher to King Wen of Zhou and were enfeoffed in Chu. When the Zhou dynasty declined, their territory was said to span five thousand li. After the Qin destroyed the feudal lords, only a descendant of Chu still held the title of King of Dian. When Han suppressed the southwestern Yi tribes, many states were destroyed; yet only Dian was again favored with a royal title. Yet the beginning of contact with the southern Yi tribes can be traced back to the sight of Goujiang sauce in Fanyu and the bamboo canes from Qiong seen in Dayu. The western Yi tribes later split, dividing into two regions, and ultimately became seven commanderies.
