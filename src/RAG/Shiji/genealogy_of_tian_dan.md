# Genealogy of Tian Dan

## 1

Tian Dan was a native of Di, and belonged to the family of King Qi's Tian clan. Dan's cousin Tian Rong, and Rong's younger brother Tian Heng, were both valiant men; their family was powerful, and they were able to win the loyalty of others.

## 2

When Chen She first rose up and proclaimed himself King of Chu, he sent Zhou Shi to pacify the territory of Wei. Advancing northward as far as Di, they found the city's defenders holding out. Tian Dan pretended to have his slave bound and brought him before a group of young men in court, intending to report the slave for execution. They encountered the magistrate of Di and attacked and killed him. Then they summoned local powerful officials and young men from prominent families, saying: "All the feudal lords have rebelled against Qin and declared themselves rulers. Qi was an ancient state; Dan is a member of the Tian clan, and should be king." Thus he proclaimed himself King of Qi and raised troops to attack Zhou Shi. Zhou Shi's army withdrew, and Tian Dan then led his forces eastward to pacify the lands of Qi.

## 3

The Qin general Zhang Han besieged King Jiu of Wei at Linji, and the situation was dire. King Wei sent a request for aid to Qi, and King Tian Dan led troops to rescue Wei. Zhang Han attacked at night in silence, heavily defeating the armies of Qi and Wei, killing Tian Dan under Linji. Dan's younger brother Tian Rong gathered the remnants of Dan's forces and fled eastward to Dong'e.

## 4

When the people of Qi heard that King Tian Dan had died, they then installed Tian Jia, the younger brother of former King Qi Jian, as King of Qi, appointed Tian Jiao as prime minister, and named Tian Jian as general, to resist the feudal lords.

## 5

When Tian Rong fled to Dong'e, Zhang Han pursued and surrounded him. Xiang Liang heard of the dire situation facing Tian Rong and led his troops to attack and defeat Zhang Han's army at Dong'e. Zhang Han retreated westward, and Xiang Liang then pursued him. Tian Rong was angry that Qi had installed Tian Jia as king, so he led his troops back and attacked King Tian Jia, driving him away. Jia fled to Chu. The prime minister of Qi, Jiao, also fled to Zhao; Jiao's younger brother Tian Jian had previously sought aid from Zhao and therefore remained there, too afraid to return. Tian Rong then installed the son of Tian Dan, Shi, as King of Qi. Rong served as prime minister and appointed Tian Heng as general to pacify the land of Qi.

## 6

After Xiang Liang had pursued Zhang Han, Zhang Han's forces grew stronger. Xiang Liang sent envoys to Zhao and Qi, requesting them to raise troops and jointly attack Zhang Han. Tian Rong said: "If Chu kills Tian Jia, Zhao executes Tian Jiao and Tian Jian, then Jian will agree to send troops." King Huai of Chu said: "Tian Jia is a king related by kinship to our state. He has come to me in distress; it would be unjust to kill him." Zhao also refused to execute Tian Jiao and Tian Jian in order to gain favor with Qi. Qi said: "If a viper bites the hand, one must cut off the hand; if it bites the foot, one must amputate the foot. Why is that?" Because otherwise it would harm the body." Now, Tian Jia, Tian Jiao, and Tian Jian are in Chu and Zhao; they are not merely close as hands or feet. Why then have you not killed them?" Moreover, if Qin regains control over the world, those who hold power will be reduced to having their tombs gnawed by rats and dogs." Chu and Zhao did not listen; Qi became angry as well, and ultimately refused to send troops. Zhang Han indeed defeated and killed Xiang Liang, crushing the Chu forces, which retreated eastward. Zhang Han then crossed the river to besiege Zhao at Julu. Xiang Yu went to rescue Zhao and thus harbored resentment toward Tian Rong.

## 7

After Xiang Yu had saved Zhao, defeated Zhang Han and others, slaughtered Xi'an in the west, destroyed Qin, and established feudal lords, he moved King Tian Shi of Qi to become King of Jiaodong instead, with his capital at Jimo. The general of Qi, Tian Du, had joined in rescuing Zhao and thus entered the Guan Pass; therefore, he was installed as King of Qi with his capital at Linzi. The grandson of former King Qi Jian, Tian An, while Xiang Yu was crossing the river to rescue Zhao, captured several cities in Jibei and led his troops in surrendering to Xiang Yu. Xiang Yu then installed Tian An as King of Jibei, with his capital at Boyang. Tian Rong had failed to support Xiang Liang by not sending troops to aid Chu and Zhao in their attack on Qin, so he did not receive a kingship; the Zhao general Chen Yu also lost his position and failed to be granted a kingship: both men harbored resentment toward King Xiang.

## 8

After King Xiang returned, the feudal lords each went to their respective states. Tian Rong sent men with troops to assist Chen Yu and ordered them to rebel against Zhao's territory; at the same time, Rong also raised his own forces to resist and attack Tian Du, who fled to Chu. Tian Rong detained King Shi of Qi and did not allow him to go to Jiaodong. Shi's attendants said: "King Xiang is strong and ruthless; if you are to be the king of Jiaodong, but do not go to your state, it will surely be dangerous." Shi was frightened and so fled to his own state. Tian Rong became angry, pursued Shi, and killed King Tian Shi of Qi at Jimo; then he returned to attack and kill King Jibei Tian An. Thus, Tian Rong declared himself King of Qi and annexed all three regions of Qi.

## 9

King Xiang heard about this and was furious; he then launched a northern campaign against Qi. King Tian Rong of Qi suffered a military defeat, fled to Pingyuan, where the local people killed him. King Xiang then burned and destroyed the cities and walls of Qi, slaughtering all inhabitants in every place he passed through. The people of Qi gathered together to rebel against him. Rong's younger brother Heng gathered the scattered forces of Qi, mustering tens of thousands of men, and launched a counterattack against Xiang Yu at Chengyang. Meanwhile, King Han led the feudal lords to defeat Chu and entered Pengcheng. Xiang Yu heard of this, withdrew from Qi and returned to attack Han at Pengcheng. He then engaged in continuous battles with Han, confronting each other at Yingyang. Because of this, Tian Heng was able to recapture the cities and towns of Qi, installed Guang, the son of Tian Rong, as King of Qi, and served as his prime minister, holding exclusive control over state affairs. No matter how great or small a political matter was, it was decided by the prime minister.

## 10

After three years of pacifying Qi, King Han sent Liu Sheng to persuade King Guang of Qi and his prime minister Heng to surrender. Heng agreed with the proposal and withdrew his forces from Lixia. The Han general Han Xin led his troops eastward to attack Qi. Initially, Qi had stationed Hua Wushang and Tian Jie at Lixia to resist Han. When envoys from Han arrived, they disbanded their defensive preparations, indulged in drinking, and even sent emissaries to negotiate peace with Han. The Han general Han Xin, having already pacified Zhao and Yan, employed the strategy of Kuaitong, crossed Pingyuan by stealth, and launched a surprise attack to defeat Qi's forces at Lixia, then entered Linzi. King Guang of Qi and prime minister Heng were furious, believing Liu Sheng had betrayed them, and boiled Liu Sheng alive. King Guang of Qi fled east to Gaomi, while the prime minister Heng fled to Boyang (Bo), and the governor Tian Guang fled to Chengyang; General Tian Ji stationed his troops at Jiaodong. Chu sent Long Ju to rescue Qi, and King of Qi joined forces with him in Gaomi. The Han general Han Xin and Cao Can defeated and killed Long Ju, capturing King Guang of Qi. The Han general Guan Ying pursued and captured the governor of Qi, Tian Guang. When he reached Boyang (Bo), Heng heard that King Qi had died, declared himself King of Qi, and returned to attack Guan Ying. Guan Ying defeated Heng's forces at Yingxia. Tian Heng fled to Liang and surrendered to Peng Yue. At that time, Peng Yue was residing in the territory of Liang, maintaining a neutral stance, sometimes siding with Han and sometimes with Chu. After Han Xin had killed Long Ju, he ordered Cao Can to advance and defeat and kill Tian Ji at Jiaodong; he also dispatched Guan Ying to defeat and kill the Qi general Tian Xi at Qiansheng. Han Xin thus pacified Qi, requested permission to proclaim himself as the provisional King of Qi, and Han accepted this request and established him in that position.

## 11

After more than a year, Han destroyed Xiang Ji; King Han was declared emperor and appointed Peng Yue as the King of Liang. Tian Heng, fearing punishment, fled with more than 500 followers to sea and settled on an island. Emperor Gao heard of this and thought that the brothers Tian Heng had originally pacified Qi; many virtuous men from Qi were attached to them. Now, if they remained in the sea without being brought back, there was a risk of future rebellion. Thus he sent envoys to pardon Tian Heng's crimes and summon him. Tian Heng replied with thanks, saying: "I boiled alive Your Majesty's envoy Liu Sheng. Now I hear that his younger brother Liu Shang is a virtuous general of Han. I am afraid and dare not obey the imperial decree; please allow me to be an ordinary man, guarding this island." The envoy returned with the report. Emperor Gao then issued an edict to Weiwei Liu Shang, saying: "If King Tian Heng of Qi arrives, anyone who dares to disturb his attendants or horses will be exterminated!" He then sent another envoy with a token to convey the decree in full, stating: "If Tian Heng comes, at worst he will be made king; at best, merely a marquis. If he does not come, we shall raise an army to punish him." Tian Heng then traveled with two of his attendants by official carriage to Luoyang.

## 12

Before reaching Luoyang by thirty li, they arrived at the stables of Shixiang Zhi. Tian Heng thanked the envoy and said: "A subject should bathe and purify himself before appearing before the Son of Heaven." He stopped there to stay. He said to his guests: "At first, Heng and King Han were both seated facing south as sovereigns. Now that King Han has become the Son of Heaven, while I have become a fugitive slave bowing northward before him; this shame is already great enough. Moreover, I boiled alive the elder brother of another man, and now his younger brother is serving our lord side by side. Even if he fears the Son of Heaven's decree and dares not act against me, should I not feel ashamed in my heart?" Furthermore, what the Son of Heaven wants to see from me is merely a glimpse of my face. Now that the Son of Heaven is in Luoyang, if I behead myself now and send my head there by fast horse within thirty li, its appearance will not yet have deteriorated and can still be seen." He then committed suicide with a sword, ordered his guests to present his head, and sent it by fast horse along with the envoy to report to Emperor Gao. Emperor Gao said: "Alas! There is indeed a reason for this! They rose from commoners, and among the three brothers each became king in turn—wasn't that virtuous!" He wept for them and appointed their two guests as Duwei, mobilized 2,000 soldiers, and buried Tian Heng with the rites reserved for a king.

## 13

After the burial, the two guests dug holes beside his tomb and both committed suicide by cutting their throats, following him in death. Emperor Gao heard about this and was greatly shocked; he marveled at how virtuous Tian Heng's followers were. I have heard that more than 500 of his remaining followers are still in the sea; I shall send envoys to summon them. When they arrived, they learned of Tian Heng's death and also committed suicide. Thus it became known that the brothers Tian were truly able to win the loyalty of men of virtue.

## 14

Grand Historian said: How profound was Kuaitong's strategy! It caused chaos in Qi and arrogance in Huaiyin. In the end, both of these men perished because of it! Kuaitong was skilled in making arguments that weighed pros and cons, discussing the shifting strategies of the Warring States era, composing a total of eighty-one essays. Kuaitong was on good terms with An Qisheng, a native of Qi; An Qisheng had once tried to advise Xiang Yu, but Xiang Yu did not adopt his strategies. Later, when Xiang Yu wanted to confer titles upon these two men, they both ultimately refused and fled away. Tian Heng's noble integrity inspired his guests to admire righteousness and follow him in death; was he not the most virtuous of men! I therefore include them here. There are not lacking skilled painters, yet none can depict it—why is that?
