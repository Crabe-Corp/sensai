# Song Wei Zi Shijia

## 1

Wei Zi Kai was the eldest son of Yin Emperor Yi and the junior brother of Emperor Zhou. After Zhou ascended to the throne, he was not wise, and indulged in licentiousness and disorder in governance. Wei Zi repeatedly advised him, but Emperor Zhou did not listen. When Zuyi learned of the virtuous conduct of Xi Bo Chang, ruler of Zhou, and his destruction of the state of Li, he feared disaster would come and informed Emperor Zhou. Emperor Zhou said: "Is it not my destiny determined by Heaven that I was born?" "What can they do about it!" At this point, Wei Zi judged that Emperor Zhou could ultimately not be advised to reform. He wanted to die for it, but when he left, he was unable to make a decision and thus asked the Taishi and Shaoshi: "The Yin dynasty has no good governance and does not manage the four directions properly." "Our ancestors thus served above, but Emperor Zhou immersed himself in wine and relied on women, thereby corrupting the virtue of Tang below." "Now, the Yin dynasty, both high and low officials, are fond of petty thefts and criminal acts. The ministers and officers do not follow proper standards; all have committed crimes and guilt. Thus, there is no one to restrain them, and common people rise together, becoming enemies and rivals." Now the Yin dynasty will surely lose its mandate! It is like crossing water without a ford or shore. "The Yin dynasty will thus be lost, and this situation has continued until now." He said: "Master Taishi, Master Shaoshi, should I leave and go out?" "Should my family preserve itself from destruction?" "Now, without cause you have informed me of this catastrophe. What should I do about it?" Taishi said: "Prince, Heaven has firmly sent down calamity to destroy the Yin state. Therefore, do not fear or be afraid; do not heed the advice of the old and experienced." "Now, the people of Yin are neglectful in their worship of gods and spirits. "If we can truly govern the state well, even if the state is restored and I die, I would have no regrets." "To die without ultimately restoring order is better to leave." Thus, he fled.

## 2

Ji Zi was a relative of Emperor Zhou. lø When Emperor Zhou first made ivory chopsticks, Ji Zi sighed and said: "If he uses ivory chopsticks, he will surely use jade cups; "Once he has the cups, he will certainly seek rare and exotic treasures from distant lands to serve himself. "The gradual descent into extravagance in carriages, horses, palaces, and dwellings will begin here; it cannot be stopped." As Emperor Zhou indulged in licentiousness, Ji Zi advised him, but he did not listen. Some people said: "You may leave now." Ji Zi said: "As a subject, to advise and not be heeded, then leave, is to expose the ruler's faults and gain favor with the people. I cannot bear to do that." Thus, he let his hair grow unbound, feigned madness, and became a slave. He then withdrew and played the qin to express his sorrow; thus it was passed down as "Ji Zi's Qinqao."

## 3

Prince Bi Gan was also a relative of Emperor Zhou. Seeing that Ji Zi had advised the emperor but not been heeded and was reduced to slavery, Bi Gan said: "If a ruler has faults yet his ministers do not die in contention for justice, then what wrong have the common people committed!" Thus, he directly advised Emperor Zhou. Emperor Zhou became angry and said: "I have heard that the heart of a sage has seven orifices; is this truly true?" He then had Prince Bi Gan killed, cut open his chest, and examined his heart.

## 4

Wei Zi said: "Father and son are bound by blood; ministers and rulers are connected through righteousness. Therefore, if a father has faults, the son should advise three times; if not heeded, then follow and cry out in lamentation. "If a minister advises three times without being heeded, then according to righteousness, it is acceptable for him to leave." At this time, the Taishi and Shaoshi advised Wei Zi to leave, so he departed.

## 5

When King Wu of Zhou attacked Emperor Zhou and conquered Yin, Wei Zi then took his ritual vessels to the military gate, exposed his upper body with hands bound in front, held a lamb in his left hand and a bundle of rushes in his right, knelt on his knees and crawled forward to make an appeal. Thus King Wu released Wei Zi and restored him to his former position.

## 6

King Wu enfeoffed Zhou's son Wugeng Luxu Fu to continue the Yin ancestral rites, and appointed Guan Shu and Cai Shu as his supervisors.

## 7

After King Wu had conquered Yin, he visited Ji Zi.

## 8

King Wu said: "Ah! Heaven has established the people below, harmonizing their dwellings; I do not know the order of its constant principles."

## 9

Ji Zi replied: "In the past, Gun blocked the great flood and confused the Five Elements. The emperor became furious; he did not follow the Nine Categories of the Great Model, thus corrupting the order of constant principles. Gun was executed for his death, and Yu then succeeded him and rose to prominence. Heaven then bestowed upon Yu the Nine Categories of the Great Pattern, which restored the order of constant principles.

## 10

"The first is called the Five Elements; the second, the Five Affairs; the third, the Eight Policies; the fourth, the Five Calendars; the fifth, the Imperial Standard; the sixth, the Three Virtues; the seventh, Investigating Doubts; the eighth, the Many Omens; the ninth, to bestow the Five Blessings and fear the Six Extremes."

## 11

"The Five Elements: first is water, second fire, third wood, fourth metal, and fifth earth. Water moistens downward; fire burns upward; wood bends or straightens; metal follows transformation; and earth produces crops. Moistening downward creates saltiness; burning upward creates bitterness; bending or straightening produces sourness; following transformation gives pungency; and producing crops yields sweetness.

## 12

"The Five Affairs: first is appearance, second speech, third sight, fourth hearing, and fifth thought. Appearance means respect; speech means compliance; sight means clarity; hearing means acuity; and thought means wisdom. Respect produces solemnity; compliance brings order; clarity yields intelligence; acuity fosters strategy; and wisdom leads to sainthood.

## 13

"The Eight Policies: first is food, second goods, third sacrifice, fourth Sikong (Works), fifth Siku (Minister of Education), sixth Sikou (Minister of Justice), seventh guests, and eighth army.

## 14

"The Five Calendars: first is the year, second the month, third the day, fourth stars and constellations, fifth calendar calculations.

## 15

"The Imperial Standard: The emperor establishes his supreme standard, gathers the Five Blessings of the time, and bestows them upon the common people. At this time, he unites the common people under your standard, granting you the ultimate protection. All the common people must not form licentious cliques; no one should rival virtue. Only the emperor establishes the supreme standard. All the common people who have plans, actions, and steadfastness—you must remember them. Those who do not align with the standard or are free from blame—the emperor shall receive their service. If they appear calm and composed, saying "This is the virtue I cherish," you must bestow blessings upon them. At this time, people should uphold the emperor's supreme standard. Do not bully the weak and fear those who are lofty and wise. If people have ability and accomplish deeds, let them be honored for their conduct, and then the state will flourish. All upright individuals shall enjoy wealth and abundant harvests. If you cannot bring goodness to your household, then these people will suffer guilt. If there is no virtue in them, even if you bestow blessings upon them, they will bring blame to you. Do not be biased or partial; follow the king's righteousness. Have no personal preferences; follow the king's way. Do not commit evil acts; follow the king's path. Be neither biased nor partisan; the royal way is broad and open. Neither be partisan nor partial; the royal path is level and just. Do not act in reverse or sideways; the royal way stands upright and straight. Gather under its supreme standard, return to its ultimate principle. "Say the imperial standard's teachings; this is peace and instruction, in harmony with Heaven." All common people should follow these teachings of the supreme standard; by doing so they will be in accordance and action, drawing near to the radiance of the Son of Heaven. "The Son of Heaven becomes the parent of the people, thus becoming king over all under heaven."

## 16

"The Three Virtues: first is uprightness; second, overcoming through firmness; third, overcoming through gentleness. In times of peace and stability, uprightness prevails; the strong who are not friendly must be overcome through firmness. Those internally friendly should be subdued with gentleness; those sinking and gradual can be overcome by firmness, while those lofty and wise should be subdued by gentleness. Only the ruler bestows blessings, only the ruler wields authority, only the ruler enjoys jade-like food. Ministers must not bestow blessings or wield authority or enjoy jade-like food on their own. If ministers do bestow blessings, wield authority, or enjoy jade-like food, it will bring harm to your family and misfortune to your state. People will then act with bias and partiality; the common people will become arrogant and deviate from proper conduct.

## 17

"Investigating Doubts: Select diviners for establishing oracles. Then command the diviners, who give responses: rain, help, tears, mist, victory, correctness, and regret—totaling seven. Five oracle bones are used for divination; two signs are interpreted to derive the meaning. lington When establishing a person as a diviner, if three people interpret the omens, follow the opinion of the majority. If you have a great doubt, consult your own mind, then consult the ministers and officials, then consult the common people, and finally consult the divination. If you agree, the oracle agrees, the divination agrees, the ministers and officials agree, and the common people agree—this is called great harmony. In this case, your body will be healthy and strong, and your descendants will encounter good fortune. If you agree, the oracle agrees and the divination agrees, but the ministers and officials disagree and the common people also disagree—this is still considered auspicious. If the ministers and officials agree, the oracle agrees, and the divination agrees—but you disagree and the common people also do—this is still auspicious. If the common people agree, the oracle agrees, and divination agrees—but you and the ministers disagree—this is also auspicious. If you agree, the oracle concurs, but divination disagrees, and both ministers and common people disagree—this brings internal auspiciousness but external misfortune. If the oracle and divination both contradict human judgment, then remaining still is auspicious; taking action will bring misfortune.

## 18

"The many signs: rain, sunshine, warmth, cold, wind, and seasonality." When these five elements are fully present in their proper order, the various plants will flourish abundantly. If one of them reaches an extreme state, it brings misfortune. If one is completely absent, it also brings misfortune. The auspicious signs are as follows: "肃 (sù)—timely rain occurs; 治 (zhì)—timely sunshine occurs;" "知 (zhī)—timely warmth occurs;" "Mou (móu)—timely cold weather occurs;" "Sheng (shèng)—timely wind occurs." The ominous signs are as follows: "Kuang (kuáng)—constant rain;" "Jian (jiàn)—constant sunshine;" "Shu (shū)—constant warmth;" "Ji (jí)—constant cold weather;" "Wu (wù)—constant wind." The king's misdeeds affect the year; ministers and officials affect the month; and Shiyin affects the day. When years, months, days, and hours remain in proper order without disorder, all grains will mature successfully; governance will be clear and effective; capable officials will be recognized by the people; and families will enjoy peace and prosperity. When days, months, years, and seasons become disordered, the grains will not mature; governance becomes confused and unclear; capable officials are overlooked by the people; families suffer from unrest. The common people correspond to stars, which may favor wind or rain. The movement of the sun and moon follows a pattern with winter and summer. When the moon aligns with certain stars, it brings wind or rain.

## 19

"The Five Blessings: first is longevity; second is wealth; third is health and peace; fourth is a love of virtue; fifth is to pass away in old age with a good reputation." "The Six Extremes: first is misfortune, short life, and early death; second is illness; third is sorrow; fourth is poverty; fifth is wickedness; sixth is weakness."

## 20

Thus King Wu enfeoffed Ji Zi in Chaoxian (Korea) but did not make him a vassal.

## 21

Later, Ji Zi paid homage to the Zhou dynasty and passed through the former ruins of Yin. He was moved by the destruction of palaces and houses, where millet now grew wild. Ji Zi felt sorrowful; he wanted to weep but dared not, and tears would seem too feminine. Thus, he composed the poem "Mai Xiu" to express his grief in song. The poem reads: "The wheat grows tall and lush, the millet green and rich." "Those cunning youths! They no longer befriend me!" The so-called "cunning youths" refer to Emperor Zhou. When the people of Yin heard this, they all wept bitterly.

## 22

After King Wu died, King Cheng was still young, so Zhou Gong Dan took over governance and administered the state affairs on his behalf. Guan and Cai doubted him, so they conspired with Wu Geng to rebel, intending to attack King Cheng and Zhou Gong. After receiving King Cheng's mandate, Zhou Gong executed Wu Geng, killed Guan Shu, and exiled Cai Shu. He then ordered Wei Zi Kai to succeed as the heir of Yin, to uphold their ancestral rites, issued "The Mandate for Wei Zi" to confirm this, and established a state in Song for him. Wei Zi was thus able to demonstrate benevolence and virtue, succeeding Wu Geng; therefore, the remaining people of Yin greatly revered and loved him.

## 23

After Wei Zi Kai died, his younger brother Yan was enthroned, who became known as Wei Zhong. When Wei Zhong died, his son Song Gong Ji succeeded him. After Song Gong Ji's death, his son Ding Gong Shen took the throne. Upon Ding Gong Shen's death, his son Min Gong Gong succeeded him. After Min Gong Gong died, his younger brother Yang Gong Xi was enthroned. When Yang Gong ascended the throne, Min Gong's son Fu Si assassinated Yang Gong and declared himself ruler, saying, "I should be king," becoming King Li. After King Li died, his son Xi Gong Ju succeeded him.

## 24

In the seventeenth year of King Xi's reign, King Li of Zhou fled to Zhi.

## 25

In the twenty-eighth year, King Xi died and was succeeded by his son Hui Gong Jin. In the fourth year of Hui Gong's reign, King Xuan of Zhou ascended the throne. In the thirtieth year, Hui Gong died and was succeeded by his son Ai Gong. Ai Gong died in his first year of reign; he was succeeded by his son Dai Gong.

## 26

In the twenty-ninth year of Dai Gong's reign, King You of Zhou was killed by the Quanrong; at this time, Qin began to be recognized as a feudal lord.

## 27

In the thirty-fourth year, Dai Gong died and was succeeded by his son Wu Gong Sikong. Wu Gong's daughter became Lady of King Hui of Lu and bore Duke Huan of Lu. In the eighteenth year, Wu Gong died; his son Xuan Gong Li succeeded him.

## 28

Xuan Gong had a crown prince named Yu Yi. In the nineteenth year, Xuan Gong fell ill and ceded his position to his younger brother He, saying: "When a father dies, the son succeeds; when an elder brother dies, the younger brother takes over. This is a universal principle." "I should make He my successor." He also declined three times before finally accepting. After Xuan Gong died, his younger brother He was enthroned; he became known as Mu Gong.

## 29

In the ninth year of Mu Gong's reign, he fell ill and summoned Da Sima Kong Fu, saying: "My predecessor Xuan Gong passed over Crown Prince Yu Yi to make me king; I have never forgotten this." "After my death, you must certainly enthroned Yu Yi." lington Kong Fu said: "All the ministers wish to make Gongzi Feng king." Mu Gong said, "Do not enthroned Feng; I cannot betray Xuan Gong's will." Thus Mu Gong sent Feng to reside in Zheng. On the Gengchen day of the eighth month, Mu Gong died; Xuan Gong's son Yu Yi was enthroned and became known as Shang Gong. The gentlemen who heard of this said: "Song Xuan Gong could truly be called a man who understood people. He enthroned his younger brother to fulfill righteousness, yet in the end, his own son still inherited the throne."

## 30

In the first year of Shang Gong's reign, Gongzi Zhouyu of Wei assassinated his ruler Wan and declared himself king. He wished to gain recognition from the feudal lords and sent a message to Song saying: "Feng is in Zheng; he will surely cause chaos. You can join me in attacking him." Song agreed, joined forces with Zhouyu to attack Zheng, and marched as far as the eastern gate before retreating. In the second year, Zheng attacked Song in retaliation for the campaign at the eastern gate. After that, various feudal lords frequently launched invasions and attacks.

## 31

In the ninth year, Da Sima Kong Fu Jia's wife was beautiful; when she went out, she encountered Tai Zai Hua Du on the road. Hua Du was captivated and gazed at her with admiration. Hua Du coveted Kong Fu's wife, so he had people publicly declare in the state: "Shang Gong has only reigned for ten years, yet there have been eleven wars. The people can no longer endure their suffering; this is all due to Kong Fu. I will kill Kong Fu to bring peace to the people." In that year, Lu assassinated its ruler Yin Gong. In the tenth year, Hua Du attacked and killed Kong Fu, taking his wife for himself. Shang Gong was furious; Hua Du then assassinated Shang Gong, and welcomed Mu Gong's son Feng from Zheng to enthroned him. He became known as Zhuang Gong.

## 32

In the first year of Zhuang Gong's reign, Hua Du served as prime minister. In the ninth year, he captured Zhai Zhong of Zheng and demanded that Duke Tu be installed as ruler of Zheng. Zhai Zhong agreed, and so Tu was finally enthroned. In the nineteenth year, Zhuang Gong died; his son Min Gong Jie succeeded him.

## 33

In the seventh year of Min Gong's reign, Duke Huan of Qi ascended the throne. In the ninth year, Song suffered a flood; Lu sent Zang Wenzhong to mourn and console them over the disaster. Min Gong blamed himself, saying: "I am at fault for failing to properly serve the gods and spirits, and for poor governance; that is why we suffered a flood." Zang Wenzhong praised these words. These were the words taught to Min Gong by Gongzi Zi Yu.

## 34

In the tenth year of summer, Song attacked Lu; they fought at Chengqiu, where Lu captured alive Nan Gong Wan of Song. The people of Song requested the return of Wan, and he went back to Song. In the eleventh year of autumn, Min Gong went hunting with Nan Gong Wan. During a game of dice, they quarreled over precedence; Min Gong became angry and insulted him, saying: "At first I respected you;" "now you are merely a Lu captive." Wan was strong and resentful of these words, so he killed Min Gong in Mengze with his own hands. When Da Fu Chou Mu heard this, he led troops to the palace gate. Wan fought with Mu; Mu's teeth were clenched against the door frame as he died. He then killed Tai Zai Hua Du and enthroned Gongzi You as ruler. The other princes fled to Xiao, while Gongzi Yueshuo fled to Bo. Wan's younger brother Nan Gong Niu led troops to besiege Bo. In winter, Xiao and the various exiled princes of Song jointly attacked and killed Nan Gong Niu, assassinated the new ruler You of Song, and enthroned Min Gong's younger brother Yueshuo. He became known as Duke Huan. Wan of Song fled to Chen. The people of Song requested that they bribe the state of Chen. The people of Chen had women give him strong wine to drink, wrapped him in leather, and returned him to Song. The people of Song made a stew out of Wan.

## 35

In the second year of Duke Huan's reign, feudal lords launched an attack on Song but withdrew after reaching its outskirts. In the third year, Duke Huan of Qi began to establish his hegemony. In the twenty-third year, they welcomed Gongzi Hui of Wei from Qi and enthroned him; he became Duke Wen of Wei. łoży Duke Wen's younger sister was the wife of Duke Huan. King Mu of Qin ascended the throne. In the thirtieth year, Duke Huan fell ill; Crown Prince Zifu ceded his position to his junior brother Mu Yi as heir. Duke Huan considered it virtuous for the crown prince and refused to accept the offer. In spring of the thirty-first year, Duke Huan died; Crown Prince Zifu was enthroned and became Duke Xiang. He appointed his junior brother Mu Yi as prime minister. Before the funeral was completed, Duke Huan of Qi convened feudal lords at Kuichiu; Duke Xiang attended the meeting.

## 36

In the seventh year of Duke Xiang's reign, in the territory of Song, falling stars rained down like a shower and fell together with rain; six yis flew backward, which was due to strong winds.

## 37

In the eighth year, Duke Huan of Qi died; Song wished to convene an alliance meeting. In spring of the twelfth year, Duke Xiang of Song convened the Lu Shang League in an attempt to gain feudal lords' support from Chu; the people of Chu agreed. Gongzi Mu Yi advised, "A small state vying for an alliance will bring disaster." He did not listen. In autumn, feudal lords met Duke Song Gong for an alliance at Yu. Mu Yi said: "Is the disaster about to happen here?" "You desire too much; how can you bear it!" At that time, Chu captured Duke Xiang of Song and attacked Song. In winter, a meeting was held in Bo to release Duke Song Gong. Zi Yu said: "Disaster is not yet over." In summer of the thirteenth year, Song attacked Zheng. Zi Yu said: "The disaster is here now." In autumn, Chu attacked Song to rescue Zheng. Duke Xiang was about to fight; Zi Yu advised: "Heaven has long abandoned Shang, we must not proceed." In winter, the eleventh month, Duke Xiang fought against King Cheng of Chu at Hong. Before the Chu forces had crossed the river, Mu Yi said: "Their numbers are greater than ours; let us attack them before they have fully crossed." The duke did not listen. After the Chu forces had crossed but were not yet deployed, he said again: "We can attack." The duke replied: "Let us wait until they are fully deployed." After the deployment was complete, Song forces attacked them. Song's army suffered a major defeat; Duke Xiang was wounded in his thigh. The people of the state all resented the duke. The duke said: "A gentleman does not trap others in a difficult situation, nor attack before they are properly deployed." Zi Yu said: "Military success is measured by victory; what use are such conventional words!" "If we must follow your words, then we would be nothing more than slaves to them. Why fight at all?"

## 38

After King Cheng of Chu had rescued Zheng, the state of Zheng hosted a banquet in his honor; he left after taking two concubines from Zheng to return with. Shu Zhan said: "King Cheng is disrespectful; will he not meet his downfall?" "Because of this, we can know that he will never achieve hegemony."

## 39

In this year, Gongzi Chonger of Jin passed through Song. Duke Xiang, having been wounded by Chu and desiring support from Jin, presented Chonger with twenty chariots as a generous gift.

## 40

In summer of the fourteenth year, Duke Xiang fell ill from his wound at Hong and eventually died; his son Gong Wangchen was enthroned as Duke Cheng.

## 41

In the first year of Duke Cheng's reign, Duke Wen of Jin ascended the throne. In the third year, Song broke its alliance with Chu and became close to Jin out of gratitude toward Duke Wen. In the fourth year, King Cheng of Chu attacked Song; Song urgently appealed for help from Jin. In the fifth year, Duke Wen of Jin rescued Song and the Chu forces withdrew. In the ninth year, Duke Wen of Jin died. In the eleventh year, Crown Prince Shangchen of Chu assassinated his father King Cheng and took the throne in his place. In the sixteenth year, King Mu of Qin died.

## 42

In the seventeenth year, Duke Cheng died. Duke Cheng's younger brother Yusha killed the crown prince and Da Sima Gong Sun Gu to proclaim himself ruler. The people of Song jointly killed King Yu and enthroned Duke Cheng's youngest son Chujiu; he became known as Duke Zhao.

## 43

In the fourth year of Duke Zhao's reign, Song defeated Chang Zhai Yuansis at Changqiu. In the seventh year, King Zhuang of Chu ascended the throne.

## 44

In the ninth year, Duke Zhao was unjust; the people did not support him. Duke Zhao's younger brother Bao Ge was virtuous and humble toward scholars. Previously, Duke Xiang's wife wanted to have relations with Gongzi Bao; when he refused, she supported him in gaining influence within the state and thus appointed Da Fu Hua Yuan as You Shi. Duke Zhao went out hunting; Lady Wang Ji, his wife, sent Wei Bo to attack and kill Duke Chujiu. His younger brother Bao Ge was enthroned; he became known as Duke Wen.

## 45

In the first year of Duke Wen's reign, Jin led feudal lords to attack Song and accused them of regicide. Upon hearing that Duke Wen had been properly enthroned, they withdrew. In the second year, Duke Zhao's son incited chaos with Wen Gong's maternal uncle Xu and the families of Wu, Mou, Dai, Zhuang, and Huan; Duke Wen executed them all and exiled the families of Wu and Mou.

## 46

In spring of the fourth year, (Zheng) [Chu] ordered (Chu) [Zheng] to attack Song. Song sent Hua Yuan as general; Zheng defeated Song and captured Hua Yuan. Before the battle, Hua Yuan slaughtered sheep to feed his soldiers; however, his charioteer did not receive a portion of mutton soup and thus harbored resentment. He galloped into the Zheng army, which is why Song's forces were defeated and Hua Yuan was captured. Song ransomed Hua Yuan by offering a hundred war chariots and four hundred fine horses. Before the ransom was fully delivered, Hua Yuan escaped back to Song.

## 47

In the fourteenth year, King Zhuang of Chu besieged Zheng. Duke Bo of Zheng surrendered to Chu; Chu then released him again.

## 48

In the sixteenth year, envoys from Chu passed through Song; because of a previous grudge, Song captured the envoy. In September, King Zhuang of Chu laid siege to Song. In the seventeenth year, as the siege by Chu on Song lasted five months without relief and food ran out in the city of Song, Hua Yuan secretly met with General Zi Fan of Chu at night. Zi Fan reported to King Zhuang. The king asked: "How is the situation in the city?" He replied: "They are boiling bones for fuel and exchanging children to eat." King Zhuang said: "Indeed, those words are true! Our army has only two days' worth of provisions." Because of this, out of respect for the truthfulness shown, he withdrew his troops.

## 49

In the twenty-second year, Duke Wen died; his son Gong Xua was enthroned as ruler. He began to be buried with great ceremony. The gentlemen criticized Hua Yuan for not being a loyal subject.

## 50

In the tenth year of Duke Gong's reign, Hua Yuan maintained good relations with General Zi Zhong of Chu and also with General Luan Shu of Jin, forming alliances with both Jin and Chu. In the thirteenth year, Duke Gong died. Hua Yuan served as You Shi, and Yu Shi served as Zuoshi. Si Ma Tanshan attacked and killed Crown Prince Fei, intending to kill Hua Yuan. Hua Yuan fled to Jin; Yu Shi stopped him, but only after reaching the river did he return, then executed Tanshan. They then enthroned the youngest son of Duke Gong, Cheng, who became known as Duke Ping.

## 51

In the third year of Duke Ping's reign, King Gong of Chu captured Pengcheng from Song and granted it to Yu Shi, the Zuoshi of Song. In the fourth year, the feudal lords jointly executed Yu Shi and returned Pengcheng to Song. In the thirty-fifth year, Prince Wei of Chu assassinated his king and declared himself ruler, becoming King Ling. In the forty-fourth year, Duke Ping died; his son Yuan Gong Zuo was enthroned as ruler.

## 52

In the third year of Duke Yuan's reign, Prince Qi Ji of Chu assassinated King Ling and declared himself king as King Ping. In the eighth year, a fire occurred in Song. In the tenth year, Duke Yuan was named Wu Xin and fraudulently killed several princes of the royal family; the ministers Hua and Xiang clans rebelled. The crown prince Jian of King Ping of Chu fled to Song, but upon witnessing the ongoing conflicts among the Hua clan, he left and went to Zheng. In the fifteenth year, Duke Yuan provided shelter for Duke Zhao of Lu who was evading the Ji clan and living outside Lu. He sought to help him return to Lu, but died on the journey; his son Duke Jing Toman succeeded him as ruler.

## 53

In the sixteenth year of Duke Jing's reign, Yang Hu from Lu fled to Song but later left again. In the twenty-fifth year, Confucius passed through Song; General Huantui of Song disliked him and wanted to kill Confucius. Confucius left in disguise. In the thirtieth year, Cao betrayed Song and then also betrayed Jin; Song attacked Cao. Jin did not come to its aid, so Cao was annihilated and came under Song's control. In the thirty-sixth year, Tian Chang of Qi assassinated Duke Jian.

## 54

In the thirty-seventh year, King Hui of Chu destroyed Chen. Mars remained in conjunction with the Heart constellation. The Heart constellation is the celestial region corresponding to Song. Duke Jing was worried about it. The astrologer Ziw ei said, "It can be transferred to the Minister of State." Duke Jing said, "The minister is my right hand and pillar." He said, "It can be transferred to the people." Duke Jing replied, "A ruler depends on his people." He said, "It can be transferred to a year's harvest." Duke Jing said: "If the harvest fails and the people suffer hardship, who would I be ruling as king!" Ziwei said, "Heaven is high but listens to the lowly." "The king has spoken three times with words of concern for his people; Mars should move accordingly." Thus they observed it, and indeed, Mars moved by three degrees.

## 55

In the sixty-fourth year, Duke Jing died. Prince Te of Song attacked and killed the crown prince to declare himself ruler; he became known as Duke Zhao. Duke Zhao was a great-grandson of Duke Yuan through a junior branch. Duke Zhao's father was Gongsun Jiu; Jiu's father was Prince Dan Qin, who was the youngest son of Duke Yuan. Duke Jing had killed Duke Zhao's father, Jiu; thus, Duke Zhao harbored resentment and assassinated the crown prince to seize power for himself.

## 56

In the forty-seventh year of Duke Zhao's reign, he died; his son Gouyou was enthroned as Duke Dao. In the eighth year of Duke Dao's reign, he died and was succeeded by his son Duke Xi Tian. In the twenty-third year of Duke Xi Tian's reign, he died, and his son Bi Bing was enthroned as King Bi. In the third year of King Bi's reign, he died; his successor was Ti Cheng. In the forty-first year of Ti Cheng's reign, Ti Cheng's younger brother Yan launched an attack and overthrew him. Ti Cheng was defeated and fled to Qi; Yan declared himself ruler of Song.

## 57

In the eleventh year of King Yan's reign, he proclaimed himself king. To the east, he defeated Qi and captured five cities; to the south, he defeated Chu and seized 300 li of territory; lords In the west, he defeated the Wei army, thus becoming an opposing state to Qi and Wei. They filled leather bags with blood, hung them up, and shot at them; this was called "Shooting the Sky." He indulged in wine and women. Whenever ministers advised him, he shot them. Thus the feudal lords all called it "Jie Song." "Cui will repeat what Zhou did, and cannot go unpunished." They informed Qi to attack Song. King Yan ruled for forty-seven years, when King Min of Qi, together with Wei and Chu, attacked Song. They killed King Yan and thus destroyed Song, dividing its territory among the three states.

## 58

Grand Historian said: Confucius remarked, "Wei Zi left the court; Ji Zi became a slave; Bi Gan advised and was killed. Yin had three virtuous men." The Spring and Autumn Annals criticized the chaos in Song, which began when Duke Xuan deposed the crown prince and enthroned his younger brother; for ten generations, the state remained unsettled. During the reign of Duke Xiang, he cultivated virtue and righteousness, aspiring to become a leader among feudal lords. His minister Zheng Kaofu praised him for this, so he traced back to Qi, Tang, and Gaozong, the reasons for Yin's rise, composing the "Shang Songs." After Duke Xiang was defeated at Hong, some gentlemen still praised him; this reflected sorrow over the lack of rites and righteousness in Central China. They commended him for his courteous and yielding conduct, as seen in Song Xiang's behavior.
