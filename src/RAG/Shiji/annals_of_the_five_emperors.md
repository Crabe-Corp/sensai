# Annals of the Five Emperors

## 1

The Yellow Emperor was the son of Shaodian, surnamed Gongsun, and his given name was Xuan Yuan. He was born with divine wisdom; as a weak child, he could already speak; in his youth, he was well-behaved and orderly; when grown, he was earnest and perceptive; and upon reaching adulthood, he became wise and intelligent.

## 2

In the time of Xuan Yuan, the Shennong clan had declined in power through successive generations. The feudal lords attacked and fought among themselves, oppressing the common people, yet the Shennong clan was unable to bring them under control. Therefore, Xuan Yuan began to train in the use of weapons and waged campaigns against those who did not pay tribute. The feudal lords all came to submit and follow him. Among them, Chi You was the most violent, and no one could defeat him. The Yan Emperor wished to invade and dominate the feudal lords, so all the feudal lords turned to Xuan Yuan for allegiance. Xuan Yuan then cultivated virtue and strengthened his military forces, regulated the Five Elements, cultivated the Five Crops, pacified the people, measured the four directions, trained troops of bears, pines, leopards, tigers, and other fierce beasts, and fought against the Yan Emperor in the plains of Banquan. After three battles, he finally achieved his goal. Chi You stirred up rebellion and did not obey the emperor's command. Therefore, the Yellow Emperor raised an army from among the feudal lords and fought Chi You in the plains of Zhuolu, eventually capturing and killing him. Thereafter, all the feudal lords honored Xuan Yuan as Son of Heaven, succeeding the Shennong clan; he was thus known as the Yellow Emperor. Whenever there were regions under heaven that did not submit, the Yellow Emperor would campaign against them. After pacifying a region, he would move on. He cleared mountain paths and opened roads, never settling in one place for long.

## 3

He traveled eastward to the sea, climbed Wan Shan, and reached Mount Tai. To the west he went as far as Kondong, ascending Jitou Mountain. To the south he reached the Yangtze River, climbing Xiong and Xiang Mountains. In the north, he drove back the Hunyuzhu, met with other feudal lords at Fushan to unify their symbols of authority, and established a settlement in the lowlands near Zhuolu. He moved about without fixed residence, using his military forces as encampment and protection. All official titles were named after clouds; they were called the Cloud Officials. lords He appointed Left and Right Grand Supervisors to oversee all the nations. With all the nations at peace, he performed numerous sacrifices and ceremonies of fengshan (sacrifices on Mount Tai) to honor gods, spirits, mountains, and rivers. He obtained a precious tripod and welcomed the sun while using divination rods for calculations. He appointed Fenghou, Limu, Changxian, and Dahan to govern the people. They followed the order of heaven and earth, interpreted omens from both visible and hidden forces, explained theories about life and death, and addressed dangers to existence and destruction. At that time, they cultivated various grains and plants, civilized the birds, beasts, insects, and moths through gentle influence, comprehensively studied the sun, moon, stars, water waves, soil, stone, metal, and jade, and diligently used their minds, strength, ears, and eyes to regulate the use of fire, water, materials, and resources. Possessing the virtue associated with the earth element, he was thus called the Yellow Emperor.

## 4

The Yellow Emperor had twenty-five sons; fourteen of them received surnames.

## 5

The Yellow Emperor lived on Xuan Yuan Hill and married a woman from the Xi Ling clan, who was named Leizu. She became his principal wife and bore him two sons; both of their descendants later ruled over all under heaven: one son was named Xuanyao, also known as Qingyang. Qingyang descended to live by the Jiang River; The other son was Changyi, who descended to settle near Ruo Shui. Changyi married a woman from the Shushan clan named Changpu, who bore him Gao Yang, who possessed great virtue and wisdom. After the Yellow Emperor died, he was buried on Qiaoshan Mountain. His grandson, Gao Yang, son of Changyi, ascended the throne; this was Emperor Zhuanxu.

## 6

Emperor Zhuanxu Gao Yang was the grandson of the Yellow Emperor and the son of Changyi. He was calm and profound, possessing wisdom; he was perceptive and understanding of affairs; He cultivated resources to make use of the land, followed the seasons to emulate heaven, relied on gods and spirits to establish righteousness, regulated vital energies for moral instruction, and maintained sincerity in sacrifices. To the north he reached Youling, to the south as far as Jiaozhi, to the west up to Liusha, and to the east as far as Panmu. All things in motion or stillness, great or small deities, wherever the sun and moon shone—none failed to submit to his authority.

## 7

Emperor Zhuanxu had a son named Qiongchan. After Emperor Zhuanxu died, Gao Xin, the grandson of Xuanyao, ascended the throne and was known as Emperor Ku.

## 8

Emperor Ku Gao Xin was the great-grandson of the Yellow Emperor. Gao Xin's father was Jiao Ji; Jiao Ji's father was Xuanyao; Xuanyao's father was the Yellow Emperor. From Xuanyao and Jiao Ji, neither of whom held the throne, it passed down to Gao Xin, who became emperor. Gao Xin was a nephew by marriage or cousin of Emperor Zhuanxu, related through clan ties.

## 9

From birth Gao Xin possessed divine wisdom and declared his own name. He universally bestowed kindness upon all things, not seeking benefit for himself. He was wise enough to understand distant matters and perceptive enough to discern subtle details. He followed the principles of heaven, understanding what urgently concerned the people. He was kind yet authoritative, generous yet trustworthy; by cultivating his own virtue, he gained the submission of all under heaven. He took resources from the land but used them with restraint, nurtured and instructed the people while teaching them for their benefit, observed the movements of the sun and moon and welcomed or sent them off accordingly, understood gods and spirits and revered them in his duties. His appearance was dignified and composed; his virtue was lofty and resolute. His actions followed the proper timing, and his attire conformed to the standards of a gentleman. Emperor Ku governed with impartiality and fairness, reaching all under heaven; wherever the sun and moon shined or where wind and rain reached, none failed to submit.

## 10

Emperor Ku married a woman from the Chenfeng clan, who bore him Fangxun. He also married a woman from the Juzi clan, who gave birth to Zhi. After Emperor Ku died, Zhi succeeded him as ruler. When Emperor Zhi ascended the throne, he proved unworthy (died), and his younger brother Fangxun took over; this was Emperor Yao.

## 11

Emperor Yao was named Fangxun. His benevolence was as vast as heaven, and his wisdom was as profound as the gods. Approaching him felt like basking in sunlight; gazing at him seemed like looking upon a cloud. He was wealthy but not arrogant, noble yet never indulgent. He wore yellow robes with pure patterns and rode in a red carriage drawn by white horses. He was able to clearly demonstrate virtuous conduct, thus uniting his nine clans. Once the nine clans were harmonious, he then organized and clarified the duties of the common people. The people became clear in their roles and virtuous; thus, all nations under heaven were united in harmony.

## 12

He then appointed Xi He to revere and follow the will of the vast heavens, to calculate the movements of the sun, moon, and stars, and to respectfully teach the people the proper seasons. He assigned Xi Zhong to reside at Yuyi, known as Yanggu. They respectfully observed the rising sun and established the schedule for spring farming activities in the east. When the sun reached its zenith, and the star Niǎo was visible, this marked the middle of spring. The people began to distinguish themselves; birds and beasts showed subtle signs of reproduction. He further assigned Xi Shu to reside in Nanjiao. They established the schedule for southern farming activities and respectfully carried out their duties. When the days were longest, and the star Huo was visible, this marked midsummer. The people followed their natural rhythms; birds and beasts began to shed or change their feathers and fur. He further assigned He Zhong to reside in the western land, known as Meigu. They respectfully observed the setting sun and established schedules for autumn harvest activities in the west. At midnight, when the star Xu was visible, this marked mid-autumn. The people lived in peace and ease; birds and beasts grew their new feathers and fur. He further assigned He Shu; to reside in the north, known as Youdu. They established schedules for winter activities and prepared to put things into storage. When the days were shortest, and the star Mao was visible, this marked midwinter. The people felt warmth; birds and beasts grew soft downy fur for insulation. A year consisted of 366 days, with an intercalary month added to regulate the four seasons. He earnestly regulated all officials, and thus many great achievements were accomplished.

## 13

Yao said: "Who can succeed in continuing these matters?" Fang Qi replied, "Your son Dan Zhu is open-minded and wise." Yao said: "Ah! Dan Zhu is stubborn and violent; he cannot be relied upon." Yao then asked again, "Who else might be suitable?" Huandou replied: "Gonggong has gathered many accomplishments and can be used." Yao said, "Gonggong speaks well, but his conduct is deviant; he appears respectful yet defies the heavens. He cannot be trusted." Yao said again, "Alas! Four Mountains, the surging floods have reached the heavens, overwhelming hills and dunes; the people below are in great distress. Is there anyone who can be entrusted to control this?" They all said that Gun could do it. Yao said, "Gun disobeys commands and harms his clan; he is not acceptable." The Four Mountains said, "That's unusual. Let us try him; if he proves unfit, we can stop using him." Thus, Yao listened to the suggestion of the Four Mountains and appointed Gun. After nine years, Gun's efforts failed to achieve results.

## 14

Yao said: "Alas! Four Mountains, I have held the throne for seventy years. Are you able to accept this mandate and assume my position?" The Four Mountains replied: "Our virtue is shallow; we are unworthy of the imperial position." Yao said, "Bring forward all my close relatives and distant kin who remain hidden or secluded." Everyone advised Yao, saying: "There is a worthy man among the people named Yu Shun." Yao said, "Yes, I have heard of him. What is he like?" The Four Mountains said: "He is the son of a blind man. His father is stubborn, his mother cunning, and his younger brother arrogant; yet he maintains harmony through filial piety, diligently managing affairs without falling into corruption." Yao said: "I shall test him." Thus, Yao married his two daughters to him and observed his virtue through their eyes. Shun instructed the two princesses at Gui Rui, treating them with proper marital etiquette as a husband should.

## 15

Yao was pleased and thus appointed Shun to carefully harmonize the Five Classics; the Five Classics were able to follow his guidance. He then entered all the hundred officials, and the hundred officials followed proper order and sequence. Shun received guests at the four gates; the four gates were dignified and orderly, and all feudal lords and distant visitors showed respect. Yao sent Shun into forests, mountains, rivers, and marshes; even in violent winds, thunder, and rain, Shun did not lose his way. Yao regarded him as a sage and summoned Shun, saying: "For three years now, your plans have been thorough and your words have proven effective. You may ascend the imperial throne." Shun declined, saying that his virtue was not sufficient. On the first day of the first month, Shun accepted the final authority at Wen Zu. Wen Zu was the great ancestral temple of Yao.

## 16

At this time, Emperor Yao grew old and ordered Shun to assume the duties of Son of Heaven in order to observe heavenly mandate. Shun then used the Xuanji Yuheng (a celestial instrument) to regulate the seven official duties. He thus offered sacrifices to Heaven, performed solemn rites for the Six Ancestries, observed rituals at mountains and rivers, and distinguished among the various deities. He received the five symbols of authority, selected a favorable month and day, met with the Four Mountains and regional governors, and distributed the symbols. In the second month of the year, Shun conducted an eastern inspection tour, reaching Mount Tai (Dai Zong), where he offered sacrifices by burning fragrant wood and observed ritual ranks at mountains and rivers. He then met with the chieftains of the eastern regions, aligning their calendars and days to harmonize laws, measurements, and weights. He revised the Five Rites, using five jade objects, three silk offerings, two live animals, and one dead animal as sacrificial tokens, just like the Five Instruments; only after completing these did he return. In May, Shun conducted a southern inspection tour; In August, he conducted a western inspection tour; In the eleventh month, he conducted a northern inspection tour: all were carried out as before. Upon returning, he arrived at the ancestral temple of his grandfather and father, where he performed rites using an entire bull as sacrifice. He conducted inspection tours once every five years, during which feudal lords from all directions paid homage four times a year. He informed them of his policies through words, tested their abilities by their achievements, and granted official ranks and ceremonial carriages according to their merits. He established twelve provinces from the beginning and regulated rivers. He set exemplary punishments based on standard laws, used exile as a substitute for the five corporal punishments, applied whippings as punishment in official matters, caning as educational discipline, and fines as redemption penalties. Those who committed unintentional offenses due to misfortune or mistakes were pardoned; Those who persisted in their crimes and became habitual thieves received punishment. How reverent, how reverent! Only through just punishments can peace be achieved!

## 17

Huan Dou recommended Gonggong to Yao, but Yao said he was unacceptable and instead tested him as an official in charge of engineering. Indeed, Gonggong proved licentious and deviant. The Four Mountains recommended Gun to control the great floods, but Yao considered him unfit. The Four Mountains strongly requested a trial, and when he was tested, he achieved no success; thus, the common people found it inconvenient. The San Miao tribes in the regions of Jianghuai and Jingzhou repeatedly caused disturbances. Thus, Shun returned and reported to the Emperor, requesting that Gonggong be exiled to You Ling in order to reform the northern Di people; Huan Dou was banished to Chong Shan to reform the southern Yi people; The San Miao were relocated to San Wei in order to reform the western Rong tribes; Gun was executed at Yu Shan to reform the eastern Yi people: with these four punishments, all under heaven submitted.

## 18

Yao reigned for seventy years before finding Shun; twenty years later, he grew old and ordered Shun to act as Son of Heaven, recommending him to Heaven. Emperor Yao abdicated the throne for a total of twenty-eight years before his death. The people mourned deeply, as if they had lost their parents. For three years, no music was played in all directions, to commemorate Yao. Yao knew that his son Dan Zhu lacked virtue and was not worthy of being entrusted with the empire; therefore, he temporarily transferred power to Shun. Transferring authority to Shun benefited all under heaven but harmed Dan Zhu; If he had transferred it to Dan Zhu, the world would have suffered while Dan Zhu alone gained benefit. Yao said: "I will never allow the suffering of all under heaven for the sake of one person's gain," and so he finally entrusted Shun with the empire. After Yao died, following a three-year mourning period, Shun yielded to Dan Zhu and withdrew to the south of the Nan He River. Feudal lords who came to pay homage did not go to Dan Zhu but went to Shun; those with legal disputes did not seek justice from Dan Zhu but from Shun; and the people sang praises of Shun rather than of Dan Zhu. Shun said, "This is Heaven's will," and thus he finally assumed the Son of Heaven position in China. He was known as Emperor Shun.

## 19

Yu Shun was a surname and given name, his name being Zhong Hua. Zhong Hua's father was Gusu, whose father was Qiaoniu; Qiaoniu's father was Jiuwang, whose father was Jingkang; Jingkang's father was Qiongchan, whose father was Emperor Zhuanxu. Zhuanxu's father was Changyi: this lineage spans seven generations to Shun. From Qiongchan down to Emperor Shun, all were obscure and lived as commoners.

## 20

Shun's father Gusu was blind, and his mother died; Gusu remarried and had a son named Xiang, who was arrogant. Gusu loved his second wife and her son more than Shun and often wanted to kill him; thus, Shun fled and avoided them; Whenever he committed minor faults, he would accept punishment. He served his father, stepmother, and brother with increasing diligence and care, never showing any resentment.

## 21

Shun was a native of Ji Zhou. Shun tilled the fields at Li Shan, fished in Lei Ze, made pottery by the banks of He Bin, crafted tools and utensils at Shou Qiu, and traded goods in Fu Xia. Shun's father Gusu was stubborn, his stepmother cunning, and his brother Xiang arrogant; all of them wanted to kill Shun. Despite this, Shun remained filial and never failed in the duties of a son, showing respect and care toward his father and brother with virtue and kindness. Whenever they tried to kill him, he could not be found; But when they searched for him, he was always nearby.

## 22

At the age of twenty, Shun became renowned for his filial piety. By thirty, Emperor Yao asked who could be entrusted with important duties; the Four Mountains all recommended Yu Shun and said he was suitable. Thus, Yao married his two daughters off to Shun in order to observe his conduct within the household and sent his nine sons to live with him to examine his behavior outside. While living at Gui Rui, Shun behaved even more carefully in managing family affairs within the home. Yao's two daughters did not dare to act with arrogance toward their husband Shun or his relatives, and they behaved in a most exemplary manner as wives. The nine sons of Yao became even more sincere and devoted. When Shun tilled the fields on Li Shan, the people there all yielded their land boundaries to him; When he fished in Lei Ze, the local fishermen gave up their best spots for him; When he made pottery by He Bin, all the wares produced there were of fine quality and free from defects. Within one year, the place where he lived became a settlement; within two years, it became a town; and within three years, a city. Yao then bestowed fine silk robes on Shun, gave him a zither, built granaries for him, and presented him with cattle and sheep. Gusu still wanted to kill Shun again and ordered him to climb onto the granary roof for repairs, then set a fire from below to burn it down. Shun protected himself with two wide-brimmed hats and slid down the side of the granary, escaping unharmed. Later, Gusu again ordered Shun to dig a well; instead, Shun secretly dug an escape tunnel on the side while working. After Shun had descended deep into the well, Gusu and Xiang threw earth down to fill it in; however, Shun escaped through his hidden tunnel. Gusu and Xiang were delighted, believing that Shun had died. Xiang said: "The original plan was mine." Xiang divided the property with his parents and declared, "Shun's two wives from Yao and the zither belong to me. I shall take them." The cattle, sheep, and granaries will go to my parents." Xiang then took up residence in Shun's house and played his zither. Shun went to visit him. Xiang was startled and uneasy, saying: "I have been thinking of Shun so much that I feel deeply distressed!" Shun said: "Indeed! You are fortunate to be alive!" Thereafter, Shun continued to serve Gusu and love his younger brother even more carefully. At this time, Yao tested Shun by assigning him the Five Constants and all government offices; in each case, they were well managed.

## 23

In the past, Gao Yang Shi had eight talented sons, and the world benefited from them; they were called the "Ba Kai." Gao Xin Shi also had eight talented sons, who were known in the world as the "Ba Yuan." These sixteen clans passed down their virtues through generations and never lost their reputations. By the time of Yao, Yao had not yet appointed them to official positions. Shun promoted the Ba Kai and assigned them to oversee the land, enabling them to manage all affairs; nothing was out of order. He also promoted the Ba Yuan and assigned them to spread the Five Teachings in all directions: fathers should be righteous, mothers kind, elder brothers friendly, younger brothers respectful, and children filial. This brought internal harmony and external success.

## 24

In the past, Emperor Hong Shi had an unprincipled son who concealed righteousness and harbored malice, delighting in committing violent evils; he was called Hun Dun by all under heaven. Shaohao Shi also had an unprincipled son, who destroyed trust and despised loyalty, promoting evil words; he was called Qiong Qi by all under heaven. Zhuanxu Shi had an unprincipled and incorrigible son, who could not be taught or disciplined and did not understand proper speech; he was called Tao Wu by all under heaven. These three clans were a source of concern for the world. By the time of Yao, he had not yet been able to remove them. Jinyun Shi had an unprincipled child, greedy for food and drink, and addicted to wealth and bribes; he was called Tao Tie by all under heaven. All under heaven despised him, comparing him with the Three Evils. Shun welcomed people at the four gates, and then banished the Four Evils to the distant frontiers, using them to guard against chimerical evils. Thus, the four gates were opened, signifying that no evil men remained within.

## 25

Shun entered Da Lu, and even amidst fierce winds, thunder, and rain, he did not become lost; thus, Yao realized that Shun was worthy of being entrusted with the empire. As Yao grew old, he appointed Shun to act as emperor and oversee state affairs, including conducting imperial tours. Shun was selected for office and entrusted with responsibilities for twenty years before Yao made him regent. Eight years after becoming regent, Yao died. After completing a three-year mourning period, Shun yielded the throne to Dan Zhu, but all under heaven turned to Shun instead. Yu, Gao Yao, Qi, Hou Ji, Bo Yi, Kui, Long, Chui, Yi, and Peng Zu had all been selected for office since the time of Yao but had not yet received specific duties. Thus, Shun went to Wen Zu and consulted the Four Yue. He opened the four gates and clearly established channels for information from all directions. He appointed twelve regional governors to discuss the virtues of the emperor, promoted great virtue, distanced himself from flatterers, and thereby the southern and northern barbarians submitted willingly. Shun said to the Four Yue: "Is there anyone who can diligently perform virtuous deeds and continue Yao's achievements, so that they may hold office and assist in governance?" They all said: "Bo Yu is the Minister of Works; he can honor the emperor's achievements." Shun said, "Ah, indeed! Yu, you are to manage the waters and earth; be diligent in this task." Yu bowed deeply and respectfully declined the appointment, yielding it to Ji, Qi, and Gao Yao. Shun said: "Very well, go ahead." Shun said: "Qi, the people are beginning to suffer from hunger; you, as Hou Ji, must sow and cultivate all kinds of grains." Shun said: "Qi, the people do not show affection for one another and the Five Relationships are not harmonious. You shall be Minister of Instruction, and you must diligently teach the Five Teachings with patience and tolerance." Shun said: "Gao Yao, the southern and northern barbarians are corrupting the Chinese, and bandits and criminals are violating laws. You shall serve as Minister of Justice; the Five Punishments must be applied fairly, with three levels of judgment for each case; the Five Exiles must follow established standards, and there should be three stages in determining their place of exile: only those who are clearly capable and trustworthy may decide these matters." Shun said: "Who can assist me with the artisans?" They all replied that Chui was suitable. Thus, he appointed Chui as Gong Gong. Shun said: "Who can assist me in managing the plants and animals above and below?" They all replied that Yi was suitable. Thus, he appointed Yi as his minister of the imperial hunting grounds (Zhen Yu). Yi bowed deeply and respectfully yielded to other ministers, Zhu Hu and Xiong Pi. Shun said: "Go ahead then; you are well-suited for the task." Thus, Zhu Hu and Xiongpi were appointed as his assistants. Shun said: "Ah! Four Yue, is there anyone who can oversee the three rites of my court?" They all said that Bo Yi was suitable. Shun said: "Ah! Bo Yi, I appoint you as Minister of Rites. Be respectful day and night; be upright and always maintain purity and tranquility." Bo Yi yielded the position to Kui and Long. Shun said: "Indeed. Appoint Kui as Minister of Music, to teach the young. Be upright yet gentle, broad-minded yet respectful, firm but not cruel, simple yet not arrogant; poetry conveys meaning, songs prolong words, tones follow the melody, pitch harmonizes with sound. The eight kinds of music must be in harmony; none should dominate or disrupt order, so that both gods and humans may find peace." Kui said: "Ah! I shall strike the stones and beat them, and all beasts will follow in dance." Shun said: "Long, I fear that slander and deceit will destroy truth and alarm my people. I appoint you as Grand Secretary to convey my commands day and night with only trustworthiness in mind." Shun said: "Ah! You twenty-two of you, be reverent. Only by doing so can you assist in the affairs of Heaven." Every three years, their performance was reviewed; after three reviews, those who were unfit were demoted and those who excelled were promoted. Thus, both near and distant officials accomplished great feats. The northern San Miao were divided.

## 26

These twenty-two individuals all fulfilled their duties: Gao Yao served as Minister of Justice, who was fair and impartial; the people submitted to him and each received what they truly deserved; Bo Yi presided over rites, and both superiors and inferiors showed mutual respect and yielded to one another; Chui was in charge of the Ministry of Works, and all artisans achieved their tasks effectively; Yi oversaw the imperial hunting grounds, and mountains and marshes were brought under cultivation; Qi was in charge of agriculture, and all kinds of grains flourished seasonally; Qi presided over the Ministry of Instruction, and the people became affectionate and harmonious; Long was in charge of foreign guests, and distant peoples came to pay homage; The twelve regional governors carried out their duties, and the nine provinces dared not defy them; Only Yu's achievements were the greatest. He traversed nine mountain ranges, connected nine marshes, dredged nine rivers, and stabilized the nine provinces. Each region sent tribute according to its duties, without failing in their proper roles. The territory extended five thousand li in all directions, reaching as far as the frontier regions. To the south, he pacified Jiaozhi and Beifa; to the west, the Rong people, Xizhi, Qiusou, Di, and Qiang; to the north, the Shanrong, Fa, and Xishen; to the east, Chang and Niao Yi. Within all four seas, everyone revered Emperor Shun's achievements. At this time, Yu composed the Nine Zhaos music to summon rare creatures; phoenixes came and circled in flight. The virtue of all under heaven began with Emperor Yu (Shun).

## 27

At twenty, Shun was known for filial piety; at thirty, Yao selected him; at fifty, he acted as emperor; at fifty-eight, Yao died; and at sixty-one, Shun succeeded Yao to the imperial throne. After reigning for thirty-nine years, he embarked on a southern inspection tour and died in the wilds of Cangwu. He was buried at Jiuyi Mountains in Jiangnan, which is known as Lingling. When Shun assumed the imperial throne, he carried the emperor's flag and went to pay respects to his father Gusu. He was reverent and cautious, behaving as a son should. He enfeoffed his younger brother Xiang as a feudal lord. Shun's son Shangjun was also unworthy, so Shun recommended Yu to Heaven in advance. He died seventeen years later. After completing a three-year mourning period for Shun, Yu also yielded the throne to Shun's son, just as Shun had once yielded it to Yao's son. When feudal lords all submitted to him, Yu then ascended the imperial throne. Yao's son Dan Zhu and Shun's son Shang Jun both held territories, to honor their ancestors' sacrifices. They wore the appropriate ceremonial robes and observed rites and music in accordance with tradition. They were received as guests by the Son of Heaven, who did not make them his subjects, to show that he dared not claim exclusive authority.

## 28

From Huangdi to Shun and Yu, they all shared the same surname but used different state titles in order to highlight their virtuous achievements. Therefore, Huangdi was known as You Xiong; Emperor Zhuanxu as Gao Yang; Emperor Ku as Gao Xin; Emperor Yao as Tao Tang; and Emperor Shun as You Yu. Emperor Yu became the ruler of Xia, changed his surname to Si Shi. Qi was the ancestor of Shang and took the surname Zi Shi. Qi was the progenitor of Zhou, taking the surname Ji Shi.

## 29

Grand Historian said: Many scholars praise the Five Emperors; this is a long-standing tradition. Yet the Book of Documents records only from Yao onward; and among the hundred schools of thought, those who speak of Huangdi present texts that are not elegant or refined, making it difficult for learned gentlemen to discuss them. Zi Ziyu's inquiry about the virtues of the Five Emperors and their lineage surnames, as transmitted by Confucius, was not passed down by some scholars. I once traveled west to Kongtong, north past Zhuolu, eastward toward the sea, and south along the rivers Changjiang and Huaihe. Wherever I went, elders often pointed out places associated with Huangdi, Yao, and Shun; their customs and teachings were indeed different, but in general, those accounts that did not deviate from ancient texts are most likely accurate. I have studied the Spring and Autumn Annals and Guoyu, which clearly elaborate on the virtues of the Five Emperours and their lineage surnames. Although I did not deeply investigate them at first, what they reveal is not unfounded. The records are incomplete in places; their lost accounts occasionally appear in other sources. Without a strong desire to study and deep reflection, one cannot truly grasp the meaning; it is difficult for those with shallow knowledge and limited experience to understand. I have compiled and arranged these accounts, selecting the most refined statements, hence I record them as the first chapter of this historical chronicle.
