# Wei Ce


## Wei 1


### Zhibo Demands Land from Wei Huanzi

Zhibo asked Wei Huanzi for territory, and Wei Huanzi did not give it to him. Ren Zhang said, "Why didn't you give it to him?" Huanzi said, "He asked for territory without reason, so I did not give it." Ren Zhang said, "To ask for territory without cause will surely alarm neighboring states; excessive desires are insatiable, and all under heaven will certainly be afraid." If you give him territory, Zhibo will surely become arrogant. When he is arrogant and underestimates his enemies, neighboring states, fearing him, will grow closer to each other. With allied forces confronting a country that is contemptuous of its enemies, the days of the Zhi clan will not be long! The "Zhou Shu" says: "If one wishes to defeat someone, one must first assist them; if one wishes to take something from someone, one must first give it to them." You would be better off giving it to him in order to make Zhibo arrogant. Why should you release the opportunity to plan against the Zhi clan with all under heaven, and instead take our state as a hostage for the Zhi clan?" The lord said, "Good." So he gave him one fiefdom of ten thousand households. Zhibo was very pleased. He then asked Zhao for the territories of Cai and Gaoliang, but Zhao did not give them up, so he besieged Jinyang. Wan and Wei rebelled from the outside, while the Zhao clan responded from within, and thus the Zhi clan perished.


### Han and Zhao Confront Each Other

Han and Zhao confronted each other. Han requested troops from Wei, saying, "I wish to borrow an army to attack Zhao." Wei Wenhou said, "I am a brother of Zhao; I dare not comply." Zhao also requested troops to attack Han. Wenhou said, "I am a brother of Han; I dare not comply." The two states could not obtain the troops and became angry, then turned back. After this, they realized that Wenhou was impartial toward them, so both paid homage to Wei.


### Yue Yang as a General of Wei Attacks Zhongshan

Yue Yang was a general of Wei and attacked Zhongshan. His son was in Zhongshan, and the ruler of Zhongshan boiled his son and sent him the broth. Yue Yang sat under a tent and drank it, finishing an entire cup. Wen Hou said to Dushi Zan: "Yue Yang, for the sake of my foreign state, ate his son's flesh." Zan replied: "If he could still eat his son's flesh, who would not be eaten!" After Yue Yang had subdued Zhongshan, Wen Hou rewarded his achievements but doubted his intentions.


### Ximen Bao as Magistrate of Ye

Ximen Bao was the magistrate of Ye and paid his respects to Wei Wenhoushou. Wenhou said, "Go ahead, you will accomplish your achievements and establish your reputation." Ximen Bao asked, "I dare to ask if there is a method for achieving success and establishing one's reputation?" Wenhou said, "Yes." "The elders of the local community who are first to sit, when you arrive, inquire about the virtuous and talented people there and treat them as your teachers. Seek out those who like to conceal others' virtues while highlighting their ugliness, and verify this through careful examination." "Many things may appear similar but are not the same. When young, yu you resembles grain; a black ox's yellow color resembles that of a tiger; white bones resemble an elephant; and a warrior's armor resembles jade. These are all cases where appearances are deceptive."


### Marquis Wenhou and a Forester Make an Appointment for a Hunt

The Marquis Wenhou made an appointment with a forester for a hunting expedition. On that day, they drank wine and were having fun when it rained in the sky. Marquis Wenhou was about to leave, and his attendants said, "Today we are drinking wine and having fun, and it is also raining. Where will you go, my lord?" Marquis Wenhou said, "I made an appointment with the forester for a hunt. Although it is enjoyable here, how could I possibly not keep my appointment?" He went there and personally called off the hunting expedition. Wei began to grow strong from this point on.


### Prince Wei Wenhou and Tian Zifang Drinking Wine While Praising Music

Prince Wei Wenhou drank wine with Tian Zifang and praised music. Wenhou said, "The sound of the zhong bells is not in harmony; the left side is higher." Tian Zifang smiled. Wenhou asked, "Why are you laughing?" Zifang said, "I have heard that when the ruler is wise, the music officials will not be unwise, and thus the musical tones will be correct. Now that you are concerned about the sounds, I fear that you may be deaf to the officials." Wenhou said, "You speak well. I respectfully accept your instruction."


### Duke Wu of Wei and His Ministers Travel Along the Western River

The Duke Wu of Wei traveled along the western river with his ministers, and remarked: "Is not the danger of rivers and mountains indeed trustworthy and solid?" Wang Zhong served King Wu, saying: "This is why the State of Jin was strong." "If it is well maintained, then the undertaking of a hegemon or emperor will be complete." Wu Qi replied: "My lord's words are the way that endangers the state; and you, my lord, support them further. This is dangerous." Duke Wu was angry and said: "Do your words have a rationale?"

Wu Qi replied: "The danger of rivers and mountains is not sufficient to guarantee safety; the undertaking of hegemons or emperors does not arise from this." In the past, when the Sanmiao people lived there, they had to their left the waves of Pengli and to their right the waters of Dongting Lake. Wenshan was to their south, and Hengshan to their north. Relying on this danger, they governed poorly, and Yu exiled them. The state of Xia Jie had to its left the shade of Tianmen and to its right the sunny side of Tianxi. Lu and Yao were to his north, while Yi and Luo flowed from his south. Possessing this danger, yet governing poorly, Tang attacked him. The state of Yin Zhou had Mengmen to its left and Zhangfu to its right, with the Yellow River in front and mountains behind. Possessing this danger as well, yet governing poorly, King Wu attacked him. Moreover, Your Majesty personally led your ministers and captured the surrendered city. The walls were not low, nor was its population small; yet it could be annexed because of poor governance. From this we can see that even the most perilous terrain is insufficient for hegemony or imperial rule!"

Duke Wu said: "Well said." Today I have heard the words of a sage! The administration of the western river shall be entrusted entirely to you."


### Duke Wei Gong Shucao as a General of the State of Wei

Duke Wei Gong Shucao was a general of the state of Wei and fought against Han and Zhao at Beipu, capturing Yue Zuo. King Wei was pleased and went to meet him outside the capital, granting him a salary of one million dou without having to till the land. Gong Shucao turned and ran away, bowing twice in farewell and saying: "To make the soldiers not collapse, to be upright without leaning on others, and to remain steadfast even when harassed or selected for special duties—this was Wu Qi's legacy. I am unable to do that." To assess the terrain and its dangers beforehand, to determine preparations for advantages and disadvantages, and to ensure that all soldiers of the three armies remain unconfused—this was the achievement of Ba Ning and Cuan Xiang. To establish rewards and punishments beforehand, so that the people clearly believe in them afterward—this is Your Majesty's clear legal system. To strike when the enemy can be attacked and not wait until exhausted—that is my duty as a subject. If Your Majesty specially rewards me for my right hand's endurance, why is that? "What strength or ability do I possess?" The king said, "Well spoken." Thus he searched for descendants of Wu Qi and granted them 200,000 mu of farmland. Ba Ning and Cuan Xiang each received ten thousand mu of farmland.

The king said, "Is Gong Shu not a man of integrity?" Having already helped me defeat a powerful enemy, and yet not forgetting the descendants of virtuous men or concealing the achievements of capable individuals—how could Gong Shu be without reward?" Therefore, he was given an additional 400,000 more mu of farmland, adding to the previous 1 million, making a total of 1.4 million mu. Therefore, the "Laozi" says: "A sage has no accumulation; he uses all for others, and thus himself becomes more abundant; having given it to others, he himself grows even more." Gong Shu is the one who embodies this.


### Duke Gong Shucao of Wei Is Ill

The Wei Gong Shucao was ill, and King Hui went to visit him. He said: "If Duke Gongshe is ill, if it cannot be concealed, what will become of the state?" Gong Shucao replied: "Shucao has a servant's son Gongsun Yang; I wish Your Majesty would entrust the affairs of the state to him." "If you do not listen to him, do not let him leave the borders [of the state]." The king did not respond. After leaving, he said to those around him: "Isn't it sad!" "Such a virtuous man as Gong Shucao, yet he thinks I must entrust the state affairs to Yang; isn't that absurd!"

After Gongsun Cao died, Gongsun Yang heard about it. After the funeral was completed, he went west to Qin, and King Xiao received him and employed him. Qin indeed became stronger day by day, while Wei grew weaker day by day. This was not Gong Shucao's folly, but King Hui's folly. The folly of a foolish person lies in regarding the one who is not foolish as being foolish.


### Su Zi for Zhao Unites Alliances: Persuading King Wei of Wei

Su Zi, acting as a mediator for Zhao to form an alliance, said to King Wei: "Your Majesty's territory in the south reaches Honggou, Chen, and Runan; it includes Xu, Yan, Kunyang, Zhaoling, Wuyang, and Xinqi;" "eastward it extends to the Huai, Ying, Yi, Huang, Zhuzao, Haiyang, and Wumang rivers;" "in the west lies the boundary of Changcheng;" "northward it reaches He Wai, Juan, Yan, Yan, and Suzao, covering a territory of one thousand li in area." "The names of these places may seem small, yet there are no fields or stables for raising cattle and horses, not even a place to graze them." "The population is numerous, the carriages and horses countless; they travel day and night without rest, no different from an army of three divisions." "I dare to say that Your Majesty's state is no less powerful than Chu." "Yet, the lateral advisors advise Your Majesty to pursue external alliances with the fierce and ferocious state of Qin, thereby encroaching upon the world. In the end, should a national disaster arise, your country will not escape its calamity." "To take advantage of Qin's power and use it to coerce one's own ruler from within is a crime without parallel." Moreover, Wei is a powerful state in the world; "Your Majesty is an enlightened ruler of the world." "Now, however, there is an intention to face west and serve Qin, declaring Wei as its eastern vassal, building imperial palaces, accepting the insignia of a subject state, performing sacrifices in spring and autumn. I secretly feel ashamed for Your Majesty."

"I have heard that King Goujian of Yue, with only three thousand scattered soldiers, captured Fu Chai at Gansui;" "King Wu led an army of 3,000 men and 300 chariots, and beheaded Zhou at the plain of Mu." "Were it because they had a large number of soldiers?" "It was truly due to their ability to inspire the seven virtues." "Now I secretly hear that Your Majesty's forces number more than 200,000 in military strength, with 20,000 Cangtou soldiers, 200,000 combat-ready troops, 100,000 attendants and laborers, 600 chariots, and 5,000 cavalry horses." "This far surpasses the forces of King Goujian of Yue and King Wu!" "Now, however, you are being swayed by the flattery of sycophantic ministers and wish to serve Qin as a subject." "To serve Qin necessarily involves ceding territory and offering hostages; thus, even before the army is deployed, the state has already been weakened." "All ministers who advocate serving Qin are sycophants, not loyal subjects." "To be a minister and yet carve up one's ruler's territory for the sake of foreign alliances, to seek momentary glory without regard for future consequences, to weaken the state for personal gain, and to use the power of mighty Qin from outside to coerce the ruler within in order to demand territorial concessions—Your Majesty should carefully examine such matters."

"The Book of Zhou says: 'Endless and unbroken, how can it be managed?'" "If even a single hair is not plucked, it will grow into an axe handle.'" "If one does not decide on precautions beforehand, great disasters will follow—what then can be done?" "If Your Majesty truly listens to me," "the six states will form a united alliance, pooling their strength and resources; then there will certainly be no threat from the powerful state of Qin." "Therefore, our lord, King Zhao, has sent me to present this humble strategy and offer a clear agreement. It is now up to Your Majesty's command." King Wei said: "I am unworthy; I have never had the opportunity to hear such wise instruction. "Now that my lord has conveyed King Zhao's decree, I will respectfully align our state with the alliance."


### Zhang Yi for Qin Advocates Horizontal Alliances to King Wei

Zhang Yi was serving Qin as a horizontal strategist and advised King Wei, saying: "The territory of the State of Wei does not exceed one thousand li in area, and its soldiers do not number more than three hundred thousand." The land is flat, the feudal states are accessible in four directions, and the roads converge without any obstruction from famous mountains or great rivers. From Zheng to Liang, it does not exceed one hundred li; from Chen to Liang, more than two hundred li. A horse can gallop and a person can hurry, reaching Liang without tiring. To the south it borders the territory of Chu, to the west that of Han, to the north that of Zhao, and to the east that of Qi. Soldiers are stationed at all four directions, with guards and outposts arranged in triple rows. Grain stores and river transport depots contain no less than one hundred thousand measures. The terrain of the State of Wei is a battlefield outside its national borders. If to the south, Wei allies with Chu but not with Qi, then Qi will attack it from the east; if to the east, it allies with Qi but not with Zhao, then Zhao will attack it from the north; if it does not ally with Han, then Han will attack it from the west; if it is not close to Chu, then Chu will attack it from the south. This is what is called a path of division into four parts and five fractures.

"Moreover, the feudal lords who form an alliance do so in order to ensure national stability, honor their ruler, strengthen their military forces, and gain fame. To form a vertical alliance is to unite the whole world, swear brotherhood, sacrifice white horses, and make an oath on the banks of the Huang Shui River in order to strengthen their commitment. Even among close brothers who share the same parents, there is still competition over money and property. And yet one wishes to rely on deceitful and treacherous schemes left over from Su Qin; it is clear that such a plan cannot succeed.

"If Your Majesty does not serve Qin, then Qin will send troops to attack the area outside the Yellow River, capturing Juan, Yan, Yan, and Suanzao, seizing Wei and taking Jinyang. Then Zhao will not move south; if Zhao does not move south, then Wei will not advance north; if Wei does not advance north, then the vertical alliance route is cut off; once the vertical alliance route is cut, then Your Majesty's state will find it impossible to remain free from danger. Qin, supported by Han, attacks Wei; Han, threatened by Qin, dares not refuse to obey. Qin and Han united as one state, the fall of Wei would be imminent; this is why I am concerned for Your Majesty. For Your Majesty's consideration, there is no better course of action than to serve Qin; if Wei serves Qin, then Chu and Han will certainly dare not act. Without the threat from Chu and Han, Your Majesty can rest easy with your head high on a pillow, and the state will surely be free of worries.

"Moreover, among all states that Qin wishes to weaken, none is more so than Chu; and among those who can weaken Chu, none are better suited than Wei. Although Chu has the reputation of being rich and powerful, in reality it is empty and weak; its soldiers may be numerous, but they are talkative and easily retreat, likely to flee northward and dare not fight resolutely. Wei's troops advancing south to attack will certainly defeat Chu. To weaken Chu and strengthen Wei, to attack Chu in accordance with Qin's interests, and internally transfer disaster to ensure the safety of our state—this is a wise course of action. If Your Majesty does not heed my advice, once Qin's troops march eastward, it will be too late even to seek submission to Qin.

"Moreover, many alliance advocates speak with great enthusiasm but are rarely trustworthy; having persuaded one feudal lord's king, they leave riding in his carriage; having secured an agreement with one state and then betraying it, they establish the foundation for becoming a marquis. Therefore, wandering scholars throughout the world do not cease day or night to clench their fists, glare fiercely, and gnash their teeth in speaking of the benefits of vertical alliances, using these arguments to persuade rulers. Rulers hear their words, are drawn into their arguments, and how could they not be dazzled? I have heard that accumulated feathers can sink a boat, numerous light objects can break an axle, and many voices can melt gold; therefore, I hope Your Majesty will carefully consider this." King Wei said: "I am foolish and dull-witted, having previously made a miscalculation. Please allow me to acknowledge Qin as my eastern suzerain, build an imperial palace, accept the coronation cap and sash, perform ancestral rites in spring and autumn, and cede the territory outside the Yellow River."


### Qi and Wei Form an Alliance to Attack Chu

Qi and Wei made an alliance to attack Chu, and Wei sent Dong Qing as a hostage to Qi. Chu attacked Qi and defeated it heavily, but Wei did not come to its rescue. Tian Ying was angry and intended to kill Dong Qing. Ganyi, on behalf of Dong Qing, said to Tian Ying: "Chu attacked Qi and heavily defeated it, but dared not advance further because Wei was about to hand [the situation] over to Qi, causing suspicion afterward. Now if you kill Dong Qing, this will show Chu that there is no support from Wei." Wei will become angry and ally with Chu; Qi must then be in danger. It would be better to honor Dong Qing so as to maintain good relations with Wei, and thus sow doubt in the mind of Chu."


### Su Qin Detained by Wei

Su Qin was detained by Wei and wanted to flee to Han, but the state of Wei closed its border passes and would not allow passage. Qi's envoy Su Li said to King Wei on his behalf: "Qi requests to use the land of Song as a fief for Jingyang Jun, but Qin will not necessarily do so. It is not that Qin would be uninterested in benefiting from an alliance with Qi to obtain the land of Song, but the reason it does not accept this offer is because it does not trust King Qi and Su Qin. Now that Qin sees how deeply Qi and Wei are at odds, then Qi will certainly not deceive Qin, and thus Qin would trust Qi. If Qi and Qin form an alliance and Jingyang Jun receives the land of Song, then this will not be in Wei's interest. Therefore, Your Majesty would do better to release Su Qin back eastward; Qin must certainly doubt Qi and will not listen [to its proposal]. "If Qi and Qin do not form an alliance, there is no worry for the world. If a campaign against Qi succeeds, then [Wei will gain] greatly expanded territory."


### Chen Zhen as an Envoy of Qin to Qi

Chen Zhen was an envoy of Qin to Qi. He passed through Wei and requested an audience with Xi Shou. Xi Shou declined Chen Zhen's request for an audience. Chen Zhen said, "The reason I have come eastward is because of official business. If you do not see me now, I will be on my way and cannot wait for another day." Xi Shou then received him. Chen Zhen said, "Do you dislike being busy? Why do you eat and drink without attending to affairs?" When there is no business, something will surely come." Xi Shou said, "Yan is not capable; I cannot obtain affairs to attend to. How could I dare to dislike being busy?" Chen Zhen said, "Please transfer the affairs of the world to you." Xi Shou asked, "How?" Chen Zhen said, "Wei has sent Li Cong with a hundred chariots as an envoy to Chu. You can take up residence among them and create suspicion." You tell King Wei, 'I have long-standing relations with Yan and Zhao. They often send people to summon me, saying that when there is no business I will surely come.' Now that I have no affairs, I shall pay a visit and go." Do not stay for long; the period should be ten or five days. "The king will certainly have no reason to stop you." "If you are allowed to go, next year speak in the court and say: 'I urgently needed to visit Yan and Zhao; I hastily arranged chariots and prepared for my journey.'" Xi Shou said, "Yes." He paid a visit to King Wei of Wei. The king agreed, and he immediately publicly announced that he was being sent as an envoy to Yan and Zhao.

When the envoys of other states heard about this, they all sent people to inform their respective kings: "Li Cong has departed with a hundred chariots to Chu, and Xi Shou is also departing with thirty chariots to Yan and Zhao." King Qi heard about this and feared that Wei would be acquired by other states before him. He entrusted affairs to Xi Shou, who accepted the responsibilities from Qi. King Wei of Wei blocked his envoy's mission. Yan and Zhao, upon hearing this, also entrusted affairs to Xi Shou. King Chu heard about this and said, "Li Cong made an agreement with me. Now Yan, Qi, and Zhao have all entrusted their affairs to Xi Shou; Xi Shou must want me, and I desire him as well." "He then betrayed Li Cong and entrusted his affairs to Xi Shou instead." King Wei said, "The reason I did not send Xi Shou was because I considered him unreliable. Now that four states have entrusted their affairs to him, I also entrust my affairs to him." Xi Shou thus took charge of the affairs of all under heaven and was once again appointed chancellor of Wei.


### Zhang Yi Slanders Chen Zhen to King Wei

Zhang Yi slandered Chen Zhen to King Wei, saying: "Zhen is good at serving Chu. He is seeking territory for them; he has gone out very far." Zuo Hua said to Chen Zhen: "Yi is favored by King Wei, who loves him dearly. Even if you try a hundred times to persuade him, he still will not listen." "You are better off using Yi's words as a resource and returning to King Chu." Chen Zhen said: "Good." He therefore sent someone to speak first to King Chu.


### Zhang Yi Wants to Exhaust Chen Zhen

Zhang Yi wanted to exhaust Chen Zhen, so he ordered Wei to summon and appoint him as chancellor in order to bring him here and enlighten him. Before he was about to leave, his son Chen Ying tried to stop his father's journey and said, "One must not fail to examine things that are profound. Zheng Qiang left the state of Qin and said, Ying is wise. The State of Wei wants to sever relations with Chu and Qi; it must therefore warmly welcome you. Those in Yingzhong who do not favor you want you to leave, and they will certainly advise the king to provide you with many carriages. When he arrived at Song, he claimed illness along the way and did not proceed further, sending someone to tell King Qi, "The reason why Wei is welcoming me is that they want to sever ties with Qi; and also Chu. "

King Qi said, "If you indeed did not go to Wei but came to see me instead, I will bestow a fief upon you." He therefore sent carriages of the rank of the Marquis of Lu to welcome him.


### Zhang Yi Flees to Wei

Zhang Yi went to Wei, and a general of Wei welcomed him. Zhang Chou advised the king not to admit Zhang Yi, but his advice was not accepted by the king. Zhang Chou withdrew and again advised the King, saying, "Have you also heard of an old concubine serving her lady?" "When a woman's beauty fades, she is valued only for her family connections." "Now my service to Your Majesty is like that of an old concubine to her lady." Therefore, Wei did not admit Zhang Yi.


### Zhang Yi's Plan to Ally Wei with Qin and Han

Zhang Yi wanted to ally Wei with Qin and Han to attack Qi and Chu. Huishi wanted to ally Wei with Qi and Chu in order to keep the army at rest. Many people spoke highly of Zhang Zi before the king. Huizi said to King Ye: "This is a small matter, yet those who say it can be done and those who say it cannot are exactly half; how much more so for major matters?" To ally Wei with Qin and Han in order to attack Qi and Chu is a major matter, yet all of the king's ministers believe it can be done. Do they not know that this is possible, or are they so convinced of its feasibility? And yet the ministers' knowledge and strategies are really this uniform? That it is possible, they have not been so clearly convinced; and the ministers' knowledge and strategies also are not entirely the same. Therefore, there must be some among them who are obstructed in their understanding. What is called a person who coerces the ruler is one who has lost half of his insight.


### Zhang Ziyi Serves as Prime Minister of Qin and Plots Against Wei

Zhang Ziyi served as prime minister of Qin and plotted against Wei. Qi and Chu were angry and wanted to attack Wei. Yong Ju said to Zhang Zi, "The reason why Wei appointed you as prime minister is because if you serve as prime minister, the state will be at peace and the people will have no worries." "Now that you are prime minister yet Wei suffers military attacks, this indicates a miscalculation by Wei." "Qi and Chu are attacking Wei; your position is certainly in danger now." Zhang Zi said, "Then what should I do?" Yong Ju said, "Please have Qi and Chu lift their attacks." Yong Ju addressed the rulers of Qi and Chu, saying, "Have you also heard about Zhang Yi's agreement with King Qin?" He said: 'If Your Majesty appoints me as prime minister in Wei, Qi and Chu will hate me and certainly attack Wei. "If Wei wins the battle, it means that the armies of Qi and Chu have been defeated, and I would then firmly gain control over Wei; "if Wei does not defeat them, Wei will certainly submit to Qin in order to preserve its state and must cede territory as a bribe to Your Majesty." "If you wish to attack again, their exhaustion would be insufficient to resist Qin."' "This is why Zhang Yi secretly formed an alliance with King Qin." "Now that you are attacking Wei where Zhang Yi serves as prime minister, it is making his strategy succeed for Qin; this is not the right way to corner Zhang Yi." The kings of Qi and Chu said, "Well spoken." They immediately lifted their attacks against Wei.


### Zhang Yi Wants to Serve Concurrently as Prime Minister of Qin and Wei

Zhang Yi wanted to serve as prime minister of both Qin and Wei. Therefore, he said to King Wei: "I request that Qin attack the three passes; Your Majesty may use this opportunity to make an agreement for Nanyang. The House of Han will then perish." Shi Yan said to Zhao Xian: "Why don't you support Zhang Yi with the help of Chu and recommend him as prime minister in Wei? The State of Han, fearing its own destruction, will certainly flee southward to Chu." "If Zhang Yi serves concurrently as prime minister of Qin and Wei, then you would surely serve concurrently as prime minister of Chu and Han as well."


### King of Wei's General and Chancellor Zhang Yi

The King of Wei's general Zhang Yi, Xi Shoufuli, so someone told the Marquis Gongshu of Han: "Zhang Yi has already united Qin and Wei." He said: "If Wei attacks Nanyang, and Qin attacks Sanchuan, the House of Han will certainly perish." Moreover, the reason why the State of Wei values Zhang Zi is because it wants to obtain territory; therefore, Han's Nanyang will be lost. Why don't you give up a little of Yaju as a pretext for gaining merit, so that the alliance between Qin and Wei can be broken? In this way, Wei will certainly turn against Qin and abandon Zhang Yi, support Han, and appoint you as its prime minister." Marquis Gongshu believed this to be true and therefore yielded to it; Xi Shou took credit for the achievement, and indeed became prime minister of Wei.


### Chu Xu Wei Liu Cheng

Chu Xu Wei Liu Cheng and he attacked Qi to preserve Yan. Zhang Yi wanted to defeat him and said to King Wei, "Qi fears the alliance of the three states. It will certainly return Yan territory to submit to Chu. Chu and Zhao must listen to this proposal but will not give Hui Cheng to Wei." This would mean that Your Majesty has lost a strategy with Chu and Zhao, while creating resentment toward Qi and Qin. Qi then attacked Zhao, captured Chenchou, reclaimed the occupied territory, and Xuchou and Dunqiu became endangered. Chu defeated the nine Yi tribes in Nanyang, captured Pei, and Xu and Yanling were now in danger. What Your Majesty has gained is only Qin Guan. Yet the roads through Song and Wei are under control, failure will drive you to Zhao, and success will allow you to annex Song and Wei." King Wei did not listen.


### Zhang Yi Informs Gong Zhong

Zhang Yi informed Gong Zhong, ordering that because of famine, King Han should be rewarded with territory near He'wai. The King of Wei was frightened and heard about Zhang Zi. Zhang Zi said, "Qin wants to rescue Qi, and Han wants to attack Nanyang. If Qin and Han unite and want to attack Nanyang, there is no difference." "Moreover, if the King of Wei consults with Qin, then if the King does not favor Qin, Han's capture of Bo will be certain." The King of Wei therefore favored Qin, trusted Han, expanded Wei, rescued Zhao, measured the Chu people, and hurriedly acted at Ge Xia. The campaign to attack Qi thus failed.


### Battle of Xuzhou

The Battle of Xuzhou, Xi Shou said to King Liang: "Why not appear to ally with Qi while secretly forming a bond with Chu?" Two states relying on Your Majesty, Qi and Chu will certainly fight. If Qi defeats Chu, and we take advantage of the situation, we are sure to capture the area beyond Fangcheng; if Chu hears that Qi has been defeated, and we take advantage of it, then the enmity of the Crown Prince will be avenged."


### Qin Defeats Dongzhou

Qin defeated Dongzhou and fought against Wei at Yique, killing Xi Wu. Wei ordered Gongsun Yan to remain in the border area taking advantage of his victory and requested humble words and ceding territory, in order to negotiate with Qin. Wei Douli said to King Wei, "I do not know whether Gongsun Yan will agree to cede much or little territory to Qin, but I can secure half of what he is willing to give and make Qin negotiate with Your Majesty." The king asked, "How?" He replied, "Your Majesty might as well bestow Guannei Hou (an internal pass marquis) title upon Douli and send him to Zhao. The king valued his mission and gave him generous provisions. He then made a public statement saying, "I have heard that Zhou and Wei sent Douli to offer Zhao to Fengyang Jun in order to listen to Qin." The Zhou ruler, Douli, and Fengyang Jun are sworn enemies of Rang Hou. Now the one carrying out the peace mission is Douli; the one deciding on territorial cession is Fengyang Jun. The Empress Dowager, fearing that the negotiations would not go through Rang Hou, and wanting to sabotage them, will certainly request a small territorial cession from Your Majesty in order to make peace with Dongzhou and Wei."


### King Qi Was About to Meet with the Chancellors of Yan, Zhao and Chu at Wei

The King of Qi was about to meet with the chancellors of Yan, Zhao, and Chu at Wei, making an agreement against Wei. King Wei was afraid and worried that they might plan to attack Wei, so he informed Gongsun Yan. Gongsun Yan said, "If the king gives me a hundred gold pieces, I will ask to defeat them." The king prepared a carriage and loaded it with one hundred gold pieces. Xishou, on the day when King Qi of Qi was expected to arrive, first sent fifty chariots ahead to Wei between Qi and Weiji. He traveled with one hundred gold pieces and used them to request an audience with King Qi, thus gaining a meeting. He then sat for a long time in ease, calmly discussing the mutual grievances among the chancellors of the three states. He said to King Qi, "Your Majesty made an agreement with the three states to encircle Wei.
